# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** American Psychological Association
- **Scope:** Publishes original empirical papers across all areas of personality and social
  psychology, organized into three independently edited sections: Attitudes and Social
  Cognition (ASC), Interpersonal Relations and Group Processes (IRGP), and Personality
  Processes and Individual Differences (PPID).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/psp/submit
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 1 full-text (Dugan, Fraley, Gillath, & Deboeck, "Testing the
  Canalization Hypothesis of Attachment Theory," PPID section, doi:10.1037/pspp0000488,
  accepted manuscript version) plus abstract/metadata review of several additional 2024–2025
  articles across sections via PsycNet/search-engine snippets. Full-text access beyond one
  article was blocked by paywalls; guidelines were sourced directly from the APA submission
  page, which is unusually detailed and section-specific, giving high confidence in the
  Hard Constraints section despite the small full-text sample.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Three sections with distinct requirements (identify target section before drafting):
- **ASC:** Introduction + Discussion combined ≤3,500 words; masked review; requires a
  200-word maximum Statement of Limitations immediately after the abstract, plus a
  limitations table (internal/statistical/generalizability assessment) and an Author
  Contributions statement (CRediT taxonomy).
- **IRGP:** Introduction + Discussion combined ≤5,000 words; maximum 5 studies presented
  in the main text (additional studies go to supplement with full reporting); Results
  sections expected to foreground figures over dense prose tables; explicit "alternative
  explanations" discussion required.
- **PPID:** No formal word limit; requires a cover letter (not just masked manuscript)
  disclosing author contact info, an originality statement, prior JPSP submission history,
  an ethical data-collection statement, and disclosure of prior publications using the
  same data.
All sections: a "Transparency and Openness" subsection within (or immediately following)
Method is mandatory, addressing sample-size justification, all exclusions, all measures,
JARS adherence, and data/code/materials location (or a stated legal/ethical reason for
withholding). This is a harder, more explicit version of FIELD_SKILL's general
"disclosure of exclusions/preregistration" norm — here it must be a labeled subsection,
not just narrative disclosure. Masked review is required for all sections (no author
names/affiliations/self-identifying grant or IRB info in the manuscript body).

### Length
Abstract capped at 250 words (see Abstract below); section-specific Intro+Discussion caps
as above; no total manuscript page limit specified beyond "as succinct as possible."

### Abstract
Exactly 250 words maximum, single paragraph, on its own page, followed by up to five
keywords/phrases. FIELD_SKILL does not specify a numeric cap; this is journal-specific
and ENFORCED (confirmed in the spot-checked article, which used a single ~200-word
unstructured paragraph ending in keywords).

### Citations
APA Style 7th edition (this is simply the field's standard manual, not a deviation).
Datasets, code, and materials must be cited in-text and in the reference list with
persistent identifiers/DOIs where available — an explicit, journal-stated extension of
FIELD_SKILL's general transparency expectations.

### Headings
No deviation from standard APA heading hierarchy; PPID papers commonly use a
"Theoretical Implications" or similarly named discussion subsection ahead of
"Limitations" (observed in spot-check) — this is PRACTICE ONLY, not a stated rule.

### Tables and Figures
Embedded within text near first reference (PPID/IRGP explicit requirement; ASC less
explicit but same practice observed). Figures: TIFF/JPG/EPS, color/halftone at 300 DPI,
black-and-white at 600 DPI; multipanel figures assembled into a single file; color
figure print fees apply ($900/$600/$450 tiered) — online color publication is free.
Dual-mode captions must describe color and grayscale versions (e.g., "the red (dark
gray) bars").

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No concurrent multi-journal submission.
- Must not withhold data for 5 years post-publication if requested for verification.
- No republishing previously published data as original.
- AI tools cannot be listed as an author; any generative AI use in drafting must be
  disclosed in the Method section and cited — a journal-specific requirement with no
  FIELD_SKILL analog.
- Manuscripts screened for text-overlap against a 40M+ document database.
- Computer code (if submitted) must use 8-point Courier New and is submitted separately
  from the main text; code exceeding 40 characters/line is rendered as an image in
  typesetting.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Beyond FIELD_SKILL's generic "Research Transparency" near-universal expectation, JPSP
requires a specifically labeled "Transparency and Openness" subsection in Method (ENFORCED
per guidelines; consistent with practice in the spot-checked article, which reported
preregistration links, OSF data/code/materials links, and exclusion criteria directly in
Method prose). The spot-checked PPID article also used footnotes for methodological
asides/caveats (e.g., clarifying a data-processing error found post hoc) rather than
folding them into main text — PRACTICE ONLY, not a stated rule, but a distinctive habit
worth noting for this journal's empirically dense papers.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Extensive use of online supplements for secondary/exploratory/robustness analyses is
the norm at ENFORCED-adjacent strength: the spot-checked article reported dozens of
supplementary tables (S1–S67) and treated the main text as reserved for primary,
pre-registered analyses, with robustness checks, alternative model specifications, and
exploratory extensions summarized in-text but detailed only in supplement. This is a
sharper version of FIELD_SKILL's general robustness-via-replication norm — here
robustness lives principally in the supplement rather than in the main narrative.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural "we," passive for procedure).
- Sentence and paragraph style: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Argumentation/CARS funnel introductions: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Statistical reporting (exact statistics, effect sizes, CIs): FIELD DEFAULT, if anything
  more rigorously followed than baseline (every focal estimate in the spot-checked
  article reported b/τ, SE, t or equivalent, p, and 95% CI).
- Hedging and causal-language caution: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Citation density and placement: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Discussion structure (restate findings, limitations, implications): FIELD DEFAULT.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting without a distinctly labeled "Transparency and Openness" subsection — this
  is checked explicitly by editors, not just implied by general open-science norms.
- Missing the section-specific word caps: an ASC or IRGP submission that treats the
  5,000-word IRGP or 3,500-word ASC combined Intro+Discussion limit as a soft guideline
  rather than a hard requirement risks desk rejection.
- Omitting the PPID cover letter's required disclosures (prior JPSP submission history,
  same-data prior publications) — this is a section-specific requirement not obvious
  from generic APA submission norms.
- Leaving identifying information (grant numbers, IRB institution names, repository
  owner names) in the masked manuscript body.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
