# Journal Writing Skill: IIM Kozhikode Society & Management Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publications (New Delhi), in association with Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode
- **Scope:** Bi-annual (semi-annual) peer-reviewed journal publishing rigorous, clear and widely accessible articles concerning business management, organizational behaviour, and broader society. Focus on connecting management research to academia, business, public institutions, NGOs, and government.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/ksm
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 articles from 2024-2025 (titles: "Alliance or Association? Exploring Work-Family Balance and Workplace Well-being"; "Hybrid workplace implementation challenges"; "Circular Economy and Consumer Behaviour"; "Fostering Stakeholders Trust through CSR Reporting"; "How Effective is Theory-Based Learning of Ethics in India"; "Tailored Conceptual Frameworks for Early Supplier Involvement")

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- **Full-fledged papers:** Standard IMRAD or variant structure (Introduction, Methods/Approach, Results/Findings, Discussion, Conclusion) appropriate to method type.
- **Brief submissions:** 1,000-2,500 words considered for inclusion in special sections; do not follow full-paper requirements.
- **Special issue papers:** Must not exceed 8,000 words (distinct from regular 10,000-word limit).
- **Regular papers:** Must not exceed 10,000 words including figures, tables, and annexures.

### Abstract
- **Format:** Unstructured (single-paragraph format without labeled sections).
- **Length:** 150-200 words (tighter than FIELD_SKILL baseline of 150-250 words).
- **Content:** Must concisely state research purpose, major findings, and conclusions.
- **No citations in abstract** (aligns with FIELD_SKILL).

### Keywords
- **Requirement:** Minimum 6-7 keywords (not optional; specified in guidelines).
- **Specificity:** Keywords must be as specific as possible to the research topic (not generic descriptors).
- **Placement:** Listed immediately after abstract.

### Citations
- **Style:** APA style (Author, Year).
- **Correspondence:** Every in-text citation must have a corresponding reference; every reference must be cited. Spelling and year must be identical between text and reference list.
- **Multiple citations:** Ordered alphabetically or chronologically (standard APA).
- **Et al. usage:** Three or more authors use "et al." from first citation (standard APA).
- **Density:** Typical for management field (FIELD_SKILL norms apply; no journal-specific relaxation noted).

### Headings
- **Convention:** Clear hierarchical heading levels required for logical organization and reader navigation.
- **Accessibility:** Visual hierarchy must support accessibility guidelines for diverse audiences.
- No detailed stylistic specification provided in guidelines; defer to FIELD_SKILL conventions.

### Tables and Figures
- **Requirement:** Clearly labeled with captions.
- **Positioning:** Placed strategically within manuscript.
- **Accessibility:** Must meet accessibility guidelines; ensure visual presentations are usable by diverse audiences (no fancy figures-only; captions are essential).
- **Word count:** Included in the 8,000-10,000 word limit (depending on article type).

### Explicit Requirements and Prohibitions
- **Double-blind peer review:** Reviewer identity withheld from authors; author identity from reviewers. Two independent reviews required for Revise/Accept decision.
- **Ethical approvals:** All papers reporting studies involving human participants, human data, or human tissue must state that relevant Ethics Committee or Institutional Review Board approved the study (full name, institution, approval number required).
- **Funding statement:** Required if applicable.
- **Declaration of conflicting interests:** Required section.
- **Data availability statement:** Required.
- **Acknowledgments:** Required section (format and content not specified in available guidelines).
- **Copyright:** Before publication, author must sign Journal Contributor's Publishing Agreement (exclusive license; author retains copyright, SAGE retains exclusive publication right).
- **Artificial intelligence policy:** Contemporary AI use in research must be disclosed/addressed (emerging requirement from SAGE).

### Desk rejection
- Papers not conforming to requirements returned for amendments before peer review.
- Papers out of scope or unsuitable desk rejected without peer review.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Word/Length Constraints: ENFORCED
**Delta:** Strict upper limit of 10,000 words (vs. FIELD_SKILL baseline of typically 10,000-15,000 for quantitative empirical work; 12,000-18,000 for qualitative; 8,000-14,000 for theoretical; 15,000-25,000 for reviews).

Journal explicitly caps at 10,000 for full papers, 8,000 for special issues. This is a RELAXATION relative to the longest FIELD_SKILL categories (review articles), but stricter than mid-tier expectations. **Impact:** Emphasize efficiency in literature engagement and evidence presentation; condense theory and discussion relative to FIELD_SKILL norms.

### Abstract Structure: ENFORCED
**Delta:** Unstructured (single paragraph), NOT structured with labeled sections (CARS framing or Purpose-Methods-Results-Implications).

FIELD_SKILL lists both structured and unstructured as acceptable. This journal mandates unstructured. **Impact:** Open with research gap and purpose, then embed methodology and results in flowing prose; avoid "Objective:", "Methods:", "Results:", "Conclusions:" subheadings.

### Keywords: ENFORCED (Specificity)
**Delta:** Minimum 6-7 keywords are non-negotiable (FIELD_SKILL does not specify keyword requirement as mandatory or minimum count).

Journal explicitly requires them and emphasizes specificity. **Impact:** After abstract, always list 6-7 keywords. Make them topic-specific, not generic labels like "management" or "organization."

### Citation Correspondence: ENFORCED (Strictness)
**Delta:** Guidelines emphasize strict one-to-one correspondence between in-text citations and reference list entries; "Corresponding citations must have identical spelling and year."

FIELD_SKILL does not flag this level of pedantry. **Impact:** Manually verify every citation against reference list; resolve any discrepancies before submission. This is a common desk-reject trigger.

### Accessibility and Inclusive Presentation: PRACTICE ONLY / EMERGING
**Delta:** Guidelines flag "accessibility guidelines" for tables/figures and "diversity, equity, and inclusion" in discourse.

FIELD_SKILL does not address this explicitly (though good practice). **Impact:** Ensure figures are not figure-only (caption + text description). Use inclusive language. Avoid idioms or culturally specific references without explanation. This is a lower-prevalence requirement but increasing in SAGE journals.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (first-person plural "we" throughout; active voice dominant; direct assertions with moderate hedging).
- **Article-type variation:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (quantitative deductive, qualitative inductive, theoretical/conceptual, and integrative review templates all used).
- **Argumentation and theory:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (explicit hypotheses/propositions; named theoretical frameworks; theory sections build and extend existing frameworks).
- **Methods reporting:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (quantitative: regression-based, fixed effects, robustness checks; qualitative: saturation, coding, triangulation).
- **Results/Findings presentation:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (tables primary vehicle for quantitative; organized by theoretical categories for qualitative).
- **Discussion structure:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (summary → theoretical implications → practical implications → limitations → future directions).
- **Hedging and epistemic stance:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (moderate hedging; "may," "suggest," "argue," "we propose").

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Abstract exceeds 200 words or contains citations:** Guard against creeping word count in abstract; citations are not permitted. This is a frequent desk-reject trigger.

2. **Mismatch between in-text citations and reference list:** The guideline is explicit: "every in-text citation must have a corresponding citation in the reference list and vice versa." A reference not cited, or a cited work missing from references, triggers desk rejection before peer review.

3. **Missing keywords or insufficient specificity:** Submitting without 6-7 specific keywords is grounds for desk rejection. "Management" and "strategy" alone are too generic.

4. **Exceeding 10,000-word limit (including tables/figures):** The word count is strict and includes all visual elements. Oversized tables or verbose figure captions add up. Trim aggressively; brief write-ups (1,000-2,500 words) are acceptable alternatives for shorter work.

5. **Structured abstract:** If you use "Objective:", "Methods:", "Results:", "Conclusion:" headings, expect desk rejection. Journal requires unstructured prose-only abstracts.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
