# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Experimental Psychology: General

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** American Psychological Association (APA)
- **Scope:** Publishes empirical articles of broad interest across experimental
  psychology, spanning social, developmental, clinical, neuroscience, and
  computational-modeling processes; work often crosses the boundaries of the
  other, more specialized JEP journals.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/xge and
  https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/xge/submit (both accessible)
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 confirmed via abstract/metadata (Bartsch, Souza,
  Mizrak, & Oberauer, 2025, "Is Working Memory a Gateway to Long-Term Memory?";
  a 2025 article on targeted recruitment ads and tokenization concerns among
  Black job applicants; general title/abstract sweep of 2024–2025 issues via
  search). Full-text access was not available (no EZProxy); assessment relies
  on APA's own guideline pages plus abstract/metadata-level checks. This is
  below the target of 15; access chain (PsycNet, APA site) is largely
  paywalled beyond abstracts, and guideline-page detail was unusually
  complete, which is the primary basis for the constraints below.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Required: title page with author affiliations, abstract, keywords (up to
five), **Public Significance Statement**, main text (Method, Results,
Discussion), a **Constraints on Generality (COG)** paragraph at the end of
the Discussion, references, tables, figures. Two article types: standard
articles (no fixed cap) and Brief Reports (see Length).

### Length
- Brief Reports: max 3,000 words (excludes title, references, affiliations,
  acknowledgments, figures/legends; includes abstract). Higher desk-rejection
  rate; must show high reliability of results, preferably with impact
  spanning more than one area of psychology.
- Standard articles: no explicit word/page cap stated.

### Abstract
Max 250 words, on a separate page. Followed by up to five keywords/phrases.

### Public Significance Statement (journal-specific, not a FIELD_SKILL item)
Required, separate from the abstract: 2–3 sentences (120–150 words),
written in plain language accessible to both professionals and lay
readers, explaining the real-world relevance of the findings.

### Constraints on Generality (journal-specific, not a FIELD_SKILL item)
Required closing paragraph of the Discussion (per Simons, Shoda, & Lindsay,
2017) that explicitly identifies and justifies the target population(s) to
which the findings are claimed to generalize.

### Citations
APA Publication Manual, 7th edition; alphabetical reference list; DOIs
preferred where available.

### Headings
No journal-specific deviation stated beyond standard APA Style heading
levels.

### Tables and Figures
Tables built with Word's table function (no tab/space alignment). Figures:
TIFF/JPG/EPS, 300 DPI (color line art) / 600 DPI (black-and-white line
art). Optional paid color printing ($900 first figure, $600 second, $450
each thereafter). Tables/figures placed near first text reference.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No concurrent or duplicate submission to other outlets; AI tools cannot be
listed as an author; no re-presenting previously published data as
original. Generative-AI use in manuscript preparation must be disclosed
per APA policy.

### Open Science / Transparency (journal-specific emphasis beyond FIELD_SKILL)
Mandatory "Transparency and openness" subsection in Method describing
compliance with TOP Guidelines: minimum Level 2 for data/code/citation
transparency, minimum Level 1 for materials/design transparency. Data and
code must be deposited in a trusted repository, or authors must state the
legal/ethical reason they cannot be shared. This is stricter/more
explicit than FIELD_SKILL's general "preregistration/materials disclosure
expected" language — here it is a required, labeled manuscript subsection
with defined compliance levels.

### Registered Reports
Journal accepts Registered Reports via two-stage review: Stage 1 (hypotheses,
rationale, design, methods) reviewed and provisionally accepted before data
collection; Stage 2 (completed study) reviewed only for adherence to the
preregistered plan, not for outcome.

### Masked Review
Optional (author's choice). If selected, author must strip identity clues
(grant numbers, IRB institution names, self-citations, repository links)
from the manuscript.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Structural Deviations
ENFORCED — Two structural elements are required at this journal beyond
generic FIELD_SKILL IMRaD expectations: the Public Significance Statement
(a distinct, separately labeled lay-audience paragraph) and the Constraints
on Generality closing paragraph. Neither is mentioned in FIELD_SKILL as a
universal field requirement, though FIELD_SKILL does note "generalizability
... expected to be explicitly qualified" — this journal operationalizes
that expectation into a mandatory, explicitly labeled paragraph.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED — The Transparency and Openness subsection with defined TOP
compliance levels (Level 2 data/code/citation, Level 1 materials/design)
is a more codified, checklist-style version of FIELD_SKILL's general
open-science expectation.

## Common Pitfalls
- Omitting the Public Significance Statement or writing it at the same
  technical register as the abstract (it must be lay-accessible).
- Omitting or under-justifying the Constraints on Generality paragraph, or
  treating it as a restatement of "Limitations" rather than an explicit
  target-population claim.
- Submitting a Brief Report without demonstrating cross-domain impact or
  high result reliability, which raises desk-rejection risk.
- Leaving out the labeled "Transparency and openness" Method subsection or
  failing to state a TOP compliance level.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
