# Journal Writing Skill: China Economic Journal

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis (Routledge), affiliated with CCER, National School of Development, Peking University, and The Chinese Economists Society (CES)
- **Scope:** Examination and analysis of current developments in the Chinese economy and government economic policy, emphasizing cutting-edge issues in China's economic development with long-term significance; interdisciplinary scholarship and commentary are explicitly welcomed alongside standard research articles.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/rcej20 (author instructions page returned 403 directly; guideline content recovered via CCER Peking University mirror at https://en.ccer.pku.edu.cn/publications/journal/241826.htm and https://ccj.pku.edu.cn/journal/SubmissionInstructions)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (titles/abstracts across Vol. 11–19, 2018–2026; full-text inaccessible behind paywall for all, so spot-check relies on titles, abstracts, and section-list metadata via IDEAS/RePEc and Taylor & Francis abstract pages). Titles included: "Local government land monopoly in China" (Qiu, 2025); "Social learning, agricultural extension, and fertilizer application in Nepal" (Bista, Duan & Xu, 2026); "Fallacy of the 'China Collapse' theory" (Lin, 2024); "Institution vs. strategy: the determinant of development" (Lin, Zhang & Liu, 2025); "Institutions, development, and causality" (Przeworski, 2025); "What does China's 'dual circulations' development paradigm mean" (Lin, 2021); "The reemergence of the issue of US 'external sustainability'" (Yu, 2022); "An Anatomy of the Chinese selectocracy" (Yao, 2018); and several forest-tenure special-issue papers (Xu, Deininger, Hyde et al., 2026).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Authors are instructed to limit heading depth to a maximum of three levels, with the article title as the first-level heading. No mandated section list (e.g., no required "Data" or "Identification Strategy" heading names) beyond this depth constraint. A cover page (title, author names, affiliations, postal address, email) is submitted separately from the anonymized manuscript body (single-anonymized peer review).

### Length
Not specified in accessible guideline text.

### Abstract
Structured differently from unstated FIELD_SKILL norm: abstract must be 100–150 words, unstructured (no required subheadings), accompanied by 3–4 keywords and JEL classification code(s). All three elements (abstract, keywords, JEL codes) are described as required for every contribution.

### Citations
No explicit citation style mandated in accessible guideline text; observed articles use author-year in-text citation, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
Maximum of three heading levels (see Structure).

### Tables and Figures
Statistics tables and figures must be supplied separately in Excel format (in addition to the Word manuscript). Referential sources (data provenance) for all tables and figures are explicitly required to be stated.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscripts must be original and not previously published "in journals, chapters of book or in any other formal ways in any language," and must not be under simultaneous consideration elsewhere without editor consent. Submissions may be in English or Chinese.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
This journal regularly publishes special issues built from short, single-author or small-team essay/commentary pieces by senior policy economists (e.g., Justin Yifu Lin, Yu Yongding, Yang Yao) that read as position papers or literature syntheses rather than the FIELD_SKILL empirical or theory-paper architecture (Data/Identification/Results, or Model/Analysis/Welfare). These pieces argue a thesis (e.g., "Fallacy of the 'China Collapse' theory") with narrative and citation-supported argumentation rather than an empirical results section. This coexists with conventional empirical papers (e.g., the Qiu land-monopoly paper, which follows a standard model-plus-IV-estimation structure matching FIELD_SKILL). PRACTICE ONLY / journal norm — authors should expect that both formats are acceptable, and that commentary/perspective-style contributions are a recognized and recurring category at this journal, unlike the FIELD_SKILL baseline which assumes empirical or theory papers exclusively.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Many issues are thematic special issues organized around a single topic (e.g., forest tenure reform, RCEP/CPTPP, central bank digital currencies, platform economy regulation), each opening with a short framing/review essay by the issue's guest editor(s). This special-issue framing is a recurring editorial practice not addressed by FIELD_SKILL. PRACTICE ONLY.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (empirical papers use "we"/active voice consistent with FIELD_SKILL; commentary pieces observed use a more discursive first-person-singular or impersonal voice appropriate to the essay format, but this is a consequence of the structural delta above, not a separate journal-wide voice rule)
- Statistical reporting: FIELD DEFAULT (empirical papers report magnitudes with quantitative specificity, e.g., IV-estimated causal effects, consistent with FIELD_SKILL)
- Citation density and format: FIELD DEFAULT (author-year, literature engaged in introduction, consistent with FIELD_SKILL)
- Hedging: FIELD DEFAULT (no evidence of a distinct hedging norm; empirical papers state findings directly, consistent with FIELD_SKILL)

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a manuscript without separately formatted Excel files for tables/figures data, which the guidelines require in addition to the Word document.
- Writing an abstract outside the 100–150 word band or omitting JEL codes/keywords, both explicitly required.
- Assuming a rigid empirical IMRAD structure is mandatory when targeting a special issue: this journal accepts and regularly publishes essay/commentary-style contributions, so authors submitting to a themed special issue should check whether the issue calls for a synthesis/perspective piece rather than a standard empirical paper.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
