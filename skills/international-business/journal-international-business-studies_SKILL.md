# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of International Business Studies (JIBS)

- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4* (World Elite)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These are ENFORCED requirements from official JIBS submission guidelines.

### Structure

**ENFORCED**

Required end-matter order (each component begins on new page):
1. Endnotes
2. References
3. Figures
4. Tables
5. Appendices

Typical manuscript structure:
- Introduction (state objective, motivation, context)
- Literature review (limited to items with direct bearing on topic)
- Theoretical framework (for theoretical papers)
- Methodology/Empirical section
- Results
- Discussion/Conclusion

Long methodological details should be placed in separate appendix.

### Length

**ENFORCED**

- **Major contributions:** ~10,000 words (≈30 pages double-spaced)
- **Narrower contributions:** ~7,000 words

Word count **includes:** abstract, text, endnotes, references  
Word count **excludes:** tables, figures, online supplemental appendices

Manuscripts considerably longer than these guidelines will be returned immediately without review.

### Abstract

**ENFORCED**

- **Word limit:** 200 words maximum
- **Format:** Unstructured, self-contained
- **Content:** Must summarize entire article, not just conclusions
- **Prohibition:** No reference citations in abstract

### Citations

**ENFORCED** - APA author-date style

**In-text citations:**
- Single source: (Offringa, 2006)
- Multiple sources: alphabetical order, separated by semicolons: (Buckley & Casson, 1976; Dunning, 1993a, 1993b; Rugman & Verbeke, 2003)
- 2 authors: both names every time (Rugman & Verbeke, 1998)
- 3-6 authors: all names first citation, then first author + "et al." for subsequent
- 7+ authors: first author + "et al." for ALL citations, including first
- Direct quotations: page number after year, colon + space: (Boddewyn, 1988: 538)

**Reference list:**
- Alphabetical order by author surname
- Format: Author, Initial. Year. Title. *Journal Name*, volume(issue): page range.
- Journal and book titles italicized

### Keywords

**ENFORCED**

3-6 keywords required. First 3 must be selected from official JIBS Keywords list.

### Headings

**GUIDELINE ONLY** (inferred from sister journal JIBP)

Three levels maximum:
- **Level 1:** Left-aligned, bold, 14-point (major sections)
- **Level 2:** Left-aligned, bold, 11-point, title case
- **Level 3:** Indented, italicized, sentence case, followed by period then text

### Formatting

**ENFORCED**

- **Font:** 11-point Times New Roman throughout
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced all body text
- **Margins:** 1 inch (2.5 cm) on all sides
- **Alignment:** Left-aligned
- **Sentence spacing:** One space between sentences, NOT two

### Tables and Figures

**ENFORCED**

- Word count excludes tables, figures, and online supplemental appendices
- Place at end of manuscript after references
- Each table/figure begins on new page
- Tables and figures cited consecutively in text
- Vertical rules should NOT be used in tables
- Every table must have descriptive title
- Units must be included in column headings for numerical measurements

### Hypotheses

**ENFORCED**

- Separately state each hypothesis
- Number each hypothesis
- Indent the hypothesis paragraph

Example format:
> Hypothesis 1. Title length is inversely related to the probability of publication.

### Statistical Reporting

**ENFORCED**

- Report **actual p-values**, not just threshold levels (not "p < .05" but the actual value)
- **Explicitly discuss effect sizes** of relevant estimated coefficients
- Provide appropriate citations for statistical methodology
- Full statistical details must be in paper itself (not relegated to appendices unless methodological details)

### Explicit Prohibitions

**ENFORCED**

1. **No "news column" style** (multi-column layout)
2. **No reference citations in abstract**
3. **No spurious citations** to JIBS articles (viewed unfavorably, interpreted as poor scholarship)
4. **No teaching materials/methods** manuscripts
5. **No pure literature reviews**
6. **No practitioner-only** manuscripts
7. **No manuscripts without theoretical contribution** to IB
8. **No manuscripts without specific relevance** to international business domain
9. **No two spaces between sentences** (use one space only)

### Writing Style

**GUIDELINE ONLY**

From official guidelines: "Write in an interesting, readable manner. Vary your sentence structure. Keep sentences short so the reader does not get lost before the end of a sentence. Do not write long, run-on paragraphs."

## Common Pitfalls

Based on explicit guidelines and prohibitions:

1. **Spurious self-citation:** Gratuitous citations to JIBS articles are viewed unfavorably and interpreted as signal of poor scholarship. Engage with extant IB knowledge authentically, not performatively.

2. **Abstract over-runs:** 200-word limit is strict. Abstracts must summarize entire article, not just conclusions. No citations allowed.

3. **Length violations:** Manuscripts considerably longer than 10,000 words (major contributions) or 7,000 words (narrower contributions) are returned immediately without review.

4. **Statistical reporting:** Failure to report actual p-values (instead reporting thresholds like "p < .05") or failure to discuss effect sizes will likely trigger revision requests.

5. **Scope mismatch:** Manuscripts must make theoretical contribution to international business AND have specific relevance to IB domain. Teaching materials, literature reviews, or practitioner-only pieces will be desk-rejected.

6. **Two spaces between sentences:** Guidelines explicitly prohibit this. Use one space only.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
