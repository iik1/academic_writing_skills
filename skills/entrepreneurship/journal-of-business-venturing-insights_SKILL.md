# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Business Venturing Insights

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** Companion journal to Journal of Business Venturing (ABS 4*). Publishes "short, high-quality insights" emphasizing rapid dissemination of novel, preliminary, or provocative entrepreneurship research that may not yet meet the completeness threshold of the flagship journal. Open to empirical findings, theoretical insights, methodological contributions, and commentary across multiple disciplines.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- **Manuscript length:** 12 double-spaced pages maximum (12-point font), excluding references, tables, and figures. This is a hard constraint and represents a substantial reduction from the standard entrepreneurship article length.
- **Reference lists, tables, and figures:** Placed on separate pages.
- **Article types accepted:** empirical research (including unusual findings, atheoretical descriptions, non-findings, and replications), theoretical contributions, simulations, scale/measurement development, methodological advances, and commentary.

### Length
- **Main text:** 12 pages maximum (double-spaced, 12pt font).
- **Abstract:** 250 words maximum, unstructured format.
- **Keywords:** 1 to 7 required for indexing; avoid multi-word phrases.
- **Highlights:** 3 to 5 bullet points required at submission, each a maximum of 85 characters (including spaces).

### Abstract
- **Format:** Unstructured (not structured with labeled fields such as Purpose/Methods/Results/Conclusion).
- **Word limit:** 250 words maximum.
- **Standalone requirement:** Must stand alone without reference citations; if citations are essential, provide author(s) and year(s).
- **Abbreviations:** Avoid non-standard or uncommon abbreviations; define all essential abbreviations at first mention within the abstract.

### Citations
- **Citation style:** Author-date in-text citations (Author, Year) in parentheses. Multiple authors: (Author1 and Author2, Year) for two; (Author et al., Year) for three or more.
- **Reference format:** Journal abbreviation: J. Bus. Ventur. Insights. ISSN: 2352-6734. Full formatting follows Elsevier's author-date convention (similar to APA).

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Tables and Figures
- **Graphical abstract:** Optional but encouraged at submission.
- **Figures and tables:** Integral to the results narrative; treated as part of the manuscript structure.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts exceeding 12 pages will not be accepted.
- Non-standard or undefined abbreviations in abstract will be flagged.
- Keywords as multi-word phrases are discouraged.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Manuscript Length and Scope Flexibility
**ENFORCED / RELAXATION**

The 12-page limit is a critical delta: this journal accepts substantially shorter manuscripts than the FIELD_SKILL baseline (which assumes full-length empirical articles with extensive Methods, Results, and Discussion sections). The journal explicitly welcomes "preliminary" research—unusual findings, non-findings, atheoretical descriptions, and replications—that would not meet the completeness or robustness thresholds of the ABS 4* Journal of Business Venturing. This represents a formal relaxation of the FIELD_SKILL expectation for full empirical grounding and theoretical explication. Authors should anticipate that Methods sections may be abbreviated, Results may be presented concisely without extensive tables, and Discussion/Limitations may be streamlined relative to FIELD_SKILL norms.

### Abstract Format and Structure
**ENFORCED**

The journal requires an unstructured 250-word abstract (no labeled Purpose/Methods/Results/Conclusion fields). This deviates from some entrepreneurship journals that use structured abstracts; verify each journal's preference individually. The unstructured format places a premium on clear prose and logical flow rather than formulaic section labels.

### Highlights and Graphical Abstract
**ENFORCED**

Highlights (3–5 bullet points, max 85 characters each) are mandatory at submission and are a journal-specific requirement not mentioned in FIELD_SKILL. These distill the novel contribution into punchy, practitioner-accessible bullet points, reflecting the journal's emphasis on "insights" for rapid dissemination. A graphical abstract is optional but encouraged. These supplementary elements are uncommon in ABS 4* entrepreneurship journals and reflect the Insights journal's emphasis on accessibility and rapid uptake.

### Rapid Publication Timeline
**GUIDELINE ONLY**

The journal targets three-month turnaround (submission to decision/publication). While this is stated as a goal, treat it as an expectation for review speed rather than a writing constraint; however, it does imply that authors should assume streamlined review and avoid overly lengthy or complex submissions that may invite extended revision requests.

### Flexibility on Empirical Completeness
**GUIDELINE ONLY / PRACTICE ONLY**

The journal's scope explicitly welcomes "unusual findings, atheoretical descriptions, non-findings or replication." This deviates from FIELD_SKILL's assumption of a complete deductive hypothesis-testing spine (Hypothesis Development section, full Methods with construct measurement, Results with regression tables, Discussion mapping contributions back to theory). Authors of single-study papers, preliminary findings, or descriptive/exploratory work should not assume they must provide the full apparatus outlined in FIELD_SKILL. A brief Methods section (e.g., one page) with less granular detail on construct operationalization, and a focused Results section with simple tables or figures, may be appropriate here.

### Citation Density
**FIELD DEFAULT**

Citation density likely remains high in the introduction and theory sections (consistent with FIELD_SKILL), but the overall shorter format may result in fewer total citations. Follow FIELD_SKILL guidance on citation placement and paraphrase-vs.-quotation norms; this journal likely adheres to those conventions.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding the 12-page limit:** This is a hard constraint. Reviewers will not engage with manuscripts that breach it. Allocate space ruthlessly: 2–3 pages Introduction, 1–2 pages Methods, 1–2 pages Results, 2–3 pages Discussion/Limitations, and lean on references.

2. **Over-justifying incompleteness or lack of robustness:** While the journal welcomes preliminary work, authors must still articulate clearly what the limitation is and why it is acceptable for this venue. A one-sentence mention of "single study" is insufficient; discuss trade-offs (e.g., internal validity vs. speed of insight) and what the paper offers despite constraints.

3. **Neglecting the Highlights section or treating it as an afterthought:** Highlights should be written with practitioners and rapid-skimming readers in mind. Generic or overly academic bullet points defeat the purpose. Each should convey a single, actionable or novel insight in plain language.

4. **Submitting a rejected JBV (ABS 4*) manuscript without substantial reworking:** JBV Insights is not a "fallback" venue for papers rejected from the flagship journal. If you are recycling a manuscript, acknowledge in your mind that you are repositioning it as a brief insight or unusual finding, not simply shortening it. Reframe the contribution accordingly.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
