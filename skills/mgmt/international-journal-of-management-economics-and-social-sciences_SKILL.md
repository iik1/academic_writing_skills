# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Management, Economics and Social Sciences

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** UCHPRO / co-published with National Doctor of Business Administration Society (NDBAS); formerly IJMESS Int'l Publishers
- **Scope:** Multidisciplinary quarterly covering management, strategic management, organizational behavior, HRM, business ethics, economics, econometrics, and social issues; welcomes empirical, theoretical, and review articles using qualitative, quantitative, or experimental methods.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.ijmess.com/instructions.php
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 abstracts across 2022–2025 (full text was not accessible — PDF fetch failed to parse; abstract pages and tables of contents used instead). Titles: "Debt-Led-Growth Hypothesis (DLGH) and Productive Constraints" (2022); "Economic Globalization and Economic Performance Nexus" (2023); "Determinants of Dividend Payout Ratio of Nigerian Deposit Money Banks" (2024); "Macroeconomic Effects of Public Debt in Nigeria" (2024); "Manufacturing Firms' Performance and Operational Innovation" (2024); "Team Level Antecedents and Consequences of Ethical Leadership: A Review and Future Directions" (2024); "The Adoption of Remote Working Practices in the Nigerian Banking Sector" (2024); "Forensic Accounting in Fraud Detection and Prevention" (2024); "Assessing the Impact of Official Development Assistance on Primary School Performance in Côte d'Ivoire" (2025); "Newcomers' Organizational Socialization: A Systematic Literature Review" (2025).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Prescriptive, numbered section order (more rigid than FIELD_SKILL's four flexible templates): Title → Abstract → Keywords → JEL Codes → Introduction → Literature Review → Theoretical framework/hypothesis → Methodology → Results → Discussion → Conclusion → Implications → Limitations and future directions → References → Acknowledgments (if applicable). "Implications" and "Limitations and future directions" are required as standalone labeled sections, not folded into Discussion/Conclusion.

### Length
Empirical articles: max 35 pages; review articles: max 40 pages (inclusive of references, appendices, tables, figures). Hard ceiling of 50 pages. Overage charged at USD 30/page. This is a page-based cap, substantially shorter than FIELD_SKILL's word-count norms for top outlets.

### Abstract
Unstructured, max 200 words, written in past tense. Must cover purpose, study design/methodology, findings, and conclusions/implications.

### Citations
Modified APA: in-text citations alphabetically ordered within parentheticals (matches FIELD_SKILL); et al. for 3+ authors (same as FIELD_SKILL); but page numbers are cited in a distinct "Author, year: page number" format for direct quotes/specific claims — not otherwise specified in FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
5 keywords required (FIELD_SKILL does not specify a count). 1–3 JEL codes required — a convention absent from FIELD_SKILL, which is written for pure management journals with no JEL classification norm.

### Tables and Figures
Tables in 10-pt Times New Roman (body text 12-pt). Tables/figures placed at manuscript end, not inline, with "Insert Table/Figure X about here" markers in text. Consecutive numbering in separate series for tables vs. figures. Statistical significance markers explicitly specified: †p<.10, *p<.05, **p<.01, ***p<.001 — notably includes a marginal/10% significance tier.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No use of Generative AI or similar technologies to generate text for the Literature Review or any other section.
- No simultaneous submission to other journals until a final decision is received.
- Conference presentation of the work must be disclosed in the cover letter.
- Blind review: all identifying information (names, affiliations, author notes) must be removed from the manuscript body.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Section-by-section tense/voice prescriptions are explicit and more granular than FIELD_SKILL's general voice guidance: Introduction (active voice, minimal first person); Literature Review (present tense, past tense only for historical context); Methodology (past tense); Results (past tense, passive voice, objective reporting without interpretation); Discussion (present tense, active voice, minimal first person, interpretive); Conclusion and Implications (present tense). ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; consistent with the empirical abstracts observed, which report findings in past tense and recommendations in present/prescriptive tense).

Standalone "Implications" and "Limitations and future directions" sections are a structural requirement beyond FIELD_SKILL's Discussion/Conclusion flexibility. GUIDELINE ONLY — abstracts alone cannot confirm section-level compliance in the full text sample available.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Sample skews heavily toward empirical papers on African/Sub-Saharan economies (Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire, pan-African panels) using applied econometrics (ARDL, panel data) alongside qualitative interview-based management studies and the occasional systematic literature review. This is a submission-pool characteristic, not a style rule — PRACTICE ONLY, not to be treated as a topical requirement.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting with word-count-style length planning instead of the journal's page-based cap (35/40 pages inclusive of everything) risks overage fees or rejection above 50 pages.
- Omitting the standalone "Implications" and "Limitations and future directions" sections — FIELD_SKILL allows folding these into Discussion/Conclusion, but this journal requires them as separate labeled sections.
- Using AI-assisted drafting for the Literature Review, which is explicitly prohibited here (not a universal field rule).
- Forgetting JEL codes and exactly 5 keywords, both explicit requirements not standard across all management outlets.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
