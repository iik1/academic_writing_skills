# Journal Writing Skill: Economic Inquiry

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell (for the Western Economic Association International, WEAI)
- **Scope:** General-interest economics journal across all fields, with an explicit editorial mandate to publish work "accessible to non-specialist economists."
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14657295/homepage/forauthors.html ; https://www.weai.org/submit-a-manuscript ; https://www.weai.org/ei-journal-policies ; https://www.weai.org/ei-no-revisions-policy
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 15 titles/abstract-level records across 2022-2026 issues (RePEc/IDEAS listings, UConn Econ department page, Wiley abstract pages where accessible). Full text was paywalled (HTTP 402) for all Wiley-hosted articles attempted; assessment relies on titles, available abstracts, and the confirmed existence of a recurring "Replication of..." article type. Titles/records reviewed: "Tariffs, taxes and wages in the age of artificial intelligence" (Gilbert, Koska & Oladi); "Regional impacts of international tourism boycott" (Greaney & Kiyota); "Social networks and spread of immigration attitudes" (Firsin); "Cash versus contactless mobile payment" (Li, Liu & Riyanto); "Replication of 'How much does immigration boost innovation?'" (Wright); "Understanding cultural persistence and change: A replication of Giuliano and Nunn (2021)" (Bertoli et al.); "Trade and ethnolinguistic differences: A replication and extension" (Gonzalez & Özak); "Assessing the benefits of education in early childhood" (Woodyard, Sass & Fazlul); "Employment effects of minimum wage indexing" (Miller, Wagner & Plemmons); "Does giving tax debtors a break improve compliance and income?" (Boning et al.); "On the design of an optimal immigration policy" (Lopez-Velasco); "Managing migration crises" (Amuedo-Dorantes, Bucheli & Lopez); "Selective reporting of placebo tests in top economics journals" (Dreber, Johannesson & Yang); "Designing school choice mechanisms" (Xu & Hammond); "Invisible hurdles: Gender and institutional differences in the evaluation of economics papers" (Ersoy & Pate); "The Evolving Impacts of COVID-19 on Gender Inequality in the U.S. Labor Market" (Couch, Fairlie & Xu, 2022 Best Article).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section list beyond the general empirical/theory paper norm; FIELD_SKILL structure applies. Title page must be a separate file from the manuscript, containing author names/affiliations/emails, the title, keywords, and JEL codes (double-blind review — main manuscript file must not identify authors).

### Length
Manuscripts should not exceed 35 pages including references, footnotes, tables, and figures; longer papers considered if warranted. Appendices of 3 pages or fewer may be typeset into the article; longer appendices go into an online annex/supporting file.

### Abstract
Maximum 100 words (tighter than the unstated field norm, which tolerates 150-250 words at top journals). Must not contain equations, diagrams, or footnotes. Opening sentence should convey the paper's major conclusion.

### Citations
No distinct citation-style mandate found beyond standard author-year; FIELD_SKILL norm applies.

### Headings
Title limited to a maximum of 75 characters and spaces (title page requirement). No other heading-format mandate found.

### Tables and Figures
Source information required in captions for all tables/figures; copyright permissions required for any reproduced copyrighted material.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Minimum three keywords and minimum three JEL codes required (principal JEL code listed first) — stricter/more explicit than typical field practice.
- Data and code from empirical papers must be archived in a public repository (e.g., openICPSR) with instructions to reconstruct key tables/figures, subject to editor-approved exceptions for privacy/ethics.
- A signed Disclosure Statement (conflicts of interest, IRB approval status for human-subjects research) is required from all authors; incomplete submissions may not be reviewed.
- Simultaneous submission elsewhere is prohibited; must be original work.
- Double-blind review: manuscript body must not reveal author identity.
- Distinctive "no revisions" submission track: authors may opt in (stated in the cover letter) to a process where the editor only accepts or rejects the paper as submitted, with no revise-and-resubmit cycle. The default track still allows revisions but is capped at one or two rounds; editors will not request revisions absent a clear path to acceptance.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
GUIDELINE ONLY / journal-specific emphasis: the journal explicitly endorses McCloskey's "Economical Writing" (Economic Inquiry, 1985) as a house style ideal — favoring short, plain sentences, cutting material that doesn't advance the argument, and placing a sentence's emphasis at its end. This pushes prose somewhat plainer and more compressed than the FIELD_SKILL default of "medium to long, technical-density" sentences, though this is an aspiration stated in submission materials rather than a strictly enforced rule; treat as a stylistic tilt toward brevity and directness rather than a redefinition of field norms. Authors are also explicitly encouraged to use gender-inclusive language and avoid bias/stereotypes in phrasing — a stated diversity/inclusion norm not present in FIELD_SKILL.

### Structural Deviations
PRACTICE ONLY, high-prevalence in this sample: Economic Inquiry runs a recognized "Replication of ___" article type — standalone papers whose explicit purpose is to replicate or extend a previously published study (3 of 15 sampled titles were replications). This is a structural/genre pattern not covered by FIELD_SKILL, which treats replication as embedded robustness checks rather than a standalone publishable article type.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED: Abstract cap of 100 words is a RELAXATION-in-reverse — actually a tightening relative to typical top-journal practice — appropriate for ABS 3 to adopt as journal-specific rather than field-wide.
ENFORCED: "No revisions" submission track is unique to this journal among sampled ABS journals; mention it when advising on submission strategy (author must decide and state the choice in the cover letter).

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 100-word abstract cap or including equations/footnotes in the abstract (explicit prohibition).
- Failing to separate the title page (with identifying info, JEL codes, keywords) from the blinded manuscript body, given double-blind review.
- Omitting the Disclosure Statement or a data-availability statement pointing to a public repository — submissions can be held up or not reviewed.
- Not declaring the "no revisions" option in the cover letter if intending to use it, or expecting multiple revise-and-resubmit rounds under the standard track.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
