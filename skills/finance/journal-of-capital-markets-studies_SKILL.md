# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Capital Markets Studies

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing, in association with the Turkish Capital Markets Association (TCMA)
- **Scope:** Broad capital markets research spanning market efficiency, asset pricing, corporate governance, capital structure, banking, cryptocurrency, and emerging/frontier market development. Platinum Open Access (no APCs), funded by TCMA.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jcms (author guidelines subpage returned 403/CAPTCHA during this review; information below is drawn from Emerald's general author guidelines, which JCMS follows, plus WebSearch snippets referencing JCMS's own guidelines page)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (title/abstract/keyword level only, via IDEAS/RePEc and search-engine snippets; full-text access blocked by Emerald CAPTCHA in this environment). Titles: "Statistical studies of financial reports and stock markets" (Sunder); "Understanding sustainability for socially responsible investing and reporting" (Bradford, Earp, Williams); "Towards a well-functioning stock market in context" (Haslam, Ji, Sun, 2018); "Asset allocation models: evolution and diversity – a systematic literature review" (2024/2025); "The impact of exchange rates on stock market performance of the Emerging 7" (El-Diftar, 2023); a financial-leverage/firm-performance study on Tokyo Stock Exchange firms. **Access limitation:** could not reach full text for any article (Emerald paywall/CAPTCHA); assessment is based on abstracts, keywords, and visible metadata only.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict. Derived from Emerald's standard journal-author guidelines (JCMS follows the standard Emerald template; a JCMS-specific guidelines page exists but was inaccessible in this environment).

### Structure
Standard Emerald structure: title page, structured abstract, keywords, article classification (paper type), main text, references. No JCMS-specific section mandate found beyond Emerald's default.

### Length
Not independently confirmed for JCMS; Emerald's general word-count convention applies (structured abstract, references, and table/figure text count toward the total, with each table/figure adding roughly 280 words to the total). Treat as GUIDELINE ONLY given inability to confirm a JCMS-specific cap.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED.** Structured abstract required, using Emerald's standard sub-headings — typically Purpose, Design/methodology/approach, Findings, and Originality/value (a Research limitations/implications and/or Practical implications sub-heading may be added where relevant) — maximum 250 words total including keywords/classification. This is a clear delta from FIELD_SKILL, which describes a single unstructured paragraph abstract as the field norm. Confirmed in spot-checked abstracts (e.g., asset allocation review: "This study aims to..."/"...employing a...methodology"/"The analysis reveals..." pattern consistent with Purpose/Design/Findings headings).

### Citations
**ENFORCED.** Harvard author-date style per Emerald convention (e.g., "(Adams et al., 2006)", with "et al." italicized), not a footnote or numbered system. This is consistent with FIELD_SKILL's author-year parenthetical norm, so largely FIELD DEFAULT — no meaningful delta in format, though Emerald's italicization of "et al." is a house-style detail not present in FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
No JCMS-specific deviation found; Emerald standard numbered headings apply. FIELD DEFAULT.

### Tables and Figures
No JCMS-specific rule found beyond Emerald's general practice (each table/figure counted toward word limit as noted above). Not otherwise distinguishable from FIELD_SKILL.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Standard Emerald originality/plagiarism and simultaneous-submission prohibitions apply; nothing JCMS-specific found.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
**ENFORCED — Structured abstract.** Unlike the single-paragraph abstract FIELD_SKILL describes as the ABS 4*/4 norm, JCMS (via Emerald house style) requires a labeled, structured abstract (Purpose / Design-methodology-approach / Findings / Originality-value, ≤250 words). Authors submitting FIELD_SKILL-style unstructured abstracts to JCMS should restructure them.

**PRACTICE ONLY — Article classification/paper type field.** Emerald submissions typically also require authors to select an explicit "paper type" (e.g., research paper, literature review, conceptual paper) at submission, a category not present in FIELD_SKILL's ABS 4*/4 description. Sample included at least one explicit systematic-literature-review paper, suggesting JCMS accepts a wider range of paper types (including SLRs and bibliometric reviews) than is typical at ABS 4*/4 finance outlets.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
**RELAXATION — Broader scope and paper types than ABS 4*/4 norm.** As an ABS 1 journal, JCMS visibly publishes descriptive/institutional pieces (e.g., "The flash crash: a review"), systematic literature reviews, and regional/emerging-market applied studies alongside conventional identification-driven empirical papers. This is a relaxation of the FIELD_SKILL expectation that papers foreground a strong identification strategy and novel contribution framing — not a new norm, but a lower and more heterogeneous bar consistent with its ABS 1 tier.

No deltas were identified in voice/register, statistical reporting conventions, or citation density from the abstracts and metadata available; full-text inaccessibility limits confidence here (see Sources).

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a single-paragraph, unstructured abstract — JCMS/Emerald requires the labeled structured-abstract format (Purpose/Design-methodology-approach/Findings/Originality-value), unlike the ABS 4*/4 norm.
- Omitting the explicit "paper type" classification required at Emerald submission (research paper, literature review, viewpoint, etc.).
- Assuming a narrow reduced-form identification-strategy paper is required — JCMS's ABS 1 tier and TCMA affiliation accommodate broader paper types (literature reviews, bibliometric studies, descriptive/institutional pieces, emerging-market applied work).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
