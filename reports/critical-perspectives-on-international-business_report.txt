Deltas Identified
=================

1. CRITICAL REFLEXIVITY AND STANCE
   Status: GUIDELINE ONLY (stated in journal mission; visible in editorials and editorial calls)
   Confidence: HIGH (backed by editorial statements and journal scope description)
   Delta: CPoIB frames itself as the only journal "exclusively supporting critically reflexive discussions" and explicitly calls authors to "creatively and critically question the hegemony of transnational corporations, managerial orthodoxy and the dominant academic discourse." This goes beyond the field baseline's expectation of balanced, scholarly contribution. The journal expects authors to adopt a reflexive, engaged, argumentative stance toward international business as a power/societal phenomenon, not just contribute empirical or theoretical knowledge in a neutral register.

   FIELD_SKILL baseline on voice: "formal, scholarly register...accessible to a broad management audience...first-person plural standard...impersonal/passive for literature synthesis"
   CPoIB expectation: Scholarly, formal, but explicitly positioned as critical/reflexive engagement with power and legitimacy of international business activity

2. SCOPE BEYOND TRADITIONAL IB (Sustainability, Environment, Gender, Decolonization, Labor, Civil Society)
   Status: PRACTICE ONLY (observed in recent article titles and special issue calls; not hard-mandated)
   Confidence: MEDIUM (inferred from editorial selections and special issue themes; not in accessible author guidelines)
   Delta: Journal accepts and appears to favor papers addressing broader societal implications (environment, labor practices, gender equity, decolonization, community effects, civil society) alongside traditional IB topics (strategy, markets, MNE behavior). While FIELD_SKILL expects discussion of "managerial/policy implications," CPoIB authors frequently extend this to systemic critique and societal impact assessment.

3. COVER LETTER AS SIGNALING DEVICE
   Status: GUIDELINE ONLY (explicitly stated that cover letters are "strongly encouraged," especially for new/early-career authors)
