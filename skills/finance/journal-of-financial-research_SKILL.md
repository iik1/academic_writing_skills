# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Financial Research

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell (for Southern Finance Association & Southwestern Finance Association)
- **Scope:** Publishes original scholarly research across all major finance areas — investments, portfolio management, capital markets and institutions, corporate finance, corporate governance, capital investment, financial accounting, experimental finance, and international finance. Aims to combine research quality with readability.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://jfresearch.org/guidelines/ (society page); https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14756803/homepage/forauthors.html (publisher page); submissions via Research Exchange
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (abstract/summary level, via search-engine and publisher/SSRN snippets): Jiang, Li, Li & Wang "Academic CEOs and corporate innovation" (2025); Azmat & Macdonald "The Psychological Cost of Debt" (2025); Floros, Singh & Suzuki "Information absorption in stocks with short-selling constraints" (2025); Jiang, Shen & Zhou "Does legal protection of trade secrets reduce the cost of debt?" (2025); Devos, Elliott, Li & Tippit "Employee diversity and litigation risk" (2026); Duong, Nguyen & Vu "The value of social capital in small business lending" (2025); Fairbanks & Winters "Are we playing the same 'research' game in business?" (2026); Bradley, Mondello, Morgeson & Williams "The causal effect of local product market shocks on equity holdings" (2026); Javakhadze, Pennathur & Silverstein "Managerial Opportunism and Corporate Investment Efficiency" (2025); Sherrill & Upton "Active ingredients: How actively managed holdings shape target date fund performance and costs" (2026).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Title page (author details, affiliations, correspondence, acknowledgements, funding disclosures) submitted separately from the anonymized main document. Abstract placed on its own page after the title page, followed by JEL classification code(s). Main body numbered from Introduction to Conclusion using Arabic numerals (1, 1.1, 1.2, etc.).

### Length
No explicit overall word/page limit stated in accessible guidelines.

### Abstract
Single paragraph, **no more than 100 words** — substantially shorter than the FIELD_SKILL norm of 150-250 words. JEL codes follow directly after the abstract.

### Citations
APA 7th edition style, author-date in parentheses. Reference list alphabetical at end of manuscript.

### Headings
Primary headings: bold, ALL CAPS, Arabic numerals, left margin. Subsection headings: bold, sentence case, left margin. (FIELD_SKILL specifies Arabic numbering only, not this capitalization/boldface convention.)

### Tables and Figures
Must "stand alone" with descriptive, self-explanatory column headings; all variables defined. Each table or figure presented on its own separate page.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Write in active voice; explicitly avoid impersonal framing such as "This paper tests..."
- Eliminate excess verbiage and redundancies.
- Do not use italics for emphasis.
- Use footnotes, not endnotes; keep footnote number and length to a minimum.
- Only original, previously unpublished work; screened via Crossref Similarity Check (iThenticate); duplication without citation is desk-rejected.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
ENFORCED / RELAXATION-adjacent tightening: guidelines explicitly forbid the impersonal "This paper tests..." construction that FIELD_SKILL treats as an occasional acceptable variant. JFR requires active, first-person ("we"/"I") framing throughout, with no impersonal fallback. Spot-checked abstracts (e.g., Bradley et al., Javakhadze et al.) consistently narrate in active constructions ("the authors find," "they find," summarizing "we" claims), consistent with the stated rule.

### Structural Deviations
ENFORCED: heading format (bold ALL CAPS primary headings) is a specific typographic convention not present in FIELD_SKILL, which only addresses numbering.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED: abstract length cap of 100 words is markedly tighter than the FIELD_SKILL 150-250 word norm — this is the single most consequential delta for drafting. Authors must compress research question, method, and finding into roughly a third of the space assumed by the field baseline.

GUIDELINE ONLY (production convention): one table/figure per page — a Wiley formatting requirement at submission, not a content or reasoning convention.

- Hedging calibration (confident on well-identified findings, hedged on mechanism/interpretation): FIELD DEFAULT
- Contribution framing and literature engagement: FIELD DEFAULT

## Common Pitfalls
- Drafting an abstract at the FIELD_SKILL default length (150-250 words) and having to cut it by more than half to meet JFR's 100-word cap.
- Using stock impersonal openers ("This paper examines...", "This study tests...") that the guidelines explicitly flag for avoidance — rewrite as "We examine..." or an active-voice equivalent.
- Formatting section headings in the generic numbered style common elsewhere without the required bold/ALL CAPS treatment for primary headings.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
