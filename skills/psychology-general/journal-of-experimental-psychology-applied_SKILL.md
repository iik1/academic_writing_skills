# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** American Psychological Association (APA), on behalf of APA Division 21
- **Scope:** Publishes original empirical investigations in experimental psychology
  that bridge practically oriented problems and psychological theory, spanning
  perception, memory, decision-making, learning, attention, and human performance,
  as well as abnormal, cognitive, developmental, educational, forensic, health,
  human factors, social, organizational, and sport psychology. Review articles
  are accepted only occasionally, when they contribute significantly to an
  important topic; empirical work is strongly prioritized. Rejection rate ~87%.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/xap/submit (and /xap/index, /xap/about)
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (sample-page listings and PsycNet abstract/PSS
  snippets; full text was not accessible — see Sources). Titles included:
  "Friend-Shield Protection From the Crowd" (De Vries & Lee, 2022);
  "Quantifying the Effects of Fake News on Behavior" (Greene & Murphy, 2021);
  "Explaining How Long CO2 Stays in the Atmosphere" (Joslyn & Demnitz, 2021);
  "The Psycholinguistic and Affective Processing of Framed Health Messages"
  (Liu, Mikels, & Stine-Morrow, 2021); "Masculinity Contest Culture Reduces
  Organizational Citizenship Behaviors" (Koc, Gulseren, & Lyubykh, 2021);
  "Spatial Cognitive Implications of Teleporting Through Virtual Environments"
  (Cherep & Lim, 2020); plus PSS-referenced articles on group-relevant
  processing and eyewitness lineup administration.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Required Method-section content includes detailed participant demographic
reporting (age, sex, gender, racial identity, ethnicity, socioeconomic
status, clinical diagnoses, disability status, sexual orientation as
relevant). Discussion must include an explicit **"Constraints on Generality"**
subsection identifying and justifying the target population(s) to which
findings are claimed to generalize. A **"Transparency and Openness"**
subsection documenting compliance with TOP Guidelines is required. Author
contributions must be reported using the CRediT taxonomy. An informed-consent/
IRB-compliance statement is required.

### Length
Abstract: no more than 200 words. Public Significance Statement: 2–3 brief
sentences, 120–150 words. No overall manuscript page limit stated.

### Abstract
Unstructured (not headed with structured labels), ≤200 words, followed by
up to five keywords/brief phrases. A separate, mandatory **Public
Significance Statement** (120–150 words) must be written in plain language
accessible to both professionals and lay readers, stating the practical
relevance of the findings.

### Citations
APA 7th edition. All data, code, and materials used or produced must be
cited both in text and in the reference list (not just described).

### Headings
Not specified beyond APA 7th-edition heading levels.

### Tables and Figures
Tables built with Word's Insert Table function (no tabs/extra spacing).
Figures as TIFF/JPG/EPS, 300 DPI (color/halftone) or 600 DPI (black/white).
Color figure publication carries a fee ($900 first figure, $600 second,
$450 each additional). Computer code must be submitted as separate files;
code lines exceeding 40 characters are rendered as images rather than text.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No PDF submissions (Word/OpenOffice only). No concurrent submission
elsewhere. No web-downloaded graphics. No duplicate publication. AI tools
cannot be named as an author. Default review is unmasked; masked review
must be explicitly requested, and if so, self-citations, institutional
identifiers, and identifying repository links must be removed from the
manuscript.

### Preregistration / Transparency
Empirical submissions (including meta-analyses) must meet at least the
"requirement" level on all TOP dimensions: preregistration status and
public protocol links, participant characteristics, psychometric
properties of measures, and the planned data-diagnostic/analytic strategy
must all be disclosed. Analysis code must be posted to a trusted
repository unless legal/ethical constraints prevent it.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Structural Deviations
Two structural elements are ENFORCED here beyond what FIELD_SKILL
describes as field-general: (1) a **Public Significance Statement** as a
distinct, separately labeled abstract-adjacent element aimed at lay
readers — FIELD_SKILL does not mention this element at all; (2) a
mandatory, explicitly labeled **"Constraints on Generality"** subsection
within Discussion, which sharpens FIELD_SKILL's general expectation that
"claims about generalizability... are expected to be explicitly
qualified" into a required, separately headed subsection with justified
target populations. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; consistent with the
journal's applied-research mission of specifying to whom findings apply).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Titles skew toward naming the applied context or real-world phenomenon
directly in the title (e.g., "fake news," "eyewitness lineups,"
"organizational citizenship," "climate change attitudes") rather than
abstract constructs alone — consistent with, and a sharper version of,
FIELD_SKILL's applied-question framing. PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence
basis (titles only; not confirmed against full text at scale), but
directly consistent with the journal's explicit "bridge practical
problems and psychological theory" mission statement, so treat as a
reasonable structural expectation for framing rather than a strict norm.

## Common Pitfalls
- Omitting or under-developing the Public Significance Statement, or
  writing it at the same technical register as the scientific abstract
  rather than in lay language — this is a distinct, separately reviewed
  requirement, not an optional extra.
- Treating "Constraints on Generality" as boilerplate rather than a
  substantive, population-specific justification tied to the actual
  sample and design.
- Submitting as PDF, or with figures/tables that don't meet the DPI/format
  specs — guidelines state noncompliant manuscripts may be returned
  without review.
- Failing to disclose all TOP-required elements (preregistration status
  and link, full participant demographics, measure psychometrics,
  analytic plan) at submission rather than only upon request.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
