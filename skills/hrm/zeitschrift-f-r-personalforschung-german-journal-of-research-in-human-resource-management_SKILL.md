# Journal Writing Skill: Zeitschrift für Personalforschung / German Journal of Human Resource Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** SAGE (formerly Rainer Hampp Verlag)
- **Scope:** Publishes qualitative and quantitative empirical research, meta-analyses, literature reviews, and conceptual/theoretical papers in human resource management, employment relations, organisational behaviour, and personnel economics. Methodologically pluralistic; open to wide range of approaches. Quarterly publication with international editorial board.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/gjh (note: full details may require SAGE access)
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** Limited due to paywall; 3 recent references identified but full-text access restricted. Reliance on guidelines, editorial statements, and journal scope descriptions (2024-2026).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure

**Standard article sections** (follows IMRaD-with-theory macro-structure consistent with FIELD_SKILL):
No deviation stated in guidelines. Empirical articles follow introduction, literature review/theoretical framing, methods, results/findings, discussion, conclusion structure as described in field baseline.

### Length

**Manuscript:** Approximately 10,000 words (word count includes abstract, body, references; precise boundaries not specified in available guidelines).
**Abstract:** 200–300 words, unstructured format.

**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL does not specify word limits. This journal imposes a moderate constraint at 10,000 words—approximately equivalent to a 25–30 page journal article. This is substantially shorter than top-tier economics journals but typical for applied management journals at ABS 2 tier.

### Abstract

**Format:** Unstructured (single paragraph or block format; no section headers such as "Background," "Methods," "Results," "Conclusion").
**Word count:** 200–300 words.
**Placement:** Between title and main body of manuscript.

**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL specifies structured abstract format in some ABS 4*/4 journals (background/objective/methods/findings/implications). This journal uses unstructured abstract. Recommended approach: concisely state purpose, major findings, and conclusions in flowing prose rather than structured sections.

### Citations

**Style:** SAGE Harvard reference style (author-date in-text; alphabetical reference list at end).

**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL does not specify citation style. APA is common in HRM field at ABS 4*/4 tier; SAGE Harvard (author-date variant) is the specific requirement here. Ensure formatting aligns with SAGE Harvard guidelines.

### Headings

No explicit guidance provided in available guidelines. Standard practice from FIELD_SKILL (numbered or hierarchical section structure) assumed.

### Tables and Figures

No explicit constraints stated in available guidelines. Standard practice from FIELD_SKILL (tables and figures integrated into text, captioned, with note sources) assumed.

### Explicit Prohibitions

- **Language:** English only (mandatory since October 2020). Submissions in German are no longer accepted.
- **Anonymization:** Manuscript must be fully anonymized for peer review. Remove all author names, identifying headers, footers, and affiliations from the main manuscript document; place identifying information on separate title page only.
- **Submission system:** Must use ScholarOne Manuscripts platform (mc.manuscriptcentral.com/ghrm); direct email submissions not accepted.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Methodological Pluralism and Scope

**Explicit statement:** Journal description emphasizes openness to "a wide range of methodological approaches" and publishes "both theoretical articles as well as empirical work." Contributions include empirical (qualitative, quantitative, or experimental) studies, meta-analyses, literature reviews, and conceptual papers.

**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL baseline (derived from ABS 4* and ABS 4 journals) reflects a bias toward hypothesis-driven, quantitative designs in leading journals, with qualitative work less dominant. This ABS 2 journal explicitly positions itself as equally welcoming to qualitative, quantitative, experimental, and conceptual contributions without a hierarchical preference.

**Implication:** Do not assume reviewers at this journal expect a quantitative hypothesis-testing design. Qualitative, interpretive, and conceptual contributions are positioned as fully legitimate. Methods sections may be shorter for conceptual papers compared to empirical work, reflecting the pluralistic scope.

### Tier-Level Relaxations

**ABS 2 versus ABS 4/4\*:** This journal is positioned at ABS 2 (lower tier). Expectations around econometric sophistication, sample size, methodological rigor, and statistical reporting may be relaxed compared to the field baseline. 

**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL baseline derives from ABS 4* and ABS 4 journals, which have higher selectivity and more demanding review standards. At ABS 2, reviewers may be more lenient on:
- Advanced quantitative methods (panel models, multilevel structures) are not required; simpler regression designs acceptable.
- Sample size constraints (smaller qualitative samples, smaller quantitative N acceptable).
- Statistical reporting precision and robustness checks (optional rather than expected).

**Note:** This is a relaxation, not a lowering of standards. Rigorous work in whatever methodology chosen is still expected.

### Internationalism and Scope Diversity

**Explicit statement:** Journal is described as "a leading peer-reviewed resource for the international HR community" with "global reach" and an "international editorial board," welcoming "scholarly work from regions outside German-speaking countries."

**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL (based on UK/USA-centric top journals) may reflect Western, Anglophone norms. This journal's positioning as a "permeable enclave" suggests openness to non-Western contexts, non-English-speaking labour markets, and perspectives from European and international scholarship traditions (e.g., German-language theoretical traditions). 

**Implication:** Regional context studies, comparative international analyses, and non-Western empirical settings are actively welcomed. Framing literature review around European or German scholarship traditions is acceptable.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Submitting in German:** Many legacy submissions from German-speaking authors may have been accepted pre-2020. English-only requirement is now absolute (since October 2020). Do not attempt German submissions.

2. **Over-complex methods for ABS 2 tier:** Avoid treating this journal as if it has the econometric or statistical sophistication expectations of ABS 4 journals. A well-designed, clearly reported simpler study is preferred to an over-engineered one with poor exposition.

3. **Insufficient anonymization:** Ensure all identifying information (author names, institutional affiliations, acknowledgments that reveal identity, self-citations with full names) is removed from the main manuscript document before upload. Include only on the title page.

4. **Underestimating qualitative contributions:** Do not assume this journal's review culture is quantitative-dominated. Qualitative and conceptual papers have equal standing. Position qualitative work with confidence.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
