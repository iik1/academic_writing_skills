# Journal Writing Skill: Australian Accounting Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell, on behalf of CPA Australia
- **Scope:** Accounting professional practice spanning financial reporting, auditing and assurance, ethics and governance, management accounting, taxation, treasury, public sector accounting, and sustainability/ESG reporting. Explicitly aims to bridge academics, practitioners, and policymakers, and welcomes case studies useful for training/educating accountants — a broader, more practitioner-facing remit than the ABS 4*/4 archival mainstream.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/18352561/homepage/forauthors.html (accessed via search-engine cache/snippets; direct fetch returned HTTP 402)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (via RePEc/IDEAS abstract pages and search snippets): Pattnaik, Kumar & Burton (2021, 31(2):150-164); Weber (2023, 33(2):110-113); Ali & Mustafa (2023, 33(3):313-332); Tarca (2020, 30(4):231-242); Timpson & Bayerlein (2021, 31(1):22-34); Bradbury & Howieson (2022, 32(4):409-410); Potter, Ravlic & Wright (2013, 23(1):18-28); Chu, Liu, Tsang & Zeng (2025, 35(3), climate-linked contracts); plus 2024/2025 issue tables of contents (Vol. 34-36) for structural/topical breadth.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Free Format submission is offered: authors may submit in their preferred formatting style at original submission (single file or separate files); only at revision stage must figures/tables be split into separate files. No rigid required-section template is enforced at initial submission, unlike the numbered-section norm implied by FIELD_SKILL for top-tier journals.

### Length
No explicit page/word limit for the main manuscript was found in accessible guideline text (Free Format submission de-emphasizes formatting rules at first submission).

### Abstract
Unstructured abstract, maximum 200 words (shorter than is typical for ABS 4*/4 journals in FIELD_SKILL, which describes a similarly short single paragraph but without a stated cap). In addition, authors must supply a separate "summary at a glance" of approximately 60 words, written for a table-of-contents audience to attract readers — a distinct requirement not present in FIELD_SKILL.

### Citations
No journal-specific citation format found beyond standard Wiley/APA-style author-date; treat as FIELD_SKILL default.

### Headings
No distinct heading/numbering convention documented; Free Format submission implies flexibility at initial submission (see Structure above).

### Tables and Figures
May be embedded in the main file or submitted separately at first submission; must be separate files at revision. No other journal-specific rule found.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Standard originality requirement: submission implies the work has not been published or submitted elsewhere except as a brief conference abstract. ORCiD is required at submission. No other explicit prohibitions found in accessible guideline text.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in the empirical articles sampled (Ali & Mustafa; Tarca; Chu et al.). However, the journal regularly publishes short editorial/commentary and "prognostication" pieces (e.g., Weber 2023; Bradbury & Howieson 2022) that carry no abstract and use a more discursive, opinion-driven voice than FIELD_SKILL's empirical register. This dual-track mix (empirical articles + practitioner commentary/review pieces) is PRACTICE ONLY — not stated in guidelines but visible across the sample and consistent with the journal's explicit practitioner/policymaker audience.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm where empirical (insufficient full-text access to confirm reporting density in detail; abstract-level evidence consistent with baseline).

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm — no delta observed at abstract level.

### Structural Deviations
Free Format submission (ENFORCED per guidelines) is a genuine relaxation relative to the rigid, numbered-section structure FIELD_SKILL describes for ABS 4*/4 journals — label as RELAXATION, appropriate to this journal's ABS 2 tier. The journal also runs literature-review/synthesis articles (e.g., Ali & Mustafa 2023, a systematic review of 54 studies) and qualitative interview-based studies (Timpson & Bayerlein 2021) alongside archival-empirical work, indicating a broader structural range than the ABS 4*/4 hypothesis-testing template — PRACTICE ONLY.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
"Summary at a glance" (~60 words, ToC-facing) is a distinct, ENFORCED requirement not addressed anywhere in FIELD_SKILL.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 200-word abstract cap or omitting the separate ~60-word "summary at a glance" — both are enforced submission requirements distinct from the main abstract.
- Assuming a rigid ABS 4*-style numbered-section template is required at first submission; Free Format submission means reviewers will not reject on formatting grounds at this stage.
- Writing only for an academic-specialist audience: given the journal's explicit practitioner/policymaker remit, framing of motivation and implications should be accessible to a professional accounting audience, not purely archival-research peers.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
