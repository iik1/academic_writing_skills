# Journal Writing Skill: Current Directions in Psychological Science

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** SAGE (in association with the Association for Psychological Science, APS)
- **Scope:** Short, invited/proposal-based review articles that distill a program of
  cutting-edge psychological research for a broad audience — psychologists,
  students, and scientifically literate laypeople — rather than reporting new
  primary data.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/current_directions/cd-submissions (also mirrored at https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/current_directions/instructions-for-authors and https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/cdp)
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (abstracts/summaries only, full text paywalled) —
  Impett, Park, & Muise (2024) "Popular Psychology Through a Scientific Lens:
  Evaluating Love Languages..."; Gold & Luck (2025) "Abnormalities in Attention
  and Working Memory in Schizophrenia: The Hyperfocusing Hypothesis"; Park, Ho,
  Hallez, Kaur, Srinivasan, & Zhao (2025) "The Psychology of Poverty: Current
  and Future Directions"; Sidney, Scheibe, Zahrn, Brown, & Thompson (2025)
  "Developing Effective Interventions for Math Anxiety"; Gentner & Forbus (2025)
  "The Structure-Mapping Engine..."; Vazire (2024) "The Next Chapter for
  Psychological Science"; Ryff (2024) "The Privilege of Well-Being in an
  Increasingly Unequal Society"; Cole & Kuhn (2024/2025) "What Can Conjuring
  Tell Us About Cognition? The Future of the Science of Magic"

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Unsolicited full manuscripts are not accepted — the journal works from brief
invited proposals (title, abstract, ≤3-page pitch, rationale for fit, likely
references); only invited/accepted authors submit full manuscripts. Final
manuscript order: cover page (word count, contact info) → title page (authors,
affiliations) → abstract (with 3–5 keywords) → main text with figures/tables
integrated at first mention → Recommended Reading (3–5 items) → acknowledgments
and endnotes → references. There is no separate Method/Results/Discussion —
this is a synthesis-review format, not IMRaD.

### Length
Strict maximum of 2,500 words for abstract + main text combined. Maximum 30
references (closer to 20 preferred). Typically 1–3 authors; 5-author cap
without prior Editorial Office approval (field baseline places no such cap).
Recommended Reading items (3–5) do not count toward the word limit.

### Abstract
10–200 words, accompanied by 3–5 keywords.

### Citations
APA 7th edition. List all authors unless 21+, in which case list the first 19
then the last, preceded by ellipsis. DOIs required for online-only journal
articles.

### Headings
Endnotes only — footnotes are prohibited; use consecutive superscript numbers.
Main headings recommended to break up text; subheadings permitted if clearly
distinguishable (looser and less prescriptive than a fixed IMRaD heading set).

### Tables and Figures
Integrated into text at first discussion, not collected at the end (explicit
exception to standard APA placement). Submit in native file format (Excel,
Word), not TIFF/JPEG except photographs. Keep simple — preferably 1–3 figures,
ideally ≤2 panels each. Captions must be fully self-explanatory without
reference to the text. Color encouraged where it aids clarity.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No simultaneous submission elsewhere; no previously published material; no
copied passages longer than two sentences from other publications; unvetted/
unpublished work is not eligible for the Recommended Reading list.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Sharper than FIELD_SKILL's "formal but not ornate": guidelines explicitly
instruct authors to write in "ordinary and everyday (jargon-free) language,"
replace technical terms with familiar words, spell out acronyms, and supply
examples for abstract concepts. The target readership is explicitly broader
than psychologists (students, general public), which is a stronger
accessibility mandate than FIELD_SKILL's baseline register. ENFORCED —
consistent across all spot-checked abstracts (e.g., Impett et al.'s love-
languages piece and Ryff's well-being piece both read as trade-accessible
prose with minimal technical vocabulary).

### Statistical Reporting
Sharp departure from FIELD_SKILL's hard norm of exact test statistics,
p-values, and effect sizes for every focal test. CDPS guidelines state
explicitly that "significance tests and p values are not necessary to
report"; means, SDs, and percentages are acceptable, and effect
sizes/correlations should be explained in plain English rather than reported
as statistics. ENFORCED — no spot-checked abstract contained inferential
statistics; all describe findings narratively (e.g., Gold & Luck's
hyperfocusing synthesis, Park et al.'s poverty-mechanisms review).

### Citation Density
Lower than FIELD_SKILL's already-graduated review-journal density: hard cap
of 30 references (≈20 preferred) for a 2,500-word article is a much sparser
citation load than empirical or even typical review articles in this field.
ENFORCED by the explicit limit.

### Structural Deviations
FIELD_SKILL already names CDPS as a review-format exception (thematic
headings, no IMRaD), but does not capture the specifics: (1) the
Recommended-Reading end-section unique to this journal, replacing/augmenting
a standard reference-heavy discussion; (2) the invited-proposal gatekeeping
model, meaning most style decisions are made in a 3-page pitch stage before
full drafting; (3) figures/tables integrated in-text rather than end-placed,
an explicit deviation from standard APA convention. ENFORCED.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Endnotes-not-footnotes and the 5-author soft cap are journal-specific
mechanics with no FIELD_SKILL equivalent. ENFORCED.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Argumentation/rhetorical funnel structure (CARS-style gap framing): FIELD DEFAULT
- Hedging and epistemic caution (causal language reserved for causally
  identified designs, generalizability qualified): FIELD DEFAULT — evident in
  Park et al.'s poverty review and Ryff's well-being piece, which frame claims
  associatively and qualify scope
- Sentence/paragraph style (short-medium, signposted transitions): FIELD DEFAULT,
  reinforced rather than contradicted by the accessibility mandate
- Disagreement with prior work framed empirically/non-personally: FIELD DEFAULT

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a full unsolicited manuscript instead of the required 3-page
  proposal — most submissions are invited or proposal-gated.
- Retaining p-values/test statistics and dense technical vocabulary carried
  over from an empirical-paper draft; CDPS explicitly discourages both.
- Exceeding the 2,500-word or 30-reference limits, which are strict rather
  than aspirational.
- Placing figures/tables at the end of the manuscript instead of integrating
  them at first mention in text.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
