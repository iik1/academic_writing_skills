DELTAS FOUND

  5 dimensions with observable deltas from FIELD_SKILL baseline:

  1. Citation Density and Engagement
     - TYPE: RELAXATION (ABS 1 vs. 4*/4 baseline)
     - Slightly lower citation saturation per claim in intro/theory sections
     - Fewer chains of 3+ supporting citations per sentence

  2. Results Reporting and Statistical Transparency
     - TYPE: PARTIAL ENFORCEMENT / PRACTICE ONLY
     - Effect sizes inconsistently reported (~60% of articles include them)
     - Significance tests and coefficients universal; effect sizes optional

  3. Practical/Managerial Implications
     - TYPE: RELAXATION
     - Brief (1–2 paragraphs) rather than substantial subsection
     - Some articles omit explicit section entirely

  4. Discussion Structure and Contributions
     - TYPE: GUIDELINE ONLY (inconsistently followed)
     - Variable subsection labeling and organization
     - Some merge "Contributions and Implications" or separate Limitations

  5. Voice and First-Person Plural Usage
     - TYPE: SLIGHT RELAXATION
     - Less frequent in methods and results sections
     - Passive voice or impersonal tone more common than FIELD_SKILL norm

  Bonus finding:
  6. Hedging Intensity
     - TYPE: SLIGHT INCREASE
     - More frequent hedging throughout (not just Limitations/Discussion)
     - Softened causal claims in Results vs. FIELD_SKILL baseline

FIELD_SKILL OVERRIDES

  Hard constraints from author guidelines that override FIELD_SKILL:

  - Word limit: 8,000 words nominal (exceptions up to 10,000 under editor
    discretion) — FIELD_SKILL silent on this

  - Abstract structure: Empirically observed as structured (purpose, method,
    findings, implications) ~150–250 words, 4–6 keywords — more specific
    than FIELD_SKILL baseline

  - Citation style: APA 7th Edition (Emerald standard) — FIELD_SKILL does
    not specify

  - Heading levels: 2–3 levels typical (H1 for main, H2/H3 for subsections)
    — FIELD_SKILL does not specify

  - Ethical approval: Mandatory detail (committee, approval #, date,
    guideline reference) for human subjects research — FIELD_SKILL mentions
    but does not detail this requirement
