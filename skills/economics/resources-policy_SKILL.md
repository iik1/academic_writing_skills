# Journal Writing Skill: Resources Policy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** Resources Policy is an international journal devoted to economics and policy issues related to mineral and fossil fuel extraction, production, and use. It publishes research analyzing public policy, economics, social science, geography, and finance in areas of mining, non-fuel minerals, energy minerals, fossil fuels, and metals, covering topics including mineral markets, project evaluation, mining and sustainable development, resource taxation and regulation, and strategic minerals.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/resources-policy/publish/guide-for-authors (paywalled; guidelines extracted from search results and reference management sites)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 (Fleming-Muñoz et al. editorial on 50-year research agenda; bibliometric analysis article; mining policy working paper; empirical study on natural resources and digitization; limited access to full-text; assessment based on abstracts, structure metadata, and reference guidelines)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Standard sections (Introduction, Methods/Empirical Strategy, Results, Conclusion) are expected. No mandatory section naming convention specified; section headings follow discipline norms (Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable per FIELD_SKILL).

### Length
No explicit word limit stated in publicly available guidelines. Abstracts capped at 250 words (ENFORCED). Standard empirical and case studies follow Elsevier journal norms; no journal-specific page/word cap discovered.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED CONSTRAINT:** Maximum 250 words (strict limit, distinctly lower than some ABS 4* journals which tolerate 300+ word abstracts).
**ENFORCED:** Unstructured format (single paragraph); must briefly state purpose of research, principal results, and major conclusions; must stand alone without references (references avoided unless essential; if essential, use author-year citation).
**ENFORCED:** Avoid non-standard abbreviations; define any essential abbreviations at first mention within the abstract itself.
**ENFORCED:** Keywords requirement: 1–7 keywords in English. Avoid multi-word keywords (using "and" or "of"); use abbreviations only if firmly established in the field.

### Citations
**ENFORCED:** Author-date (parenthetical) citation style. In-text citations use format (Author, Year); multiple authors use "et al." for 4+ authors; multiple sources separated by semicolons and arranged chronologically.
**ENFORCED:** Reference format flexible—any style is acceptable *provided it is consistent*. Author names, journal/book titles, chapter/article titles, year, volume, and article numbers or page numbers must be included where applicable. DOI use is recommended.
**ENFORCED:** References do not follow a single mandated format (unlike ABS 4* journals which enforce author-year strictly); References can vary in presentation style as long as consistent.

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. No journal-specific heading naming or numbering convention beyond discipline norms.

### Tables and Figures
**ENFORCED:** Tables numbered consecutively; captions required; tables submitted as editable text, not images. Table notes placed below table body.
**ENFORCED:** Figures numbered consecutively; captions required. Artwork standards apply per Elsevier policy (high resolution, editable formats preferred).
**ENFORCED:** All tables and figures must be cited in the text in numerical order.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No mixed American and British English (choose one or the other, not both).
- Article highlights are required at submission (ENFORCED at submission stage; not a published element).
- Exclusive topics NOT covered: agriculture, forestry, fisheries (if paper's primary focus is one of these, it is out of scope).
- Discussion and debate articles without significant research component are not accepted (though editors may consider at discretion).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Constraints
**RELAXATION:** Abstract word limit is 250 words, aligning with many mid-tier journals but slightly more conservative than top-tier practice (some ABS 4* journals accept 300–350 word abstracts). This is not a delta from FIELD_SKILL baseline (which does not specify abstract length) but an *additional* constraint imposed by this journal.

### Citation Format Flexibility
**DELTA (GUIDELINE ONLY):** References can be in "any style or format" as long as consistent, with no mandated author-date formatting of the reference list itself. The FIELD_SKILL baseline (derived from ABS 4*/4 journals) encodes strict author-year formatting and numerical citation avoidance as norms. Resources Policy relaxes this: while in-text citations must be author-date, the reference list formatting is flexible. This represents a **RELAXATION of the ABS 4*/4 norm**, consistent with the journal's ABS 2 tier.

### Article Type Acceptance
**ENFORCED (PRACTICE):** Case studies and literature reviews are explicitly accepted types (not standard in ABS 4* journals, which privilege empirical and theory papers). This is a **RELAXATION**, permitting article types that ABS 4* outlets discourage.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** FIELD DEFAULT. First-person plural ("we") expected per ECON field norms; no deviation noted in spot-checked articles.
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** FIELD DEFAULT. Medium-to-long sentences, high paragraph density, technical vocabulary density—all consistent with ECON norms.
- **Argumentation and rhetorical moves:** FIELD DEFAULT. Introduction structure follows the CARS-variant pattern (motivation, research question, approach, results preview, contributions, roadmap) as encoded in FIELD_SKILL.
- **Methods conventions:** FIELD DEFAULT. Mixed tense (past for procedures, present for logic), sufficient procedural detail, identification strategy clearly stated (for empirical work) all match ECON field norms.
- **Results and reporting norms:** FIELD DEFAULT. Integration of results and interpretation, tables as primary vehicle, statistical narration with quantitative specificity, null results reported directly—all consistent with FIELD_SKILL.
- **Discussion and conclusion norms:** FIELD DEFAULT. Synthesis rather than summary; policy/theoretical implications; honest acknowledgment of limitations; brief future directions all match FIELD_SKILL.
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** FIELD DEFAULT. Low overall hedging intensity, clear claims preferred, hedging reserved for external validity, causal interpretation beyond identified parameter, data limitations, and mechanism vs. reduced-form—all consistent with ECON field baseline.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Mixing reference list formatting with in-text format.** In-text citations must be author-date (Author, Year), but authors sometimes assume the reference list must also follow strict author-year format without realizing the journal permits flexible reference formatting as long as consistent. Ensure consistency within your reference list but do not assume a specific format is mandated.

2. **Exceeding the 250-word abstract limit.** This is a strict constraint and may result in desk rejection before peer review. Count carefully and prioritize principal results and conclusions over context or literature review detail.

3. **Mixing English variants.** The guideline explicitly states "American or British English" but "not a combination of both." Proofread for consistent spelling, terminology, and punctuation conventions (e.g., "-ise" vs. "-ize" endings).

4. **Forgetting article highlights at submission.** These are required at the submission stage in Elsevier's system and may delay processing if omitted.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access Limitations

The journal's full author guidelines page on ScienceDirect is access-restricted (HTTP 403). Extracted guidelines derive from:
- WebSearch aggregations of publicly visible guideline text (Elsevier's own website cache and mirrors)
- Citation style references (Paperpile, CitationSy)
- Author disclosures on the journal's IDEAS/RePEc page
- Spot-checked open-access editorial and preprint metadata

A maximum of 4 accessible article structures were examined (1 editorial/perspective, 1 bibliometric analysis, 2 empirical preprints/open-access references). Full-text paywall access prevented deep stylistic analysis of 8+ additional recent articles (2024–2026). Assessment is based on abstract structure, section metadata, and authorial conventions visible in metadata, combined with explicit guideline constraints. No contradictions between available guidelines and spot-checked articles were found.

## Classification Summary

- **ENFORCED** (guidelines + confirmed in articles): Abstract word limit (250), unstructured format, keywords requirement, author-date in-text citations, article highlights at submission, table/figure numbering and captions, English language consistency.
- **GUIDELINE ONLY** (stated, rarely checked in spot-check due to access limits): Reference list formatting flexibility.
- **PRACTICE ONLY**: None definitively identified from spot-check; access limitations prevented deep confirmation.
- **RELAXATION** (relative to ABS 4*/4 baseline): Case studies and literature reviews as primary article types; flexible reference formatting.
