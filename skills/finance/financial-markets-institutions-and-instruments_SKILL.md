# Journal Writing Skill: Financial Markets, Institutions and Instruments

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** Publishes original studies in financial markets, institutions, and instruments, explicitly aiming to bridge academic and professional finance communities (contributions from practitioners at bodies like the SEC and Federal Reserve alongside academics). Historically organized many issues around a single theme examined in depth, with an annual issue surveying developments in money and banking, derivatives, corporate finance, and fixed income; recent volumes (2023-2026) increasingly mix standalone empirical papers across issues.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14680416/homepage/forauthors.html (accessible via search-engine cache/summary only; direct fetch returned HTTP 402)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (abstracts/front-matter only, via IDEAS/RePEc, SSRN, and Wiley abstract pages) — "Do high-centrality CEOs influence audit outcomes?" (Mazboudi et al., 2025); "Does it cost to be politically connected?" (2025); "Synergistic Gains and National Cultural Distance" (Hussain et al., 2025); "Estimating Hedge Fund Leverage: A Three-Step Estimation Protocol" (Karagiorgis & Drakos, 2025); "Interplay Between Competition Networks, Strategy Uniqueness, and Hedge Fund Performance" (Kooli & Zhang, 2025); "How Do Banks Respond to Supplier IPOs?" (Bae et al., 2025); "Opportunistic Insider Trading During the COVID-19 Pandemic" (Das, 2025, open-access PDF); "Climate regulation costs and firms' distress risk" (Lambertides & Tsouknidis, 2024); "Flight from Liquidity and Corporate Bond Yields" (2023). Full text was accessible only for the Das (2025) open-access PDF; all others were assessed from abstract/front-matter.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscript must include a title page, a table of contents, and a short abstract. A table of contents as a required manuscript component is not a standard finance-journal expectation and is not covered by FIELD_SKILL.

### Length
Guidelines specify a manuscript of approximately 75 journal pages — substantially longer than the typical ABS 3-4 finance paper length implied by FIELD_SKILL. Note this figure is unusually high relative to comparable journals and may reflect legacy formatting (double-spaced including footnotes/references); treat as a guideline ceiling rather than a target.

### Abstract
"Short abstract" only — no structured format, no explicit word count found in accessible guidance. Consistent with FIELD_SKILL's single-paragraph abstract norm; no delta beyond the "short" emphasis.

### Citations
No journal-specific citation style found in accessible guidance; footnotes are numbered in text and grouped at the end of the manuscript (endnote-style footnote placement), which differs from in-page footnotes but does not affect in-text citation format (author-year per FIELD_SKILL still applies for reference citations).

### Headings
No journal-specific heading convention found; nothing contradicts FIELD_SKILL's Arabic-numeral norm.

### Tables and Figures
Position of diagrams/figures must be clearly marked in the manuscript; camera-ready copy preferred where possible. Figure files accepted in .tif/.tiff, .eps, .jpg/.jpeg, .gif, .png, .ps, or .pdf. This is a production/formatting instruction, not a style delta.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None identified beyond standard Wiley submission requirements (ORCID required for all authors; manuscripts tracked through Wiley's author portal).

### Other
A brief author biography is required for each author, with any acknowledgements placed on the same page as the biography. This is a distinctive submission-package requirement not found in FIELD_SKILL.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in all spot-checked abstracts (active constructions, "we find/document," confident declarative claims for results).

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm where visible (e.g., Karagiorgis & Drakos report specific magnitudes — "average sectorial leverage of 3.3," "58.2%"; Bae et al. report "23.7 basis points"/"roughly 20%" — consistent with FIELD_SKILL's economic-significance emphasis).

### Citation Density
Not assessable from abstract-only front matter for most articles; no delta observed in the one full-text article checked (Das, 2025).

### Structural Deviations
RELAXATION (ABS 3, cannot redefine baseline): the required table of contents in the submitted manuscript is a journal-specific administrative artifact of the submission package, not a change to the published article's section structure, which otherwise follows the FIELD_SKILL canonical structure (introduction, data, results, conclusion) in the articles checked.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY: several sampled articles concern practitioner-relevant institutional topics (hedge fund leverage/prime-broker risk, bank responses to supplier IPOs, insider trading during COVID) consistent with the journal's stated aim of bridging academic and practitioner audiences, but this did not manifest as a detectable prose-style difference (register, hedging, or citation practice) in the sampled front matter.

## Common Pitfalls
- Omitting the required table of contents or author biography page from the submission package — both are journal-specific requirements not typical of finance-journal submissions generally.
- Treating the "~75 journal pages" figure as a hard word-count target rather than a generous ceiling; comparable ABS 3 finance papers are typically much shorter, and this figure likely reflects legacy double-spaced formatting norms rather than current typical manuscript length.
- Placing footnotes at the foot of each page rather than grouping them at the end of the manuscript as instructed.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
