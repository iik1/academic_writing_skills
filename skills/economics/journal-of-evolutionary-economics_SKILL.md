# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Evolutionary Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature
- **Scope:** The journal promotes application of evolutionary theory and methods to economics, with emphasis on disequilibrium processes, innovation, technological change, and dynamic structural change in economies. Follows the tradition of Joseph A. Schumpeter.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/191/submission-guidelines (accessible via Springer Nature; requires authentication for full details)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 recent articles across 2024–2026 issues

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows standard empirical/theory paper structure expected by FIELD_SKILL. No journal-specific departures noted.

### Length
No explicit word or page limit stated in publicly accessible guidelines. ABS 2 tier suggests papers are typically shorter than ABS 4*/4 outlets, but no formal maximum is enforced by the journal.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED:** 150–250 words. Must not contain undefined abbreviations or unspecified references.

### Keywords
**ENFORCED:** 4–6 keywords required for indexing.

### Citations
**ENFORCED:** Author-year (name and year) parenthetical citations. Format: single author "(Parsch 2011)"; multiple authors "(Author1 and Author2 Year; Author3 Year)"; three+ authors use et al. format "(Tsai et al. 2010)."

Bibliography format: Author Last Initial (Year) Title. Journal Name Volume:page range; analogous formats for books, chapters, etc.

### Headings
No journal-specific convention stated. FIELD_SKILL norm (Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable) applies.

### JEL Codes
**ENFORCED:** An appropriate number of JEL codes must be provided. Observed practice ranges from 3–5 codes per article.

### Tables and Figures
No explicit journal-specific rule. FIELD_SKILL norm applies (both common; figures illustrate key variation, tables hold quantitative results).

### Ethical & Disclosure Requirements
**ENFORCED:** Author contribution and competing interests information must be submitted via the submission interface (not in manuscript). Interests within the last 3 years must be disclosed. Corresponding author should be prepared to provide documentation of ethical compliance if requested.

### Manuscript Format
**ENFORCED:** Submit in editable formats (.docx, LaTeX, etc.). All editable source files must be provided at every submission and revision; incomplete file sets will result in desk rejection.

### Explicit Prohibitions
**GUIDELINE ONLY:** Authors should not add/delete authors after submission; changes to authorship cannot be made after acceptance.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Scope and Disciplinary Framing
**PRACTICE ONLY:** Articles explicitly engage evolutionary theory and Schumpeterian concepts (innovation, structural change, disequilibrium, diffusion). This framing is not unique to this journal (evolutionary economics journals share it), but it is a consistent and visible feature of published work. Contrast with general-audience economics journals, which may use market-efficiency or rational-agent frameworks.

**Implication:** Papers targeting this journal should position research within the evolutionary/Schumpeterian tradition explicitly—cite Schumpeter, Dosi, Nelson & Winter, or similar canonical figures in the introduction; frame the research question as a gap in dynamic/evolutionary understanding, not merely empirical/policy curiosity.

### Empirical Orientation
**PRACTICE ONLY:** The journal publishes both empirical and theory papers, but empirical work dominates (ca. 60–70% of observed sample). Theory papers are present but less frequent than in ABS 4* outlets. Articles often combine theory and empirics—introducing a model, then calibrating or testing it on data.

**Relaxation vs. FIELD_SKILL:** FIELD_SKILL baseline drawn from ABS 4* outlets, which maintain a 50/50 theory-empirics balance. This journal admits more applied/empirical work. However, this is a relaxation in tier, not a redefining of norms—pure empiricism without theoretical scaffolding is still not the norm.

### Citation Density and Positioning
**PRACTICE ONLY:** Introduction engagement with literature follows FIELD_SKILL pattern (high citation density, explicit positioning relative to prior work). However, observed articles show slightly *lower* citation counts in introductions compared to ABS 4* samples (ca. 30–40 cites vs. 45–60 typical in top-tier). This reflects both the lower tier and the focus on canonical evolutionary economists, who are cited repeatedly rather than a broad literature scan.

**Implication:** Literature review should be focused on the most relevant prior work, with deep engagement with a few key papers rather than broad survey. Positioning statements ("We extend X by adding Y") should be explicit but concise.

### Table and Figure Conventions
**PRACTICE ONLY:** Figures are very frequently used in empirical papers, often as the primary vehicle for presenting results. Maps, box plots, scatter plots, and distributional charts are common. This slightly exceeds FIELD_SKILL norm, which permits but does not emphasize figures.

**Implication:** For empirical papers, consider using a figure to present a key finding prominently, not just tables.

### Hedging and Tentativeness
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. PRACTICE ONLY: No observed departure. Claims are typically stated directly; hedging is limited to external validity qualifications and mechanism uncertainty.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. No observed journal-specific convention differs.

## Dimensions With No Delta

The following dimensions align with FIELD_SKILL baseline and show no journal-specific deviation:
- Voice and person: First-person plural ("we") standard; FIELD DEFAULT
- Sentence and paragraph structure: Medium-to-long sentences; paragraph density high; FIELD DEFAULT
- Argumentation: Results announced in introduction; explicit contributions stated; FIELD DEFAULT
- Methods tense and procedural detail: Mixed tense (past for procedures, present for implications); FIELD DEFAULT
- Conclusion conventions: Synthesis over summary; limitations acknowledged; future work suggested; FIELD DEFAULT
- Role of theory vs. empirics: Both valued; FIELD DEFAULT (though empirical work slightly more frequent at this tier)

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Absence of explicit evolutionary framing:** Submitting an applied empirics paper without situating it within the Schumpeterian tradition (innovation, structural change, disequilibrium) risks desk rejection or reviewer skepticism. Even if the work is methodologically sound, the journal editors seek papers that *advance evolutionary economics theory*, not papers that happen to study a topic economists care about.

2. **Incomplete or missing source files:** Submission of incomplete editable source files (missing LaTeX preambles, detached figures, unresolved references) will trigger desk rejection before peer review. Always provide a complete, compiling/rendering manuscript.

3. **Over-hedging causal claims from observational data:** While hedging on external validity is expected (FIELD_SKILL norm), pages of "we cannot rule out that..." statements reduce impact. Identify the specific threat, note it concisely, and move on. Readers trust your identification strategy if you state it clearly.

4. **Underuse of figures in empirical work:** Many applied papers in this journal use 6–14 figures to communicate results. A 15-table empirical paper with no figures will appear dense and is less likely to engage readers quickly.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

- Citation style documentation from Paperpile

No divergences from FIELD_SKILL baseline were found that rise to the level of *enforced* norms beyond the hard constraints listed above. Observed deltas (empirical emphasis, evolutionary framing, figure use) reflect ABS 2 tier characteristics and evolutionary economics tradition, not journal-specific rules.
