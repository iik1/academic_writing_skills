# Journal Writing Skill: Academy of Management Perspectives

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Academy of Management
- **Scope:** Publishes evidence-based papers on timely management issues aimed at "thought leaders" (educators, consultants, executives, policymakers). Explicitly prioritizes managerial relevance and readability over theoretical contribution; not a venue for theory-building or opinion pieces.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/author-and-reviewer-resources/author-resources/submitting-to-perspectives/ (accessible); https://journals.aom.org/journal/amp (403, blocked)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Standard articles use a fixed, journal-specific section template that replaces the generic MGMT quantitative/qualitative templates: **Introduction (max 2 pages) → Problem Statement (2-4 pages) → What We Know (max 4 pages) → What We Do Not Know (max 2 pages) → Conceptual/Empirical Analysis → What We Have Learned → Conclusion**. Detailed methods, robustness tests, and extensive tables belong in a supplement labeled "Additional Materials," not the main body. Three specialty formats exist with their own structures: Constructive Confrontations (Abstract/Title → Introduction → Issue Description → Conflicting Perspectives → Points of Debate → Clarifying Analysis → Results/Implications → Future Research → Conclusion; requires a pre-submission proposal to the editor and co-authors from opposing published positions; double-blind), Practitioner Perspectives (Abstract/Title → Introduction → Issue Description → Practical Insights → Scholarly Insights → Research Roadmap → Conclusion; requires a senior-practitioner co-author paired with a scholar; single-blind), and Book Briefs (Abstract/Title → Introduction → Issue Description → Book Insights → Augment the Insights → Path Forward → Conclusion; must synthesize at least three bestseller-list business books on one managerial problem; author needs relevant peer-reviewed publication record).

### Length
Standard articles and Constructive Confrontations: max 20 double-spaced pages. Practitioner Perspectives and Book Briefs: 10-20 double-spaced pages.

### Abstract
Max 200 words (tighter than the FIELD_SKILL 150-250 range). Must cover: the managerial issue, analysis approach, findings, and practical implications — practical-implications content is a required abstract element here, not optional as in FIELD_SKILL.

### Citations
AMP uses **endnotes**, not in-text (Author, Year) citations, reference lists, or footnotes. This replaces the FIELD_SKILL APA in-text citation convention entirely for this journal.

### Headings
Section headings follow the fixed template above rather than being freely labeled by argument (contrast with FIELD_SKILL's Theoretical/Conceptual template, where headings are named by substantive argument).

### Tables and Figures
Tables, detailed methodology, and extensive data are pushed to a supplementary "Additional Materials" file; the main text favors summary tables/figures only, to conserve the tight page limit.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Opinion pieces are explicitly rejected. Papers that are purely theory-driven with managerial implications merely appended at the end are explicitly discouraged — the managerial issue must be central throughout, not a coda. Manuscript must not be under review elsewhere or have been previously submitted to AMP. AI use must be disclosed in the cover letter and article acknowledgements.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Plain, concise, jargon-avoiding language is an explicit editorial requirement, stronger than FIELD_SKILL's general "academic but direct" register — AMP targets practitioners and non-specialists as primary readers, not fellow theorists. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; consistent with titles/framing sampled, e.g. "Why Do Companies Go Woke?", "The Show Must Go On").

### Structural Deviations
The fixed What We Know / What We Do Not Know / What We Have Learned section template is a distinctive structural signature not present in any FIELD_SKILL template (quantitative, qualitative, theoretical, or review). ENFORCED.

### Citation Density
Endnote-only citation format (no in-text author-year, no footnotes) is a hard formatting delta from the FIELD_SKILL APA convention. ENFORCED.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Explicit "relevant / rigorous / readable" tripartite editorial standard, with managerial relevance held as the primary and continuous focus of the paper rather than a secondary practical-implications section. ENFORCED. Article-type diversity (Constructive Confrontations, Practitioner Perspectives, Book Briefs) with distinct co-authorship and blinding rules is unique to this journal among the MGMT field-baseline set. ENFORCED.

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing a conventional theory-first empirical paper and appending managerial implications at the end — AMP explicitly discourages this; the managerial issue must be the throughline.
- Using standard APA in-text citations instead of endnotes.
- Exceeding the 200-word abstract limit or omitting practical implications from it.
- Submitting heavy methods/tables in the main text instead of an Additional Materials supplement, blowing past the 20-page limit.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
