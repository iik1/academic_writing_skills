# Journal Writing Skill: Australasian Accounting, Business and Finance Journal

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** University of Wollongong
- **Scope:** Double blind peer-reviewed, open-access journal publishing interdisciplinary research in accounting, finance, financial planning, environmental accounting, forensic accounting, and related fields. Accepts full research articles, technical notes, case studies, responses to previous articles, and book reviews.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.uowoajournals.org/aabfj/site/policies/
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 articles across Volume 18 (2024) and Volume 20 (2026)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Specify article type clearly in submission: full academic article, brief technical note, case study, response to previous articles, or book review.
- FIELD_SKILL canonical structure applies for full research articles (Introduction, Hypothesis Development/Theory, Data and Methods, Results, Discussion/Conclusion).
- Technical notes, case studies, and book reviews follow less formal structures; no standardized template provided by journal.

### Length
- **Total manuscript:** 3,500–8,000 words including all elements.
- **Maximum:** 25 pages.
- Wide flexibility for article types: editorials and notes may be 1–6 pages; full empirical articles typically 18–49 pages.

### Abstract
- Required for full research articles; format not explicitly specified by journal.
- Follows Harvard citation format for any in-text references within abstract.
- Keywords: maximum 8 (comma-separated).
- JEL Classifications: up to 4 codes required.

### Citations
- **Harvard format mandatory.** Author-date in-text citations; reference list at end.
- Enforce Harvard strictly: "Author (Year)" or "(Author Year)" in text; alphabetical order in reference list.
- FIELD_SKILL Harvard conventions apply; no journal-specific deviations noted.

### Headings
- **Main headings (Level 1):** Bold, left-justified, with title or sentence case (journal not explicit; observe existing articles for consistency).
- **Subheadings (Level 2):** Title case, bold.
- **Sub-subheadings (Level 3):** Italicized.
- Section numbering not enforced; articles observed use both numbered and unnumbered approaches.

### Font and Formatting
- **Font:** Times New Roman, 12-point throughout (exceptions: title in 16-point bold; footnotes in 10-point; tables/figures may be 10-point or smaller).
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced with standard margins.
- **Alignment:** Body text fully justified.
- **Page numbers:** Centered at bottom, starting with 1.
- **Submission format:** Microsoft Word only. Electronic submission via journal homepage.

### Tables and Figures
- Position tables and figures where they should appear in publication (not at end, unless appendix).
- No explicit restrictions on size or density beyond readability in 10-point font.

### Author Information and Anonymity
- Author names, affiliations, and acknowledgements must NOT appear in the Word document submitted.
- Provide this information separately in the electronic submission system to maintain double-blind review.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Simultaneous submission to other journals prohibited.
- Plagiarism without appropriate acknowledgement is unacceptable.
- No author/affiliation identification in manuscript file (double-blind requirement).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Length Constraints: ENFORCED
FIELD_SKILL does not specify a hard word limit or page ceiling. AABFJ enforces strict limits (3,500–8,000 words; max 25 pages). This is a **substantial relaxation** from ABS 4* norms, which permit longer papers (often 40–50+ pages in JAE/JAR). Authors must economize and prioritize core findings.

### Manuscript Format: ENFORCED
FIELD_SKILL does not specify file format or technical submission details. AABFJ requires Microsoft Word only, double-spaced, with specific fonts and margins. This is a **formatting constraint specific to this journal** with no field-baseline equivalent.

### Heading Conventions: RELAXATION
FIELD_SKILL notes that heading conventions vary by journal (Roman vs. Arabic numerals, ALL CAPS vs. title case). AABFJ uses bold left-justified main headings with title case and italicized sub-subheadings, but does not require numbered sections. This is more **permissive than ABS 4* norms**, which enforce strict hierarchical numbering (Roman/Arabic) and ALL CAPS in some journals.

### Article Type Flexibility: RELAXATION
FIELD_SKILL assumes a single canonical research article structure (Introduction through Conclusion). AABFJ explicitly welcomes technical notes, case studies, and book reviews with no specified structure. This is a **deviation from field baseline** — ABS 4* journals rarely publish these forms. Authors of non-standard article types should not force FIELD_SKILL structure.

### Anonymity and Submission: ENFORCED
FIELD_SKILL does not address double-blind review mechanics. AABFJ requires strict author anonymity in the submitted Word file, with affiliation information provided separately. This is a **procedural requirement** specific to this journal.

### Double-Blind Review: ENFORCED
FIELD_SKILL notes review processes but does not specify double-blind protocol. AABFJ enforces double-blind peer review with required suggestions of four potential reviewers (names, affiliations, email addresses in cover letter). This is more **structured than field baseline**.

## Dimensions With No Delta

The following dimensions show no deviation from FIELD_SKILL norms and apply unchanged:

- **Voice and Person:** First-person plural "we" remains the standard; active voice dominates.
- **Sentence and Paragraph Style:** Medium to long sentences, dense paragraphs, high technical density — unchanged.
- **Hedging and Epistemic Caution:** Medium-level hedging calibrated to identification strength — unchanged.
- **Citation Density by Section:** High in Introduction and Theory; medium-low in Results — unchanged.
- **Methods Conventions:** Past tense throughout, procedural detail, identification strategy emphasis — unchanged.
- **Results Reporting:** Tables as primary vehicle, prose interpretation, significance reporting — unchanged.
- **Discussion and Conclusion:** Merged format, restatement of findings, limitations paragraph — unchanged.
- **Paraphrase vs. Quotation:** Paraphrase is norm; direct quotation from prior literature is absent (except in qualitative/case-study articles) — unchanged.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding word/page limits:** Authors from ABS 4* backgrounds may submit 40+ page papers. AABFJ's 8,000-word cap (roughly 25 pages) is a hard ceiling. Submissions violating this are desk-rejected. Economize ruthlessly: cut non-essential robustness checks, merge related subsections, and prioritize the main story.

2. **Author/affiliation information in manuscript:** Forgetting to remove author names, affiliations, or acknowledgements from the Word document violates double-blind requirements and may trigger desk rejection. Use Track Changes to strip all identifying information, then re-save as clean file.

3. **Citation format inconsistency:** AABFJ enforces Harvard format strictly. Mixing Harvard with APA, Chicago, or numeric styles will prompt rejection. Use reference management software (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) set to Harvard output, or manually audit all 50–150+ citations for consistency.

4. **Simultaneous submission:** Submitting to AABFJ while the paper is under review elsewhere violates the journal's policy. Declare all prior submissions and rejections in the cover letter; failure to do so risks sanctions.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
