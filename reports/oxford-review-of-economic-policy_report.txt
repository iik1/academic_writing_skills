1. VOICE AND REGISTER (RELAXATION)
   - Field baseline: "first-person plural is near-universal" in ABS 4* economics
   - Oxford Review practice: Third-person voice strongly preferred in policy-oriented articles
   - Reason: Broader, non-specialist audience; policy-brief conventions
   - Classification: RELAXATION (lower formality than field baseline)

2. STRUCTURAL COMPRESSION (ENFORCED)
   - Hard constraint: 9,000-word limit (including all text, figures, tables, footnotes)

   - Classification: ENFORCED (explicit in guidelines, confirmed in articles)

3. AUDIENCE AND TONE (ENFORCED)
   - Requirement: "written with an intelligent audience in mind, who...may not be familiar with existing academic literature"

   - Classification: ENFORCED

4. CITATION DENSITY (PRACTICE ONLY)

ASSESSMENT
==========

The Oxford Review of Economic Policy is positioned as a **policy-public-facing journal** within economics, not a top-tier research outlet. It explicitly deprioritizes technical exposition in favor of accessibility to policymakers and non-specialist readers. This is a material departure from the ABS 4* baseline (FIELD_SKILL) and must be reflected in submission strategy.

Key recommendation for authors: If writing for Oxford Review, adopt third-person policy-brief voice, compress ruthlessly to fit 9,000-word limit, explain technical concepts more generously than for top-tier outlets, and establish editorial interest before full submission (given thematic commissioning model).
