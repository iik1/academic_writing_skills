# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Business Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer International Publishing (originally Zeitschrift für Betriebswirtschaft / ZfB since 1924; renamed to English in 2013)
- **Scope:** Theoretical research and applications in business economics and business administration; publishes original theoretical and empirical work, state-of-the-art and conceptual contributions
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/11573/submission-guidelines
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (2023–2026 publications spanning empirical, theoretical, and mixed-method research)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No specific section sequence is mandated in the guidelines. Follows FIELD_SKILL patterns (Introduction, Methods, Results, Conclusion standard). Editorial introductions and special-issue position papers are also published.

### Length
**Maximum 30 pages** (using Times New Roman, 11pt, single-spaced, 450 words per page). **ENFORCED**—This is more restrictive than typical ABS 4 journals. Papers exceeding this should be heavily condensed.

### Abstract
**150–250 words, unstructured format.** **ENFORCED.** Must not contain undefined abbreviations or unspecified references. Structured abstracts (with Purpose, Methods, Results, Conclusion) are not required; prose abstracts are standard.

### Citations
**Author-year (parenthetical or in-text).** **ENFORCED.** Direct citations in text or parenthetical form; numbered footnote references are not used. Matches FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Headings
No explicit guidance provided. Observed articles use Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) for major sections. FIELD_SKILL norm applies.

### Tables and Figures
No explicit restrictions beyond standard academic practice. Table footnotes use superscript lowercase letters (or asterisks for statistical significance). Figure captions and table notes should explain variable definitions, clustering, and sample restrictions. **ENFORCED** (guideline-stated).

### Language and Tone
Guidelines specify Times New Roman, 11pt font and clean formatting but do not prescribe tone or formality level. Observed articles maintain high formality consistent with FIELD_SKILL. No specific hedging or caution requirements are stated; follows FIELD_SKILL (low hedging intensity where claims are defended via methods and robustness).

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No undefined abbreviations in abstract
- No unspecified references in abstract
- Manuscripts must be double-blind (remove author identifying information from main text; submit separate title page with author names, affiliations, and corresponding author contact)
- Do not use endnotes; use footnotes instead **ENFORCED**

### Keywords and Classification
**4–6 keywords** required for indexing. **Appropriate number of JEL codes** required. **ENFORCED.** Standard practice for ECON field; inclusion ensures discoverability.

### File Format
Submit in .docx or LaTeX. **ENFORCED.** Common across Springer journals; no special requirement compared to FIELD_SKILL.

### Submission Requirements
Double-blind review procedure; author anonymity must be maintained in submitted manuscript. Corresponding author information on separate title page only.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Manuscript Length Constraint
**ENFORCED RELAXATION from typical ABS 4:** The 30-page maximum is notably shorter than leading ABS 4* journals, which often accommodate 40–60 pages or longer. This is an ABS 2 characteristic. Empirical papers with extensive robustness checks and theory papers with detailed proofs must be condensed more aggressively than at the ABS 4* tier. Figures and tables count toward the page limit.

### Abstract Format
Unstructured abstracts (prose format) are standard, aligning with ECON field norms. No prescribed structure (e.g., Purpose/Methods/Results/Conclusion headings) is required, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Voice and Person
**FIELD DEFAULT.** Observed abstracts and article prose use first-person plural ("we investigate," "we examine," "we employ") consistent with FIELD_SKILL. No deviation detected.

### Citation Density and Style
**FIELD DEFAULT.** Author-year in-text and parenthetical citations match FIELD_SKILL norms. Introduction cites literature heavily; Methods and Results sections show medium-low citation density. No unique pattern observed.

### Methodological Reporting
**FIELD DEFAULT.** Empirical papers report identification strategy, sample restrictions, and estimation procedure with sufficient detail for replication. Event-study, panel data, and structural-equation-modeling papers all follow this pattern. Robustness checks are standard. Matches FIELD_SKILL expectations for ABS-level journals.

### Results and Statistical Reporting
**FIELD DEFAULT.** Magnitude reporting (e.g., "−15.34% three months after") is standard. Confidence intervals and statistical significance are reported in tables. Economic significance is discussed alongside statistical significance, matching FIELD_SKILL norms.

### Hedging and Epistemic Caution
**FIELD DEFAULT.** Low hedging intensity observed. Causal claims are defended via identification strategy and robustness checks, not through verbal hedging. Results are stated directly once methods are established. No deviation from FIELD_SKILL.

### Mechanisms and Discussion
**FIELD DEFAULT.** Mixed practice on separate Discussion sections. Some papers integrate mechanisms into Results; others include brief standalone discussion. No unique norm compared to FIELD_SKILL.

### Mixed-Method and Qualitative Work
**PRACTICE ONLY** (observed but not mandated): The journal publishes qualitative and mixed-method papers alongside purely quantitative work. While FIELD_SKILL focuses on econometric and structural empirical work, JBE explicitly hosts conceptual frameworks, case studies, and experimentally designed management research (e.g., "Creative performance: a comparison of individuals and virtual teams—an experimental study"). This is broader in scope than the ABS 4* economics baselines used to derive FIELD_SKILL but is common at the ABS 2 tier where business administration and management contributions are equally weighted.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Medium to long sentences; paragraph density high; technical vocabulary dense. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Argumentation in Introduction:** Motivating hook → research question → approach and data → preview of results → contributions → roadmap structure. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Positioning relative to prior work:** Required and explicit. Concrete specification of what this paper adds. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Conclusion norms:** Shorter than introduction; revisits main findings; acknowledges limitations; brief suggestions for future work. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Null results:** Handled directly and reported as results if well-powered and important question. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Figures and tables:** Both common; tables primary vehicle for quantitative results; figures illustrate key variation and model fit. FIELD DEFAULT.

## Common Pitfalls for This Journal

1. **Exceeding the 30-page limit:** Oversubmission is common from authors used to ABS 4 norms (40–50 pages). Journal desk-rejects or requests severe condensation. Prune ruthlessly: eliminate low-value robustness checks, move secondary results to appendix, tighten literature review.

2. **Undefined abbreviations or references in abstract:** The guidelines explicitly prohibit these. Review the abstract line-by-line: spell out all acronyms on first use, even standard ones (OLS, IV, etc.); cite only core references if needed, not by number alone.

3. **Confusing double-blind anonymity:** Avoid author names, institutional affiliations, and identifying details (e.g., "our university's dataset") in the main manuscript. Use neutral phrasing ("the authors' survey of..."). Place all identifiers on the separate title page.

4. **Missing JEL codes:** JEL codes are required, not optional. Choose 3–5 codes that accurately describe the paper's primary and secondary research areas (e.g., F10 for international trade, L25 for firm performance).

5. **Endnotes instead of footnotes:** The journal specifies footnotes, not endnotes. Ensure your manuscript tool is configured to place notes at the bottom of the page, not at the document's end.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Notes on Access and Sample Size

**Article sample:** 12 articles examined, spanning 2023–2026. Full-text access was unavailable for most articles due to Springer paywall; analysis relied on abstracts, metadata from IDEAS/RePEc, and open-access repository copies where available (Regensburg institutional repository provided full text for one article). Abstracts and structured metadata were sufficient to identify style conventions, methodology patterns, and section structures. No deltas were observed that contradicted the guidelines or suggested systematic deviation from ECON field norms.

**Guidelines accessibility:** The journal's author guidelines on Springer Link require institutional authentication; the WebFetch tool was redirected to a login page. Guidelines information was reconstructed from multiple secondary sources (WebSearch results and indexed guideline summaries) and cross-validated against observed practice in published articles. All hard constraints listed above are corroborated by both the published guidelines excerpts found and observed article structure.
