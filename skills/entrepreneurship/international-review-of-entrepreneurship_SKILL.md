# Journal Writing Skill: International Review of Entrepreneurship

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** Diamond Ireland Press / Senate Hall
- **Scope:** Disseminates cutting-edge knowledge on entrepreneurship within universities and business practice. Welcomes empirical, conceptual, and review articles; special issues on themed topics (e.g., eco-entrepreneurship, leading in entrepreneurial contexts).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.esr.ie/index.php/ire/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (Vol. 23 No. 2 [2025]: 3 articles; Vol. 22 No. 2, 4 [2024]: 4 articles; Vol. 21 No. 4 [2023]: 3 articles, accessed via abstract/metadata/front-matter)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- **Page 1:** Title, author names, institutional affiliations, postal address, country code, phone, email.
- **Page 2:** Abstract (150 words maximum) followed by Keywords.
- **Page 3 onward:** Main manuscript (section structure not prescriptively ordered in guidelines; authors have flexibility).
- For **case studies:** must include a teaching note outlining analytical insights, learning points, and teaching strategies (both case and note are peer-reviewed).

### Length
- No explicit word or page limit specified in guidelines; submission framing suggests flexible length.

### Abstract
- **Format:** 150 words maximum (structured or unstructured not specified, but sampled articles use narrative prose).
- **Keywords:** Listed directly below abstract; suggest 4-7 terms for search engine optimization and categorization.
- No explicit requirement for structured subsections (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions) within the abstract.

### Citations
- **Style:** Author-date (inferred from practice; not explicitly named in guidelines).
- **Order:** Alphabetical at end of article.
- **Format rule:** Use **full journal titles** (no acronyms); include volume, issue, article number, year, and page range.
- **URLs required:** Provide URLs for all references where available.

### Headings
- No explicit heading conventions stated in guidelines.
- Observed practice: standard hierarchical levels (H1 for main sections, H2 for subsections); section names vary by article type (Methods, Results, Discussion for empirical; thematic organization for reviews/conceptuals).

### Tables and Figures
- Must be **created within Microsoft Word** (no cut-and-paste from PDF, JPEG, GIF, or PowerPoint to avoid typesetting delays).
- **Placement notation:** Indicate desired placement with markers (e.g., "{Table 1.2 near here}") rather than embedding inline.
- **Labeling:** Tables must be clearly labeled and titled; figures should be self-explanatory.
- **Supporting data:** If tables/figures are based on non-public statistics, supply the underlying data.

### Formatting
- **Font:** 12pt, A4 paper size.
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced throughout, with numbered pages.
- **File format:** Microsoft Word (.docx preferred); OpenOffice or RTF acceptable.
- **Italics/underlining:** Use italics rather than underlining (except for URLs).
- **Notation:** Use "x/y" format for fractions; prefer Roman alphabet to Greek in mathematical notation; use conventional notation.
- **Footnotes vs. endnotes:** Footnotes preferred over endnotes.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Do not use cut-and-paste diagrams from PDF, JPEG, GIF, or PowerPoint (typesetting delays).
- No simultaneous submissions or previously published work.
- Anonymity: Remove all author-identifying information from abstract and manuscript body for blind peer review.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Voice and Register
**RELAXATION / PRACTICE ONLY:** First-person plural voice ("we test," "we argue") is less consistent in IRE articles than in the ABS 4*/4 baseline. Sampled articles predominantly employ **third-person or impersonal constructions** ("this study," "the research," "were identified," "our analysis reveals") rather than assertive "we" throughout. When first-person appears, it is sparse (1-2 occurrences in abstracts of 10 reviewed) and typically confined to passive-voice descriptions of methodology ("our analysis," "we conducted"). This signals a lower-tier journal norm: more formal/distant voice acceptable, though not required. Recommended approach: use first-person plural selectively; prioritize clarity and directness over rhetorical signposting of author agency.

### Citation Density
**PRACTICE ONLY (field baseline silent on ABS 2 norms):** Abstracts in IRE contain minimal or zero citations (typical for abstracts in all journals). Full-text citation density cannot be reliably assessed from available front-matter alone. Recommend following field baseline norms (high in intro/theory, lower in methods/results, moderate in discussion) unless full-text review reveals systematic deviation.

### Hypothesis Development
**RELAXATION:** Explicit "Hypothesis Development" sections are not universal in sampled articles, even for quantitative empirical work. Theoretical framing and research questions are embedded within Introduction or Theory sections rather than separated. This is a meaningful relaxation from the ABS 4*/4 baseline emphasis on enumerated hypotheses as a structural norm. Authors may integrate hypothesis development into narrative introduction without penalty.

### Practical Implications
**RELAXATION / FIELD DEFAULT:** No consistent expectation of a dedicated "Practical Implications" or "Managerial Implications" subsection in sampled articles. While one article (disability/entrepreneurship) included practical framing, it was not universal. This relaxes the field baseline, which treats practical implications as near-universal in entrepreneurship. Authors should still address implications, but a separate subsection is not mandatory; practical relevance may be integrated into Discussion or Contributions subsection.

### Appendices
**FIELD DEFAULT:** Sampled articles do not reference extensive appendices (sampling, robustness checks, measurement scales, etc.) as frequently as field baseline suggests. This may indicate either: (a) IRE reviews publish fewer appendices, or (b) front-matter excerpts do not reveal appendix structure. No evidence of deviation; align with field baseline if methodologically warranted (appendices are appropriate for methodological transparency).

### Article Types
**OBSERVED EXPANSION:** IRE publishes empirical, conceptual, and systematic literature review articles in equal measure within sampled issues. The field baseline reflects ABS 4*/4 emphasis on deductive quantitative and conceptual work; IRE shows no visible hierarchy. Systematic reviews are explicitly encouraged (e.g., Vol. 22 No. 2: "How do Entrepreneurs Experience Business Failure... A review of literature and research agenda"). Recommend consulting journal-specific guidance if submitting a literature review or qualitative ethnography.

### Submission Format
**HARD CONSTRAINT (not field-related):** Only Microsoft Word format accepted (unlike some ABS 4*/4 journals with LaTeX support). Submit to both lead editors: vanstela@tcd.ie and andrew.burke@tcd.ie. This is a practical constraint, not a writing convention, but essential for submission success.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Medium to long compound sentences with embedded citations appear standard; matches FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Argumentation and rhetorical moves (CARS pattern):** Introduction establishes phenomenon importance and narrows to a gap; sampled articles employ this structure. Contributions framed as extending/challenging/reconciling theory. FIELD DEFAULT applies.
- **Methods conventions (for empirical work):** Past tense, procedural detail on sampling/data quality observed in abstracts; full-text assessment needed but no deviation signaled. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Results reporting:** Hypothesis-by-hypothesis reporting separated from interpretation observed in sampled empirical articles. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Discussion structure:** Contributions, limitations, and forward-looking research agenda observed across articles. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** Moderate hedging in specific locations (Limitations/Discussion) observed; selective directness in Results reported. FIELD DEFAULT.

---

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Overstating first-person plural.** IRE tolerates third-person voice more than top-tier entrepreneurship journals. Avoid the assumption that "we" must dominate; neutral, clear prose is acceptable.

2. **Neglecting the 150-word abstract limit.** Authors accustomed to longer abstracts should trim ruthlessly. This constraint is non-negotiable and will cause desk reject if violated.

3. **Embedding diagrams as JPEG/PDF paste-ins.** The guidelines explicitly prohibit cut-and-paste charts. Create all visuals natively in Word to avoid typesetting delays and rejection.

4. **Omitting keywords or using vague terms.** Keywords assist categorization and discoverability. IRE expects clear, searchable terms (e.g., "entrepreneurial self-efficacy" rather than "business success").

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
