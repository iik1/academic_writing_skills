# Journal Writing Skill: Applied Health Economics and Heath Policy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature (Adis journals imprint)
- **Scope:** Publishes practical health economics research, reviews, and opinion pieces aimed at informing real-world healthcare decisions, covering healthcare interventions (devices, diagnostics, drugs) and health policy initiatives; positioned as a resource for decision makers and health economists rather than a theory-development outlet.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/40258/submission-guidelines (paywalled/login-gated on fetch); full text obtained from https://media.springer.com/full/springer-instructions-for-authors-assets/pdf/1501741_Additional%20Information%20for%20Authors%20(40258).pdf ("Additional Information for Authors," accessed directly as PDF)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 confirmed AHEHP articles (PMC9206925 – personalised medicine financing/reimbursement systematic review; PMC9596577 – Stockholm3 vs PSA cost-effectiveness decision tree; PMC10119248 – generic entry and opioid drug costs interrupted time-series; PMC4871905 – TURis NICE medical technology guidance; PMC12364733 – population net health benefit resource allocation; s40258-025-00988-3 – TDABC systematic literature review [abstract/metadata only, full text login-gated]; PMC11466881 – editorial, "Global excellence in health economics: Africa," used only to confirm editorial-format conventions, not counted toward structural sample)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Decimal-numbered headings required throughout (1, 2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.2.1, 2.2.2, 3, 4...), starting with the Introduction. This is stricter than FIELD_SKILL, which treats Roman or Arabic numbering as equally acceptable and does not mandate decimal subsection numbering.

Required front-matter elements, in order: Title page (title/subtitle, running head ≤100 characters) → Abstract → **Key Points for Decision Makers** (2-3 bullet points, non-technical language, must not repeat abstract text verbatim) → main text → **Declarations** section (subsections: Funding, Conflicts of Interest, Availability of Data and Material, Ethics Approval [clinical trials], Consent [clinical trials], Author Contributions) → Acknowledgements (if applicable).

### Length
Article-type-dependent word limits, not stated in FIELD_SKILL: Original Research articles up to 6,000 words (plus ESM); Review Articles up to 8,000 words; Leading Articles up to 4,000 words; Current Opinion and Practical Application articles up to ~3,000 words each. Systematic reviews may run longer (observed up to ~10,000 words per secondary source).

### Abstract
Format depends on article type, not uniform: narrative reviews use unstructured (no-heading) abstracts; clinical trial reports use CONSORT-structured abstracts; systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis) use PRISMA-structured abstracts. Standard word limit is 250 words but may be extended if the topic or reporting-guideline compliance requires it. This is a more granular abstract regime than FIELD_SKILL, which does not specify abstract structuring at all.

### Citations
Numbered bracketed citation style (e.g., [1], [2]) with an ordered reference list — not author-year. This directly overrides FIELD_SKILL's stated near-universal author-year norm. Authors must avoid citing non-peer-reviewed material (conference posters/abstracts, unpublished data, preprints) except sparingly and clearly flagged, and must not cite "predatory" journals.

### Headings
Decimal numbering system mandatory (see Structure above). Abbreviations used in a table or figure must be defined in an alphabetically ordered abbreviations list beneath the table or in the figure legend.

### Tables and Figures
Abbreviation lists required beneath tables/in figure legends, alphabetically ordered. No other journal-specific table/figure formatting rules stated beyond standard Springer conventions.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No list of required keywords (explicitly waived — "a list of keywords is not required," unusual for a medical-adjacent journal). Duplicate/prior publication generally prohibited, though preprints, trial registry postings, conference abstracts/posters, and meeting press releases are explicitly NOT considered prior publication. Data from predatory journals must not be used to support claims.

### Reporting Guidelines (journal-specific, not in FIELD_SKILL)
Mandatory adherence to study-type-specific reporting standards: CHEERS for economic evaluations (with completed checklist submitted, and authors "strongly encouraged" to submit their model for peer review); CONSORT for RCTs; STROBE for observational studies; PRISMA (checklist + flow diagram) for systematic reviews, with PROSPERO registration encouraged and the registration number required in both the methods section and at the end of the abstract if registered; MOOSE for meta-analyses of observational studies. Survey research requires submission of the full survey instrument alongside ethics approval evidence.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Confirmed impersonal/passive-leaning register across spot-checked articles ("population NHB can inform strategic decision making" rather than "we recommend"), with numbered citations reinforcing a clinical/health-services register rather than the first-person-plural econometrics voice FIELD_SKILL treats as near-universal. This is consistent with the journal's practical, policy-facing, medically-adjacent audience rather than a general economics readership. ENFORCED (consistent across all 7 spot-checked articles; matches the medical-journal lineage of the Adis imprint).

### Citation Density
Not assessed quantitatively beyond FIELD_SKILL's guidance; no delta observed in density, only in format (see Citations above).

### Structural Deviations
Two structural elements absent from FIELD_SKILL and mandatory here: (1) a **Key Points for Decision Makers** box (2-3 non-technical bullets) placed directly beneath the abstract — present in every original-research and review article spot-checked (PMC9206925, PMC9596577, PMC10119248, PMC4871905, PMC12364733); (2) a **Declarations** section with six enumerated subsections (Funding, Conflicts of Interest, Data Availability, Ethics Approval, Consent, Author Contributions) replacing the more loosely specified disclosure conventions FIELD_SKILL is silent on. ENFORCED — guideline-stated and confirmed in every full-text article checked.

Optional **Plain Language Summary** (up to 250 words, one paragraph, placed after the abstract under that heading) is available but not compulsory; none of the spot-checked articles included one. GUIDELINE ONLY / low prevalence in practice.

Empirical/economic-evaluation papers frequently embed a **numbered "Box"** presenting background concepts (e.g., "Box 1: Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs)") inline in the Methods or Introduction — a didactic device not part of FIELD_SKILL's structural repertoire. PRACTICE ONLY, observed in PMC12364733; not stated in guidelines.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Titles/subtitles of original-research manuscripts must describe the intervention/methodology/setting rather than the results — an explicit instruction not present in FIELD_SKILL, which is silent on titling norms. ENFORCED (guideline-stated; not independently verifiable via spot-check without systematic title-vs-finding comparison, but no counter-example observed).

Digital features (video abstracts, slide decks, infographics, podcasts) are actively solicited alongside articles and peer-reviewed if submitted at the same time as the manuscript — a publisher-level feature absent from FIELD_SKILL and from typical economics-journal practice. GUIDELINE ONLY (author-optional; prevalence not assessable from front matter alone).

## Common Pitfalls
- Omitting the Key Points for Decision Makers box or writing it in technical language / verbatim-copying abstract sentences into it — this is an enforced, distinctive requirement of the journal.
- Using author-year citations instead of the required numbered bracketed format.
- Submitting an economic evaluation without a completed CHEERS checklist, or a systematic review without a PRISMA checklist and flow diagram — both are treated as basic submission requirements, not optional extras.
- Writing a results-describing title for an original-research paper instead of one that describes the intervention/methodology/setting, contrary to explicit guideline instruction.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
