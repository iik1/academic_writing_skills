# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Financial Market Infrastructures

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Infopro Digital Risk Ltd (Risk.net / Incisive Media journals platform)
- **Scope:** The first journal specializing in peer-reviewed research on financial market infrastructures (FMIs) — payment systems, central counterparties, central securities depositories, trade repositories, settlement risk, collateral/liquidity management, and FMI oversight/regulation, including emerging topics like stablecoins, DLT, and quantum-readiness.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.risk.net/static/risk-journals-submission-guidelines (publisher-wide Risk Journals guidelines, applicable to JFMI); https://www.risk.net/static/call-for-papers#jfmi-section; https://www.risk.net/static/about-the-journal-of-financial-market-infrastructures
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (abstracts/front-matter only, full text paywalled): "On par: a money view of stablecoins" (Aldasoro, Mehrling, Neilson); "Skin in the game: risk analysis of central counterparties" (Cont, Ghamami); "Quantum-readiness for the financial system: a road map" (Auer et al.); "A liquidity black hole..." ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict. Sourced from the publisher-wide Risk Journals submission guidelines page, which governs JFMI as an Infopro Digital Risk journal; JFMI itself does not publish a separate detailed style guide.

### Structure
Manuscript file order: (1) separate title page with author names/affiliations/contact details, (2) figure and table legends, (3) abstract, (4) keywords, (5) key messages (bullet list), (6) title, (7) main text (author-anonymized for blind review), (8) declarations of interest and acknowledgements, (9) references. Recommended main-text section flow: Introduction, Literature Review, Data, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Conclusion — but JFMI explicitly also accepts non-empirical "policy-oriented papers" (forum discussions), so this structure is a default, not a requirement.

### Length
Research/technical papers: max 10,000 words. Forum (policy-oriented) papers: max 7,000 words. Publisher retains discretion on final length.

### Abstract
Single paragraph, 150–200 words, stating purpose, principal results, and major conclusions. No references in the abstract. Should reuse key phrases from the paper (keyword-bearing, presumably for indexing/SEO).

### Citations
In-text: author-date per Chicago Manual of Style. Reference list: APA format, alphabetical. DOIs included where available. This is stricter/more formal than the loose author-year convention typical of finance journals — cite Chicago-style in text but format the list APA-style.

### Headings
No journal-specific heading conventions beyond the recommended section flow above (Arabic-numbered sections per FIELD_SKILL norm; no deviation observed).

### Tables and Figures
Number of tables/figures should be minimized. Vector formats required (EPS, SVG, PDF) or minimum 300dpi if raster; rasterized formats (TIF/JPG/PSD) prohibited. Submitted both embedded in the main PDF and as separate editable files.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No submissions already published or under review elsewhere.
- No author-identifying information in the blind-review manuscript body.
- No rasterized (low-quality) graphics.
- No posting of the published paper online post-acceptance (per publisher policy).
- Footnotes used sparingly, max two sentences each.
- AI tool usage (data extraction, background research, editing) is permitted but must be explicitly disclosed and its use specified.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates.

### Voice and Register
This journal draws heavily on central-bank, BIS, and regulator-affiliated authors (e.g., BIS-authored quantum-readiness roadmap, central-bank co-authored payment system studies). Several sampled articles read as policy roadmaps/frameworks rather than pure hypothesis-testing empirical papers — prescriptive language ("emphasizes the need to start the transition today," "may involve cryptographic agility, defense in depth...") is more prominent than in typical ABS 4/4* empirical finance papers. PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION: the field-baseline norm of confident declarative claims for well-identified results still holds for the empirical papers in the sample (e.g., Heijmans and van der Woerd's failing-participant detection timing), but the policy-paper track legitimately permits more overtly normative, forward-looking, recommendation-style prose that FIELD_SKILL treats as atypical for finance journals generally.

### Structural Deviations
JFMI formally accepts two distinct submission tracks — technical (research) papers and policy-oriented "forum" papers — with different word limits (10,000 vs 7,000). FIELD_SKILL assumes a single canonical empirical-paper structure; this journal's forum-paper track can depart from that structure entirely (no requirement for Data/Methodology/Results sections). ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, consistent with the mixed-genre sample observed, including a purely conceptual/frameworks paper on responsible AI use in FMIs).

### Citation Density
No delta from FIELD_SKILL discernible from abstracts alone; front matter of sampled articles cites both academic literature and central-bank/regulatory publications (BIS papers, national payment system reports) at similar density to FIELD_SKILL's introduction norm. FIELD DEFAULT.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Abstracts in the accessible sample (e.g., Cont and Ghamami's CCP paper, Auer et al.'s quantum-readiness paper) run noticeably longer and more result-dense than the stated 150–200 word guideline, suggesting the length cap is GUIDELINE ONLY rather than strictly enforced pre-typesetting — treat 150–200 words as the target but not a hard ceiling the journal visibly polices at the publication stage.

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating the abstract word-count cap as immovable — sampled published abstracts regularly exceed 200 words; aim for the stated range but don't over-trim substance to hit it.
- Forgetting the "key messages" bullet list required in the manuscript file (distinct from the abstract) and the required declarations-of-interest section — these are publisher-mandated file components easy to omit if using a generic finance-journal template.
- Missing that this journal is genuinely bi-modal: a technical/empirical paper and a policy "forum" piece are both native to this venue, so authors should not force a policy contribution into a rigid empirical-paper template.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
