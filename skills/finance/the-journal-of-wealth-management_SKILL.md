# Journal Writing Skill: The Journal of Wealth Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Portfolio Management Research (PMR)
- **Scope:** Practical, actionable research on wealth management for high-net-worth investors, family offices, and financial advisors. Asset allocation, tax-aware investing, goals-based investing, manager selection, cross-generational wealth planning.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://pmr-journals.pm-research.com/index.php/jwm/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 articles identified from Fall 2024 and historical issues (titles: ICAPM for Goals-Based Investing, Presidential Elections and Asset Returns, REIT Performance and COVID-19, Carbon Footprint Index, Net-Zero Transition Guidelines)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Introduction must comprise **exactly four elements in order:**
1. Research question being addressed
2. Why the question is important to the wealth management industry (not academia)
3. Whether a solution will be proposed or only analysis
4. Roadmap to sections

Main text begins on page three. Conclusion must briefly summarize content reflecting abstract.

### Length
- **Target:** 4,000 words (excluding abstract, exhibits, references)
- **Acceptable range:** 2,500–7,500 words
- **Outside this range:** rarely accepted
- Online supplements permitted for detailed results/proofs (don't count toward length)

### Abstract
- **Word limit:** Exactly 160 words
- **Format:** Non-technical, single paragraph
- **Language:** Plain English, minimize jargon
- **Content:** Written for practitioners, not academics. Must help journal achieve its mission.
- **Structure:** Unstructured (no labeled sections like Background/Methods/Results)
- **Metadata:** Keywords and JEL codes appear on second page as separate fields, not in abstract body

### Citations
Chicago Manual of Style, 17th Edition, Chapter 15 (author-date format). No footnote citations; author-date parenthetical format throughout. Direct quotation is extremely rare.

### Headings
Arabic numerals (1, 2, 2.1). Pages numbered at bottom center.

### Tables and Figures
Called **"Exhibits"** (not "Tables" or "Figures"). 
- Number consecutively using Arabic numerals
- Must be distinguishable in both color and black-and-white printouts
- Maintain consistent fonts, capitalization, decimal alignment within exhibits
- Charts created in MS Word as objects (not pictures) with embedded data
- For equations: display on separate lines, aligned with text, numbered at right margin, spaces around operators
- All exhibits placed within text at appropriate points, not at end

### Formatting Standards
- **File format:** MS Word or LaTeX
- **Font:** 12-point Times New Roman (main text); 10-point (footnotes); 9-point or larger (exhibits)
- **Spacing:** Single-spaced with 12-point space before paragraphs
- **Page size:** 8.5 × 11 inches, 1-inch margins
- **Line numbering:** Page numbers centered at bottom

### Copyright and Distribution
Authors assign exclusive worldwide copyright to PMR. Severe restrictions: posting to SSRN, ResearchGate, or conference distribution prohibited without written permission. Permitted uses limited to: posting title/abstract with links, using charts/tables on presentation slides only.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No technical jargon without justification
- No papers written primarily for academics (rejected at desk)
- No papers that lack actionable investment guidance
- Cannot republish elsewhere during review process

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
**ENFORCED DELTA:** This journal explicitly targets **practitioners, not academics**. The submission guidelines state "plain English, minimizing the use of technical jargon." Manuscripts written primarily for academics are rejected at preliminary review.

FIELD_SKILL expects high technical density and domain vocabulary used freely. JWM reverses this: technical terms must be justified and defined for practitioners. Voice remains confident but accessible—explain identification strategies and econometric choices in practitioner-friendly language, not assuming familiarity with advanced methods.

**Example deviation:** A paper describing instrumental-variable identification in FIELD_SKILL would name the instrument and discuss validity assumptions briefly, assuming reader competence. In JWM, the same paper should explain why the instrument solves the problem and what assumption makes it credible, as if writing for a sophisticated portfolio manager, not an econometrician.

### Abstract Format
**ENFORCED DELTA:** Non-technical, unstructured 160-word limit. FIELD_SKILL describes abstracts as concise summaries (150–250 words) but does not constrain structure or language. JWM explicitly demands plain English and non-technical framing; abstracts for academic papers may include methodological detail. JWM abstracts read more like executive summaries for practitioners.

### Target Audience Specificity
**ENFORCED DELTA:** FIELD_SKILL describes finance journals serving "reviewers in this field" with high technical competence. JWM explicitly serves "investment professionals (investors, intermediaries, accountants, lawyers, analysts)" managing taxable, high-net-worth portfolios. This reframes evidence and examples: papers should discuss wealth transfer, tax brackets, alternative assets, and family governance—not portfolio theory for its own sake.

### Introduction Structure
**ENFORCED DELTA:** FIELD_SKILL describes a typical 8-move introduction: establish significance, identify gap, state question, preview approach, preview findings, state contributions, review literature, close with roadmap. JWM **mandates exactly four elements** (question, industry importance, solution type, roadmap) and prohibits academic framing. The "industry importance" move is explicit and required; papers must justify relevance to wealth managers, not to finance scholarship.

### Copyright and Reproducibility
**ENFORCED DELTA:** FIELD_SKILL assumes data availability statements and replication packages are "routinely required." JWM assigns exclusive copyright and restricts author distribution severely. Authors cannot post to SSRN or ResearchGate without permission—a major deviation from open-science norms in higher-tier finance journals. This suggests **lower pressure for reproducibility at publication**, though reproducibility is still stated as a core value.

### Conclusion
**ENFORCED DELTA:** FIELD_SKILL describes conclusions as 1–4 paragraphs covering question, findings, implications, limitations, and future directions. JWM mandates conclusions be "brief summaries reflecting abstract content"—much tighter coupling and more modest scope. Extensive future-work suggestions or lengthy limitation acknowledgment would violate this constraint.

### Exhibits (Tables/Figures)
**TERMINOLOGY DELTA:** Exhibits, not tables and figures. Requires color + black-and-white distinguishability explicitly. FIELD_SKILL notes table standards and footnoting but does not require explicit dual-mode design. Practical journals catering to presentation-heavy audiences often add this constraint.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Finance-typical medium-to-long sentences with embedded clauses apply. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Statistical significance reporting:** Standard notation (*, **, ***) implied; Chicago Manual applies. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Citation density by section:** High in introduction, low in conclusion. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** Calibrated language for findings vs. interpretations applies. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Person and active voice:** First-person plural expected for multi-authored; active voice dominates. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Methods conventions tense and detail:** Present tense for approach, high procedural detail expected. FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Robustness:** Systematic robustness treatment expected (alternative samples, specifications). FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Mechanism and channel analysis:** When mechanism is central, dedicated section expected. FIELD DEFAULT.

## Common Pitfalls for This Journal

1. **Overly academic framing:** Papers positioning contributions primarily to theory rather than practice (e.g., "we extend the CAPM literature") are desk-rejected. Frame every contribution as actionable for wealth managers.

2. **Jargon without context:** Using terms like "stochastic dominance," "cross-sectional dependence," or "dynamic programming" without plain-language explanation violates the journal's philosophy. Explain methods as if the reader is a smart portfolio manager, not a PhD student.

3. **160-word abstract violation:** Exceeding 160 words or including references/technical detail in the abstract is an immediate red flag. Write a crisp, practitioner-facing summary.

4. **Four-element introduction omission:** Missing any of the four required introduction elements (question, industry importance, solution type, roadmap) leads to desk rejection or major revision requests.

5. **Copyright-blind pre-posting:** Submitting to SSRN or ResearchGate before acceptance violates the journal's exclusive rights policy and can result in desk rejection or copyright action. Check with the editor first.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
