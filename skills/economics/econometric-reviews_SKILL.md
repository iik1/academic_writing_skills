# Journal Writing Skill: Econometric Reviews

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis
- **Scope:** A forum for state-of-the-art econometric theory and methods, retrospective/critical literature surveys, and shorter reports of new results, evidence, or comments on previously published papers. Content extends beyond econometrics into advanced empirical economics, statistics, and other social/life sciences.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://files.taylorandfrancis.com/lecrauth.pdf (Instructions to Authors, accessible directly; the tandfonline.com "about this journal" and article abstract/full-text pages return 403 to automated fetch but are visible via search-engine snippets and third-party mirrors)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (full text: Galvao & Lamarche, "A practitioner's guide to panel data quantile regression," ER 45:6, 774-799, DOI 10.1080/07474938.2026.2642065, open access. ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section architecture is specified in the guidelines beyond the required first-page elements (title, running title, abstract, keywords, JEL codes). Three article types exist with different expectations: (A) Regular research article (submission by invitation only), (B) Brief report (15 pages or less), (C) Note (7 pages or less). Regular articles are typically theory/methods surveys or new econometric methods papers; Notes and Brief reports carry shorter, more targeted contributions or comments on previously published papers.

### Length
Brief report: 15 pages or less. Note: 7 pages or less. Regular articles have no stated page cap in the guidelines, but this is an invitation-only category. Manuscripts must be double-spaced with minimum one-inch margins, pages numbered consecutively.

### Abstract
Strict cap of **100 words or fewer** — tighter than any general field norm. Guidelines explicitly instruct authors to **avoid abbreviations, diagrams, and references to the text within the abstract**. Running title required, maximum 75 characters/spaces (some journal pages state 50; treat 75 as the documented figure from the current Instructions to Authors PDF, use the more conservative 50 if in doubt). Abstract accompanied by no more than 4 keywords and no more than 4 JEL classification codes.

### Citations
Author-date in text, e.g., "(Smith, 1983)" — consistent with FIELD_SKILL default. Reference list alphabetical (not numbered) at the end. Distinct formatting given per source type (journal article, book, book chapter, government publication, software) — notably, software and government technical reports are explicitly included as citable reference types, which is not addressed in FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
No explicit heading-level convention stated (numbering style, capitalization) beyond the standard requirement of a title and running head.

### Tables and Figures
Tables and figures must NOT be embedded in text; submitted as separate files/sheets. Short descriptive title above each table, legend and footnotes below, all units included. Figures fully labeled, captions typed double-spaced on a separate sheet. Digital illustration files: 300 dpi or higher, sized to fit the journal page, EPS/TIFF/PSD format only. Color illustrations incur author-borne publication charges ($900 first page, $450 each of next three pages, custom quote beyond four pages).

### Explicit Prohibitions
Simultaneous submission elsewhere is prohibited; a statement of non-duplication is required with submission. Authors must obtain permission for copyrighted material and sign copyright transfer. Abstract must not contain abbreviations, diagrams, or text cross-references (see above).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm — the sampled full-text article (Galvao & Lamarche) uses first-person plural ("we offer," "we survey," "we develop") consistently and matches FIELD_SKILL's active-voice, direct-claim register.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in the sampled survey/methods article; no deviation observed in the abstract-only sample.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm, though as a venue for literature surveys and critical reviews, Regular articles (the survey/retrospective type) may run citation-dense throughout rather than concentrating citations mainly in the introduction — this reflects the journal's survey-article niche rather than a stated stylistic rule. PRACTICE ONLY, observed in the one full-text sample; not confirmed as a general norm across article types.

### Structural Deviations
GUIDELINE ONLY / RELAXATION: unlike ABS 4/4* empirical papers with a canonical Data/Empirical Strategy/Results structure, this journal's guidelines impose no mandated section architecture, and a meaningful share of its content is explicitly survey- or methods-theory-oriented (per Aims and Scope: "reviews of topical areas," "critical discussion and debate," "comprehensive literature reviews"). Authors writing for this journal have more structural latitude than the FIELD_SKILL empirical-paper template assumes. This is a RELAXATION consistent with ABS 3 status, not a new norm.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
The journal explicitly solicits comments on previously published papers as a standing article type, with commentary appearing in a subsequent issue — this correspondence-style contribution is not addressed in FIELD_SKILL.

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing an abstract over 100 words, or including an abbreviation, diagram reference, or in-text cross-reference inside the abstract — the guidelines flag this explicitly.
- Embedding tables/figures directly in the manuscript text instead of submitting them as separate files.
- Submitting a Regular research article without first securing an editorial invitation — this category is invitation-only; unsolicited long-form submissions should target the Brief report (≤15pp) or Note (≤7pp) category instead.
- Exceeding 4 keywords or 4 JEL codes on the title page.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
