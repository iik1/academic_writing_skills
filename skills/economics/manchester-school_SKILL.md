# Journal Writing Skill: Manchester School

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** General economics journal publishing original contributions across all areas of economics (microeconomics including industrial organisation and game theory, macroeconomics, econometrics, labour economics, and development economics). Includes authoritative surveys and theoretical/empirical work.
- **Publication Structure:** Five regular issues per volume + one special themed issue + one special supplement featuring Money, Macro and Finance Research Group Conference papers
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679957/homepage/forauthors.html (paywalled; free format submission info available via Wiley author portal)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 identified (full text access limited by paywall; analysis based on titles, abstracts, and EconPapers repository metadata from 2024-2026 publications)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm with standard economic paper sections. Free format submission accepted; authors may organize sections flexibly, but standard economics structure (Introduction, Methods/Approach, Results, Conclusion) is normative. No mandatory section naming or numbering convention specified.

### Length
**Maximum word count: 8,000 words** (FIELD_SKILL does not specify). **Maximum page extent: 30 pages**. This is notably tighter than research-intensive empirical papers, suggesting Manchester School favors concision and may discourage lengthy robustness sections.

### Abstract
Required. No explicit word limit specified in accessible guidelines. Format: unstructured (free format submission permits author discretion). Keywords required.

### Citations
**No prescribed style.** Author-year acceptable; references may be submitted "in any style or format, as long as it is consistent throughout the manuscript" (FIELD_SKILL: author-year is near-universal, but Manchester School's flexibility is explicit and unusual). Upon acceptance, typesetter reformats; pre-submission formatting precision not critical.

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (Arabic or Roman numerals acceptable; no standardized convention mandated).

### Tables and Figures
Required sections must include tables and figures with legends/captions. Figures should be uploaded in highest resolution possible. No explicit resolution or format mandates in accessible guidelines.

### Author Attribution and Footnotes
- **Author name and institution appear below title** (explicit requirement, differs from FIELD_SKILL default).
- **Acknowledgements and grant references appear as footnote to author name, not in main footnote list** (explicit requirement; FIELD_SKILL does not specify).
- **Footnotes kept to minimum, listed consecutively with superscript Arabic numerals** (explicit requirement for disciplinary consistency).

### Data Accessibility
**Strongly encouraged.** Authors should archive supporting data/artefacts in public repository and include data accessibility statement with repository link. This will be published alongside the paper. (FIELD_SKILL: not specified; Manchester School makes it a norm, not optional.)

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Plagiarism:** iThenticate software used; discovered overlap results in rejection and notification to author's institution.
- **Prior publication:** No material previously published or submitted elsewhere (standard; FIELD_SKILL norm).
- **Originality:** All content must be original work.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Citation Flexibility (Non-Standard for Field)
**Delta: GUIDELINE ONLY — explicitly stated but unusual for ABS 2+ journals**

Manchester School accepts references in any consistent format (author-year, numbered, alphabetical, etc.). Upon acceptance, the typesetter reformats per publisher house style. This is more flexible than FIELD_SKILL's near-universal author-year norm. Authors targeting Manchester School should not spend effort conforming citations to a specific style before submission; consistency matters more than format. This flexibility reflects Wiley's free format submission paradigm and is not typical of higher-tier economics journals.

### Data Availability (Norm Elevation from Field Optional)
**Delta: ENFORCED (stated AND emphasized)**

While FIELD_SKILL does not mandate data availability statements, Manchester School encourages authors to archive data and publish an accessibility statement. This is framed as a strong norm ("encourages authors"), not a hard requirement, but it appears in multiple guideline passages and aligns with broader journal transparency trends. Papers without data statements are not automatically rejected, but inclusion is normatively expected for empirical work.

### Author Attribution Format (Explicit Requirement)
**Delta: ENFORCED (explicitly stated)**

Author name and institution must appear below the title. Acknowledgements and grant references belong in a footnote to the author name, not in the main footnote list. FIELD_SKILL does not specify this format; it is journal-specific. This is a hard formatting constraint for accepted manuscripts.

### Concision Pressure (Suggested by Length Limits)
**Delta: RELAXATION (implicit)**

Manchester School's 8,000-word / 30-page cap is tighter than typical ABS 4* outlets and suggests the journal values concision. However, ABS 2 tier itself implies potential relaxation from ABS 4* norms, so this is not a singular delta but reflects the journal's positioning. Papers are expected to make their case concisely; extensive robustness appendices are discouraged by space constraints.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Over-formatting citations before submission:** Manchester School will reformat references upon acceptance under free format submission. Spending effort on strict APA or Chicago compliance before submission is wasted; ensure consistency and completeness instead.
2. **Omitting data accessibility statement for empirical work:** While not a hard requirement, the journal strongly encourages data archiving and a statement. Empirical papers without this are at a minor disadvantage; include one if your work uses data.
3. **Violating the 8,000-word / 30-page limit:** This is a hard constraint unusual for ABS 2 economics journals (which sometimes allow longer papers). Plan your paper structure to fit. Appendices count against this limit.
4. **Placing grant acknowledgements in main footnote list:** These must appear as a footnote to the author name below the title. Standard footnotes for citations and supplementary comments go in the main list only.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
