# Journal Writing Skill: Economics and Sociology

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Centre of Sociological Research, in cooperation with University of Szczecin (Poland), Széchenyi István University (Hungary), Mykolas Romeris University (Lithuania), and Dubček University of Trenčín (Slovakia)
- **Scope:** Quarterly open-access journal covering interdisciplinary economics and sociology topics — welfare states, labour markets, financial literacy, management, tourism, demography, and social policy, with emphasis on empirical and mixed-methods studies, often with a country/regional comparative focus.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.economics-sociology.eu/?en_instructions-for-authors%2C4= (accessible, detailed)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (all Vol. 15, No. 1, 2022, via direct article pages: DOI 10.14254/2071-789X.2022/15-1/6 through /10, plus 4 others from the same issue — Baranowski & Jabkowski; Aliyev, Gasimov & Eynalov; Moreno-Carmona et al.; Astorquiza Bustos et al.; Swadźba; Florek & Lewicki; the Turkish healthcare leadership study; Zeka & Veri). Sample limited to one issue because search-engine and site access to other volumes' full abstract pages was inconsistent; the guidelines page and this issue's abstracts were reliably fetchable and internally consistent, so no further access attempts were pursued (ABS 1, good-faith sample per instructions).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Mandatory front matter before the Introduction: author name/title/ORCID, full institutional affiliation, title, abstract, keywords (max 5, must reference the country/countries of the research), JEL codes. Introduction must explicitly state novelty, urgency, research goals, methodology, and limitations. Body uses meaningful headings. Conclusions section is mandatory and must contain no new data (Discussion/Recommendations sections optional and welcome). Empirical papers require a dedicated methodology chapter with organization/dates, data source and sample details, sampling error, exact survey question wording, and analysis methods.

### Length
10–15 pages total, 1.0 line spacing, Times New Roman 12pt.

### Abstract
150–200 words, unstructured single paragraph, discloses main idea AND results (not just topic).

### Citations
APA style, in-text author-year, alphabetical reference list.

### Headings
Section headings limited to 1–7 words.

### Tables and Figures
Separate numbering per type; placed immediately after first textual reference; reused tables/figures require scanned written permission from original source submitted to editorial office.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No previously published or simultaneously submitted work. Footnotes discouraged/minimized. No reuse of others' tables/figures without permission.

### Other administrative requirements
ORCID, Google Scholar link, and LinkedIn/ResearchGate profile required for identity verification at desk review; multi-author submissions must disclose each author's percentage contribution and its nature (e.g., model design, literature review, data analysis) plus a hand-signed statement. Processing fee of 650 EUR + VAT on acceptance.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
No delta in formality, but tone is more explicitly policy-prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL's reticence: abstracts and conclusions routinely end with direct policy recommendations ("The authors recommend policies strengthening institutional trust..."). PRACTICE ONLY, consistent across sampled articles — treat as a genuine journal norm despite small sample.

### Statistical Reporting
Methodological toolkit skews toward applied social-science methods rarely emphasized in ABS 4*/4 econ (fsQCA, mediation analysis, exploratory factor analysis, qualitative interview coding via software like MAXQDA) alongside standard econometrics (difference-in-differences, OLS, multilevel models). FIELD DEFAULT reporting precision (quantitative claims, magnitudes) still holds where regression-based methods are used, but qualitative/mixed-methods papers are common and acceptable — a RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's implicit econometrics/theory-centric methods scope, appropriate to this journal's interdisciplinary remit and ABS 1 tier.

### Structural Deviations
None beyond the Hard Constraints above — introduction sequencing (motivation, question, approach, results preview) and conclusion norms match FIELD_SKILL. FIELD DEFAULT.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Keywords must include the country/countries studied — nearly all sampled articles are single- or multi-country comparative studies (Colombia, Turkey, South Africa, Poland/Czech Republic/Hungary, post-Soviet states), reflecting the journal's comparative/regional focus. GUIDELINE, confirmed as ENFORCED across all 9 sampled articles.

## Common Pitfalls
- Missing country/region keyword — explicitly required and consistently present in every sampled article; omitting it is a guideline violation.
- Abstract that only states topic without results — the guideline and all sampled abstracts disclose findings, not just aims.
- Insufficient methodology detail for empirical papers — the journal's guidelines specify an unusually granular list (exact survey wording, sampling error, organization conducting the study) beyond typical ABS 4*/4 practice.
- Missing ORCID/Google Scholar/LinkedIn profile links at submission — stated as a possible desk-rejection reason.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
