# Journal Writing Skill: Vezetestudomany Budapest Management Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Corvinus University of Budapest (Corvinus University Press / journals.lib.uni-corvinus.hu)
- **Scope:** General management journal (est. 1970), the primary Hungarian venue for business, management, and organizational studies. Publishes empirical and theoretical work across organizational structure, processes, performance, and cultural/behavioral/technological topics, at individual, group, organization, and network levels. Explicitly welcomes multidisciplinary work (sociology, psychology, economics, political science, communication) with a stated "Central European perspective." Rated MTA "A" by the Hungarian Academy of Sciences; SCOPUS-indexed from 2022.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.lib.uni-corvinus.hu/index.php/vezetestudomany/information/authors (also see https://journals.lib.uni-corvinus.hu/index.php/vezetestudomany/aimsandscope)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed section template is mandated in the guidelines beyond standard IMRaD-style expectations implied by "methodological rigor"; no explicit required section list published.

### Length
Manuscript: 6,000–9,000 words total, **including references, figures, and tables**. Title maximum 15 words.

### Abstract
Maximum 150 words, single paragraph (not structured into labeled subheadings), but must address: (1) problem statement and importance, (2) relevant theories, (3) methodology/research design, (4) key findings and implications, (5) limitations (optional). Abstract and title are required in **both English and Hungarian** in the submission metadata. Keywords: 3–7, discipline-specific.

### Citations
APA 7th edition. Author-year in text; first author + "et al." for three or more authors. Quotations require page numbers. DOIs hyperlinked where possible.

### Headings
No explicit heading-label rules beyond APA conventions; italics only for emphasis (no bold, no underline).

### Tables and Figures
Numbered consecutively with titles; source attribution required; grayscale preferred; minimum 10-point lettering; must "enhance textual arguments" (not decorative).

### Explicit Prohibitions
No prior publication or simultaneous submission elsewhere. No author-identifying information in submitted files (double-blind review). No footnotes — use endnotes only if necessary. AI-tool use in writing, image/graphic production, or data collection/analysis must be disclosed transparently.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
**Bilingual publication with Hungarian-only full text (ENFORCED / journal-specific, not covered by FIELD_SKILL).** The journal publishes ~10 issues/year in Hungarian and ~2 in English. Across the spot-checked sample, article title/abstract/keywords are consistently presented in English on the landing page, but the linked full-text PDF is frequently Hungarian-only (e.g., "Colours and attention in FMCG retail," "Research on... digital signature adoption," "Operational features of science and technology parks" all show "PDF (Hungarian)" as the only full-text option despite an English abstract). Authors targeting this journal should confirm in advance which language the target issue requires, since an English abstract does not guarantee the manuscript itself will run in English.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
**Abstract structure is more prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL's generic guidance (RELAXATION/specification, not a contradiction).** FIELD_SKILL specifies 150–250 words with no fixed content checklist; this journal caps at 150 words (tighter than field norm) but supplies an explicit five-part content checklist (problem/importance, theories, methodology, findings/implications, limitations) that authors should follow like a mental structured abstract even though it stays a single unlabeled paragraph.

**No footnotes permitted (journal-specific prohibition not addressed in FIELD_SKILL).** FIELD_SKILL does not mention footnote policy; this journal disallows footnotes outright and permits endnotes only when necessary.

**Word/page limit is comparatively short for the field (RELAXATION).** 6,000–9,000 words inclusive of references/tables/figures is markedly shorter than the 10,000–18,000+ word norm FIELD_SKILL documents for AMJ/ASQ/JOM-tier quantitative or qualitative papers, consistent with this journal's ABS 1 tier — expect proportionally thinner literature review and robustness sections than a top-tier submission.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming an accepted English abstract guarantees an English-language publication — many issues (10 of 12 per year) run in Hungarian, and full text for English-abstracted articles is sometimes Hungarian-only.
- Exceeding the 150-word abstract cap by treating it like the looser 150–250 word FIELD_SKILL range.
- Including footnotes (not permitted; use endnotes).
- Underestimating how much shorter this journal's word budget is relative to top-tier MGMT outlets — over-long literature reviews will blow the 6,000–9,000 word ceiling once tables/references are counted.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
