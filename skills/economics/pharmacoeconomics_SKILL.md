# Journal Writing Skill: PharmacoEconomics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature
- **Scope:** Benchmark journal for peer-reviewed, authoritative articles on the practical application of health economics and outcomes research to optimize healthcare resource allocation. Focus on health technologies (drugs, diagnostics, devices, personalized medicine, surgical interventions).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/40273/submission-guidelines (behind paywall; extracted from search and article samples)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 full articles (Ardito et al. 2025, Centanni et al. 2025, Johnson et al. 2025)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
ENFORCED: Standard sections include Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, and Conclusions. No deviations from field baseline observed.

### Length
ENFORCED: Manuscripts typically span 3,000–8,000 words. No explicit page limit stated in accessible guidelines, but observed articles in 2025 issues span pages 5–29, 31–43, 45–52, suggesting flexible length tied to content quality.

### Abstract
ENFORCED: 150–250 words (may be extended to 450 words for complex studies or when required by reporting guidelines). Structured abstract with explicitly labeled sections:
- Background and Objective (or Background/Aims)
- Methods
- Results
- Conclusions

Keywords: 3–10 keywords required after abstract.

### Citations
ENFORCED: Numbered sequential system in square brackets [1], [2], etc. Multiple references formatted as [1,2] or ranges [1–4]. Full DOI links required in reference list where available. No author-year parenthetical citations; numbered format is mandatory.

### Headings
ENFORCED: Decimal system with maximum 3 hierarchy levels (e.g., 1, 1.1, 1.1.1). No roman numerals; arabic numerals only.

### Tables and Figures
Follows field norm. Tables and figures are the primary vehicle for quantitative results; captions should be descriptive. Supplementary materials referenced as "Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM)" or "Online Resource" with numbered references (Table 1 ESM, Fig. 2 ESM).

### Submission Format
Manuscripts in Word document, plain font (10-point Times Roman preferred). Authors must declare competing interests and provide author contributions statement.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None observed beyond standard academic integrity requirements. All sampled articles include Open Access Creative Commons licensing statements, suggesting the journal supports open publication.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structured Abstract and "Key Points"
ENFORCED: PharmacoEconomics requires a structured abstract with labeled subsections (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions), departing slightly from the field baseline's flexible abstract approach. Additionally, all sampled recent articles include a "Key Points for Decision Makers" section immediately after the abstract—a journal-specific feature not present in top-tier ECON journals. This section typically presents 3–5 bulleted take-aways for policy/decision makers, reflecting the journal's applied health-policy focus.

### Citation Numbering (No Parethetical Author-Year)
ENFORCED: PharmacoEconomics mandates numbered bracketed citations [1], [2], while the ECON field baseline permits either numbered or author-year formats. This is a genuine delta. All observed articles use the numbered system exclusively.

### Decimal Heading System
ENFORCED: The decimal heading system (1, 1.1, 1.2) is stated in guidelines and observed in all articles. The ECON field baseline does not enforce a particular heading convention; PharmacoEconomics specifies decimal numbering.

### Supplementary Materials Labeling
PRACTICE ONLY: All three articles sampled reference "Online Resource" or "Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM)" with systematic numbering. This convention is observed consistently but not explicitly stated in accessible guidelines.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT. First-person plural ("we") is standard; active voice preferred throughout.
- Sentence and paragraph style: FIELD DEFAULT. Medium to long sentences with high technical vocabulary density; paragraphs 4–10 sentences, each developing a single idea.
- Argumentation and rhetorical moves: FIELD DEFAULT. Introduction announces results quantitatively; explicit contribution statements; research question stated early; conclusion is shorter than introduction and omits new results.
- Methods conventions: FIELD DEFAULT. Mixed tense (past for procedures, present for model properties); sufficient detail for replication; identification strategy clearly stated for empirical work; robustness checks expected.
- Results and reporting norms: FIELD DEFAULT. Results and interpretation integrated; tables are primary; statistical and economic significance both reported; null results handled directly.
- Discussion and conclusion norms: FIELD DEFAULT. Conclusion provides genuine synthesis, acknowledges limitations, suggests future directions; no new results in conclusion.
- Hedging and epistemic caution: FIELD DEFAULT. Low hedging intensity; clear claims preferred; hedging only for external validity, causal limits, data gaps, and mechanism uncertainty.

## Common Pitfalls (Journal-Specific)

1. **Forgetting the "Key Points" section.** Editors expect a bulleted summary for decision-makers after the structured abstract. This is not optional for empirical articles.

2. **Using author-year citations.** PharmacoEconomics enforces numbered bracketed citations [1]. Submissions with (Author, Year) format will require reformatting.

3. **Unstructured abstracts.** The journal requires explicit labels (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions). Free-form abstracts will be rejected at desk review.

4. **Exceeding 450 words in abstract without strong justification.** While extended abstracts are permitted for complex methods, reviewers expect clear justification (e.g., adherence to PRISMA-ScR or similar reporting guidelines).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
