# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Entrepreneurship and Public Policy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** Emerald Group Publishing Ltd
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed journal dedicated to high-quality research exploring the dynamic interplay between entrepreneurship, enterprise development, and public policy. Welcomes empirically oriented academic papers and accepts a wide variety of empirical evidence (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-methods).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jepp (paywalled; author guidelines information available through publisher submission portal)
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (multiple issues, 2023–2024)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows standard IMRaD (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion) pattern for empirical articles. Conceptual/viewpoint pieces may substitute a more thematic structure. Common subsections observed in Discussion: Contributions, Practical/Policy Implications, and Limitations and Future Research. No explicit section numbering required by guidelines; however, clear heading hierarchy is standard practice across sampled articles.

### Length
No explicit word or page limit stated in publicly available guidelines. Observed article lengths range from approximately 15–30 pages (including references and appendices), with typical empirical studies around 18–25 pages. No evidence of strict cap enforcement.

### Abstract
Structured abstract is required (Purpose/Design/Findings format observed across all sampled articles). Typical length: 150–200 words. Design/Methodology/Approach and Findings are always present; sometimes includes Practical Implications subsection.

### Citations
Author guidelines note anonymity in peer review: "ensure the manuscript does not contain any information that might help the reviewer identify you." Self-citations should follow the phrasing "previous research has demonstrated" not "our previous research has demonstrated." No explicit citation style stated in publicly available guidelines; observed articles use APA-style parenthetical citations and alphabetized reference lists.

### Headings
No explicit restrictions. Observed practice: clear hierarchical structure (primary, secondary, sometimes tertiary levels) with sentence-case or title-case headings. Subheadings within sections (e.g., "Theoretical Framework," "Data Collection," "Robustness Checks") are common and encouraged.

### Tables and Figures
Tables and figures are integral to results presentation. Descriptive statistics and correlation matrices are standard for quantitative studies. Effect-size visualizations (interaction plots, mediation diagrams) are common in discussion sections. No explicit style guide provided; practice follows APA or Emerald house style (minimal, professional appearance).

### Explicit Prohibitions
**Generative AI policy:** Copywriting (creating, drafting, or writing) any part of a submission using generative AI tools and technology is not permitted. Copy-editing (correcting, editing, formatting, modifying, or refining) all or part of an author's own original existing work using generative AI tools is permitted.

Manuscripts must be original; no author names should appear in the manuscript text, figures, or captions to maintain anonymous review.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Article Type Diversity
RELAXATION: While entrepreneurship-field-writing-style emphasizes empirical (quantitative/survey) and conceptual articles, JEPP visibly publishes a wider range of article types including empirical studies, viewpoint pieces (McCaffrey 2023 flagged as "exploratory...intended to motivate further research"), and data-focused methodological contributions (Norris et al. 2024 introducing a new dataset). The journal accepts research originating from diverse methodological traditions (regression, SEM, case studies, archival analysis).

### Generative AI Transparency
ENFORCED but not stated in FIELD_SKILL: This journal explicitly prohibits generative AI for copywriting but permits copy-editing. This is more permissive than some top-tier policies and should be noted in drafts submitted here.

### Results Section Tone
PRACTICE ONLY: Results sections in sampled articles (Khan et al., Tibaingana et al., Segantini) report findings in direct, unhedged language ("Findings reveal," "results indicate") with minimal interpretive framing; interpretation is strictly deferred to Discussion. This aligns with FIELD_SKILL norm but is observed consistently across all sampled empirical articles.

### Practical/Policy Implications Placement and Emphasis
ENFORCED: All sampled articles (including conceptual pieces) include explicit Practical Implications or Policy Implications sections as part of the Discussion or as a dedicated subsection. Emphasis on actionable, policy-relevant takeaways is more prominent than in some ABS 4*/4 journals, reflecting the journal's explicit focus on the policy-entrepreneurship nexus. Recommendations to policymakers, business leaders, or public sector stakeholders are near-universal.

### Multi-Study Designs and Longitudinal Approaches
FIELD DEFAULT but with emphasis: JEPP does not require multi-study designs; single empirical studies are accepted and common. However, longitudinal or panel approaches (Segantini's 11-year panel, Mueller's two-wave design) are well-represented and encouraged where appropriate, reflecting the journal's interest in temporal dynamics and causal inference.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Generative AI use for initial drafting or copywriting:** Explicitly prohibited. Ensure all prose composition is your original work; copy-editing assistance is permitted but not initial creation.
2. **Identifying the author in manuscript text or figures:** Any reference that reveals authorship (e.g., "as we showed in our prior work," institutional affiliations in example captions, or self-identified case studies) will compromise anonymous review and may result in desk rejection.
3. **Weak policy or practical implications:** This journal's scope centers on policy-entrepreneurship linkages. If your empirical contribution lacks clear relevance to policy, regulation, or practice, it will be flagged as out of scope. Ensure Practical/Policy Implications section directly addresses "so what?" for policymakers or practitioners.
4. **Unclear positioning relative to policy debate:** JEPP seeks articles that say "something interesting about the relationships among public policy and entrepreneurship, entrepreneurship and economic development, or all three." Ensure your introduction clearly situates your work within this policy-entrepreneurship nexus, not merely within entrepreneurship theory or policy analysis in isolation.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

This skill was developed using publicly accessible front-matter and abstract information, supplemented by spot-checking of full texts where available. No access limitation prevented verification of key structural norms (article structure, heading conventions, practical implications prominence, generative AI policy). The sample size (8 articles across 4 issues, 2023–2024) is sufficient for an ABS Tier 1 journal to identify consistent structural and stylistic patterns.
