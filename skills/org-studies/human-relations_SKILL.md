# Journal Writing Skill: Human Relations

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** ORG STUD
- **Publisher:** SAGE, on behalf of The Tavistock Institute, London
- **Scope:** International journal publishing empirical and conceptual research
  on social relationships at and around work, spanning quantitative,
  qualitative, and theoretical/critical-review contributions.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/pb-assets/cmscontent/HUM/Human%20Relations%20How%20to%20prepare%20your%20submission-1684321352.pdf
  (detailed "How to prepare your submission," version May 2023); supplemented by
  https://www.humanrelationsjournal.org/journal/submission-guidelines/
- **Field baseline:** organisation-studies-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (abstract-level access to 7; one full-text
  conceptual/theoretical article read in full). Titles/DOIs: "Coping with
  personhood limbo" (Derakhshan et al., 2025, 78(2)); "Reimagining agency:
  Subjectivity as a radical lens on institutional work" (Fotaki, Prasad and
  Shymko, forthcoming 2026, full text via Warwick WRAP repository);
  "Beautifying a stigmatized occupation" (Soral et al., 2025); "Relations
  between reflexivity and institutional work" (Tuominen, 2025, 78(8));
  "Precarious work: A critical review" (Doshi, Segarra and Śliwa,
  forthcoming); "Creating inclusivity through boundary work?" (van Eck,
  Dobusch and van den Brink, 2024, 77(2)); "'And then I undress the work'"
  (Turunen et al., 2025). Full-text access was blocked by SAGE paywall for
  most items; abstracts, keywords, and section structure were assessed via
  publisher abstract pages, university repositories, and search-engine
  snippets. This is a good-faith spot-check; the access chain was exhausted
  rather than arbitrarily stopped at 8.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No single mandated section template beyond the field default (this journal
runs both hypothesis-testing and inductive-qualitative traditions, per
FIELD_SKILL). Two structural specifics not in FIELD_SKILL: (1) tables and
figures are placed together after the references list, not embedded in
text, with "INSERT TABLE X ABOUT HERE" markers at the intended location;
(2) essential notes go at the end of the text, before the references, not
as page-bottom footnotes. Critical Reviews are a distinct, gated article
type: they may only be submitted in response to an annual special-issue
call; unsolicited critical reviews submitted as regular manuscripts are
automatically desk-rejected.

### Length
Hard cap of 45 correctly formatted pages total (abstract, text, tables,
figures, notes, references, appendices, everything included). Manuscripts
exceeding this are returned for reformatting rather than reviewed. If a
manuscript exceeds 40 pages, authors must justify this in the cover
letter. Times New Roman 12pt, 1-inch margins, double-spaced throughout
except tables/figures (single-spaced). One table or figure per page.

### Abstract
100–200 words (tighter and more prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL's "well
under 250 words, unstructured"). Content must address: research topic and
questions, participants, methods used, results, data analysis,
conclusions, practical implications, and suggested future research. The
journal explicitly encourages an "inviting and enticing" framing — posing
a question, making a clear statement, or a controversial claim — aimed at
readers outside the author's specialism, for discoverability/SEO reasons
stated directly in the guidelines.

### Citations
SAGE Harvard, author–date. Notable specifics not in FIELD_SKILL: no ibid./
op. cit./infra/supra; author initials without spaces or full points;
three-or-more-author citations use "et al." from the first citation
(no full listing on first mention); multiple citations by the same author
cited together separate years with commas, oldest first; personal
communications are cited in text only, never in the reference list;
institutional/authorless sources are cited by institution or publication
name. Double-blind review is enforced: self-citations must use third
person ("in their previous study," not "in our previous study"); "Author,
2006"-style anonymized citations are explicitly prohibited — if a
citation cannot be phrased to avoid identifying the author(s), it must be
omitted entirely from both text and reference list.

### Headings
Exactly three heading levels, each explicitly specified: (i) bold
lower-case with initial capital; (ii) italic lower-case with initial
capital; (iii) italic lower-case with initial capital, run into the start
of the paragraph followed by several spaces and then regular text
(run-in heading). This is a specific typographic scheme not covered by
FIELD_SKILL.

### Tables and Figures
Single-spaced, one per page, placed after the references (not embedded
in-text); each needs a short descriptive title, capitalized only in the
first word of title/headings/labels; notes and sources go underneath with
acronyms spelled out after a "Notes:" heading. Colour figures must remain
comprehensible in black-and-white (captions must not reference colour).

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No simultaneous submission or prior online publication (exceptions:
  institutional repository postings and conference proceedings with a
  rewritten abstract).
- Quotations should be minimized and kept to 100 words or fewer where
  possible; italics and quote marks should be avoided for in-text terms
  where possible.
- Quotations exceeding 400 words require prior publisher permission.
- Rejected papers may not be resubmitted unless the action editor
  explicitly invites resubmission.
- AI usage is permitted only for copy-editing/proofing, must be declared
  during submission, and must be detailed in the cover letter; the
  journal requires an "AI usage declaration" statement in the manuscript
  (observed in spot-checked article).
- Numbers below 10 spelled out unless paired with units; decimals use
  full points with a leading zero (e.g., 0.125); large numbers use spaces
  not commas (10 000); dates in the form "22 March 2015."

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Conceptual/theoretical papers (as distinct from empirical studies) end
with a short "Concluding remarks" section rather than a full Discussion,
and are followed by Acknowledgements, Funding, an AI usage declaration,
and author Biographies — all of which appear as standard end-matter in
the spot-checked full-text article. Epigraphs (a short quotation preceding
the introduction) appear as an accepted stylistic device in at least one
theoretical article. PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence for the epigraph; the
end-matter sequence (Acknowledgements/Funding/AI declaration/Biographies)
is ENFORCED insofar as AI declaration is concerned, PRACTICE OTHERWISE for
biographies (not mentioned in the guidelines PDF but present in the
sampled article, consistent with SAGE's standard journal template).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
The journal explicitly foregrounds "ongoing threads of conversation
across works published in the journal" — the cover letter must describe
which specific prior Human Relations articles the submission builds on.
This is a submission-process norm (cover letter), not a manuscript-text
convention, but is distinctive enough to note. GUIDELINE ONLY (cover
letter content, not manuscript prose).

  consistent with observed sample).
- Statistical reporting: FIELD DEFAULT (not independently distinguishable
  from field norm in the spot-check; no quantitative-heavy article was
  fully accessible).
- Citation density: FIELD DEFAULT (dense in intro/theory, sparse in
  results, consistent with FIELD_SKILL pattern in the full-text sample).
- Hedging and epistemic caution: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Argumentation (CARS model, explicit contribution statements): FIELD
  DEFAULT, confirmed in the full-text sample ("We argue that...",
  "we advance four contributions").

## Common Pitfalls
- Using "Author, Year" or "XXXX" placeholders for self-citations to
  preserve anonymity — the journal explicitly prohibits this; omit the
  citation entirely if it cannot be phrased in third person.
- Writing an abstract as a general unstructured summary (the FIELD_SKILL
  default) instead of covering all eight required content elements
  (topic/questions, participants, methods, results, analysis,
  conclusions, practical implications, future research) within the
  100–200 word cap.
- Submitting an unsolicited literature/critical review as a regular
  research article — Critical Reviews are only accepted via the dedicated
  annual special-issue call and are otherwise desk-rejected.
- Embedding tables/figures within the manuscript text rather than placing
  them after the references with "INSERT TABLE X ABOUT HERE" markers.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
