# Journal Writing Skill: Investment Management and Financial Innovations

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** LLC "Consulting Publishing Company 'Business Perspectives'" (Sumy, Ukraine)
- **Scope:** Theoretical and empirical research on financial management, corporate governance, investments, and innovations (including quantitative methods), aimed at an international community of academic and practitioner financiers. Founded 2004; quarterly; fully open access since January 2017; indexed in Scopus.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://businessperspectives.org/journals/investment-management-and-financial-innovations (submission-guidelines page returned 403 in this environment; structural/format requirements below are reconstructed from search-engine summaries of the guidelines plus direct observation of 7 full-text articles, cross-checked for consistency).
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 full-text articles read in full, plus front-matter/abstracts of several more via search snippets (12+ total touched). Full-text sample: Rahayu et al. (2026, IMFI 23(1)) financial literacy/PLS-SEM; Cho, Oh & Kim (2025, IMFI 22(2)) CEO overconfidence/deferred tax assets; Tsaurai & Danquah (2025, IMFI 22(2)) FDI/human capital threshold; Ustundag, Sivri & Ari (2025, IMFI 22(2)) options trading optimization; Chaudhary et al. (2025, IMFI 22(1)) heuristic bias/SEM; Al Barak (2025, IMFI 22(1)) cash holdings/panel regression; Lyeonov et al. (2025, IMFI 22(4)) bibliometric analysis. Sample spans Korea, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Nepal, South Africa/Ghana, and a Poland/Slovakia/Ukraine team — consistent with a globally-submitted, non-elite-affiliation ABS 1 outlet.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Papers must comply with **AIMRaD** (Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion) for empirical/research papers, or **AITbRaD** (Abstract, Introduction, Theoretical Basis, Results, and Discussion) for theoretical papers. Review papers use Abstract, Introduction, Literature review, generalization of main statements, Discussion. Manuscripts not conforming to the submission guidelines are rejected at the initial screening stage. Confirmed in all 7 sampled articles: unlabeled/untitled "INTRODUCTION" (always first, in caps, no number), then numbered sections beginning at **"1."** — almost always "1. LITERATURE REVIEW" (sometimes merged with hypotheses as "1. LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESES"), followed by "2. METHOD(S)/METHODOLOGY", "3. RESULTS", "4. DISCUSSION", then unnumbered all-caps "CONCLUSION" (singular, not "CONCLUSIONS" in most samples though the bibliometric paper used "CONCLUSIONS"), "AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS", optional "ACKNOWLEDGMENTS"/funding statement, "REFERENCES".

### Length
4,000–6,000 words for full research papers (1,000–2,000 for short communications), excluding author info, title, abstract, keywords, references, and appendices. Reference list capped at ~50 sources (observed articles range 38–74 references, several exceeding the stated cap — GUIDELINE ONLY / loosely enforced in practice). Supplementary materials ≤5 pages. Submissions in Word (.DOC/.DOCX).

### Abstract
Single unstructured paragraph (no subheadings), but noticeably longer and more narratively complete than the FIELD_SKILL norm (150–250 words) — observed abstracts commonly run 200–320 words and routinely state the sample, method, headline coefficient(s)/statistics, and practical implications within the abstract itself, not just in the body. JEL Classification codes and Keywords appear as separate labeled fields directly beneath the abstract on the same page (not embedded in abstract text). Keywords are short (single words or 2–3 word phrases, comma-separated, 5–10 items), not full descriptive phrases.

### Citations
Author-year parenthetical, matching FIELD_SKILL. No delta.

### Headings
Numbered sections use Arabic numerals starting at 1 for Literature Review (Introduction is unnumbered). Subsections use decimal numbering (e.g., "2.1. Respondent profiles"). Consistent across all sampled articles — matches FIELD_SKILL general norm but the fixed label set (Introduction / Literature Review / Method(s) / Results / Discussion / Conclusion) is more rigid and formulaic than typical ABS 4/4* flexibility.

### Tables and Figures
Every table and figure is numbered and captioned ("Table 1.", "Figure 1."), heavily footnoted with significance-level legends (*** p<0.01, ** p<0.05, * p<0.1 or equivalent). Cover page displays "NUMBER OF REFERENCES / NUMBER OF FIGURES / NUMBER OF TABLES" as metadata — figures are frequently 0–2 even in empirical papers; tables (regression outputs, descriptive statistics, robustness panels) are the dominant visual vehicle, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No explicit prohibitions found in accessible guideline text beyond standard double-blind formatting requirements (no author-identifying information in the manuscript body) and the reference-count/word-count ceilings above.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
- **Mandatory unnumbered "INTRODUCTION"** followed by **Literature Review as Section "1"** (not the introduction). FIELD_SKILL treats the introduction as the natural home for literature positioning; this journal instead pushes the bulk of the literature review into a separate numbered section, often merged with explicit numbered hypotheses (H1, H2, H3...) in a dedicated hypothesis list — a much more formulaic, textbook-style hypothesis presentation than the "Related literature" subsection embedded in the intro that FIELD_SKILL describes. ENFORCED (consistent across all 7 articles).
- **"DISCUSSION" is consistently a separate, numbered section distinct from both "RESULTS" and "CONCLUSION"** — all three appear as discrete sections in every sampled article. FIELD_SKILL notes that ABS 4*/4 finance papers "most... merge discussion and conclusion into a single 'Conclusion' section," with standalone Discussion "uncommon... appears more frequently in experimental papers." At this journal, standalone Discussion is the universal norm regardless of paper type (econometric panel studies, SEM studies, optimization studies, bibliometric studies all use it). ENFORCED / RELAXATION-in-reverse (a stricter, not looser, structural requirement than the ABS 4*/4 baseline).
- **Mandatory detailed "AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS" section using full CRediT taxonomy** (Conceptualization, Data curation, Formal analysis, Funding acquisition, Investigation, Methodology, Project administration, Resources, Software, Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing – original draft, Writing – review & editing), with each category attributed by author name. This is universal, detailed, and appears in 100% of sampled articles, including single-author papers (where every category is attributed to the sole author). Not mentioned in FIELD_SKILL at all. ENFORCED.
- **Cover/front-matter metadata block** (Authors, Article Info citation, DOI, Received/Accepted/Released dates, License, Journal/ISSN/Publisher, Number of References/Figures/Tables, Conflict-of-interest statement) precedes the abstract on a dedicated first page, then the abstract/title/author-affiliation block repeats on page 2 alongside JEL Classification and Keywords fields. This publisher-template layout is a formatting artifact rather than a prose convention but is worth noting for anyone assembling a submission-ready draft.

### Statistical Reporting
Coefficient + t-statistic (or z-statistic) + significance stars reported together in tables is near-universal (e.g., "β = 0.614, T = 24.591, p < 0.001" stated explicitly in running prose, not just tables) — slightly more exhaustive in-text restatement of table values than the FIELD_SKILL norm of "narrate the most important coefficient(s)... direct the reader to the table for full detail." Sampled articles routinely restate coefficient, test statistic, and p-value together for every hypothesis in the text, hypothesis-by-hypothesis (H1 supported/not supported framing). PRACTICE ONLY (not stated in guidelines but observed in the majority of the sample, likely driven by the mandatory numbered-hypothesis structure).

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm overall (confident declarative results, hedged interpretation), but the explicit "Therefore, H1 is supported" / "H4 is not supported" sentence formula appears verbatim after nearly every hypothesis discussion in SEM/regression-hypothesis papers — a much more mechanical, checklist-style reporting idiom than the flowing prose FIELD_SKILL describes for ABS 4*/4 results sections. PRACTICE ONLY, prevalent but not universal (absent in the bibliometric and options-optimization papers, which have no discrete hypotheses).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- Non-Anglophone author teams and single-country case-study designs (Korea, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Nepal, Turkey, Ghana/South Africa, Ukraine) dominate the sample; papers are frequently framed as "evidence from [single country]" contributions rather than cross-country or US/UK-centric studies typical of ABS 4*/4 finance. This shapes framing in the introduction: motivation sections lean heavily on national statistics/regulatory context (e.g., a national financial-literacy survey, a national tax authority's regulatory announcement) rather than global market-size framing. RELAXATION of the FIELD_SKILL "Opening move — Establish significance... broad, high-stakes economic context," which is often localized to a single national context here rather than global/systemic framing.
- Sample sizes and methods skew toward survey-based SEM/PLS-SEM studies of individual investor behavior (behavioral finance, financial literacy) alongside more conventional panel/firm-level regression studies — a broader methodological mix than the identification-strategy-heavy causal empirical norm FIELD_SKILL describes for ABS 4*/4 finance. Many papers are cross-sectional survey designs without a strong causal identification strategy; robustness treatment is typically alternative proxies/PSM/subgroup splits rather than instrument validity or parallel-trends discussion. RELAXATION (ABS 1 tier).
- JEL classification codes are more granular/multiple (often 3–5 codes) and always displayed prominently as a standalone field, consistent across the sample.

## Common Pitfalls
1. Omitting the separate, detailed "AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS" (CRediT) section — this journal requires it in full even for single-author papers.
2. Merging Discussion into the Conclusion, or omitting a standalone numbered Discussion section — both are treated as separate required sections here, unlike the more flexible ABS 4*/4 norm.
3. Placing the literature review inside the Introduction rather than as numbered Section "1" — reviewers expect Introduction to motivate and preview only, with the formal literature review and (for quantitative papers) an explicit numbered hypothesis list appearing as a discrete subsequent section.
4. Writing an abstract under ~200 words or omitting the sample/method/headline-result detail that this journal's abstracts consistently include — FIELD_SKILL's 150–250-word guidance is on the short side for this journal's actual practice.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
