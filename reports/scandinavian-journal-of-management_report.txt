HARD CONSTRAINTS IDENTIFIED
===========================
Source: Elsevier publication ethics policy, journal homepage editorial principles, accessibility guidelines

1. STRUCTURE
   - Three article types: full-length, focused discussion (3,000–5,000 words), book reviews
   - Standard order: Introduction → Methods/Research Setting → Results/Findings → Discussion → Conclusion
   - Clear intro required for non-specialists

2. ABSTRACT
   - Format: Unstructured (no labeled sections)
   - Length: Max 250 words
   - Content: State purpose, principal results, major conclusions; avoid references unless essential (cite author/year only)
   - Standalone requirement: can be presented separately from article

3. KEYWORDS
   - Count: 1–7 required
   - Language: English
   - Format: Avoid multi-word keywords with "and"/"of"; no non-standard abbreviations unless firmly established

4. CITATION STYLE
   - Format: APA author-year (Author, Year) or Author (Year)
   - Multiple citations: alphabetical or chronological order
   - Et al.: used for 3+ authors from first citation

5. HEADINGS
   - Maximum: 3 levels
   - Differentiation: via font size, bold, or italics

6. ETHICS
   - Elsevier Publishing Ethics Policy required
   - Human subjects: ethics approval + informed consent required
   - No patient/subject identifying information allowed (names, initials, hospital numbers, dates of birth, etc.)
   - Competing interests, data availability, acknowledgments at article end

7. WORD LIMITS
   - Focused discussion texts: 3,000–5,000 words (explicitly acceptable)
   - Full-length articles: no specific maximum disclosed in accessible sources

All hard constraints are either explicitly stated in guidelines or align with standard Elsevier Journals templates.

DELTAS FROM FIELD_SKILL BASELINE
=================================

DELTA 1: Reflexivity and Researcher Positioning [ENFORCED]
-----------------------------------------------------------
Field baseline (MGMT): Emphasizes methodological rigor and identifies qualitative methods as including reflexivity checks (member-checking, triangulation).

SJM delta: Journal explicitly requires authors to "address and reflect on the role, positioning, and impact of the researcher/s in researching and theorizing."
This is a formal editorial principle ("Reasoned Reflexivity"), not merely a qualitative best practice.

Evidence: Found in all accessible editorials and policy statements; no article sample needed for this principle-level finding.

Classification: ENFORCED (stated in editorial principles + expected in manuscript approach)

Implication: Qualitative and theoretical papers must integrate reflexive positioning into the narrative, not relegate it to methods section.

---

DELTA 2: Contextual Awareness and Decentering of Management [ENFORCED]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Field baseline (MGMT): Focuses on organizational and management theory; assumes management as primary analytical lens.

SJM delta: Journal explicitly states that "management is embedded in society and nature" and calls for "decentering management" while "foregrounding greater contextual sensitivity."
Authors must address broader societal and environmental implications, not merely organizational relevance.

Evidence: Found in all editorial principle statements on SJM website.

Classification: ENFORCED (stated editorial policy)

Implication: Discussion and conclusion sections must explicitly situate findings within social/ecological contexts; scope conditions should address contextual boundaries (e.g., Nordic/democratic/stable contexts).

---

DELTA 3: Emphasis on Challenging Existing Knowledge [PRACTICE ONLY]
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Field baseline (MGMT): Discourages purely confirmatory tests of well-established predictions; values extension or complication.

SJM delta: Journal more explicitly invites research driven by "curiosity" and "challenging inquiries" that question management orthodoxy.
Editorial states: "original research driven by curiosity over mundane organizational life" and welcomes inquiries that are "challenging."

Evidence: Found across editorial statements; article sample (e.g., "Entrepreneurship as a family resemblance concept," "The embedded-agency paradox revisited") shows titles framing work as reconceptualizations or challenges, not confirmations.

Classification: PRACTICE ONLY (evident in editorial framing and article titles, but not explicitly stated as requirement in author guidelines)

Implication: Frame contributions as challenges, complications, or reconceptualizations rather than confirmations. Introductions should position niche as a gap or contradiction to be challenged.

---

DELTA 4: Non-mainstream Methodology, Epistemology, Ontology [ENFORCED]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Field baseline (MGMT): Tolerates diverse methodologies (quant, qualitative, mixed, review); expects methodological rigor.

SJM delta: Journal explicitly "welcomes non-mainstream research approaches in terms of methodology, epistemology and ontology."
This is stated as a core editorial policy, not a secondary accommodation.

Evidence: Found in journal scope/mission statements and editorial principles.

Classification: ENFORCED (stated policy)

Implication: Non-conventional epistemologies (constructivist, realist, post-modern) and novel methodologies should be framed as central to the research design, not as alternatives. Methodological sections should articulate epistemological/ontological foundations and justify them.

---

DELTA 5: Focused Discussion-Type Article Format [ENFORCED]
-----------------------------------------------------------
Field baseline (MGMT): Publishes empirical studies, theoretical papers, and reviews; no explicit short-format discussion category.

SJM delta: Journal explicitly publishes "focused discussion-type texts (around 3,000–5,000 words), empirically or theoretically oriented."
This is a named, accepted article type, not a note or correspondence section.

Evidence: Found in journal scope statement and submission guidelines.

Classification: ENFORCED (stated article type)

Implication: Short, tightly argued theoretical critiques or conceptual pieces (3,000–5,000 words) that might be rejected elsewhere as too brief are an appropriate submission category.
