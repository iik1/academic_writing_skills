# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Management and Enterprise Development

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** Inderscience Publishers
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed research on enterprise development, managerial capabilities, innovation, strategic practices, and management processes in international and cross-cultural contexts. Addresses how organisations, particularly MSMEs and growth-oriented enterprises, improve performance, competitiveness, and sustainability.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.inderscience.com/mobile/inauthors/index.php?pid=70
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (titles and abstracts from 2025-2026 publications; full-text access limited by paywall)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
Follows standard empirical article structure: Title, Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, Sections (no prescribed exact labels but typically theory/literature review, methods, results, discussion), Conflict of Interest Declaration, References. Appendices acceptable for supplementary material. Manuscript must not exceed 7000 words excluding figures, tables, and references; articles above this may be considered if high-quality but are exception rather than norm.

### Length
- **Article length:** 5000–7000 words (excluding figures, tables, and references)
- High-quality articles exceeding 7000 words may be considered

### Abstract
- **Format:** Structured, less than 150 words
- **Required elements:** aim, methodology, conclusions
- **Constraints:** avoid references; define uncommon abbreviations at first mention

### Keywords
- **Count:** approximately 10–15 words or phrases
- **Purpose:** for searchability

### Citations
- **Style:** Harvard (name-date) system with examples "Hamel (2000)" or "Nelson and Winter (1982)"
- **References:** Full references must include authors' names/initials, publication date, article title, publication title (italicized), volume/issue, publisher, page numbers, DOI when available
- **Websites:** include access date; URL shortened to maximum 90 characters

### Headings
Numbered section headings acceptable and common, though specific conventions not mandated in guidelines

### Tables and Figures
- Figures placed at end of manuscript, numbered sequentially
- Color figures appear in color online; print versions in black and white
- Full captions required for figures

### Language and Spelling
- English (UK spelling and terminology required)
- Text must be "checked, edited and corrected by the authors"

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Conference papers are not acceptable unless complete rewriting and copyright clearance obtained
- Briefs and research notes are not published in this journal
- Articles cannot be under consideration elsewhere
- "Previously published in English" material disallowed without disclosure of prior publication in other languages

### Mandatory Declarations
- **Conflict of Interest:** Authors must declare conflicts
- **Informed Consent:** Required declaration for human participant research
- **AI Disclosure:** Authors using AI tools must "include a note in the article's Acknowledgements section describing the technologies used and their purpose"
- **Author List:** Definitive at submission; changes only permissible during review with EIC approval
- **Preprint Servers:** Authors may post to ArXiv, ora.ox.ac.uk, Research Square with acknowledgment of journal citation upon acceptance

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding Word Limits Without Justification:** The 5000–7000 word target is firm; submissions significantly above 7000 words risk desk reject unless of demonstrably high quality. Plan structure to fit within this envelope from the outset.

2. **Omitting Mandatory Declarations:** Conflict of interest, informed consent (if applicable), and AI tool disclosure (if used) are non-negotiable. Missing these delays review.

3. **Insufficient Abstract Precision:** Abstracts must explicitly state aim, methodology, and conclusions in less than 150 words. Vague abstracts ("This paper explores X") without methodological detail commonly require revision.

4. **Non-Harvard Citation Format:** While author guidelines mandate Harvard style, submissions with Vancouver or numeric formats trigger formatting revision cycles. Audit references early.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Notes

This journal operates at the ABS 1 tier (lower than the 4*/4 baseline journals grounding the field skill). Consequently, while it maintains professional standards and follows entrepreneurship field conventions broadly, it is more permissive in structure (e.g., no mandatory subsections), more practitioner-oriented in scope, and more accessible to case-study and commentary formats. The author guidelines are publicly available and unambiguous on formatting; compliance is expected and audited by editorial staff before review. The journal's explicit AI disclosure requirement reflects recent publication policy (post-2023); all submissions using generative AI tools must acknowledge this.

Access to full-text articles was constrained by Inderscience paywall; spot-check was conducted via article titles, abstracts, and metadata from IDEAS/RePEc and publisher listings for 2025–2026 publications. No contradictions between stated guidelines and observed practice were detected, but limited visibility into in-text style (voice, paragraph structure, hedging placement) due to access constraints. FIELD_SKILL norms are assumed to apply where no journal-specific guidance is stated.
