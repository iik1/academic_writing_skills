# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of International Marketing

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** MKT
- **Publisher:** American Marketing Association (distributed via SAGE)
- **Scope:** International marketing theory, research, and practice, explicitly aimed at both academics and senior/mid-level international marketing practitioners; standardization/adaptation, cross-cultural consumer behavior, foreign market entry, global branding, and international marketing strategy are core topics.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/jig (points to shared AMA guidelines: https://www.ama.org/submission-guidelines-american-marketing-association-journals/)
- **Field baseline:** mkt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 with accessible abstract/metadata (Poulis 2024, "Standardization and Adaptation as a Coconstituted Process," Vol. 32(2); Donthu, Kumar, Pandey & Lim 2021 bibliometric retrospective; Özsomer 2026 editorial "What Is International Marketing?"), plus journal landing-page descriptions of ~5 additional recent articles/features (AI-translated video content study, product-coolness cross-cultural study, dynamic capabilities framework piece, trademark/Madrid Protocol study, Executive Insights AI/global-disruption piece). Full text was paywalled for all; abstracts/section structure inferred from search-engine snippets, publisher metadata, and the journal's own landing page. Access-limited — see Sources.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict. Sourced from the shared AMA journals guidelines page, which JIM's SAGE submission page points to.

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (quantitative empirical or behavioral templates as applicable). One AMA-wide structural rule: the opening section of the main text is never labeled "Introduction" — it begins directly under the title with unlabeled opening commentary.

### Length
- 50-page maximum (title, abstract, keywords, text, footnotes, references, tables, figures, print appendices combined); web appendices excluded.
- Title: 25 words maximum. Footnotes: used sparingly, ≤40 words each.
- **JIM-specific article type not in FIELD_SKILL: "Insight Papers"** — under 4,000 words total, no reference-count limit, expedited two-round review. Intended for rapid-dissemination findings or confirmatory/disconfirmatory extensions of an already-published JIM article, requiring initial empirical evidence.

### Abstract
- 200 words maximum, unstructured, third person.
- Minimum of 3 keywords (JIM guidance specifies "minimum 3"; AMA general guidance says "up to 8").

### Citations
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (author-year, APA-style). AMA-wide detail: up to three authors listed in-text; four or more use "et al."; full first names (not initials) required in the reference list.

### Headings
AMA-wide, not FIELD_SKILL default: three heading levels max, no numbered sections, primary headings centered/bold/title-case, secondary left-aligned bold-italic title-case, tertiary left-aligned italic sentence-case. Minimum of two secondary headings per major section.

### Tables and Figures
- Tables/figures numbered consecutively, placed in text (not at the end); tables must be editable (not images).
- Figures: Arial font preferred, both axes labeled, error bars specified, colorblind-safe palettes, author-borne color-printing costs.
- **Empirical papers must report exact p-values throughout (not thresholds like p < .05), standard errors of parameter estimates in tables, and effect sizes.** This is a formal AMA-journals reporting policy explicitly stated on JIM's guidelines page — stricter than the FIELD_SKILL default of asterisked significance stars; report both exact values and stars where a table format requires it.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No mail/email submissions; no identifying author info in the main document; no page numbers/headers/footers on submission.
- Do not label the opening section "Introduction."
- Do not insert tables as images.
- AI-generated content must be disclosed in Methods and Acknowledgments.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
JIM offers a distinct short-form article type, "Insight Papers" (<4,000 words), absent from FIELD_SKILL's three templates (behavioral/quantitative/conceptual). Classify as ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, and the journal's own landing page actively promotes this format alongside regular research articles).

### Statistical Reporting
Exact p-value / effect-size / SE reporting mandate is ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; consistent with what visible article structures show, e.g., tables described as including effect sizes). This tightens FIELD_SKILL's default of significance-star tables — report actual values in addition to (or instead of) stars.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- Recurring "Executive Insights" feature/article type bridging academic findings and practitioner relevance — PRACTICE ONLY / GUIDELINE, evidenced by journal landing-page content (e.g., "Executive Insights in the Age of AI and Global Disruption") but not detailed in the formal author guidelines. When targeting this format, favor practitioner-facing framing over the standard academic contribution-statement structure.
- Practitioner orientation is explicit at the journal level ("aimed at both...scholars and practitioners at senior- and mid-level international marketing positions") — RELAXATION/emphasis shift consistent with ABS-3 tier allowance to relax rather than redefine FIELD_SKILL's managerial-implications norm; managerial implications should be weighted at least as heavily as theoretical contribution, more so than the typical MKT baseline.

## Common Pitfalls
- Labeling the opening section "Introduction" (AMA-wide prohibition; will trigger a required revision before review).
- Reporting only significance stars/thresholds (p < .05) instead of exact p-values, SEs, and effect sizes as JIM's guidelines explicitly require.
- Submitting a short empirical extension as a full-length regular article when it would fit — and move faster — as an Insight Paper (<4,000 words, two-round review).
- Underweighting managerial/practitioner implications relative to theoretical contribution, given JIM's explicit dual academic-practitioner audience.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
