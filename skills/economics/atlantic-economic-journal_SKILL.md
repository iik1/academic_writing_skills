# Journal Writing Skill: Atlantic Economic Journal

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature (for the International Atlantic Economic Society)
- **Scope:** Publishes theoretical and empirical articles and public policy papers tracing economic changes and developments globally; aims to facilitate cross-national synthesis of economic research. Broad topical range (public finance, monetary policy, labor, trade, applied micro, sports/behavioral economics).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.iaes.org/publications/atlantic-economic-journal/aej-submission-instructions/ (primary; Springer's link.springer.com/journal/11293/submission-guidelines is paywalled/login-gated but corroborates length, abstract, and format rules)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 (abstract/front-matter level; full text paywalled) — "The Deadweight Losses from 'Liberation Day'" (Neill, 2025, DOI 10.1007/s11293-025-09836-0); "Cultural Preference for Redistribution in the United States" (Richwine, 2025, DOI 10.1007/s11293-025-09833-3); "Skilled Immigration and Business Diversity: Evidence from the H-1B Lottery" (Ma, 2025/2026, DOI 10.1007/s11293-025-09841-3); "Income Inequality and Water Quality in the U.S." (Silva Lecuna & Cohen, 2025, DOI 10.1007/s11293-025-09827-1); "Third-Party Grading as a Useful Measure of Demand: Evidence from NBA Cards" (2025, DOI 10.1007/s11293-025-09835-1); "The Persistent Rise in Corporate Cash Holdings" (Hill, 2025, DOI 10.1007/s11293-025-09823-5); "Are NBA Players Equally Valued by Team Owners and Trading Card Collectors?" (2024, DOI 10.1007/s11293-024-09798-9)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section architecture beyond a required first page containing title, abstract, JEL classifications, and keywords. No numbered headings requirement.

### Length
Maximum 25 pages total (12pt font, 1-inch margins), including references, footnotes, tables, and figures. Entire manuscript double-spaced, pages numbered consecutively. This is materially shorter than typical ABS 4/4* manuscript length.

### Abstract
150–250 words, unstructured (no labeled subsections). 4–5 keywords required. Acronyms should be avoided in the abstract.

### Citations
Author-date, alphabetical reference list. In-text: "and" when embedded in a sentence, "&" inside parentheses; three-or-more-author works use "et al." Same-author/same-year works distinguished with lowercase suffixes (2015a, 2015b).

### Headings
No numbered section headings. Main headings set in bold; subheadings set in italics — a distinct visual hierarchy from the numbered Roman/Arabic scheme FIELD_SKILL treats as standard.

### Tables and Figures
Numbered in citation order with descriptive titles and explicit data sources. Tables must be editable text (not images), placed on separate pages within the manuscript. Figures must be black-and-white in the main manuscript; color permitted only in online supplementary appendices. Electronic figure formats: EPS, TIFF, JPG, or PSD, 600–1200 dpi.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- PDF-only manuscript submissions are rejected; must submit editable Word or LaTeX (with PDF) source files.
- "Computer output dumps" are not acceptable as tables or figures — results must be formatted into proper tables.
- Color figures are prohibited in the main manuscript body.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
GUIDELINE ONLY, but ENFORCED in practice: the guidelines explicitly instruct authors to "minimize first-person pronouns ('I,' 'we') except for personal opinions" and to use past tense for completed work. This directly contradicts FIELD_SKILL's stated norm that "we" is the near-universal default at the ABS 4/4* level. All seven spot-checked abstracts confirm this: authors write in impersonal third person ("This study compares...", "The paper examines...", "The analysis found...") rather than "we compare" or "we examine." Drafts for this journal should default to impersonal, agentless constructions rather than first-person plural.

### Structural Deviations
PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION: given the strict 25-page cap, articles are visibly more compact than ABS 4/4* norms — e.g., one spot-checked article ran only 3 published pages (Hill 2025, pp. 109–111), consistent with the journal accepting short notes/comments alongside full articles. This is a length relaxation, not a structural redefinition; no distinct section architecture beyond the standard Introduction–body–Conclusion pattern was observed.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED: heading typography (bold main headings, italic subheadings, unnumbered) differs from FIELD_SKILL's numbered-heading assumption and should be applied when formatting a manuscript for this journal.

## Common Pitfalls
- Drafting the abstract or body in first-person plural ("we find," "we show") — this journal's guidelines explicitly ask authors to minimize "I"/"we" outside of stating personal opinions.
- Submitting a manuscript over 25 pages inclusive of references/tables/figures, or exceeding the 150–250 word abstract range.
- Using numbered section headings or a Roman-numeral scheme instead of bold/italic unnumbered headings.
- Submitting color figures in the main manuscript, or PDF-only source files.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
