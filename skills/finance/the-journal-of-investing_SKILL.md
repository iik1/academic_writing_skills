# Journal Writing Skill: The Journal of Investing

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Portfolio Management Research
- **Scope:** Investment management, asset allocation, performance measurement, benchmarking, and practical portfolio strategies for institutional buy-side professionals. Quarterly publication since 1992.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://pmr-journals.pm-research.com/index.php/JOI/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** Limited access; 47 articles published in 2024; key example: Richard Ennis, "Have Alternative Investments Helped or Hurt?" (Feb 2024)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscript must follow a fixed three-page format:
- **First page:** Title (max 12 words), date, and complete author contact information with role/affiliation
- **Second page:** Title, date, abstract (~160 words), keywords, and JEL codes
- **Third page onward:** Main article text begins

### Length
- **Target:** 4,000 words (excluding abstract, exhibits, references)
- **Acceptable range:** 2,500–7,500 words
- **Title:** Maximum 12 words

### Abstract
- Approximately 160 words
- **Non-technical** (key departure from FIELD_SKILL)
- Must NOT contain references (ENFORCED)
- Define all acronyms on first use
- Serves as discovery mechanism for potential readers

### Citations
Chicago Manual of Style (CMS), 17th Edition, Chapter 15 (author-date format). Footnotes used for peripheral or tangential commentary, NOT for references.

### Formatting Specifications
- **Paper size:** 8½ × 11 inches
- **Margins:** 1 inch all around
- **Main text font:** 12 point Times New Roman
- **Footnotes:** 10 point Times New Roman
- **Tables:** 9-point minimum
- **Spacing:** Single spacing with 12-point space before paragraphs
- **Paragraph indents:** First-line indents (one tab stop)
- **Page numbers:** Bottom center
- **File format:** MS Word or LaTeX only

### Headings
No explicit convention stated in guidelines. Observe standard finance conventions from FIELD_SKILL.

### Tables and Figures
- All exhibits must be camera-ready for black-and-white printing (ENFORCED RELAXATION from color norms)
- Equations displayed separately with Arabic numbering
- No color or grayscale gradients

### Key Takeaways
- **ENFORCED unique requirement:** Include three (3) key takeaways highlighting main findings
- Appears to be a distinct submission element beyond abstract and conclusion

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts written "primarily for academic audiences face preliminary rejection" (ENFORCED)
- Plagiarized text never acceptable
- Must be original, unpublished work only
- Upon acceptance, author must remove all prior versions from SSRN and other websites

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice, Register, and Audience
**RELAXATION (lower tier):** The journal explicitly targets "investment professionals" and demands "plain language, minimizing the use of technical jargon." This is a significant departure from FIELD_SKILL's "high technical density" norm. The instruction that manuscripts written "primarily for academic audiences face preliminary rejection" suggests that the paper must prioritize practitioner accessibility over academic rigor in tone and vocabulary choice.

Implication: Reduce domain-specific terminology relative to ABS 4*/4 baseline. Avoid econometric jargon where plain-language explanation is possible. Use accessible examples and practical framing.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines); PRACTICE ONLY (suggested by preliminary rejection threat, but enforcement level unclear from guidelines alone).

### Abstract Format
**ENFORCED DEVIATION:** Requirement for non-technical abstract that excludes references is stricter than FIELD_SKILL norm (which simply requires 150–250 words). The explicit "non-technical" mandate reinforces the practitioner audience.

**Classification:** ENFORCED.

### Tone Toward Academic Audience
The prohibition on papers "written primarily for academic audiences" suggests a lower tolerance for academic framing conventions (e.g., literature positioning, theoretical contributions) than FIELD_SKILL assumes. However, limited article access prevents confirmation of how strictly this is enforced in practice.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (stated); PRACTICE ONLY (unknown enforcement level).

### Statistical Reporting and Methodology
FIELD_SKILL demands high procedural detail and replication sufficiency. The Journal of Investing's focus on "practical investment ideas" and professional audience may relax this expectation, but author guidelines do not explicitly address statistical precision or robustness reporting. No evidence of departure observed in limited sample.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT (no explicit deviation stated or confirmed).

### Citation Density
No explicit guidance deviates from FIELD_SKILL. No delta observed.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT.

### Structural Deviations
**ENFORCED requirement for Key Takeaways:** The journal requires "three key takeaways highlighting main findings" as a distinct manuscript element. This is not mentioned in FIELD_SKILL and appears unique to this journal. Likely position is at end of introduction or start of conclusion, or as a separate box/callout in submission.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (unique to journal).

### Length and Economic Significance
The wide acceptable range (2,500–7,500 words, targeting 4,000) is broader than typical ABS 4*/4 papers. Combined with practitioner audience, this may signal less emphasis on exhaustive literature review and more emphasis on concise, actionable insight.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).

### Results Presentation
FIELD_SKILL expects tables as primary vehicle and precise coefficients/standard errors. Guidelines do not explicitly relax this. However, the "plain language" mandate and "key takeaways" requirement suggest that interpretation and accessibility may rank higher relative to exhaustive statistical detail.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT (no explicit delta, but practitioner tone may imply different emphasis in practice).

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Submitting with academic framing as primary focus.** The journal explicitly warns that papers "written primarily for academic audiences face preliminary rejection." Lead with practitioner relevance, not literature contribution.

2. **Using technical jargon without explanation.** The "plain language" mandate is enforced. Define domain terms or use lay-friendly analogies.

3. **Exceeding word count or missing three key takeaways.** The 4,000-word target and mandatory three key takeaways are strict submission requirements. Non-compliance risks desk rejection.

4. **Forgetting the fixed three-page manuscript format.** The first and second page structure (title page + abstract page) is required. Deviating from this template may result in preliminary rejection.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access Limitations & Notes

**Full-text article access was not available** in the public domain during this research. Author guidelines were extracted from the official submission page (now hosted on pmr-journals.pm-research.com). Spot-checking was limited to one confirmed 2024 article abstract (Ennis) and general information about journal scope from Wikipedia and publisher materials. The journal is published by Portfolio Management Research (PMR) and indexed in EBSCO, ProQuest, and Scopus, confirming academic visibility despite ABS 1 tier.

**Early stop was NOT triggered.** The guidelines were comprehensive enough to extract clear hard constraints. The primary limitation is inability to verify how strictly the "non-academic audience" requirement and "plain language" mandate are enforced in practice, which would require access to several recent full-text articles.

**Recommended verification:** Upon submission, consult the latest author guidelines at https://pmr-journals.pm-research.com/index.php/JOI/about/submissions and contact the managing editor (m.gang@pm-research.com) with questions on tone and framing expectations for borderline cases.
