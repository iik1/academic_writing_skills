# Journal Writing Skill: Entreprises et Histoire

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** BUS HIST & ECON HIST
- **Publisher:** Éditions ESKA (Paris)
- **Scope:** International journal founded 1992, publishing articles in French or English on business history, management history, and economic history. Thematic issues with 4-5 main articles per issue (20-25 pages each), plus debates, archival documents, thesis reviews, and book announcements.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journaleska.com/index.php/eeh/authorsguidelines
- **Field baseline:** business-economic-history-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (editorial, THQ video game company monograph, urban by-products markets, circular economy issue collection)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
**Opening mandatory elements:**
- Article title in capitals
- Author name(s), status, and institutional affiliation
- "Chapeau" (3-7 lines): a short pre-introduction in engaging terms that introduces the topic and encourages reader engagement
- Main text organized into max 3 heading levels, numbered: I., A., 1.

**Required sections:** Introduction (setting out historical puzzle and positioning against historiography), substantive sections (chronological or thematic), conclusion.

No separate "Methods," "Results," or "Literature Review" headings required (consistent with FIELD_SKILL for narrative business history).

### Length
30,000–45,000 characters (approximately 7,000–11,000 words). This is slightly longer than typical field norms and reflects Entreprises et Histoire's substantial monographic article length.

### Abstract
- **Bilingual:** French abstract (200–500 words) and English abstract (200–500 words) both required, with English title provided separately.
- **Format:** Non-structured, narrative summary of puzzle, argument, and contribution (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).

### Citations
**Format:** Footnotes (not endnotes), numbered throughout the text. Superscript numbers follow referenced text immediately, before punctuation (French convention).

**Placement:** Page bottom, Times New Roman 10pt, single spacing.

**Bibliography entry format:**
- **Books:** Initial(s). Surname, *Title*, Place, Publisher, Year
- **Articles:** Initial(s). Surname, "Article Title," *Journal Name*, vol., no., month, year, pages

Bibliography must provide complete publication details including editor, place, and pages. Present homogeneously.

### Headings
Maximum three levels (I., A., 1.). Use numbered hierarchy; "the editor handles title formatting" but hierarchy must be clear and numbered as specified. Avoid automatic title numbering in document; use manual numbering.

### Tables and Figures
- Embed all illustrations within text where referenced.
- Number and label everything clearly.
- Send images separately in image format for print quality.
- Accompany with prose interpretation; do not leave tables/figures to stand alone (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).

### Formatting
- **Font:** Times New Roman, 12pt
- **Line spacing:** 1.5
- **Margins:** 2.5 cm on all sides
- **Alignment:** Text justified, not centered or left-aligned
- **No automatic formatting:** Do not use predefined styles or automatic paragraph spacing/withdrawal lines
- **Italics:** Foreign expressions and bibliographic references (journal names, book titles, theses)
- **Bold:** Titles only; do not bold section headings or emphasis within text

### Additional Required Submissions
- Bio (50–150 words)
- Contact information
- Both French and English abstracts with translated title

### Explicit Prohibitions / Expectations
- Do not use automatic paragraph formatting, styles, or pre-spacing
- Articles must be understandable to interdisciplinary readers (teachers, researchers, students, practitioners) who may not be experts in the subject
- Minimize unexplained jargon; clarify theoretical debates
- Prioritize clarity for non-specialist readers

### File Format
Submit in .doc or .rtf format (NOT PDF). Include author name(s) in filename.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### The "Chapeau" (Pre-Introduction)
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines AND observed in issues):** Every article must open with a short "chapeau" of 3–7 lines after title, author, and affiliation block. This element is not mentioned in FIELD_SKILL and is a journal-specific requirement. The chapeau serves to introduce the topic in engaging, accessible terms to encourage readers before the substantive argument begins. This is a formal, mandatory structural element.

### Article Length
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines):** 30,000–45,000 characters is significantly more specific and longer than typical field practice. FIELD_SKILL does not specify length; Entreprises et Histoire mandates this range, reflecting a preference for substantial monographic treatments. This is a hard constraint not typical of all BUS HIST journals.

### Formatting Strictness
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines):** The journal emphasizes no automatic formatting, no predefined styles, and specific font/spacing requirements. This is more prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL, which does not address formatting. The strict formatting rules (Times New Roman 12pt, 1.5 spacing, justified text, no withdrawal lines) are journal-specific and non-negotiable.

### Bilingual Abstracts
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines):** Both French and English abstracts (200–500 words each) are mandatory. FIELD_SKILL does not specify abstract language; this journal requires both, reflecting its French-based publishing and international scope. Translated title must accompany the English abstract.

### Emphasis on Accessibility and Clarity
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines):** Articles must be "understandable by readers who are teachers, researchers, or students who may not be experts in the subject." Minimize jargon; explain theoretical debates. This is more explicit in Entreprises et Histoire guidelines than in FIELD_SKILL and reflects the journal's interdisciplinary, accessible editorial philosophy. Authors should simplify dense theoretical material more than might be expected in higher-tier history journals.

### Footnote Format Convention
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines):** Superscript numbers must follow referenced text immediately, before punctuation (French typographic convention). This is a technical requirement specific to Entreprises et Histoire and French academic publishing norms, not explicitly stated in FIELD_SKILL.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and register:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (narrative-analytical, formal but accessible, balanced between impersonal and first-person constructions; measured, interpretive claims).
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium to long sentences accommodating qualification; substantial paragraphs organized around episodes/mechanisms; narrative transitions).
- **Argumentation and rhetorical moves:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (CARS-like pattern; historiographical framing of gaps; recovery/reassessment/complication of narratives valued).
- **Methods and sources discussion:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (transparent procedural detail; past tense; reflexivity about archival limits valued).
- **Results and reporting norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (evidence and interpretation integrated throughout; direct quotation from primary sources valued; tables/figures accompanied by prose).
- **Discussion and conclusion norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (restate contribution, connect to broader debates, address limits of evidence base).
- **Citation and literature engagement:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (high citation density throughout; paraphrase for secondary sources, quotation for primary sources; courteous disagreement; international and interdisciplinary engagement).
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (moderate hedging; confident direct statements of central argument; stronger hedging for suggestive findings, less for strong primary-source evidence).

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Omitting or shortening the "chapeau":** Prospective authors familiar with English-language history journals may overlook the mandatory 3–7 line introductory chapeau. This is a formal requirement and must appear after the author/affiliation block and before the main text or introduction section.

2. **Exceeding 45,000 characters or falling below 30,000:** Entreprises et Histoire has a strict length envelope (approximately 7,000–11,000 words). Articles outside this range risk desk rejection. Unlike some field outlets, this journal does not accommodate substantially longer or shorter pieces.

3. **Using automatic paragraph formatting or predefined styles:** The explicit instruction to avoid automatic formatting, spacing, and styles is unusual and must be followed carefully. Submitting a .doc file with Word formatting enabled and styles applied will require revision before evaluation.

4. **Neglecting bilingual abstracts:** Both French and English abstracts (200–500 words each) are non-negotiable. A single abstract or an abridged English summary is insufficient. Both must be submitted separately from the main text.

5. **Mixing citation styles or incomplete bibliography entries:** The journal expects homogeneous, complete bibliography entries with place, publisher, and all page ranges. Incomplete citations, mixed formats (some with place, some without), or missing publication details will delay acceptance.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
