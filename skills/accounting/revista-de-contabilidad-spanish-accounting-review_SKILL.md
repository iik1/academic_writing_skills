# Journal Writing Skill: Revista de Contabilidad-Spanish Accounting Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing (transition from Elsevier/University of Murcia platform)
- **Scope:** International peer-reviewed journal for theoretical and empirical accounting research, including auditing, financial reporting, management accounting, public sector accounting, and emerging interdisciplinary topics at the intersection of accounting with finance, entrepreneurship, and sustainability.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emerald.com/sar/pages/author-guidelines
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (diverse types: bibliometric review, experimental study, earnings management analysis, cost hysteresis, systematic literature review, and impression management)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Standard sections required: Introduction, Methods/Research Design, Results/Findings, Discussion/Conclusion. The journal also accepts specialized article types: Research Articles, Technical Papers, Case Studies, Book Reviews, and Editorials, each with distinct scope and length parameters.

### Length
**Research Articles:** 6,000–9,000 words total (including abstract, tables, figures, references, and footnotes). **Technical Papers and Case Studies:** 4,000–7,000 words. **Editorials:** 1,000–2,000 words. Allow approximately 250 words per table or figure in word count calculations.

### Abstract
**Structured format mandatory.** The abstract must include four required sections:
- **Purpose:** Research objective and context
- **Design/methodology/approach:** Research design and analytical methods
- **Findings:** Key empirical or analytical results
- **Originality/value:** Contribution to the field

Optional sections: research limitations/implications, practical implications, social implications.

**Maximum 250 words** including all sections and keywords. Abstracts are presented as a single comprehensive paragraph (not separated by section headings in the final manuscript). **No references in abstract** except author/year citations.

### Keywords
Include **up to 12 keywords** capturing principal topics and research themes. Editorial team may modify keywords for consistency.

### Citations
**APA style required.** Author-date in-text citations with names inverted (Author Year). For multiple authors: list first 6 authors, then ellipsis (…), then last author if 8 or more. **Mandatory inclusion of DOI when available**, preferably in URL format (https://doi.org/...). All references in alphabetical order by author surname.

### Headings
**First-level headings:** Bold. **Subsequent subheadings (second level and below):** Medium italics. Headings should be concise and precede the corresponding text on separate lines.

### Tables and Figures
- **Tables:** Submit as editable text (not images). Number using **Roman numerals** (Table I, II, III, etc.). Include complete captions defining all symbols and abbreviations.
- **Figures:** Number using **Arabic numerals** (Figure 1, 2, 3). Submit in original source format (.ai, .eps, .jpeg, .bmp, .tif). Provide at highest resolution possible (minimum 300–1000 dpi depending on content type).
- Both require placement near relevant text or grouped at manuscript end with clear references in prose.

### Language
No explicit language restriction stated. Journal publishes articles in **Spanish and English**. Bilingual titles (Spanish/English) are standard practice in published articles.

### File Format and Submission
Submissions must be in **Microsoft Word (.docx/.doc)** format. PDFs alone unacceptable. LaTeX acceptable only with accompanying PDF.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Generative AI may be used only for copy-editing existing work, not for drafting new content.
- All work must be original and submitted through Crossref Similarity Check.
- All funding sources and conflicts of interest must be disclosed.
- Human participant research requires informed consent documentation.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates.

### Word Limits (ENFORCED CONSTRAINT)
**FIELD_SKILL specifies no word limits; this journal enforces strict limits.** ABS 1 journals may relax the implicit 12,000–15,000-word norm of ABS 4*/4 journals: RC-SAR caps research articles at 6,000–9,000 words. This is a substantial constraint requiring authors to condense methods and results sections relative to elite-tier standards. The brevity requirement may necessitate reduced robustness check reporting or abbreviated hypothesis development compared to ABS 4*/4 norms.

### Structured Abstract Format (ENFORCED CONSTRAINT)
**FIELD_SKILL describes standard accounting abstracts without prescribing internal structure.** RC-SAR mandates a four-part structured abstract (Purpose, Design/Methodology/Approach, Findings, Originality/Value), with optional additional sections. This is more prescriptive than the field norm and must be followed exactly in submission. The 250-word maximum (vs. field unspecified norm) compounds the brevity pressure.

### Abstract Paragraph Style (ENFORCED PRACTICE)
**FIELD_SKILL does not prescribe paragraph format for abstracts.** RC-SAR published abstracts present structured sections as a single comprehensive paragraph (not separated by section headings in the final article). During drafting, the structured sections should be written as distinct paragraphs for clarity, but on submission, they should be merged into one paragraph maintaining the logical flow between Purpose → Design → Findings → Originality.

### Bilingual Titles (ENFORCED PRACTICE)
**FIELD_SKILL does not address title language.** All sampled RC-SAR articles present **bilingual titles: Spanish followed by English translation in parentheses or as a subtitle.** This is a consistent journal practice and should be replicated by authors regardless of article submission language.

### Heading Hierarchy Formatting (ENFORCED GUIDELINE)
**FIELD_SKILL notes that accounting journals vary in heading style (Roman numerals vs. Arabic numerals, ALL CAPS vs. title case).** RC-SAR specifies: first-level headings bold (no number prefix observed in samples); subsequent subheadings medium italics. This is stricter than the field baseline and must be followed exactly.

### Table and Figure Numbering (ENFORCED GUIDELINE)
**FIELD_SKILL does not prescribe table/figure numbering style.** RC-SAR mandates: **Tables use Roman numerals** (Table I, II, III), **Figures use Arabic numerals** (Figure 1, 2, 3). This differs from some accounting journals and must be observed strictly.

### Keywords Field (ENFORCED GUIDELINE)
**FIELD_SKILL does not mandate keywords.** RC-SAR requires 3–12 keywords (editorial team may edit for consistency). Keywords are mandatory and should be curated carefully.

### Generative AI Prohibition (ENFORCED GUIDELINE)
**FIELD_SKILL does not address AI usage.** RC-SAR explicitly prohibits using generative AI for drafting; it may only be used for copy-editing. This is an explicit journal policy and must be observed.

- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium overall; calibrated to identification strength; limitations acknowledged directly; formulaic hedging not valued).

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding the word limit:** Authors accustomed to ABS 4*/4 journals (which accommodate 12,000–15,000 words) frequently exceed RC-SAR's strict 6,000–9,000-word cap. This necessitates ruthless condensation of hypothesis development and robustness sections. Reviewers will penalize submissions that breach this limit.

2. **Incomplete or improperly structured abstracts:** The four-part structured abstract (Purpose, Design/Methodology/Approach, Findings, Originality) must be present and complete. Abstracting the abstract into a single paragraph without all four sections is a frequent error. The 250-word limit is also strict; verbose abstracts will be rejected.

3. **Mixing table and figure numbering styles:** Authors must use Roman numerals exclusively for tables (Table I, Table II) and Arabic numerals exclusively for figures (Figure 1, Figure 2). Mixing formats or using inconsistent styles is a formatting error that reviewers notice.

4. **Omitting bilingual titles:** While not explicitly stated in English-language guidelines, all published articles in the journal use bilingual titles (Spanish + English). Authors should provide both versions at submission; failure to do so may delay acceptance or require revision before publication.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
