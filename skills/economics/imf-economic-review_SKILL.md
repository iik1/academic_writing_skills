# Journal Writing Skill: IMF Economic Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature (Palgrave Macmillan imprint), on behalf of the International Monetary Fund
- **Scope:** Open-economy macroeconomics, international finance, sovereign debt, exchange rates, monetary and fiscal policy, and related fields — often addressing questions with direct policy relevance to the IMF's mandate.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/41308/submission-guidelines (Springer paywall-gated for full detail; core content indexed via search cache and mirrored at palgrave.com/authors/submission)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 17 (titles/abstracts across Vol. 72 Issue 2 (2024) through Vol. 74 Issue 1 (2026)): "Fiscal Multipliers in Small Open Economies with Heterogeneous Households" (Druedahl et al.), "Fiscal Stimulus with Supply Constraints" (Fornaro), "International Investment Income" (Donato & Tille), "Natural and Neutral Real Interest Rates" (Obstfeld), "What Explains Global Inflation" (Ha, Kose, Ohnsorge, Yilmazkuday), "Do Temporary Cash Transfers Stimulate the Macroeconomy?" (Ramey), "Sovereign Haircuts: 200 Years of Creditor Losses" (Graf von Luckner, Meyer, Reinhart, Trebesch), "Trade Partners' Responses to US Tariffs", "Turning Points in Inflation" plus ~8 further titles/abstracts drawn from Vol. 72 Issues 2–4 and Vol. 73 Issues 1–4 and Vol. 74 Issue 1 tables of contents.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed mandatory section list beyond field norms; guidelines specify formatting/metadata requirements rather than a required section order. Opening (unnumbered) footnote must give each author's title and institutional affiliation and may carry acknowledgements — this is an explicit requirement, not merely customary.

### Length
Not specified in accessible guidelines (no explicit word/page cap found).

### Abstract
Abstract must be *italicized* and precede the paper's JEL classification codes. This italicization requirement is an explicit house-style rule, not just formatting preference.

### Citations
Style is *The Chicago Manual of Style* (author-date variant, consistent with FIELD_SKILL's author-year norm — no delta in citation format itself, but the explicit style-guide anchor is journal-specific).

### Headings
No journal-specific heading convention beyond Chicago style; follows FIELD_SKILL default (numbered or unnumbered sections both acceptable in practice — observed articles use numbered sections).

### Tables and Figures
Figures must be submitted as separate files (TIFF, GIF, JPG, PDF, PostScript, or EPS); tables and graphs as separate Word or Excel files — a submission-mechanics requirement, not a style rule.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscript must be unpublished and not under review elsewhere (standard). Author biography required per author, capped at 80 words — an explicit, journal-specific numeric limit.

### Other Hard Constraints
- **Replication mandate (ENFORCED, journal-specific):** the journal states it publishes empirical/simulation/experimental papers only where a replication package (data + code) is clearly documented and made available prior to publication. This is stricter and more explicit than a generic "data availability" norm.
- **JEL codes are mandatory** and placed immediately after the abstract (not merely encouraged, as in many outlets).

### Structural Deviations
**PRACTICE ONLY / journal-specific feature:** several issues are organized around thematic "Forum" or symposium sections with a short (1–3 page) unsigned or guest-edited introduction article framing a cluster of papers (e.g., "Global Challenges and Channels for Fiscal and Monetary Policy," pp. 651–653, prefacing a five-paper cluster on fiscal/monetary policy; "The Future of Macroeconomic Policy," pp. 941–943). Authors invited into such a symposium should expect their paper to be read alongside a short thematic framing piece rather than in isolation. This is a recurring editorial format at this journal, not a general field norm.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Many published articles are near-identical versions of NBER, CEPR, or central-bank working papers (Obstfeld/NBER w31949, Ramey/NBER w33503, Graf von Luckner et al./NBER w32599, Druedahl et al., etc.), reflecting the journal's role as a venue for policy-facing macro/international-finance research first circulated as working papers. Authors targeting this journal can reasonably draft in working-paper form (title-page footnote with affiliations, JEL codes, abstract) and expect minimal structural conversion. This is PRACTICE ONLY (not stated in guidelines) but consistently observed.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting the abstract in roman (non-italic) type or placing JEL codes before rather than after the abstract — both explicit house-style requirements.
- Omitting per-author biographies (80-word cap) or the opening footnote with author titles/institutions — both are mandatory metadata elements distinct from the paper text itself.
- Treating the replication package as an optional appendix: for empirical/simulation/experimental papers, the journal will not publish without a documented data-and-code package.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
