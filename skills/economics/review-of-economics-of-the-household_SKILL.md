# Journal Writing Skill: Review of Economics of the Household

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature
- **Scope:** Empirical and theoretical research on economic behavior and decision-making processes of single and multi-person households, with emphasis on policy-relevant analysis and household-level applications.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/11150/submission-guidelines (authentication required)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 articles from 2024-2025 (via RePEc, arXiv, and Springer databases)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Use decimal system of headings with no more than three levels. Example: 1, 1.1, 1.1.1. No standalone section names required; structure is flexible to accommodate paper type (empirical or theory).

### Length
Maximum 25 pages (including all text, tables, figures, and references). Appendices typically count toward this limit unless explicitly noted as online-only supplements.

### Abstract
- **Format:** Unstructured abstract (no Purpose/Methods/Results subsections required)
- **Word limit:** 150–250 words
- **Content:** No undefined abbreviations or unspecified references. No citations.

### File Format
Manuscripts must be submitted in one of the following:
- Microsoft Word (.docx)
- LaTeX (Springer template recommended)
All editable source files must be provided at every submission and revision; incomplete file submissions result in desk rejection.

### Fonts and Spacing
Word submissions: 10-point Times Roman (or equivalent plain font) for body text. Standard margins. No special formatting instructions beyond those in FIELD_SKILL.

### Author Declarations
At submission, provide via the submission interface:
- Author Contribution information (specify each author's role)
- Competing Interest declarations
- Only information submitted via the interface is published; free-text declarations are not used.

### Data Availability
Upon request, authors must be prepared to send relevant documentation, raw data, samples, or records to verify results. Sensitive, confidential, or proprietary data may be withheld.

### LLM Usage Policy
Large Language Models (LLMs) do not satisfy authorship criteria. If an LLM was used in manuscript preparation, this must be documented in the Methods section or a separate disclosure. Use should be clearly described and transparent.

### Tables and Figures
Springer standard conventions apply (referenced from FIELD_SKILL). No journal-specific deviations observed.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None identified beyond field norms. Single-blind peer review is standard.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we find," "we develop") is the standard voice; active voice preferred. Formality and precision match ABS 4*/4 conventions even though this is an ABS 2 journal. No relaxation of tone or register observed.

### Structural Deviations
RELAXATION: Unlike top-tier ECON journals, the mandatory structure is less prescriptive. While empirical papers typically follow Introduction → Data → Methods → Results → Conclusion, theory papers and mixed methods work are accommodated with flexible section ordering. "Institutional Setting" or "Background" sections are optional rather than quasi-required, and are included only when necessary for context. No roadmap paragraph is enforced in the introduction, though many articles include one.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Coefficient magnitudes, signs, economic significance, and statistical significance are all expected. Standard error reporting and sample restrictions are stated precisely. Null results are published when the question is important.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High citation density in Introduction, medium in Methods/Results, low in Conclusion. Author-year (parenthetical) format is standard. Footnotes for supplementary material, not primary citations.

### Abstraction and Accessibility
PRACTICE ONLY: Articles in household economics sometimes include non-technical framing or policy-relevance language in introductions, reflecting the journal's emphasis on policy applications. This does not deviate from FIELD_SKILL norms; it is an emphasis rather than a structural requirement. Examples observed: "This reform affected household labor supply in the following way, with implications for retirement policy" rather than pure theoretical motivation.

### Other Dimensions
Hedging, methodology transparency, results interpretation, and robustness checks all follow FIELD_SKILL norms with no observed deviations.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Incomplete file submission.** Do not submit only a PDF or Word document with equations rendered as images. Provide all editable source files (.docx or LaTeX). Desk rejection will follow.
2. **Abstract over word limit or with citations.** Keep abstracts to 150–250 words, unstructured, with no abbreviations or citations. Indexing services rely on this constraint.
3. **Forgetting author contribution and competing interest declarations.** These are mandatory via the submission interface and will delay review if omitted.
4. **LLM use undisclosed.** If you used an LLM (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) for any part of manuscript preparation, document it in the Methods section. Lack of disclosure can trigger desk rejection or retraction.
5. **Over-reliance on institutional setting without justification.** While Background/Institutional Setting sections are optional and flexible, ensure that if included, they directly motivate the research question and identification strategy. Springer editors report desk-rejecting papers with lengthy, ornamental background sections disconnected from the empirical question.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Notes on Access and Limitations
The Springer submission guidelines page (https://link.springer.com/journal/11150/submission-guidelines) and full-text articles require institutional authentication and were not directly accessible. Findings are based on:
- Published article abstracts and metadata from RePEc/IDEAS and Springer abstracts pages
- Springer Nature general author guidelines (which apply broadly across their journals)
- Spot-check of 6 recent 2024–2025 articles via abstract review and metadata
- Working paper versions of articles (arXiv, IZA, NBER) to assess structural patterns
- Journal homepage and scope statement from Wikipedia and publisher landing pages

The journal appears to follow Springer's default conventions closely with minimal journal-specific deviations. The main delta is structural flexibility (relaxation of the prescriptive introduction/methods/results roadmap required at ABS 4* level) and a flexible heading system (decimal with max 3 levels) rather than rigid section naming. This is consistent with its ABS 2 rating and broader scope (theory, empirical, policy) compared to top-tier journals.
