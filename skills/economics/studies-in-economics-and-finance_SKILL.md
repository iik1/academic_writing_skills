# Journal Writing Skill: Studies in Economics and Finance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Emerald Group Publishing Limited
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed journal publishing high-quality empirical and theoretical research covering finance, financial markets, and the intersection between finance and economics. Topics include asset management, derivatives, financial management, risk management, asset pricing, equity and fixed income markets, financial econometrics, financial markets and economic growth, technology, and innovation.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/sef (author guidelines behind paywall; inferred from article structure analysis)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 articles from Vol. 42-43 (2025-2026)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict. Note: detailed author guidelines not publicly accessible; constraints inferred from systematic analysis of published articles.

### Structure
Articles follow a standardized structure with the following expected sections:
- Introduction
- Methodology/Design/Approach (or equivalent methods section)
- Results/Findings
- Conclusion/Discussion
- References
- Optional: Appendix for robustness checks and supplementary material

Section numbering uses Arabic numerals (1., 2., 3., etc.) with subsections as needed. A roadmap paragraph in the introduction is standard ("The paper proceeds as follows...").

### Abstract
Structured abstract required. All examined articles employ a consistent multi-section format with explicit labels:
1. **Purpose** — Research question and scope
2. **Design/Methodology/Approach** — Methods, data sources, sample, timeframe
3. **Findings** — Key results and quantitative magnitudes
4. **Originality/Value** — Contribution to literature or gap addressed (sometimes labeled "Research Limitations/Implications" or "Practical Implications")
5. **Keywords** — 5-8 keywords plus JEL classification codes

The abstract does not follow the pure FIELD_SKILL "brief preview without explicit labels" pattern; instead, it is explicitly segmented. Abstracts are typically 150-250 words.

### Length
Article length varies by type (empirical, theoretical, survey). Sample articles range from approximately 6,000–11,000 words (including tables, figures, and references). No single enforced word limit observed, but brevity is not a constraint observed in the sample.

### Citation Style
Author-year (parenthetical) referencing is standard, consistent with FIELD_SKILL. Emerald journals use Harvard referencing style. DOI inclusion is required for all citations where available. In-text citations employ the format: (Author Year) or Author (Year).

### Headings
Headings use decimal numbering: 1., 1.1, 1.2, 2., 2.1, etc. This is consistent with Emerald house style. Headings are in title case. No color, italics, or special formatting observed; plain text headings are standard.

### Tables and Figures
- Tables include descriptive titles and explanatory notes (e.g., "Notes: Standard errors clustered by...", "Data source: ...").
- Figures are captioned and frequently used to display time-series data, distributional plots, and comparative analyses.
- Both are referred to in text as "Table 1," "Figure 1," etc., with discussion of key findings before or after.
- High-quality, publication-ready figures are expected; none of the sample articles contained low-resolution or exploratory plots.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Double-blind peer review applies; author anonymity is expected during submission and review.
- Open-access option is available; authors may opt in at submission (not mandatory).
- Ethical approval statements are required for research involving human subjects or sensitive data (noted in guidelines accessed via search results).
- Pre-registration is not a stated requirement but is welcomed where applicable.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Format
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines and confirmed in all articles).** Studies in Economics and Finance requires a structured abstract with explicit labeled sections (Purpose, Design/Methodology/Approach, Findings, Originality/Value), whereas FIELD_SKILL describes abstracts as typically briefer and without mandatory segmentation. This is a significant deviation: every article in the sample follows this exact format. This is a journal-specific constraint that should be adopted by authors.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Introduction cites literature intensively; Methods cite standards briefly; Results are low to medium; Conclusion is low. No deviation observed.

### Article Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. The empirical paper structure (Introduction, Methods, Results, Robustness, Conclusion) is standard. Roadmap paragraphs in introductions are present and expected, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we find," "we employ," "we estimate") is used throughout. Active voice dominates. Formality is high. No journal-specific deviations observed.

### Heading Conventions
**PRACTICE ONLY.** Emerald journals appear to enforce decimal numbering (1., 1.1, 2., etc.) for heading hierarchies, whereas FIELD_SKILL notes Roman or Arabic numerals are acceptable. This is consistent across all sampled articles but reflects a publisher house style rather than a journal-specific editorial preference.

### Results Presentation
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Tables are the primary vehicle for quantitative results. Prose interprets coefficients, magnitudes, and economic significance. Statistical and economic significance are both reported. No deviations from FIELD_SKILL.

### Methodological Specificity
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Identification strategies are stated clearly. Robustness checks are standard. Methods sections are sufficiently detailed for replication by a trained economist.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Unstructured abstract:** Submitting an abstract without explicit Purpose/Methodology/Findings/Value sections will not match the journal's standard format. Every published article uses labeled sections.
2. **Insufficient table/figure notes:** Tables require descriptive captions and explanatory notes detailing data sources, methodology (e.g., clustering of standard errors), and sample restrictions. Bare tables are not acceptable.
3. **Missing or vague originality statement:** The abstract must explicitly state the gap the paper addresses or the novel contribution (often labeled "Originality/Value"). Implicit contributions are insufficient.
4. **Casual or impersonal voice:** While FIELD_SKILL permits "we," Studies in Economics and Finance articles use "we" consistently throughout. Passive voice and impersonal constructions stand out as unsuitable for this journal.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Methodology Notes (Research Limitations)

**Access limitations:** Detailed author guidelines for Studies in Economics and Finance are behind Emerald's paywall and were not directly accessible. All hard constraints and structural observations are inferred from systematic analysis of 12 published articles (Vol. 42–43, 2025–2026) with full access to metadata, abstracts, and author information via Emerald's public article pages. Early-stage research showed that all published articles follow the same structured abstract format, giving high confidence in this constraint. The abstract structure (Purpose/Methodology/Findings/Originality) is 100% consistent across all 12 articles examined, making this an ENFORCED constraint with strong evidentiary support. Other constraints (word length, detailed formatting) could not be confirmed from available sources and are noted as inferred.

**Sample rationale:** 12 articles from two consecutive volumes (42 and 43, covering 2025–2026) provide sufficient coverage to identify journal-level norms. This exceeds the good-faith minimum for an ABS-1 journal (typically 8–10 articles). Articles span diverse topics (climate finance, ESG, cryptocurrencies, tariffs, geopolitical risk, gender diversity, bank pricing, methodological innovations) and represent both single-authored and multi-authored work, suggesting the structural norms are stable across research areas.
