# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Enterprising Communities

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ENT-SBM (Entrepreneurship & Small Business Management)
- **Publisher:** Emerald Group Publishing Limited
- **Scope:** The journal publishes rigorously written empirical, conceptual, and review papers, as well as case studies that explore the interconnected and co-evolving systems of people, communities, and their enterprises globally.
- **Guidelines URL:** http://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/products/journals/author_guidelines.htm?id=jec (access restricted; public summary available at https://www.emerald.com/jec)
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 2 (one empirical research article using SMMR; one book review)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL macro structure. Empirical papers: Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, Theory/Literature Review, Methods, Results, Discussion (including Contributions and Practical/Managerial Implications), Limitations and Future Research, Conclusion. Book reviews are formatted with front matter (title, author, publication details) before review text.

### Length
Not publicly specified in accessible author guidelines. Empirical articles in recent issues range 40-55 pages (full PDF). Acceptance rate reported as 22.6% with 53.8 days to first decision.

### Abstract
Structured abstract format observed in empirical articles: Purpose, Design/Methodology, Findings, Research Limitations, Practical Implications, Social Implications, Originality/Value. Word limit not publicly stated.

### Citations
Author guidelines require disclosure of ethical approvals and institutional review board details where applicable. No explicit citation style stated in accessible guidelines; observed articles use APA-style inline citations with references section.

### Headings
Hierarchical numbered section headings (1. Introduction, 2. Theoretical Background, 2.1. Institutional environment and entrepreneurship, etc.) with descriptive titles.

### Tables and Figures
Data-heavy empirical research includes multiple tables (calibration results, truth tables, comparative analyses, typical case assessments). Figures include conceptual models and diagrams (e.g., multilevel perspective framework). All integrate seamlessly into narrative.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Must include ethical statement with institutional review board approval number and national/international guidelines followed (observed in empirical articles).
- Informed consent requirements stated explicitly.
- No mention in guidelines of explicit writing prohibitions beyond standard academic integrity requirements.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Methodological Sophistication
Journal shows pronounced emphasis on method rigor and novel approaches. Empirical articles employ advanced set-theoretic and process-tracing methodologies (SMMR) that go beyond the standard regression/SEM approaches mentioned in FIELD_SKILL. Process-tracing, QCA, and multimethod research designs appear more prevalent than in typical entrepreneurship journals at this tier.

### Causal Mechanism Focus
Strong emphasis on identifying and explaining causal mechanisms underlying entrepreneurial phenomena, rather than simply testing direct effects. This reflects a mechanistic orientation that is more prominent in JEC than in the general entrepreneurship field baseline.

### Structural Emphasis on Implications
Practically identical to FIELD_SKILL but consistently well-developed: Practical Implications and Social Implications are given substantial weight in abstracts and discussion sections, often with policy-oriented takeaways for policymakers and practitioners.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** First-person plural throughout; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Citation density:** High in theory sections, moderate in discussion; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** Explicit discussion of limitations and future research; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Citation style:** Paraphrase-dominant; direct quotation sparse and reserved for technical definitions; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Medium to long, compound sentences with embedded citations; dense multi-citation paragraphs in theory sections; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Argumentation (CARS pattern):** Establish phenomenon importance, narrow to literature gap, pose research questions; FIELD DEFAULT.

## Common Pitfalls
Based on limited sample and published guidelines overview:

1. **Incomplete ethical disclosure.** Author guidelines emphasize institutional review board approval number, date, and national guidelines followed. Omitting or vaguely stating ethical compliance will likely trigger desk rejection or revision requests.

2. **Methodological under-specification.** The journal values transparency in causal mechanism identification. Empirical papers should explicitly detail procedure for moving from QCA/process-tracing findings to mechanistic claims; hand-waving here invites reviewer critique.

3. **Underdeveloped practical implications.** Entrepreneurship journals expect applied relevance, and JEC is no exception. Generic or brief practical implications sections weaken otherwise strong papers.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Limitations

**Access constraints:** Author guidelines at Emerald Publishing and ScienceDirect platforms are paywalled. This skill is based on two articles and publicly visible journal metadata. A fuller picture of editorial preferences would benefit from direct guideline review (requires institutional access or author contact).

**Sample size:** Only 2 articles examined (1 empirical, 1 review). While both follow FIELD_SKILL norms closely, a larger sample (8-10 articles) would provide more confidence in identifying journal-specific deltas at this lower ABS tier.

**Conclusion:** Journal of Enterprising Communities closely adheres to field conventions as outlined in entrepreneurship-field-writing-style_SKILL. The primary delta is methodological emphasis (causal mechanisms via advanced set-theoretic approaches), which is less a writing-style issue and more a research-design choice. Writers should focus on ethical compliance, mechanistic clarity, and robust practical implications rather than expecting stylistic novelty.
