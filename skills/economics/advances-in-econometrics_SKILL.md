# Journal Writing Skill: Advances in Econometrics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Emerald Group Publishing Limited
- **Format:** Peer-reviewed book series (not a journal)
- **Scope:** Original scholarly econometrics papers on designated themes selected annually by series editors; covers theory, methods, and applications of econometric techniques across economics and related disciplines.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://advancesineconometrics.com/ (does not accept direct submissions; operates via themed call-for-papers)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 volume overviews; 9 chapter titles and abstracts from recent volumes (Vol. 46 Essays in Honor of Subal Kumbhakar, Vol. 45 Essays in Honor of Joon Y. Park, Vol. 44 Essays in Honor of Fabio Canova)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Submission Process
This is NOT a traditional journal. **Researchers do not submit papers directly.** Instead, the series operates through a Call for Papers system. Series editors announce themed volumes and invite submissions aligned with that theme. Papers are selected by volume editors, undergo peer review, and accepted manuscripts are published as chapters in the edited volume.

### Structure
**Chapter-based format.** Individual chapters follow this order:
1. Title and author(s) with affiliations
2. Abstract
3. Keywords (up to 6)
4. Main text
5. Acknowledgements (if applicable)
6. Appendices (if applicable)
7. References

Chapters are typically self-contained units within a themed edited volume. Near-universal structure follows FIELD_SKILL norms (Introduction, Methods, Results, Conclusion), but specific section names and ordering are not mandated by the series publisher.

### Length
No fixed word-count limit stated in public guidelines. Emerald's contract system specifies a maximum word count negotiated on a case-by-case basis. Public information suggests chapters typically range from approximately 8,000–20,000 words based on recent volume compositions, but this is NOT an explicit requirement.

### Abstract
**250 words maximum** per chapter. Abstract is mandatory for all chapters.

### Keywords
**Up to 6 keywords** required. Keywords encapsulate principal topics and aid discoverability online.

### Citations
**APA 7th Edition preferred** (though APA 6th is acceptable if subject conventions permit). Author-year format. Direct quotation is extremely rare (FIELD DEFAULT); paraphrase and cite is the norm.

### Headings
No explicit prohibition stated. Follows FIELD_SKILL norms: section headings use Roman or Arabic numerals, or are named descriptively. Running head limit: 65 characters.

### Tables and Figures
- Type figures and tables as part of the manuscript rather than as separate graphic elements.
- Indicate preferred position in text by typing "Take in figure (No.)" or "Take in table (No.)" on a separate line.
- Appendix figures and tables numbered: A1, A2, A3, etc. (even if only one appendix exists).

### Explicit Prohibitions
None stated explicitly in public guidelines. Standard academic integrity and peer-review norms apply (plagiarism, data fabrication, etc. are understood prohibitions in all Emerald publications).

### Submission Format
- Submit as **Word document** (.doc or .docx).
- Every contributor must complete a **Contributor/Chapter Transfer Agreement form** before publication (provided by the Book Project Editor).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this series deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Submission Mechanism
**ENFORCED DELTA**: This series does not accept direct submissions like a traditional journal. Instead, volume editors issue calls for papers on specific themes. Researchers interested in publishing must wait for a call aligned with their research, then submit to that call. This is a structural constraint fundamentally different from the journal-submission model assumed in FIELD_SKILL.

### Article Type Diversity
**PRACTICE ONLY**: The series frequently publishes **festschrift volumes** (essays in honor of prominent econometricians, e.g., Vol. 46 on Subal Kumbhakar, Vol. 45 on Joon Y. Park). These chapters are often shorter, more reflective, and more expository than the empirical papers FIELD_SKILL baseline emphasizes. Contributions may be survey chapters, methodological reviews, or applied case studies rather than original research with novel identification strategies.

### Hedging and Confidence
**FIELD DEFAULT**: Observed chapters follow FIELD_SKILL hedging norms (low overall hedging, clear claims defended by evidence). No journal-specific relaxation or intensification detected.

### Citation Density
**FIELD DEFAULT**: Observed abstract and chapter introductions follow FIELD_SKILL norms (high citation density in introduction, medium-low in methods and results).

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural "we" is standard; active voice preferred)
- Sentence and paragraph style: FIELD DEFAULT (medium-to-long sentences, high paragraph density)
- Argumentation and rhetorical moves: FIELD DEFAULT (introduction announces results early; position relative to prior work is required)
- Statistical reporting: FIELD DEFAULT (quantitative specificity expected; coefficient magnitudes and economic significance required)
- Methods conventions: FIELD DEFAULT (mixed-tense logic, identification strategy required for empirical papers, robustness checks expected)
- Results and reporting norms: FIELD DEFAULT (tables primary vehicle; interpretation integrated with reporting)
- Discussion and conclusion norms: FIELD DEFAULT (genuine synthesis expected; limitations acknowledged; future directions suggested)

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Submitting directly to the series**: This is the most common error. Advances in Econometrics only accepts papers via Call for Papers announcements. Check https://advancesineconometrics.com/ regularly for themed volume calls.
2. **Missing abstract or keywords**: Both are mandatory for all chapters in Emerald's editorial workflow. Omitting either will cause editorial rejection at the desk stage.
3. **Exceeding 250 words in abstract**: Emerald's typesetting system enforces this limit strictly; longer abstracts will be truncated during production.
4. **Incomplete Contributor Agreement**: Failure to submit the signed Contributor/Chapter Transfer Agreement form will delay or block publication.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
