# Journal Writing Skill: Global Journal of Emerging Market Economies

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publications (India), in association with the Emerging Markets Forum, Washington DC, and promoted by the Emerging Markets Institute, Beijing Normal University
- **Scope:** Publishes original empirical and theoretical research papers, policy papers, essays, and book reviews on emerging global economies; also publishes selected papers from the Emerging Markets Forum's annual global meetings.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/eme (SAGE standard author-instructions template; also a PDF at sagepub.com/docs/default-source/msg/submission-guidelines_-global-journal-of-emerging-market-economies)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 abstracts read in full + 35 titles/authors surveyed across 5 issues (Vol 17 Iss 1–3, Vol 18 Iss 1–2, 2025–2026) via RePEc/IDEAS (series sae/emeeco), plus 1 full-text article (Chattopadhyay et al. 2022, BRICS FDI, DOI 10.1177/09749101211067091). SAGE's own site is JavaScript-rendered and not accessible to automated fetch, so RePEc/IDEAS abstract mirrors were the primary source.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No journal-specific section structure is mandated beyond generic peer-review manuscript conventions (title page separated from anonymized main text for double-blind review). No distinct IMRaD template is specified.

### Length
No explicit word/page limit stated in accessible guidelines.

### Abstract
150–250 words, unstructured (single paragraph), with a minimum of 4–6 keywords following the abstract. This is narrower/more prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL, which does not specify abstract length.

### Citations
Not specified in accessible guidelines; observed articles use standard author-year economics citation format, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
Not specified.

### Tables and Figures
Guidelines require "high-quality" tables/figures and that authors hold/supply permissions for any reproduced copyrighted material (figures, tables, lengthy quotations).

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscript must be fully anonymized for double-blind review (all identifying information moved to a separate title page). No simultaneous submission to other journals. Standard COPE-based originality/misconduct prohibitions (plagiarism, undisclosed conflicts of interest).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Mixed: several abstracts use impersonal/third-person construction ("This study examines...", "The study employs...") rather than the FIELD_SKILL default of first-person "we." At least one recent article (decarbonization/ESG paper) does use "we developed and empirically tested." Both registers appear across the sample — RELAXATION of the FIELD_SKILL "we"-dominant norm, not a replacement convention. PRACTICE ONLY, not stated in guidelines.

### Statistical Reporting
Abstracts generally report methods and directional/qualitative findings (e.g., "harms economic growth," "significantly higher than") more often than the precise quantitative magnitudes FIELD_SKILL treats as near-universal at the ABS 4*/4 level. Specific point estimates in abstracts are less consistent — some report a headline statistic (e.g., "nearly 68% of India's OFDI flows... to offshore financial centers"), others stay qualitative. RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's quantitative-specificity norm. PRACTICE ONLY.

### Structural Deviations
Journal scope explicitly includes non-standard-research articles (essays, policy papers, book reviews, and forum-derived survey pieces) alongside conventional empirical papers — e.g., a descriptive/editorial-style piece surveying "30 Emerging Market Countries" development trends appeared as a lead article, structured as a broad narrative survey rather than the Introduction–Data–Strategy–Results–Conclusion template. This is a genuine journal-specific pattern tied to the Emerging Markets Forum affiliation, not present in FIELD_SKILL. ENFORCED in practice (confirmed by guidelines' explicit article-type list and by observed content).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Applied topic focus skews heavily toward policy-relevant panel econometrics on developing/emerging economies (FDI, financial inclusion, remittances, trade, commodity dependence, governance indices) rather than structural/theoretical economics. This is a scope characteristic rather than a prose-style delta, but authors should calibrate framing (policy implications foregrounded) accordingly — consistent across nearly all sampled titles. PRACTICE ONLY / scope-driven, not a stated stylistic rule.

## Common Pitfalls
- Do not assume a rigid empirical-paper template is required — the journal accepts essays and policy papers, so a purely descriptive/survey framing is acceptable if positioned as such.
- Do not omit a title-page/main-text split — anonymization for double-blind review is explicitly required.
- Given the journal's applied/policy orientation, do not over-invest in theoretical framing at the expense of clear policy implications, which recur across almost all sampled abstracts.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
