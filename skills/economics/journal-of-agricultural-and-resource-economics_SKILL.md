# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Agricultural and Resource Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Western Agricultural Economics Association (WAEA)
- **Scope:** Empirical and theoretical research in agricultural economics, resource economics, agricultural policy, commodity markets, and natural resource management.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://jareonline.org/author-guidelines/
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 articles from May 2026 and preprints (Adhikari et al., Perez-Quesada & Hrozencik, Fu et al., Zhang et al., Bakhtavoryan & Capps, Aguiar & Devadoss, Wang & Capps, Richards et al., Zia et al.); access limited to abstracts and metadata; structure inferred from author guidelines, citation analysis on Paperpile, and comparative agricultural economics literature.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Flexible: authors determine section headings and order (no prescribed structure like "Methods," "Results," etc., although Intro is expected).
- May follow IMRAD/IMRADC structure (Introduction, Materials/Methods, Results, Analysis, Discussion, Conclusion) but not mandatory.
- Appendices are standard for supplementary material, robustness checks, and additional data.

### Length
- **Maximum 35 pages total** (including reference list, tables, and figures).
- Introduction is longer than conclusion (field norm).
- Conclusion is 1–3 pages, typically.
- No page minimum stated.

### Abstract
- **Maximum 100 words** (strict, stated in guidelines).
- Unstructured (no mandated sections like "Background/Methods/Results/Conclusion").
- Final version requires up to 8 keywords or phrases.

### Citations
- **Format:** Author-year, parenthetical or in-text (e.g., "(Author, Year)" or "Author (Year) showed...").
- **Multiple authors:** Use "and" for two; "et al." for three or more.
- **Multiple sources:** Semicolon-separated; e.g., "(Smith, 2020; Jones and Brown, 2021)".
- **Reference list:** Do NOT abbreviate journal titles (write "Journal of Agricultural and Resource Economics," not "J. Agr. Res. Econ."); do NOT abbreviate agency names (write "US Department of Agriculture," not "USDA").
- **Issue numbers:** Required (include vol./issue/pages, e.g., "25(3): 102–115").
- **Page ranges:** Full form required (e.g., "102–115," not "102–15").
- **Publisher name:** Use shortest recognizable form (e.g., "Elsevier" not "Elsevier Scientific"), but use full name for university presses (e.g., "Cambridge University Press").
- **Citation management:** .csl files available for Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote; authors may download JARE-specific style files.

### Headings
- Section headings are **author-determined and flexible** (not prescribed by journal).
- Headings should be concise and descriptive.
- Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable (no journal-specified convention).
- No restriction on heading depth; use nested hierarchies as needed for clarity.

### Tables and Figures
- **Maximum 12 combined** in main text (hard limit).
- Additional tables/figures may appear in appendices.
- Submit as **separate Excel sheets** (one table/figure per sheet in Excel workbook, not embedded in manuscript).
- **Image format:** .png, .pdf, or .jpg (high-quality required).
- **Table notes:** Explain variable definitions, standard error clustering, and sample restrictions (follow FIELD_SKILL norm).
- **Figure captions:** Clear and self-contained.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **No author identification** in manuscript, headers, footnotes, or appendices (anonymity for double-blind review).
- **No acknowledgments in initial submission** (added only to final accepted version).
- **Case studies and survey-only papers:** JARE does not publish case studies or research reporting survey/field trial results unless essential for economic analysis (distinguishes from applied/extension journals).
- **Human subjects research:** Requires IRB approval documentation; no deception of human subjects permitted.

### Submission Process
- **Format:** PDF only (submitted via online portal; native Word or LaTeX files required upon acceptance).
- **Peer review:** Double-blind (author and reviewer identities masked).
- **Page charges:** $79/page for WAEA members; $89/page for non-members (applies after acceptance).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
**RELAXATION:** JARE guidance allows greater use of passive voice and weaker authorial voice than FIELD_SKILL norm. While "we" is not discouraged, JARE does not enforce the near-universal "we" construction of ABS 4* journals. Passive constructions such as "A discrete choice experiment survey showed..." (observed in article abstracts) are acceptable. This reflects lower-tier (ABS 2) practice: formality remains high, but singular vs. plural authorial assertion is less prescriptive. **Practice Only:** abstract analysis showed mixed voice; not explicitly stated in guidelines but evident in recent publications.

### Section Headings
**RELAXATION / FIELD DEFAULT:** FIELD_SKILL states no standard naming convention exists; JARE aligns with this flexibility but provides even lighter guidance—section headings are entirely author-determined with no prescribed order or format (beyond clarity). No Roman/Arabic numeral convention specified by JARE, unlike FIELD_SKILL. **Guideline Only:** stated in author guidelines; flexibility implies limited enforcement.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High in introduction, medium in methods, low to medium in results, low in conclusion.

### Structural Deviations
**None significant.** JARE empirical papers follow or approximate the IMRAD/IMRADC structure common to field (intro, methods/data, results, discussion, conclusion) but do not mandate it. Optional sections (Background/Institutional Setting, Literature Review, Robustness) are treated as in FIELD_SKILL. No standalone Related Literature section observed in recent articles; literature engagement is integrated into Introduction. **Field Default.**

### Citation Style
Follows FIELD_SKILL author-year norm precisely. No numbered footnote citations. See hard constraints above for specific formatting details (full journal titles, issue numbers, page ranges, publisher names).

### Hedging
Follows FIELD_SKILL: low overall hedging intensity; clear claims preferred. Confidence intervals and identification logic carry epistemic caution. No explicit journal prohibition or encouragement observed. **Field Default.**

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
**Length constraints:** The 35-page hard cap is notable for a lower-tier journal and represents a tightness not typical of ABS 4* journals. This will compress introduction, results, and discussion more aggressively. Authors should trim example narratives, limit literature engagement breadth, and prioritize quantitative results over lengthy interpretation. **Enforced (in guidelines).**

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Manuscript exceeds 35 pages:** Delete 2–4 pages of literature review or discussion narrative to fit hard cap. Compress literature engagement into one paragraph per major prior-work cluster rather than separate paragraphs per paper.
2. **Author identification in headers or footnotes:** Strip all running heads, author names, and institutional affiliations from initial submission; anonymity is strictly enforced.
3. **Abbreviating journal or agency names:** Write "Journal of Agricultural and Resource Economics" and "US Department of Agriculture" in full; do not use "J. Agr. Res. Econ." or "USDA" in reference list.
4. **Ambiguous or oversized table/figure count:** Exceeding 12 combined items (or submitting multi-panel figures as separate "figures") will trigger desk rejection. Plan graphics merging and supplementary appendix placement early.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
