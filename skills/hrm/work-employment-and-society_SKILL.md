# Journal Writing Skill: Work, Employment and Society

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** SAGE (for the British Sociological Association)
- **Scope:** International sociology-of-work journal; welcomes any theoretical,
  methodological or empirical approach to employment, unemployment, and paid/
  unpaid work, but requires an explicit and original contribution to
  *sociological* understanding rather than to management, psychology, or
  economics as such.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/wes ;
  full Notes for Contributors PDF: https://www.britsoc.co.uk/media/24980/wes_author_guidelines_2019.pdf
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (front-matter/abstract level; full text
  paywalled) — Umney et al. 2024 (platform labour unrest, DOI
  10.1177/09500170231209676); Farrugia 2025 (lay moralities of young
  workers, DOI 10.1177/09500170251380740); Otto & Abraham 2025
  (robotisation and workforce dynamics, DOI 10.1177/09500170251351260);
  Herman, Hebson & Rubery 2025 (precarious work to precarious lives, DOI
  10.1177/09500170251359125); Blanck, Brzinsky-Fay & Powell 2025 (Special
  NEETs, DOI 10.1177/09500170251361814); Ashley & Sommerlad 2025 (Diamonds
  in the Rough, DOI 10.1177/09500170251380025); Lo 2025 (Queer Bonds at
  Work, DOI 10.1177/09500170251343278); Giazitzoglu 2025 (Men at Work, DOI
  10.1177/09500170241294221); plus two PMC-hosted WES editorials used to
  confirm citation/heading conventions (PMC11239568, PMC10966127).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Four submission tracks with distinct requirements: **Main Research
Articles** (empirical, review, or conceptual — fully anonymised); **Debates
and Controversies (D&C)**; **Research Notes (RN)**; **On the Front Line
(OtFL)**. All are double-blind peer reviewed except OtFL is co-authored
with a worker participant and is explicitly exempted from standard
"academic excellence" criteria — it ends with the worker's first-person
narrative rather than an academic-style discussion/conclusion. D&C and RN
are not permitted as resubmissions of a rejected main article unless an
editor specifically requests it.

### Length
Main articles: **maximum 8000 words**, and this limit includes title,
abstract, keywords, text, references, notes, tables and figures (tables/
figures counted by literal word count) — a stricter and more inclusive cap
than typical HRM&EMP field practice. D&C and RN: maximum 5000 words
including tables, endnotes and references. OtFL: maximum 5000 words.
Quantitative papers may submit a supplementary appendix (descriptive
stats, correlation matrices, full regression tables, sensitivity analyses)
that is explicitly **excluded** from the 8000-word limit and is
peer-reviewed and published alongside the article; a quantitative article
submitted without such an appendix "may be returned to the author."

### Abstract
No structured/sectioned abstract format specified; no stated word limit
distinct from the overall 8000-word cap. Author guidelines give no
explicit abstract template — treat as unstructured, count toward the
overall limit.

### Citations
SAGE Harvard (author-date) style throughout text, tables, references, and
supplementary material — confirmed in spot-checked articles/editorials
(e.g., "Crenshaw, 1989"). No JEL classification codes are used (unlike
economics-adjacent journals) — keywords instead are free-text sociological
terms (e.g., "platform work," "labour unrest," "precarious work").

### Headings
No fixed heading list is mandated in the guidelines beyond the implicit
IMRaD-with-theory structure; anonymisation requirements affect headings
and citations (self-citations must appear as "Author, 2000" style with no
identifying reference-list entry).

### Tables and Figures
Word-counted literally toward the 8000-word cap for main articles (not
excluded, unlike some journals) — a stricter constraint than field default.
Quantitative appendix tables/figures are exempt from the count.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Papers over the word count or improperly anonymised are returned without review.
- Desk rejection is explicitly flagged as the outcome for submissions
  lacking a "sustained and original theoretical contribution to... the
  sociology of work and employment" — i.e., empirical rigor alone,
  without sociological theoretical framing, is insufficient.
- Resubmission of a previously WES-rejected manuscript is not permitted
  unless rewritten into "a completely new article," with the prior
  submission declared.
- D&C submissions must be "serious in intent and considered in tone – no
  diatribes."
- D&C/RN/OtFL: underdeveloped versions of a standard article, and (for
  OtFL specifically) recycled/reconstituted pre-existing interview data,
  are explicitly unacceptable.
- Poor standard of English is grounds for reduced likelihood of being sent to review.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Spot-checked abstracts skew toward sociological/critical-theoretical
vocabulary (e.g., "moral economy," "dialectical approach," "critical
discursive psychology," "value relations") more than the psychological/
managerial register FIELD_SKILL treats as one of two co-equal options.
This is consistent with the guidelines' explicit requirement that
submissions be framed around sociological contribution rather than
management or HR practice. PRACTICE ONLY / journal-lean, not a stated
rule, but strong and consistent across the sample — authors targeting WES
should lean sociological/institutional rather than managerial framing by
default.

### Statistical Reporting
For quantitative papers, WES enforces (ENFORCED) an unusual reporting
split not present in FIELD_SKILL: the main text should report only
results "most pertinent to the overall argument," with full model
results, robustness/sensitivity analyses, and descriptive/correlation
detail relegated to a separate peer-reviewed online appendix rather than
compressed into text or footnotes. This is a structural accommodation for
the tight 8000-word cap, not merely a stylistic preference.

### Citation Density
Field default citation style confirmed (paraphrase-preferred, dense in
intro/discussion); no journal-specific deviation observed beyond the
mandated SAGE Harvard format noted under Hard Constraints.

### Structural Deviations
WES's non-standard article types (D&C, RN, OtFL) have no equivalent
described in FIELD_SKILL, which assumes a single empirical/conceptual
article format. OtFL in particular inverts the field-default discussion/
conclusion norm: FIELD_SKILL expects discussion sections to close with
limitations and future research, but OtFL articles are required to end
with unmediated worker narrative and explicitly should NOT have a
reflective academic-style conclusion.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Word-limit inclusiveness (title/abstract/keywords/references/tables/
figures all counted) is stricter than typical field practice and should
be factored into drafting from the outset rather than trimmed at the end.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Overall IMRaD-with-theory macro-structure for main research articles: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Qualitative findings organised thematically with illustrative quotation: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Hedging intensity and cautious causal language: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Explicit statement-of-contribution convention at end of introduction: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Practical/policy implications expected in discussion: FIELD DEFAULT (though WES's "policy/practice" leans more toward worker/policy than managerial framing — see Voice and Register above).

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a methodologically sound empirical paper that reads as
  management/HR-practice-oriented rather than making an explicit,
  sustained sociological theoretical contribution — the guidelines
  identify this as a leading cause of desk rejection.
- Exceeding 8000 words by treating tables, figures, notes, or references
  as exempt from the count, when WES counts them literally.
- Submitting a quantitative article without a supplementary appendix,
  risking return for insufficient evaluative detail.
- Treating D&C as a shorter, easier main article rather than a distinct,
  debate-engaging, polemical-but-serious contribution — the guidelines
  explicitly reject "underdeveloped versions of a standard article" here.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
