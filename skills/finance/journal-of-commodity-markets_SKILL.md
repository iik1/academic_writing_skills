# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Commodity Markets

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** Publishes theoretical, empirical, and policy-related research across economics and finance of commodity markets — financialization, derivatives pricing/hedging/risk, real options, portfolio allocation, forecasting, corporate finance for commodity-exposed firms, econometric analysis, market organization/regulation, trading, and supply chains, spanning energy, metals, and agricultural markets.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-commodity-markets/publish/guide-for-authors
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 15 (titles/abstracts/keywords sampled via IDEAS/RePEc and search-engine snippets; full text paywalled on ScienceDirect). Titles included: "Financial investors and cross-commodity markets integration" (Isleimeyyeh, 2025); "Tail risk spillover effects in commodity markets: A comparative study of crisis periods" (Naeem, Hamouda & Karim, 2024); "The evolution of commodity market financialization: Implications for portfolio diversification" (2025); "Commodity currencies revisited: The role of global commodity price uncertainty" (2024); "Effects of global liquidity and commodity market shocks in a commodity-exporting developing economy" (2023); "The effect of oil prices on the US shipping stock prices" (2025); "Commodity market downturn: Systemic risk and spillovers during left tail events" (2024); "Examining perceived spillovers among climate risk, fossil fuel, renewable energy, and carbon markets" (2025); "The impact of financialization on the efficiency of commodity futures markets" (2023); "Econometric evidence from the grain markets, 1982-2024" (Steen, Westgaard & Gjolberg); "Supply and Demand shocks: the short-term and long-term drivers of oil price uncertainty" (Bermpei & Triantafyllou); "How good are weather shocks for identifying energy elasticities?" ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed mandatory section template stated beyond standard IMRaD-style expectations implicit in the scope description. No deviation from FIELD_SKILL structure identified in guidelines.

### Length
No explicit word/page limit stated in the guide for authors.

### Abstract
Unstructured, single-paragraph abstract (consistent with FIELD_SKILL). Up to 6 JEL classification codes required. Keywords required. Article Highlights (3-5 bullet points capturing novel results/methods) are encouraged but optional at submission, submitted as a separate file — this is an Elsevier-wide feature not covered by FIELD_SKILL.

### Citations
No fixed reference format required at submission — any consistent style is accepted, provided author names, title, year, volume, and pagination/article number are included (DOI use recommended); the house style is applied at proof stage. This is more permissive than a fixed-format requirement and is an Elsevier submission-system convention rather than a journal-specific voice choice.

### Headings
No explicit heading rules beyond standard numbered sections; follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Tables and Figures
Editable source files required for all figures/tables/text graphics (.doc/.docx or .tex); PDF not accepted as a source file. Single-column layout for Word submissions; double-column permitted only for LaTeX. This is a file-format/submission-mechanics requirement, not a style delta.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Strikethrough and underlined text must be removed unless scientifically meaningful. Institutional names must use full standard form or standard abbreviation (research-integrity verification). Standard Elsevier ethics/originality declarations apply (no dual submission, etc.) — routine and not journal-specific.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
RELAXATION (ABS 3, ties to guideline text): topic scope is broader and more methodologically eclectic than a typical ABS 4/4* finance journal — sampled titles show heavy use of connectedness/spillover/quantile-network methodologies (CAViaR, quantile connectedness, higher-moment spillovers) applied to narrow commodity-pair or commodity-sector settings, more so than the identification-driven causal-inference emphasis described in FIELD_SKILL. This is a PRACTICE ONLY pattern (observed across many sampled titles, not stated in guidelines) and reflects the journal's applied/empirical commodity-finance niche rather than a redefinition of the field baseline. Authors should still follow FIELD_SKILL's identification and robustness expectations; the topical concentration is a fit consideration, not a license to relax rigor.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a PDF as the only source file for figures/tables/text graphics — Elsevier requires editable .doc/.docx or .tex source files.
- Omitting JEL codes or keywords, both of which are required fields distinct from the abstract body.
- Framing a paper purely as a novel econometric technique applied to an arbitrary commodity pair without engaging the economic mechanism — reviewers in this niche journal still expect the FIELD_SKILL-level identification and economic-significance discussion, not just a new time-series method.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
