# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Income Distribution

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Ad Libros Publications Inc. (hosted by Glendon College, York University)
- **Scope:** Publishes empirical and theoretical work on income and wealth distribution, inequality, mobility, and opportunity, including taxation/redistribution, poverty, demographic and identity-based inequality, and distributional measurement methodology.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://jid.journals.yorku.ca/index.php/jid/about/submissions and https://jid.journals.yorku.ca/index.php/jid/information/authors (both are thin; see Access Limitations)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (full text or abstract-level): "Tackling Income Inequality: What Works and Why?" (Cuesta et al. 2018, DOI 40357); "Inequality and Conflict" (Esteban 2018, DOI 40379, lecture transcript); "Income redistribution through taxation – how deductions undermine the effect of taxes" (Hümbelin & Farys 2018, DOI 40330); "Redistributive Effect and the Progressivity of Taxes and Benefits: UK 1977-2018" (Hérault & Jenkins 2022, DOI 40542); "A Fitting Pareto Tails to Wealth Survey Data" (Wildauer & Kapeller 2019, DOI 40447); "Capital in the Twenty First Century after Capital in the Twenty First Century" (Kumar 2025, DOI 40635); plus abstract/title-level checks of Rising Income Inequality and Living Standards in OECD Countries (Thewissen et al. 2018), Does Clientelism Affect Income Inequality? (Rana & Kamal 2018), Entrepreneurship and Income Inequality: A Spatial Econometric Approach (Atems & Shand 2020), and Population, Education and Income Inequality (Parcero 2020).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Not specified in public guidelines. In practice, no single mandated structure: standard empirical papers follow the FIELD_SKILL empirical ordering (Introduction → Literature/Background → Data and Methods → Results → Conclusion), but the journal also regularly publishes invited/lecture-based theoretical pieces (e.g., Esteban 2018, explicitly labeled "Summary of the Lecture") and single-author review/commentary articles (e.g., Kumar 2025) that do not follow IMRAD at all.

### Length
No stated word or page limit found in public guidelines. Observed articles range widely: from short commentary pieces to a 48-page policy review (Cuesta et al. 2018). No enforced ceiling is evident.

### Abstract
Unstructured, single paragraph, positioned on the title page beneath author affiliations (not on a separate abstract page). Followed immediately by a "Keywords:" line and, in most but not all sampled articles, a "JEL Classifications:" line. JEL codes were present in the two 2018 papers sampled but absent from three later (2019–2025) article landing pages — inconsistently enforced.

### Citations
Author-year in-text citation, consistent with FIELD_SKILL. No deviation observed.

### Headings
No numbering convention mandated. Sampled articles use unnumbered descriptive headings (e.g., "Redistribution through the tax system," "The effect of deductions: the hidden welfare state") rather than "Section 2," "Section 3." This differs from FIELD_SKILL's neutral stance permitting either numbered or unnumbered headings — this journal's practice leans unnumbered.

### Tables and Figures
No stated rules. Figures observed use simple source-line captions beneath the graphic (e.g., "Source: Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP)..."). No deviation from FIELD_SKILL in table/figure norms otherwise.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Only the generic dual-submission prohibition and accepted file formats (OpenOffice, MS Word, RTF, PDF, or TEX) are stated. No prohibitions on content, style, or presentation were found.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in the empirical papers sampled (quantitative claims, e.g., "27 percent more likely," reported with specificity).

### Citation Density
The Cuesta et al. (2018) policy-review article shows markedly higher citation density than FIELD_SKILL's norm even for its Introduction — near-continuous string citations supporting a literature synthesis rather than a gap-and-contribution argument. PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence: this reflects the review-article subgenre this journal accepts alongside standard empirical papers, not a general journal norm. Standard empirical/theoretical articles in the sample follow FIELD_SKILL citation density.

### Structural Deviations
This journal accepts, alongside standard empirical/theoretical IMRAD-style papers, (a) invited-lecture transcripts retaining spoken-register framing ("I will start by giving a sense of...", "Summary of the Lecture" heading) and (b) single-author review/critique essays without a Data or Methods section. PRACTICE ONLY: at ABS 1, structural conformity to FIELD_SKILL's canonical empirical/theory templates is relaxed (RELAXATION) rather than strictly enforced — authors should default to FIELD_SKILL structure for a standard empirical submission, but should not be surprised to see non-conforming pieces in print.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
JEL classification codes appear inconsistently (present in some articles, absent from others) — GUIDELINE ONLY / low-prevalence, not enforced. Author affiliation and correspondence email are given as title-page footnotes (asterisk/dagger symbols) rather than a numbered author note — PRACTICE ONLY, a cosmetic house convention.

## Common Pitfalls
- Do not assume a single mandatory manuscript template — the journal publishes a mix of standard empirical papers, invited lectures, and review essays, so match structure to the paper type rather than forcing IMRAD onto a synthesis piece.
- Do not omit keywords; nearly all sampled articles include them even when JEL codes are dropped.
- Do not expect strict word/page limits; none are publicly stated and observed lengths vary widely.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
