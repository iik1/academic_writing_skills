# Journal Writing Skill: Acta Commercii

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN (Management)
- **Publisher:** AOSIS (pty) Ltd (South Africa)
- **Scope:** Promotes refreshingly new approaches to research in management and related disciplines, including disciplinary, multi-disciplinary, and problem-oriented work.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://actacommercii.co.za/index.php/acta/pages/view/submission-guidelines
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (2025–2026)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
For **Original Research articles** (the primary manuscript type):
- Mandatory sections in order: Title, Introduction, Research methods and design, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, Acknowledgements, Competing interests, Author contributions, Funding, Data availability, Disclaimer
- The structure aligns with FIELD_SKILL quantitative templates but with stricter section-naming requirements

**Introduction must include:** social value, scientific value, conceptual framework, aims/objectives

**Results and Discussion sections must be separate** (not combined as alternatives in FIELD_SKILL)

### Length
- **Original Research articles: 7,000 words maximum** (excluding abstract, tables, figures, references). This is a **hard constraint and significantly restrictive** compared to typical management journal norms (10,000–15,000 words in FIELD_SKILL).
- Project Papers: 2,000 words max
- Book Reviews: 2,000 words max

### Abstract
**ENFORCED and differs markedly from FIELD_SKILL:**

- Maximum 250 words
- **Mandatory seven structured sections** (must appear in this order):
  1. **Orientation** — contextual problem statement
  2. **Research purpose** — primary research aim
  3. **Motivation for study** — justification of the research gap
  4. **Research design, approach and method** — methodological description
  5. **Main findings** — key results or discoveries
  6. **Practical/managerial implications** — applied value
  7. **Contribution/value-add** — knowledge advancement claim
- Written in **past tense** (e.g., "The study found…")
- **No citations**
- **No excessive abbreviations**

This structured format is mandatory; unstructured abstracts will be rejected at desk review.

### Citations
- **Harvard referencing style required** (not APA as in FIELD_SKILL baseline)
- All DOIs must be verified via CrossRef Simple Text Query before submission
- **Maximum 60 references per article** (Book Reviews: 5 max). This is a **hard constraint and notably restrictive** compared to typical management research (40–80 in FIELD_SKILL).
- Limited self-referencing expected

### Headings
Follows standard academic hierarchy (implied in guidelines but not explicitly detailed). Spot-checked articles use numbered section headings in quantitative work.

### Tables and Figures/Graphs
- **Maximum 7 total per article** (a hard constraint). This is **significantly restrictive** compared to typical practice.
- Must be cited in three contexts: figure legend, body text, and reference list
- Adhere to AOSIS House Style Guide illustration requirements
- Must have descriptive captions and source attributions

### Language
- **British English, according to the Oxford English Dictionary** (ENFORCED; FIELD_SKILL does not specify regional variant)
- Standard font family and size
- 1.5 line spacing required
- Page and continuous line numbers required

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Research grounded in "Critical Theory perspectives will not be considered"** for publication. This is a unique and explicit prohibition not present in FIELD_SKILL.
- Locked or protected Microsoft Word documents cannot be submitted
- Methodology papers and opinion pieces are not accepted

### Submission Compulsories
- Blinded (anonymized) manuscript version required
- Full unblinded manuscript with author information
- Completed "Authorship, Disclosure Statements, Copyright, and License Agreement Form" (compulsory cover letter)
- Ethical clearance documentation (approval number, informed consent method, confidentiality measures, non-human research declaration)
- ORCID iD for all authors
- Disclosure of prior dissemination (thesis, preprint, conference presentations)
- **Explicit disclosure of AI-assisted technology use with specific tools named**
- All competing interests disclosed
- Data availability status declared
- Funding sources identified

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Structure
**ENFORCED DELTA:** Acta Commercii mandates a seven-section structured abstract with specific subsection labels (Orientation, Research purpose, Motivation, Research design, Main findings, Practical/managerial implications, Contribution/value-add). FIELD_SKILL permits either structured or unstructured abstracts. This journal's format is non-negotiable; abstracts deviating from the seven-section template will be desk-rejected.

Practical impact: Each abstract section must be clearly labeled with its subsection heading and conform to the specified semantic content of that section.

### Word Limit
**ENFORCED DELTA:** 7,000 words maximum for original research is substantially tighter than the 10,000–15,000 word range typical in FIELD_SKILL journals. This requires more aggressive synthesis and section compression. Methods sections are typically shorter; literature engagement is more condensed.

### Reference Ceiling
**ENFORCED DELTA:** 60 references maximum is notably restrictive compared to FIELD_SKILL expectation of 40–80 for empirical papers. Selective engagement with prior work is necessary; comprehensive literature reviews must be condensed or excluded in favor of focused citation of most-recent and most-central works.

### Language Variant
**ENFORCED DELTA:** British English per Oxford English Dictionary is explicitly required (vs. no regional specification in FIELD_SKILL). This affects spelling (colour, centre, organisation), vocabulary (car park vs. parking lot), and date formats (DD/MM/YYYY).

### Table/Figure Limit
**ENFORCED DELTA:** 7 or fewer total tables and figures is a restrictive constraint. Complex multi-panel displays, supplementary tables, and diagnostic figures must be consolidated or omitted. Models and hypotheses diagrams count toward the limit.

### Ethical Approval Documentation
**ENFORCED DELTA:** All research involving humans or animals requires explicit documentation of ethical approval (committee name, approval number), informed consent method (written or verbal), confidentiality measures, and non-human research declaration. FIELD_SKILL expects ethical statements in Discussion/Methods but Acta Commercii requires formal approval documents as compulsory submission materials, not optional.

### AI Technology Disclosure
**ENFORCED DELTA:** Authors must explicitly declare use of AI-assisted tools with specific tool names. This is not covered in FIELD_SKILL and reflects Acta Commercii's transparency requirements for emerging technologies.

### Critical Theory Prohibition
**ENFORCED DELTA:** Research grounded in Critical Theory perspectives is explicitly rejected. This is a unique and highly unusual prohibition not present in FIELD_SKILL or typical management journals. The journal accepts Positivist, Interpretive, and Phenomenological approaches only.

Practical impact: If your work employs Critical Theory as a foundational lens, this journal is not a viable outlet.

### Results/Discussion Separation
**ENFORCED DELTA:** Acta Commercii requires Results and Discussion as separate, clearly labeled sections (not optional combination as in FIELD_SKILL alternatives). Both quantitative and qualitative work follow this separation.

### Citation Style
**ENFORCED DELTA:** Harvard referencing style required (not APA). In-text citations follow (Author Year) format; all DOIs must be verified against CrossRef. Multiple citations are comma-separated within parentheses.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Unstructured abstract:** Submitting an abstract without the mandatory seven labeled sections will trigger desk rejection. Each section must be explicitly labeled and contain appropriate content (e.g., "Orientation:" not just the opening sentence).

2. **Exceeding word limit:** 7,000 words is a strict ceiling for original research. Papers exceeding this limit will be desk-rejected. Methods sections, literature reviews, and results discussions must be compressed compared to typical management journal norms.

3. **APA instead of Harvard referencing:** Using APA style instead of Harvard will require correction before publication. All in-text citations must be author-date format in parentheses (Jones, 2020); DOIs must be verified via CrossRef.

4. **Reference count over 60:** Exceeding 60 references will be flagged as non-compliant. Select the most critical works; comprehensive literature reviews must be distilled into key citations only.

5. **Unacknowledged AI use:** Failing to disclose AI-assisted technology (e.g., ChatGPT for drafting, generative models for analysis) will delay review and may trigger rejection. Name the specific tools and describe their role in the research process.

6. **Missing ethical approval documentation:** If human/animal subjects are involved, formal ethical clearance documentation (approval number, committee name, consent method) must be included as a compulsory submission element, not mentioned casually in the Methods section.

7. **Critical Theory framing:** Explicitly grounding the work in Critical Theory (e.g., critical discourse analysis, Marxist theory, postcolonial theory) will result in desk rejection. Reframe using Interpretive or Phenomenological perspectives if the research aligns with those paradigms.

8. **Combined Results and Discussion:** Submitting a single "Results and Discussion" section instead of two clearly separate sections will be flagged as non-compliant and require revision before review.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
