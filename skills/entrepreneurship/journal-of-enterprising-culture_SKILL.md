# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Enterprising Culture

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** World Scientific Publishing Co. Pte. Ltd.
- **Scope:** Business articles on entrepreneurship, enterprising culture, entrepreneurship development, and case studies. Publishes both quantitative and conceptual work.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.worldscientific.com/worldscinet/jec (access-restricted; public guidelines unavailable)
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (titles and metadata from IDEAS/RePEc, 2023-2026 volumes)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> World Scientific's public author guidelines pages are access-restricted (HTTP 403). The constraints below are inferred from journal metadata and World Scientific's general publishing practices.

### Structure
Follows standard academic empirical structure: Title, Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, Theory/Literature Review, Methods, Results, Discussion (with subsections for Contributions, Implications, Limitations), and Conclusion. Appendices for detailed procedures and robustness checks are acceptable and common.

### Length
Empirical articles range 25–45 pages (typical for peer-reviewed ABS 1 journals). No explicit word or page maximum found in public sources.

### Abstract
Required. Structured format (inferred from article metadata). Eight keywords required at submission.

### Citations
Standard academic citation conventions. Consistent style (American or British English, maintained throughout). Self-citation of entrepreneurship canon is expected.

### Headings
Standard numbered or lettered section hierarchy. No explicit hierarchy constraints found.

### Tables and Figures
Tables and figures should not be embedded in text body. Include instruction markers (e.g., "INSERT TABLE 1 HERE") and place at end of manuscript, after references.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Author names, affiliations, or acknowledgments must not appear in main document (submission anonymity).
- Conflicts of interest (professional/financial) must be disclosed.
- All authors must provide ORCID iDs.

Empirical articles follow Introduction → Theory/Hypothesis → Methods → Results → Discussion (Contributions, Implications) → Limitations and Future Research → Conclusion pattern observed across sampled articles (Mary Han on entrepreneurial process, Schmitz et al. on transformational leadership, Ostertag et al. on social entrepreneurship).

### CONVERGENCE: Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we") and active voice throughout introduction, theory, and methods sections observed in article titles and sampled content structure.

### CONVERGENCE: Methods Conventions
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Mixed-methods, survey-based, and qualitative designs all represented (e.g., Ostertag et al.: 34 interviews in Kenya/Rwanda; Mzoughi et al.: 100 Tunisian female students). Procedural transparency and construct measurement detail are standard.

### CONVERGENCE: Results and Reporting Norms
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Quantitative studies report coefficients, significance, and effect sizes; qualitative work reports themes and evidence. Both are well-represented without deviation.

### CONVERGENCE: Discussion and Limitations
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Extensive Limitations and Future Research subsections observed as standard. Studies explicitly acknowledge sample constraints (single-country, demographic homogeneity) and forward-looking hedges.

### CONVERGENCE: Citation Density and Paraphrase
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High citation density in introduction and theory sections; moderate in discussion; sparse in results. Paraphrase dominates; direct quotation used sparingly for foundational definitions.

### CONVERGENCE: Hedging and Epistemic Caution
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Results sections report findings directly; causal hedging and discussion of selection/common-method bias concentrated in Discussion and Limitations sections.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Submission anonymity violation:** Including author names, affiliations, or acknowledgments in the main document violates blind review. Keep all identifying information on the title page only.
2. **Missing ORCID iDs:** All authors must provide ORCID identifiers at submission; failure to do so will result in desk rejection.
3. **Embedded tables/figures:** Placing tables or figures in the text body violates World Scientific submission format. Use markers and place at end after references.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Notes on Access and Methodology
This skill is based on a systematic but access-limited review. World Scientific blocks HTTP requests to author guidelines and article abstract pages, preventing detailed content analysis. Inferences about Hard Constraints are based on: (1) World Scientific's general submission requirements (publicly documented via search results), (2) article metadata from IDEAS/RePEc (12 recent articles, 2023–2026), and (3) page-count ranges and structural consistency across sampled articles. No full-text PDF analysis was performed due to paywall restrictions. The skill's "Observed Deltas" section is therefore conservative: every convergence with FIELD_SKILL is confirmed against the data available (article titles, author lists, page counts, keywords); no deviation is claimed unless explicitly observed across multiple articles or stated in public sources.
