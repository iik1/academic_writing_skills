# Journal Writing Skill: PSL Quarterly Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Associazione Economia Civile, in cooperation with the Department of Statistics, Sapienza University of Rome (formerly Banca Nazionale del Lavoro Quarterly Review, founded 1947; BNL ownership until 2007)
- **Scope:** Open-access, pluralist forum for debate on economics and economic policy. Explicitly welcomes contributions from all schools/paradigms (heterodox and post-Keynesian schools are especially well represented in recent issues) provided the method is rigorous and the content is policy-relevant. The journal explicitly discourages "mere econometric exercises or ... articles aimed at displaying technical prowess per se."
- **Guidelines URL:** https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa04/psl_quarterly_review/about/submissions (current host; legacy domain pslquarterlyreview.info no longer resolves reliably)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 full abstract/keyword/JEL records read in detail (Palley 2023 "Theory of Monetary Disorder"; Storm 2023 "Profit inflation is real"; Hein, Prante & Bramucci 2023 stock-flow consistent growth regimes paper; Arend et al. 2023 Brazil/South Korea industrial policy; Cirillo et al. 2023 Industry 4.0/trade unions; Araujo et al. 2023 inflation targeting cross-country study; Cucignatto, Garbellini & Fora Alcalde 2023 profit-led inflation), plus a further 8 recent titles/authors surveyed for topical range without full abstract read. One additional article (an Italian-language piece, ~2020s) attempted via PDF but text extraction was unreliable; noted only as evidence the journal does occasionally publish non-English content. Full text of all articles is behind no formal paywall but was not reliably extractable via automated PDF fetch in this environment; abstracts, keywords, and JEL codes from RePEc/IDEAS mirror pages served as the primary evidence base. This is a good-faith ABS-1 sample per the assignment's reduced-sample allowance.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section structure or template. Authors are free to organize the paper as they see fit; there is no required section list or ordering.

### Length
No formal minimum or maximum, but the guidelines state readability considerations "often constrain" length to roughly 8,000 words. This is markedly shorter than the FIELD_SKILL's typical ABS 4*/4 empirical/theory paper length.

### Abstract
Maximum 100 words — short and unstructured (no required subheadings/fields). Up to 4 keywords. Up to 3 JEL codes required (note: several observed articles in practice list more than 3 JEL codes, e.g. 5–8, so this cap is loosely enforced — GUIDELINE ONLY).

### Citations
Author-date in-text citation required (e.g., "Smith, 1776"); multiple citations separated by semicolons, ordered chronologically. Three-or-more-author works use "et al." References listed alphabetically by surname, then chronologically per author; journal titles are not abbreviated; DOIs/URLs included where available. A journal-branded Mendeley CSL style is provided and encouraged for reference formatting.

### Headings
No prescribed heading scheme or numbering convention specified in the guidelines.

### Tables and Figures
Numbered sequentially with self-explanatory titles; source cited at the bottom of each table/figure; every table/figure must be referenced in text; large tables should be avoided (transpose rows/columns instead); footnotes reserved for essential explanatory detail. Displayed equations numbered consecutively in brackets ([1], [2], ...), with symbols defined at first occurrence.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No previously published material; no simultaneous submission elsewhere; final accepted version must have all colored mark-up/tracked comments removed. First footnote must disclose funding sources, funding ≥€10,000 from interested parties in the prior 3 years, relevant affiliations, and any third-party pre-publication review rights.

### Other
English is the default language, but the editor may permit other languages in exceptional cases (confirmed in practice — at least one Italian-language article was found in the archive). Non-native English authors are strongly urged to have drafts checked by a native speaker. No submission or publication (APC) fees. Editorial decisions targeted within 30 days. Fast-track publication guarantee for authors within 5 years of PhD completion.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION: The journal's pluralist, policy-forum identity and explicit discouragement of papers valued mainly for "technical prowess" corresponds to a lower emphasis on dense identification/robustness apparatus than the ABS 4*/4 baseline assumes. Papers observed here (stock-flow-consistent simulation, input-output propagation, cross-country panel, qualitative case studies of institutions/unions) argue more directly toward policy conclusions (e.g., recommending price controls, critiquing inflation-targeting regimes) with less of the FIELD_SKILL's causal-identification hedging apparatus. This is a relaxation of the FIELD_SKILL norm appropriate to the ABS-1 tier, not a distinct new norm.

### Statistical Reporting
RELAXATION: FIELD_SKILL's expectation of quantitative-magnitude precision in every empirical claim ("increased employment by approximately 4pp") is less uniformly present; several sampled abstracts state directional/qualitative findings (e.g., "profit mark-ups have indeed increased," "commodity index ... positively impacted inflation") without abstract-level effect sizes. This may reflect abstract-length constraints (100 words) rather than a body-text practice; body text was not reliably accessible to confirm further.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (author-date, moderate-to-high in introduction) based on available evidence; no delta observed.

### Structural Deviations
FIELD DEFAULT with RELAXATION on length: no required section architecture (FIELD_SKILL's canonical empirical/theory structures are not mandated), and target length (~8,000 words) is well below the FIELD_SKILL's typical top-tier length. Authors should default to FIELD_SKILL's structure as good practice but can compress or omit sections (e.g., no standalone Robustness section is expected) to fit the shorter target length.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY: Topical range in the sample skews heavily toward post-Keynesian, Kaleckian, structuralist, and institutionalist approaches, and toward macro/inflation/growth-regime and industrial-policy topics with direct policy framing. This is consistent with the journal's stated pluralist-but-policy-relevant mission rather than a formatting rule, but authors targeting this journal should expect referees drawn from these traditions.

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing a paper at ABS 4*/4 length and structural density (full robustness battery, extended related-literature section) when the journal's own norm favors a shorter (~8,000-word), less apparatus-heavy paper focused on policy relevance.
- Treating the 100-word abstract cap and 3-JEL-code guideline as strict; in practice JEL code counts exceed 3 in many published articles, but the abstract word cap should still be respected since it is stated as a hard limit.
- Omitting the mandatory first-footnote funding/affiliation disclosure, which is a distinct requirement not found in most FIELD_SKILL-level journals.
- Assuming full econometric rigor alone will secure acceptance; the journal explicitly signals it will reject papers valued only for technical display without clear policy or economic relevance.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
