# Journal Writing Skill: Brookings Papers on Economic Activity

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Brookings Institution Press (distributed by Johns Hopkins University Press)
- **Scope:** A semi-annual conference-and-journal hybrid pairing rigorous, empirically oriented economic research with real-time policy analysis; papers are presented and discussed at biannual conferences before publication alongside discussant comments and a summary of the floor discussion.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.brookings.edu/bpea-for-authors/
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (Fall 2025 issue table of contents: Erten/Stiglitz/Verhoogen et al. on CHIPS Act employment; Hassan/Mertens/Wang et al. on trade war and the dollar anchor; Rachel/Auclert/Del Negro on r*; Liu/Papanikolaou/Schmidt et al. on technology and labor markets; Mahler/Tertilt/Yum et al. on low fertility policy; Clausing/Knittel/Wolfram et al. on climate inaction) plus one General Discussion record (Fall 2024, English/Sack/Evans/Romer/Romer symposium) and one prior conference draft (Obstfeld, Spring 2025). Full text was inaccessible (PDF binary, no rendering available); analysis relies on guidelines page, publisher issue landing pages, and RePEc/IDEAS metadata.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Papers open with an **unlabeled introductory section** — the opening section must NOT be headed "Introduction." Subsequent content uses up to four levels of subheadings (I, A, then all-caps, then italicized). Each conference paper is published together with formal **discussant comments** (typically two per paper) and, separately, a **General Discussion** item summarizing the floor conversation across the session's papers — the General Discussion is itself a standalone published item with its own short descriptive abstract, not part of any single paper.

### Length
- Full papers: 45 pages maximum, inclusive of figures, tables, and references.
- Comments (discussant responses): 15 pages maximum.
- Short papers (thematic panel format): 15 pages maximum.

### Abstract
100-200 words. (No stated field/structured requirement beyond length.)

### Citations
Chicago author-date style, with journal-specific deviations from generic Chicago: use "and others" rather than "et al." for works with more than three authors; author names must match the original publication exactly, including accents and initials, with full first names preferred where available; BPEA self-citations use issue numbers pre-2006 and season names (Fall/Spring) post-2006.

### Headings
Four-level scheme: Roman numerals (I) for top level, letters (A) for second level, all-caps for third level, italicized for fourth level, with graduated indentation. No "Introduction" label on the opening section (see Structure).

### Tables and Figures
Placed near the relevant text with sources noted. Figures must be redesigned in black and white (avoid referring to color in text). Submit in SVG, EPS, PDF, or Excel format with underlying data included. Reproduced figures require copyright permission; adapted figures require both permission and attribution.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscripts substantially written by generative AI are not accepted, and AI tools cannot be listed as (co-)authors; any AI tool use must be disclosed at the publishing-agreement stage (model, version, prompts). Data/code sufficient for replication must be deposited for any paper containing empirical work; proprietary data requires documented access-method disclosure in lieu of the data itself.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Two deltas beyond FIELD_SKILL, both ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, and structurally distinctive to this venue):
1. The introduction is never labeled "Introduction" — a specific formatting rule not found in FIELD_SKILL, which treats "Introduction" as the default heading.
2. The publication unit is not just the paper: each paper is accompanied by discussant comments and a shared General Discussion summary, both independently citable and separately abstracted. Authors targeting this venue should expect their paper to be read alongside published critical commentary, and should anticipate the kind of direct, policy-oriented cross-examination that discussants raise (e.g., the Fall 2024 record shows the General Discussion synthesizing debate across three related papers rather than treating each in isolation).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Page limits (45pp for full papers, 15pp for comments/short papers) are ENFORCED constraints tighter and more specific than anything in FIELD_SKILL, which is silent on length. This is a RELAXATION-compatible ABS-3 context in the sense that it adds a hard numeric ceiling rather than a stylistic norm — authors should be more economical with robustness/extension material than a hypothetical unconstrained ABS 4* paper, compressing supporting material into referenced appendices rather than the main 45 pages.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural, active voice, direct claims — consistent with the accessible-but-rigorous tone visible in available abstracts/titles)
- Statistical reporting: FIELD DEFAULT (no evidence of deviation from quantitative-specificity norm in available front matter)
- Citation density by section: FIELD DEFAULT
- Hedging norms: FIELD DEFAULT
- Argumentation structure (motivating hook, preview of results, roadmap): FIELD DEFAULT, though the "hook" more often foregrounds a live policy debate (tariffs, fertility policy, CHIPS Act, monetary framework) than a stylized empirical fact — PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence-confirmed pattern given the sample titles, not elevated to a stated norm since guidelines do not require it.

## Common Pitfalls
- Labeling the opening section "Introduction" — guidelines explicitly require it be unlabeled.
- Exceeding the 45-page all-inclusive limit by treating figures/tables/references as separate from the page count.
- Using "et al." instead of the required "and others" in citations.
- Submitting color-dependent figures or referring to color in text; figures must work in black and white.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
