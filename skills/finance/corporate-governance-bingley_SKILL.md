# Journal Writing Skill: Corporate Governance (Bingley)

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing (full title: "Corporate Governance: The International Journal of Business in Society"; ISSN 1472-0701)
- **Scope:** Covers governance-related themes broadly — boardroom effectiveness, control and regulation, executive leadership, the role of non-executive directors, corporate governance models, firm-stakeholder relationships, ESG/CSR-related disclosure, and the impact of regulatory regimes on corporate behaviour. Encourages diverse methodological approaches (quantitative, qualitative, case study, experimental, and literature review/bibliometric).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emerald.com/cg/pages/author-guidelines (standard Emerald journal template; page returned CAPTCHA/403 on direct fetch, so guideline content below is reconstructed from Emerald's general author-guidelines documentation, which Emerald confirms applies uniformly across its journals unless a journal page states otherwise).
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9, via abstract/metadata-level access (full text paywalled/CAPTCHA-blocked): "Corporate governance and environmental disclosure: a comparative analysis" (CG-01-2024-0063); "Corporate governance in SMEs: a systematic literature review and future research" (CG-04-2023-0135, Teixeira & Carvalho, 2024); "Corporate governance effectiveness and firm performance in global crisis: evidence from GCC countries" (CG-12-2023-0518, Alshdaifat et al., 2025); "Bibliometric analysis on corporate governance topics published in the journal of Corporate Governance" (CG-03-2022-0135); "Corporate governance and anti-corruption disclosure" (CG-06-2022-0275); "Do anti-corruption disclosure and good corporate governance moderate the relationship between REM and cost of equity?" (CG-10-2024-0551); "The determinants of corporate anti-corruption disclosures: evidence from construction companies in the Asia-Pacific" (CG-04-2023-0152, Utami & Barokah, 2024).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Standard Emerald journal template. No journal-specific deviation found from the generic Emerald structure (title page, structured abstract, keywords, article classification, body, references, tables/figures).

### Length
No journal-specific word limit found on the public guidelines page (blocked by CAPTCHA). Emerald's general policy applies unless stated otherwise: total word count includes the structured abstract, references, and all table/figure text, with ~280 words added per table/figure to the running total. Journal-specific caps could not be confirmed — flag this as an access limitation.

### Abstract
Emerald's standard 7-field structured abstract, ~250 words maximum including keywords/classification: **Purpose**, **Design/methodology/approach**, **Findings**, **Research limitations/implications**, **Practical implications**, **Social implications**, **Originality/value**. This is an Emerald house convention, not a Corporate Governance-specific one, but it is a hard deviation from the FIELD_SKILL's single-paragraph unstructured abstract norm.

### Citations
Harvard (author-date) referencing, Emerald house style. Three-or-more-author citations use "et al." (italicized per Emerald style). Every reference must be cited in text; DOIs included where available.

### Headings
No journal-specific numbering convention found beyond generic Emerald formatting (numbered headings, sentence case).

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific rule found beyond generic Emerald formatting and the word-count-inclusion policy above.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Double-blind review: manuscripts must contain no author-identifying information, including in figures/captions; self-citation must use third-person phrasing ("previous research has demonstrated," not "our previous research has demonstrated"); unpublished/unaccepted work may not appear in the reference list. Generative AI may be used only to copy-edit the author's own original text (structure, clarity, grammar), not to generate content.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
FIELD_SKILL's "we"/"I" first-person norm is constrained by the double-blind self-citation rule above (RELAXATION/prohibition, ENFORCED): authors must avoid first-person possessive references to their own prior work in the reference discussion, even though first-person voice for the current paper's own claims ("we find," "we show") is otherwise unaffected and follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Structural Deviations
Abstract structure is a hard ENFORCED deviation from FIELD_SKILL (see Hard Constraints > Abstract) — this is a FIELD DEFAULT-overriding Emerald house convention, not unique to this journal, but is flagged because FIELD_SKILL assumes unstructured abstracts.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION (ABS 2 tier): the spot-checked sample shows a notably higher share of systematic-literature-review and bibliometric-analysis papers (e.g., CG-04-2023-0135, CG-03-2022-0135) than would be typical at ABS 4*/4 finance journals, consistent with the journal's stated openness to diverse methodologies. Empirical papers in the sample skew toward emerging-market and single-region settings (GCC, Asia-Pacific, China) with moderate sample sizes (hundreds to low thousands of firm-year observations) rather than the large, often multi-country/multi-decade panels typical of ABS 4*/4 empirical finance papers — a RELAXATION of scale/scope expectations, not a stated journal norm.

## Common Pitfalls
- Using first-person possessive phrasing when citing one's own earlier work ("our previous study") — violates the double-blind anonymity requirement.
- Submitting an unstructured, single-paragraph abstract — Emerald's ScholarOne system expects the 7-field structured format.
- Leaving identifying information in figure captions or acknowledgments sections instead of uploading them as separate files.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
