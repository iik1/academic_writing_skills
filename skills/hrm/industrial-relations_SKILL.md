# Journal Writing Skill: Industrial Relations

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell (published for the Institute for Research on
  Labor and Employment, UC Berkeley)
- **Scope:** Industrial Relations: A Journal of Economy and Society covers
  employment relations, labor markets, and the organization and future of
  work, with a strong emphasis on labor economics and applied
  microeconometrics rather than organizational-behavior or HR-management
  topics. (Note: distinct from the UK-based "Industrial Relations Journal,"
  a different Wiley title.)
- **Guidelines URL:** https://irle.berkeley.edu/publications/industrial-relations-journal/
  and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/1468232x/homepage/forauthors.html
  (main Wiley guidelines page paywalled/inaccessible in this environment;
  IRLE Berkeley page provided full guideline detail)
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (abstracts/summaries only, full text paywalled):
  Meszaros & Quistorff (2025, "Right-to-Work revisited," 10.1111/irel.12353);
  Handwerker & Dey (2024, "Some facts about concentrated labor markets in
  the United States," 10.1111/irel.12341); Gagliardi, Grinza & Rycx (2023,
  "Workers' tenure and firm productivity," 10.1111/irel.12309); Blasco,
  Rochut & Rouland (2025, "Displaced or depressed?," 10.1111/irel.12356);
  Breda & Manning (2025, "Diversity and social capital within the
  workplace," 10.1111/irel.12384); Braakmann & Hirsch (2024, "Unions as
  insurance," 10.1111/irel.12344); Svarstad (2024, "Do unions care about
  low-paid workers?," 10.1111/irel.12349); Dobbins et al. (2023,
  "Comparative impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on work and employment,"
  10.1111/irel.12328); plus editorial board/scope review.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section list beyond standard IMRaD; no distinct "implications
for practice" subsection required.

### Length
Articles should not exceed 25-30 double-spaced pages (1-inch margins),
inclusive of all tables, notes, and references. Title limited to two
lines, maximum 33 characters and spaces per line.

### Abstract
Required, unstructured, **under 100 words** — notably shorter than typical
HRM&EMP journal abstracts.

### Citations
APA reference style, but in-text citations use a plain parenthetical
author-date form without embedded page-level detail as the default (e.g.,
"Freeman and Weitzman 1986"). Reference list entries must include volume
numbers, issue months, and page numbers. Notes must be endnotes only
(no footnotes), numbered consecutively, used for substantive asides.

### Headings
No distinctive heading convention specified beyond standard practice.

### Tables and Figures
Tables and figures must appear on separate pages from text (not embedded
inline); figures require camera-ready format; tables numbered with Arabic
numerals. Variable names in tables/text should use descriptive English
words with initial capitalization rather than econometric abbreviations.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No PDF submissions; no Track Changes/marked-up revisions accepted (Word
.doc/.docx/.rtf only). Manuscript must be anonymized for double-blind
review, with a separate title page.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Voice and Register
ENFORCED (by practice, not guidelines) / PRACTICE ONLY. Despite its
ABS classification in HRM&EMP, this journal's actual content and framing
is overwhelmingly labor-economics in character, not organizational-
behavior or HRM-theoretic. Articles are framed around causal
identification questions (e.g., "does X cause Y") rather than
theory-testing or construct-validation questions common in FIELD_SKILL's
AMO/job-demands-resources tradition. Authors should write as labor
economists: lead with an empirical/policy question and an identification
strategy, not with a psychological or managerial theoretical lens.

### Statistical Reporting
PRACTICE ONLY. Quantitative articles consistently rely on quasi-
experimental identification (difference-in-differences, propensity score
matching, control-function/IV approaches, synthetic control) applied to
large administrative or matched employer-employee panel datasets, rather
than the survey-based multilevel/regression designs common elsewhere in
FIELD_SKILL. Endogeneity and identification strategy are treated as the
central methodological concern, discussed prominently rather than as one
robustness check among several.

### Structural Deviations
PRACTICE ONLY. Literature review sections are typically brief and
economics-style (a short "related literature" positioning rather than an
extended theory-development section culminating in a numbered hypothesis
list). Explicit numbered hypotheses are rare; papers instead pose a
research question and describe the identification strategy directly.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY. Qualitative and purely institutional/comparative pieces
do appear (e.g., cross-country industrial relations institutional
analysis) but are a minority; when present, they lean toward comparative
political economy framing (varieties of capitalism, institutional
comparison) rather than the sociological/interpretivist framing common
in FIELD_SKILL's qualitative baseline.

  anything reinforced, given the journal's identification-strategy focus,
  but not a stated deviation)
- Ethics/IRB disclosure expectations: FIELD DEFAULT

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing an HRM-style theory-driven introduction (AMO, psychological
  contract, JD-R) with a hypothesis list — this journal expects an
  economics-style research question and identification strategy instead.
- Abstract over 100 words — the journal's limit is tighter than typical
  HRM&EMP practice.
- Embedding tables/figures inline in the manuscript rather than on
  separate pages, or submitting as PDF/with Track Changes.
- Treating robustness/endogeneity discussion as secondary rather than a
  central, foregrounded part of the methods narrative.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
