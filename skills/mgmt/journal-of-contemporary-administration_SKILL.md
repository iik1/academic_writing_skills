# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Contemporary Administration

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** ANPAD (Brazilian Academy of Management), hosted on SciELO/OJS
- **Scope:** Also known as Revista de Administração Contemporânea (RAC). Publishes theoretical and empirical work across all administration/management subfields, with an explicit editorial emphasis on ethics, sustainability, diversity, and social relevance ("contemporary administration" framed as critically engaged, not merely present-day) rather than profit-maximization framing alone.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://rac.anpad.org.br/index.php/rac/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 (theoretical-empirical: "Assessing the Sustainability Disclosure Practices of Portuguese PSI Companies" doi 10.1590/... ; "Beyond Hype: Empirical Evidence and Guidelines on Generative AI in Qualitative Analysis" RAC 29(6), 2025; "Antecedents of Commitment to Budgetary Goals"; methodological/tutorial: "The Qualitative Approach Interview in Administration: A Guide for Researchers" RAC 26(4), 2022; theoretical essay: "Markets Interaction: Food Market Interdefinition Agencing" RAC 28(3), 2024; invited/non-peer-reviewed piece: "Traditional Research versus Advanced Algorithms: 'Is Survey in the Last Days?'" RAC 28(4), 2024; editorial: "Reflecting on Contemporary Administration"). Full text was accessible for most via SciELO HTML pages; two PDFs on the ANPAD OJS site could not be parsed (binary/encoded) and were skipped rather than retried repeatedly.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Seven distinct article types, each with its own word limit and abstract fields (see below): Theoretical-empirical articles, Theoretical Essays, Methodological Articles, Provocations, Executive Letters, Technological Articles, Cases for Teaching. This is a wider menu of accepted formats than FIELD_SKILL's four generic templates (quantitative deductive / qualitative inductive / theoretical-conceptual / integrative review).

### Length
- Theoretical-empirical: up to 10,000 words (all elements included)
- Theoretical essays: up to 6,000 words
- Methodological articles: up to 6,000 words
- Provocations: 2,000–3,000 words
- Executive letters: up to 4,000 words
- Technological articles: up to 10,000 words
- Cases for teaching: up to 8,000 words (teaching note submitted separately)
These are shorter than FIELD_SKILL's typical 10,000–18,000+ word ranges for AMJ/ASQ/JOM-tier outlets — a RELAXATION consistent with ABS 1 tier.

### Abstract
Structured, up to 250 words, fields vary explicitly by article type:
- Theoretical-empirical: Objective, Theoretical Approach, Method, Results, Conclusions
- Theoretical essays: Objective, Thesis, Conclusions
- Methodological articles: Objective, Proposal, Conclusions
- Provocations: Objective, Provocations, Conclusions
- Technological articles: Objective, Method, Results, Conclusions
No citations or non-standard abbreviations permitted. Title capped at 12 words, no abbreviations.

### Citations
APA (2020) style, in-text citations include author surname, year, and page number when applicable (page number mentioned even for paraphrase, not just direct quotes — broader than typical APA practice). DOI or URL required for each reference where available.

### Headings
Not formally specified beyond "follow disciplinary standards appropriate to each article type." Observed article headings run in English even for Portuguese-medium pieces once translated (see Sources below).

### Tables and Figures
Explicit formatting rule: Times New Roman 12pt for body text, 10pt for interview excerpts, tables, and figures. High-resolution, professional-quality tables/figures required. Copyrighted images require a formal letter of authorization from the rights holder submitted with the manuscript.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts with excerpts similar/identical to previously published work are rejected outright.
- No simultaneous submission elsewhere (preprints on SciELO Preprints, SSRN, OSF are exempted).
- No author-identifying information in submitted files (blind review).
- Title/abstract abbreviations prohibited.
- Maximum 5 authors per submission.
- Teaching cases describing business experiences over 36 months old are deprioritized (not a firm prohibition, but explicitly disfavored).
- CRediT contributor-role statements and ORCID for all authors are mandatory at submission.
- Post-desk-review plagiarism screening via iThenticate.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Beyond the four FIELD_SKILL templates, this journal formally hosts non-empirical, non-essay formats absent from FIELD_SKILL: "Provocations" (short opinion pieces meant to spark debate, 2,000–3,000 words, structured as Objective/Provocations/Conclusions) and "Executive Letters" (practitioner opinion pieces). ENFORCED — stated in guidelines and represented among sampled outputs (editorial piece observed follows a related freer essay structure with sections like "Preâmbulo"/Preamble rather than IMRaD). GUIDELINE ONLY for Provocations/Executive Letters specifically, since none were sampled directly in the spot-check.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Multilingual publication model: manuscripts may be submitted in Portuguese, Spanish, or English, but an English version is mandatory for the version of record and is treated as authoritative. This trilingual/bilingual-title requirement (title given in two languages, up to 12 words each) is not present in FIELD_SKILL and is ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, and observed — sampled articles carry both Portuguese and English titles/versions on SciELO).

Editorial framing of "contemporary" administration as requiring engagement with ethics, sustainability, diversity, and social justice (rather than value-neutral managerial framing) is stated explicitly by the editor-in-chief in the sampled editorial piece. This is a scope/thematic emphasis, not a structural or stylistic delta — PRACTICE ONLY / editorial-stance signal, worth noting for framing contribution statements but not a formatting rule.

Explicit encouragement of inclusive-language conventions (APA Inclusive Language Guidelines referenced directly) is stated in guidelines. GUIDELINE ONLY — not something the spot-check could confirm or disconfirm from abstracts alone.

## Common Pitfalls
- Using a generic unstructured abstract instead of the article-type-specific structured fields (Objective/Theoretical Approach/Method/Results/Conclusions, etc.) — RAC's structured-abstract requirement is stricter and more type-differentiated than most ABS 1 management journals.
- Submitting without an English version or with a title exceeding 12 words / containing abbreviations — both are explicit rejection triggers at desk-review stage.
- Omitting CRediT contribution statements or ORCID IDs at submission, which this journal requires upfront (not merely at acceptance).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
