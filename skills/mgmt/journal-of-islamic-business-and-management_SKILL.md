# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Islamic Business & Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Riphah Centre of Islamic Business (RCIB), Riphah International University, Islamabad, Pakistan
- **Scope:** Islamic economics, business, finance and management (theoretical or applied); Islamic banking, takaful, financial markets; management topics (marketing, HR, organizational behaviour, Islamic work ethics, business ethics, microfinance) analyzed from an Islamic perspective.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.riphah.edu.pk/index.php/jibm/information/authors ; https://jibm.org/guidelines/
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 full-text articles read in full, plus guideline pages. Sample: Omar, Avdukic & Khan (2025) "Impact of Islamic Finance and Islamic Banking on Financial Stability: A Systematic Literature Review," JIBM 15(1); Sohail & Arshed (2023) "Does Equity Finance Improve Entrepreneurship Potency of Financial Sector Development?" JIBM 13(1); Javaid, Raquib & Nasir (2021) "Creation of the Islamic Self for Sustainability," JIBM 11(2); Khaleequzzaman, Mansuri & Rashid (2019) "An Empirical Study of Sharī'ah Legitimacy of Islamic Banking Operations in Pakistan," JIBM 9(2); Rashid & Shah (2019) "Do Bank Size and Liquidity Position Matter in the Monetary Policy Transmission Mechanism?" JIBM 9(2). This is a lower-tier (ABS 1) journal; access was via open-access full-text PDFs on the publisher's own site (journals.riphah.edu.pk), so full text (not just abstracts) was available for all five — a stronger sample than typically achievable for ABS 1 journals.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscript must contain an introductory section stating objectives and methodology, and a concluding section summarizing main points/conclusions. No other section labels are mandated; observed articles use a mix of styles (see Structural Deviations below). Papers are labeled by type directly under the title (e.g., "PRIMARY RESEARCH," "CRITICAL REVIEW," "QUALITATIVE RESEARCH").

### Length
Guidelines state "preferably between 5000–8000 words." In practice this is loosely enforced (RELAXATION/GUIDELINE ONLY) — one spot-checked empirical paper (monetary policy transmission, Rashid & Shah 2019) runs well beyond 8000 words with extensive tables and robustness discussion.

### Abstract
Structured abstract required, explicitly labeled with bolded sub-headings: **Purpose, Design/Research Design/Method, Findings/Results, Implications** (practical/social/research), and sometimes **Significance**, **Originality**, **Future Research**. Maximum 250 words. This is an Emerald-house-style structured abstract, not the unstructured 150–250 word abstract FIELD_SKILL describes as typical for MGMT.

### Citations
APA in-text style, consistent with FIELD_SKILL, but with journal-specific additions: Qur'ānic verses cited as (Sūrah:Āyah), e.g., "(Qur'ān, 24:37)" or "(2:275)"; ḥadīth given with full source citation (collection name, book/ḥadīth number), e.g., "Sahih al-Bukhari; 5996; In-book reference: Book 78, ḥadīth 27." Arabic technical terms are italicized and often transliterated with diacritics (e.g., *mushārakah*, *ribā*, *maqāṣid al-Sharī'ah*).

### Headings
Main section headings appear in ALL CAPS, centered (INTRODUCTION, RESEARCH METHOD/METHODOLOGY, RESULTS AND DISCUSSION, CONCLUSION). This differs from FIELD_SKILL's title-case heading convention.

### Tables and Figures
Placed within text at the point of reference (per guidelines), consistent with FIELD_SKILL. Empirical papers report standard econometric tables (coefficients, SE, significance stars) rather than qualitative data-display tables, even though the field baseline covers both.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Self-plagiarism; "salami-publishing"; data fabrication/manipulation; ghost/guest/gift authorship; simultaneous submission; no defamatory, malicious, or offensive content. Detailed mathematical derivations must be submitted separately and are not published in the main text.

### Other
- **Every article carries a KAUJIE Classification code** (Knowledge for Al-Ummah Journal Indexing and Evaluation, an Islamic-economics-specific classification scheme) alongside the standard JEL Classification. This appears immediately after the abstract in all five sampled papers and is not mentioned anywhere in FIELD_SKILL.
- 6–8 keywords plus JEL classification if applicable.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
No strong delta in the empirical/quantitative papers — they read as standard applied econ/finance papers (first-person plural "we," moderate hedging), closer to ECON field conventions than to MGMT organizational-behavior prose. The one qualitative/critical-review article (Javaid et al. 2021, on parenting/SIWE/entrepreneurship) is markedly more discursive and essayistic than FIELD_SKILL's qualitative-inductive template, blending psychology, Islamic theology, and entrepreneurship literature without a distinct "Findings" section built from empirical data — PRACTICE ONLY, observed in the review/critical-review article type specifically, not journal-wide.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for quantitative papers (coefficients, SE, significance stars, hypothesis-style interpretation), though hypotheses are sometimes labeled "Ha1, Ha2…" rather than "H1, H2…" — PRACTICE ONLY, low prevalence (seen in one of five papers).

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm — citation-dense literature review/introduction sections, sparser in results.

### Structural Deviations
- KAUJIE Classification code required in addition to JEL — ENFORCED, journal-specific, not in FIELD_SKILL.
- Structured Emerald-style abstract (Purpose/Design/Findings/Implications/Originality) is ENFORCED and is a clear departure from FIELD_SKILL's unstructured-abstract default for MGMT.
- ALL CAPS section headings — ENFORCED, cosmetic departure from FIELD_SKILL's title-case convention.
- Papers self-identify a research-type label under the title (PRIMARY RESEARCH, CRITICAL REVIEW, QUALITATIVE RESEARCH) — ENFORCED/journal-specific, not present in FIELD_SKILL.
- "Limitations" and "Future Research Direction" as separate labeled subsections near the end (rather than folded into Discussion) appear in the systematic-review-type paper — PRACTICE ONLY, low prevalence.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- Heavy use of untranslated Arabic/Islamic terminology with diacritics throughout body text (not just in a glossary), e.g., *Sharī'ah*, *ribā*, *gharar*, *maqāṣid*, *mushārakah*, *muḍārabah* — expected house style, not seen in FIELD_SKILL.
- Papers frequently ground economic/financial arguments in direct Qur'ān and ḥadīth citations interwoven with conventional academic citations — a genre-defining practice specific to Islamic economics/finance journals, absent from FIELD_SKILL.
- Given the ABS 1 tier, methodological rigor is uneven across the sample: one paper (SLR on Islamic finance/stability) shows some internal inconsistencies in its PRISMA numbers and repeats large blocks of text between Introduction and Conclusion — a RELAXATION of the polish expected at higher ABS tiers, not something to emulate but useful to know as a norm-setting signal for this outlet.

## Common Pitfalls
- Omitting the KAUJIE Classification code — this journal expects it alongside JEL codes; its absence would be an easy signal of unfamiliarity with the outlet.
- Submitting an unstructured abstract — this journal requires the labeled structured format (Purpose/Design/Findings/etc.), unlike most MGMT-field outlets.
- Treating the 5000–8000 word guideline as a hard ceiling — observed practice shows longer empirical papers are accepted, so this is a soft target, not a rejection trigger.
- Under-citing or mistranslating Qur'ānic/ḥadīth references — the guidelines are explicit that Qur'ān citations need sūrah:āyah format and ḥadīth need full source attribution, not just an informal mention.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
