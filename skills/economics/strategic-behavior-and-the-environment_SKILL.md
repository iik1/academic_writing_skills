# Journal Writing Skill: Strategic Behavior and the Environment

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Now Publishers / Emerald Publishing
- **Scope:** Game theory, strategic behavior, and environmental policy. Welcomes submissions from economics (including experimental and political economy), political science, international relations, negotiation, and related disciplines examining the interaction between environmental/natural resource management policy and the strategic behavior of those involved in policy design and implementation.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.nowpublishers.com/Journal/AuthorInstructions/SBE (access paywalled; publicly visible requirements below)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 ([Hannesson 2024](https://www.emerald.com/sbe/article-abstract/10/1-2/1/1332860/The-Green-Paradox-and-Learning-by-Doing), [Yamagami et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlsbe/102.00000103.html), [Van Long 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlsbe/102.00000009.html), [Touza & Perrings 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlsbe/102.00000005.html), [Zetland 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlsbe/102.00000076.html), [Borghesi & Zhu 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlsbe/102.00000095.html), and 2 additional titles from volume 10, 2024)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows standard economics empirical/theoretical structure (Introduction, Methods/Model, Results, Conclusion) consistent with FIELD_SKILL. No journal-specific structural mandate stated; articles observed show disciplined organization typical of ECON field norms.

### Submission Format
- Electronic submissions in PDF format only.
- Accepted manuscripts are copyedited and proofread by the publisher; camera-ready copy is not required, though adherence to the journal's approved style files is recommended (LaTeX style files available; see [Now Publishers LaTeX page](https://nowpublishers.com/Home/HomeForAuthorsLatexStyles)).

### References
- List references alphabetically by author's last name, then by year.
- **All authors must be listed in full; et al. is not acceptable.** This is a hard constraint that supersedes any FIELD_SKILL guidance permitting et al. abbreviation in reference lists.
- Verify references carefully; they must correspond exactly to citations in text.

### Author Declaration
Authors must declare that:
- The manuscript has not been previously published
- It is not under consideration for publication elsewhere
- Its publication is approved by all coauthors and relevant authorities
- If accepted, it will not be published elsewhere in similar form

### Review Process
Single-blind review (authors anonymous to reviewers; referees remain anonymous to authors). This is not explicitly stated as a constraint but is the documented process.

### Length and Abstract
No explicit word, page, or abstract word-limit stated in publicly accessible guidelines. Articles sampled range from short reviews (e.g., 219–267 pages for Borghesi & Zhu 2020) to standard empirical papers. If length constraints exist, they are not publicly visible; assume FIELD_SKILL norms apply (introduction typically shorter than body, conclusions shorter than introductions).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Citation Format (References Section)
**ENFORCED:** Full author names in all reference list entries; no et al. allowed. This is stricter than typical ECON field practice, which often permits et al. for 3+ authors. This journal explicitly requires all authors be listed in full.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- Voice and Register: FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural "we," active voice, high formality, economical prose)
- Sentence and Paragraph Style: FIELD DEFAULT (medium-to-long sentences, dense paragraphs, high technical vocabulary)
- Argumentation and Introduction Structure: FIELD DEFAULT (motivating hook, research question, approach, preview of results, contributions, roadmap)
- Methods Conventions: FIELD DEFAULT (mixed tense, procedural sufficiency, identification strategy stated, robustness checks expected)
- Results and Reporting Norms: FIELD DEFAULT (quantitative specificity, tables primary, integration of interpretation, economic significance expected)
- Discussion and Conclusion Norms: FIELD DEFAULT (genuine synthesis, policy/theoretical implications, honest acknowledgment of limitations, brief future directions)
- Hedging and Epistemic Caution: FIELD DEFAULT (low overall hedging; causal claims defended via identification and robustness, not verbal hedging)
- Statistical Reporting: FIELD DEFAULT (confidence intervals carry uncertainty; regression coefficients reported with standard errors and significance levels)

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Et al. in References:** The most easily avoidable error. Every single published article inspected must list all authors in the reference list. Submitting with et al. (even for 5+ authors) will trigger a copyedit request and delay.
2. **Incomplete Coauthor Approval:** The mandatory author declaration requires all coauthors and relevant institutional authorities to approve before submission. Verify this before submitting.
3. **Previously Published or Under Review Elsewhere:** Single submission is a hard constraint. Ensure the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.
4. **Insufficient Reference Detail:** References must match citations exactly. Inconsistent capitalization, missing page numbers, or author-name discrepancies will require correction in copyedit.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
