# Journal Writing Skill: Australian Economic Papers

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** General economics journal (est. 1965, University of Adelaide / Flinders) publishing theoretical, empirical, and policy economics; historically Australia-focused but the current submission mix is dominated by international (largely Chinese) empirical contributions alongside Australian policy pieces.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14678454/homepage/forauthors.html
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (abstracts/titles; full text paywalled) — Adabor & Ayesu "Unemployment and Transport Poverty" (2025, 70003); Li, Liu & Si "Do Minimum Wages Increase Female Employment? A Meta-Analysis" (2026, 70010); Nygaard "Is Housing Supply an Effective Remedy for Australia's House Price Affordability Trend?" (2026, 70014, Policy Analysis); Nawaz & Su "Time-Varying Influence of Australian Economic Policy Uncertainty on Traditional Energy Sector" (2025, 70019); Sun, Chen & Wang "Climate Finance: Employment Opportunity or Alternative Crisis" (2025, 70005); Durmaz, Yang & Wang "Sector-Specific Exchange Rate Movements in Hong Kong Stock Market" (2026); Zhou, Cao & Huang "Digital Economy, Industrial Structure Optimization, and Agricultural Green Development Efficiency" (2026, 70009); Nygaard "Earnings, Productivity and Housing Expenditure" (2025, 12380); La "Effects of Family Business Inheritance Tax Reduction on the Wage Gap" (2026); Sun, Sirisankanan & Kananurak "Social Security Schemes, Economic Crisis, and Child Education" (2025). Access limited to abstracts, titles, and RePEc/IDEAS listings; no full text was accessible.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
The journal accepts four distinct manuscript types, each with its own scope and length ceiling — this is the most important journal-specific fact and has no FIELD_SKILL analogue:
- **Original Article** — standard empirical/theoretical paper, up to 12,000 words.
- **Short Note** — a concise, highly targeted argument or analytical contribution building on well-established literature; up to 5,000 words. Guidance explicitly states anything requiring more space should be an Original Article instead.
- **Survey Article** — synthesis and critique of existing literature on a topic, highlighting gaps and proposing future directions.
- **Policy Analysis** — investigation of a contemporary economic/social policy issue or evaluation of a specific policy/program; up to 8,000 words. Observed in the sample (Nygaard, housing affordability) as a shorter, more directly prescriptive piece than a standard Original Article, ending in explicit policy recommendations rather than a research agenda.
All required sections (abstract, introduction, methods, results, conclusions) must be contained within the manuscript.

### Length
Word limits are type-dependent (see Structure above): 12,000 (Original Article), 5,000 (Short Note), 8,000 (Policy Analysis), unspecified but implicitly shorter/literature-driven (Survey Article). **Each table or figure counts as 200 words** toward the limit — a hard constraint with no FIELD_SKILL equivalent.

### Abstract
Required for all manuscript types; no journal-specific word limit surfaced in the guidelines.

### Citations
The journal uses APA reference style but offers **Free Format submission** at initial submission — authors do not need to format references in the journal's style until acceptance. This relaxes the usual pre-acceptance formatting burden but does not change the eventual reference style (APA, not the author-year-in-running-text style implied generically by FIELD_SKILL — confirm APA punctuation/ordering specifics at revision stage).

### Headings
No explicit prescriptive numbering/naming convention found beyond requiring all core IMRaD-type sections be present.

### Tables and Figures
**Strict limit of 6 tables and/or figures in total** — a hard numerical ceiling not present in FIELD_SKILL. Each counts as 200 words against the manuscript word limit.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts must be anonymised for double-anonymised review (author identity on a separate title page, not in the main file).
- Papers failing basic language/grammar/spelling standards are returned to authors for editing before external review is even initiated — an explicit pre-review quality gate.
- Authors must suggest at least 3 potential reviewers, none from the same institution as any submitting author.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
This journal formally segments submissions into four manuscript types (Original Article, Short Note, Survey Article, Policy Analysis) with different length ceilings and different rhetorical goals — FIELD_SKILL's single generic empirical/theory structure applies most directly to the "Original Article" type only. Authors targeting a Short Note or Policy Analysis should compress the FIELD_SKILL introduction/literature/contribution scaffolding proportionally; the Policy Analysis type in particular is expected to end in concrete policy prescriptions rather than a standard research-agenda conclusion (PRACTICE ONLY, single example observed).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
The visible submission mix skews toward econometric/empirical papers with an international (frequently Chinese-authored, China-focused) empirical setting, alongside a smaller stream of Australia-specific policy and labour/housing economics work — consistent with an ABS 1 general-interest outlet with broad geographic reach rather than a Australia-only remit implied by the journal's name (FIELD DEFAULT in content scope; not a style delta, noted for context only).

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a narrow, single-contribution paper as an Original Article when it fits the Short Note ceiling (5,000 words) — reviewers/editors expect proportionate scope-to-length matching.
- Exceeding the 6 table/figure limit, or miscalculating the word count by forgetting each table/figure adds 200 words against the limit.
- Submitting with visible author identity in the main manuscript file rather than a separate title page, violating the double-anonymised review requirement.
- Submitting without the required 3 reviewer suggestions.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
