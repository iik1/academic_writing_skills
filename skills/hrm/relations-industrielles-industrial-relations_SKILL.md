# Journal Writing Skill: Relations Industrielles/Industrial Relations

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Département de relations industrielles, Université Laval, Québec, Canada
- **Scope:** Relations Industrielles/Industrial Relations is a bilingual quarterly journal (since 1945) covering employment relations, HR management, labor law, and the future of work, with emphasis on Canadian and international contexts. The journal publishes original empirical and conceptual research in English or French.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.riir.ulaval.ca/en/publication-process/instructions-authors
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (across Volumes 78-79, 2023-2024)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section list beyond standard IMRaD. Articles follow: title, abstract(s), keywords, main text, and references. Qualitative, comparative, and theoretical articles follow similar structure but may vary emphasis.

### Length
Manuscript must be **7,000–10,000 words (including references)**. This is notably more restrictive than many ABS 4-level journals and tighter than typical HRM&EMP practice, reflecting this ABS 2 journal's emphasis on concision.

### Abstract
**Required, approximately 300 words maximum** — substantially longer than field baseline. This is a binding constraint and unusually generous compared to top-tier journals. Abstracts must present research objective, methodology, results, and conclusions in narrative form (unstructured).

### Language and Bilinguality
Manuscripts accepted in English or French. Author-submitted abstract in submission language; journal provides or requests translation into the alternate language (English or French) as part of the publishing process. This is a journal-specific requirement with no parallel in monolingual journals.

### Keywords
**5–8 keywords** (not appearing in the title) in the language of submission; keywords are translated as part of production.

### Citations
In-text citations use author-date format with optional page number: "(Author, Year)" or "(Author, Year: Page)". Full bibliographic references follow **APA 6th or 7th edition**. This aligns with FIELD_SKILL but is stated explicitly in guidelines.

### Headings
No distinctive heading convention specified; standard IMRaD conventions apply.

### Tables and Figures
**Tables and figures must be integrated into the main text at appropriate locations** — this is explicitly forbidden in many top-tier journals (which require them on separate pages). Use Times New Roman 12-point font for all table and figure text.

### Formatting
- Font: Times New Roman, 12-point
- Spacing: Double-spaced throughout
- Alignment: Justified paragraphs
- Footnotes: Limited use, numbered consecutively (treated as endnotes)
- Appendices: **Not permitted**

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No appendices
- Manuscript must be original and not previously published
- Maximum 3 self-citations in initial submission (enforced only at submission; may be relaxed post-acceptance)
- No PDFs; Word documents (.doc, .docx) only
- Manuscripts must be anonymized for double-blind review

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Bilingual Publication and Abstract Length
ENFORCED. This journal is uniquely bilingual (English/French with full translations provided or arranged by the journal). The 300-word abstract requirement is substantially longer than FIELD_SKILL baseline and reflects this journal's role as a bridge publication between Canadian anglophone and francophone scholarship. Authors must be prepared for editorial requests to translate or revise abstracts for the alternate language.

### Article Length and Concision
ENFORCED. The 7,000–10,000-word limit (including references) is stricter than typical FIELD_SKILL practice. This enforces a lean literature review and methods section. Unlike ABS 4-level journals where extended literature development and theory-building are expected, this journal demands upfront clarity: research question, identification of gap, methods, findings, and implications must be delivered in compressed form.

### Table and Figure Placement
ENFORCED. Tables and figures are embedded in the main text rather than placed on separate pages. This affects manuscript layout and readability; authors should avoid very large or multi-column tables and use smaller fonts or simplified designs to fit within running text without excessive line-spacing disruption.

### Scope and Framing Diversity
PRACTICE ONLY (observed across sample). While FIELD_SKILL emphasizes empirical research (quantitative and qualitative), this journal's recent content includes: (1) empirical surveys and experiments (e.g., perceived discrimination, cultural intelligence); (2) qualitative longitudinal studies (e.g., religious identity job-crafting); (3) theoretical and conceptual articles (e.g., decoupling theory, conflict conceptualization); and (4) comparative/institutional analyses (e.g., government intervention in bargaining). Authors should not assume this journal is primarily quantitative-empirical; qualitative, theoretical, and comparative papers are equally valued.

### Labor Law and Institutional Perspective
PRACTICE ONLY (observed in content). Compared to FIELD_SKILL's emphasis on psychological/managerial and HR-centric framing, this journal frequently publishes articles from labor-law, labor-economics, and comparative-institutional perspectives. Articles examining collective bargaining, union strategy, labor regulation, and cross-national employment systems are prominent. Authors from these traditions should frame contributions as industrial relations scholarship rather than HRM scholarship, and engage labor-law and labor-economics literatures alongside HRM literatures where relevant.

### Hedging on Causal Claims
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm with no additional strictness observed. Observational studies appropriately qualify claims (e.g., "associated with," "linked to"), and qualitative studies bound generalizability.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Overestimating space for literature review and theory development.** The 7,000–10,000-word ceiling means a typical ABS 4-level 2,000-word intro + 2,000-word lit review won't fit. Authors must integrate these sections or compress dramatically.
- **Placing tables and figures on separate pages.** This journal explicitly embeds them in text. Reformat accordingly; use smaller fonts or simplified designs as needed.
- **Submitting abstracts longer than 300 words.** This is a hard limit and unusual; the journal enforces it strictly.
- **Forgetting that bilinguality is non-negotiable.** Plan for translation or revision of your abstract and key terminology for the alternate language.
- **Writing an HRM-centric introduction without acknowledging labor-law or labor-economics perspectives.** This journal's editorial vision encompasses industrial relations broadly; papers that ignore labor regulation, collective bargaining, or cross-national comparison risk appearing parochial to reviewers from institutional-relations backgrounds.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
