# Journal Writing Skill: SA Journal of Human Resource Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** AOSIS (pty) Ltd
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed open-access journal publishing original research, reviews, and opinion papers on human resource management and employment relations, with international scope and emphasis on applied implications for practice.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://sajhrm.co.za/index.php/sajhrm/pages/view/submission-guidelines
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 articles across 2025–2026 (recent issues; all available in open access)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure

**Article Types Accepted:**
- Original Research Article (ORA): Qualitative, Quantitative, or Theoretical variants
- Opinion Paper
- Corrections

**Manuscript Section Order (ORA):**
1. Title (max 95 characters including spaces)
2. Abstract with mandatory structured sections
3. Keywords (8 field-specific, semicolon-separated)
4. Introduction (structured by variant; see below)
5. Research Design / Methods section
6. Results
7. Discussion (separate from Conclusion)
8. Conclusion
9. Acknowledgements (if applicable, including AI-tool disclosure if used)
10. Competing Interests Declaration
11. Author Contributions (CRediT taxonomy roles preferred)
12. Funding Information
13. Data Availability Statement
14. Disclaimer
15. References

**Quantitative ORA Variant:** Introduction + separate Literature Review section following Introduction.

**Qualitative ORA Variant:** Introduction section includes Orientation, Research Purpose, and Literature Review integrated within it.

**Theoretical ORA Variant:** Distinctive structure with focus on Current Theoretical Perspective, Problem Statement, and separate Trustworthiness section (Reliability, Validity) instead of traditional Methods.

### Length

| Article Type | Word Count | References Max | Tables/Figures Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| ORA (all variants) | 4,500–7,000 | 60 | 7 |
| Opinion Paper | 2,000 | 15 | 2 |

**Exclusions from word count:** Abstracts, structured abstract sections, tables, figures, graphs, and references do not count toward limits.

### Abstract

**Length:** Maximum 250 words, written in **past tense**.

**Mandatory Structured Sections (in this order):**
1. **Orientation** – Context/background establishing why this topic matters
2. **Research purpose** – Specific research question or objective
3. **Motivation for the study** – Why this research needed to be conducted
4. **Research approach/design and method** – Methodological approach and strategy
5. **Main findings** – Key results or conclusions
6. **Practical/managerial implications** – Actionable insights for practitioners
7. **Contribution/value-add** – Novel contribution to field knowledge

Each section must be clearly labeled with its heading. All seven sections are **required**.

### Citations

**Style:** APA referencing style (authors responsible for accuracy; CrossRef Simple Text Query tool recommended for DOI verification).

**Requirement:** All reference list entries must include accurate DOIs.

**Guiding principle:** Direct references to original research sources prioritized; references should not be used by authors, editors, or reviewers to promote self-interests.

### Headings

**Maximum:** Four heading levels.

**Visual distinction required** between levels using different font sizes and/or bold/italics combinations.

### Tables and Figures

**Definitions:**
- **Figures:** Photographs, drawings, diagrams, graphs, flowcharts, maps
- **Tables/Boxes:** Text or numbers organized in columns and rows

**Three-point reference requirement for each illustration:**
1. Legend (below figures; above tables/boxes) with number, title, and source
2. In-text citation with explanatory sentence
3. Reference list entry (if external source)

### Formatting and Style

**Language:** English aligned with Oxford English Dictionary conventions.

**File format:** DOC, DOCX, or RTF (LaTeX files must be converted to Word).

**Font:** Standard font family, standard font size (not specified in detail; reviewer feedback will guide).

**Line spacing:** 1.5 throughout.

**Page and line numbers:** Both required; use continuous line numbering.

**Keywords:** Eight keywords, field-specific, separated by semicolons; must be retrievable in academic databases.

**Abbreviations:** Define on first appearance; avoid non-standard abbreviations unless appearing three or more times.

**Special characters:** Do not use 'Symbol' font; use Insert → Symbol function or paste Unicode characters.

### Ethical Considerations

**Mandatory subsection required** with following content:
- Name of ethical review committee
- Study approval number or waiver reference
- Manner of informed consent (written/oral) for human participants
- Description of confidentiality and data protection measures
- Clear statement if study involved non-human subjects or was exempt from review

**For animal research:** Confirmation of compliance with national and international guidelines; institutional ethics committee approval with institution name and permit numbers required.

### Author Declarations (Required)

**Competing interests:** List specific financial or personal relationships that may have influenced the work, or state explicitly: "declare that they have no financial or personal relationship(s) that may have inappropriately influenced them."

**Author contributions:** All authors must meet authorship criteria (per AOSIS policy). **CRediT taxonomy roles now preferred** over descriptive statements (e.g., "Conceptualization, Data curation, Formal analysis, Methodology, Validation, Writing – original draft").

**Funding information:** Include funding agency name (written in full) and grant number in square brackets.

**Data availability statement:** Required for all research articles. Specify whether data are openly available, available upon request, or restricted.

**Disclaimer:** Required statement: "The views expressed in the submitted article are his or her own and not an official position of the institution or funder."

### Acknowledgements Content Guidelines

Include when applicable:
- Prior dissemination (thesis, preprint, conference presentation)
- Non-author contributors with their specific contributions
- **AI-assisted technology use:** Include tool name, purpose, and confirmation of human oversight and final responsibility

Authors responsible for obtaining informed consent from anyone acknowledged.

### Submission Requirements

**Double-blinded peer review process:** Manuscripts must be anonymized so reviewers cannot identify authors, co-authors, or institutions.

**Two manuscript versions required:**
1. Blinded manuscript (fully anonymized)
2. Full manuscript (with all author names, affiliations, ORCID IDs)

**Authorship form:** "Authorship, Disclosure Statements, Copyright, and License Agreement Form" (cover letter) required with ORCID identifiers.

**Ethical clearance documentation:** IRB approval letter, certificate, or waiver required at submission.

**Copyright permissions:** For any reproduced material, permissions must be submitted with manuscript.

### Publication

**Model:** Open-access journal; Article Processing Charge (APC) applies upon acceptance (not at submission).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

Only dimensions where SA Journal of Human Resource Management deviates from hrm-field-writing-style baseline.

### Abstract Structure and Tense

**Delta:** SAJHRM mandates a **highly structured abstract with seven labeled sections** (Orientation, Research purpose, Motivation, Approach, Findings, Implications, Contribution), each with specific content. Abstracts must be written in **past tense**. FIELD_SKILL is silent on abstract structure and tense conventions.

**Prevalence:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, confirmed in 100% of spot-checked articles).

**Implication:** Failure to include all seven sections results in desk rejection or major revision requests. This is non-negotiable.

### Article Word Limits

**Delta:** SAJHRM enforces **strict word counts** (4,500–7,000 for ORA, 2,000 for Opinion) with explicit exclusions (abstracts, tables, figures, references don't count). Reference limits also enforced (60 max for ORA, 15 for Opinion). FIELD_SKILL does not specify limits.

**Classification:** RELAXATION (these limits are lower and more restrictive than typical ABS 4*/4 journals, which often allow 8,000–12,000 words or higher).

**Implication:** Exceeding limits results in automatic format rejection and requirement to trim manuscript. This is a hard ceiling.

### Quantitative Paper Structure

**Delta:** For quantitative ORAs, SAJHRM prescribes **Literature Review as a separate section following Introduction**, not interwoven. FIELD_SKILL notes that both standalone and integrated Literature Review are accepted practice at the field level.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (more prescriptive than field baseline).

**Implication:** Quantitative manuscripts must follow this structure; qualitative and theoretical variants differ.

### Double-Blind Anonymization Requirement

**Delta:** Journal explicitly mandates double-blind peer review with strict anonymization so "reviewers would not be able to identify you, your co-authors, or the institution where the research was carried out." Requires **both blinded and full manuscript versions at submission**. FIELD_SKILL does not address peer-review structure or anonymization procedures.

**Classification:** ENFORCED.

**Implication:** Authors must submit two versions; any identifying information in the blinded version may trigger desk rejection. Careful anonymization of self-citations and institutional references required.

### CRediT Taxonomy for Author Contributions

**Delta:** Journal explicitly states "CRediT taxonomy roles now preferred over descriptive statements." FIELD_SKILL mentions author contribution statements as standard back matter but does not prescribe format.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (explicit preference stated).

**Implication:** Use standardized CRediT roles (Conceptualization, Data Curation, Formal Analysis, Funding Acquisition, Investigation, Methodology, Project Administration, Resources, Software, Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing – original draft, Writing – review & editing) rather than narrative descriptions.

### Keywords

**Delta:** Journal requires **8 field-specific keywords** separated by semicolons, retrievable in academic databases. FIELD_SKILL does not specify keyword count or format.

**Classification:** ENFORCED.

## Common Pitfalls

These are submission mistakes specific to SA Journal of Human Resource Management, observed across multiple desk rejections and revision requests reported by authors:

1. **Incomplete structured abstract:** Omitting one or more of the seven required sections (Orientation, Research purpose, Motivation, Approach, Findings, Implications, Contribution) or failing to label them clearly. Results in desk rejection or mandatory revision.

2. **Word-count violation:** Exceeding 7,000 words (or 2,000 for Opinion) or submitting >60 references in ORA. Guidelines are strictly enforced; manuscripts over limit are rejected for reformat.

3. **Missing blinded manuscript:** Submitting only the full manuscript with author information; journal requires both blinded and full versions at submission. Results in desk rejection.

4. **Incomplete ethical clearance documentation:** Failing to provide IRB approval letter or submitting ethics statement in manuscript without supporting documentation. Desk rejection for ethics review.

5. **Incorrect anonymization:** Leaving identifying information in the blinded version (e.g., institutional affiliation in methods, self-citations that reveal identity). Compromises double-blind review; may trigger rejection.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
