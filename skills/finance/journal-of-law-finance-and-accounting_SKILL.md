# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Law, Finance, and Accounting

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Now Publishers Inc. (articles also mirrored/hosted on Emerald Publishing)
- **Scope:** Deliberately interdisciplinary. Submissions must be of interest to scholars in at least two of the journal's three home disciplines — accounting, finance, and law. Authors are required to state in the cover letter which two (or three) domains apply.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.nowpublishers.com/Journal/AuthorInstructions/LFA
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (abstract/front-matter level, via SSRN/RePEc/NBER/publisher mirrors — see Sources). "Some Fallacies in Corporate Finance: A Coaseian Perspective" (Aivazian & Callen, 2025); "Minimum Capital and Cross-Border Firm Formation in Europe" (Gelter, 2025); "Half the Firms, Double the Profits" (Roe & Wang, 2025); "The Effect of Policy Uncertainty on VC Investments Around the World" (Litov, Liu & Sitorus, 2024); "Deciphering Private Equity Incentive Contracting and Fund Leverage Choice" (Riddiough, 2024); "Common Ownership and the (Non-)Transparency of Institutional Shareholdings" (Steuer, 2024); "Diversity through Turnover" (Jiang, 2023); "Shadow Trading and Corporate Investments" (Lee, Liu & Romano, 2023); "Liquidity Risk at Large U.S. Banks" (Ball, 2023).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section template is published. Interdisciplinary scope means articles range from standard empirical-finance IMRaD structures to law-review-style discursive essays (e.g., the Aivazian & Callen theoretical critique). No fixed structure is enforced beyond what discipline convention normally dictates.

### Length
No stated word/page limit found in public guidelines. Manuscript formatting: double-spaced, standard paper size (8.5"×11" or A4), 12-point font. Footnotes and table/figure notes may be single-spaced, no smaller than 10-point font.

### Abstract
No structured/fielded abstract format specified; unstructured single-paragraph abstracts observed in practice, consistent with FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Citations
Dual citation format explicitly permitted — authors may use either "law" format (Chicago Manual of Style footnote-heavy citation, with a preference for including a reference list even when footnotes are used) or "finance/accounting" format (author-year parenthetical, per FIELD_SKILL). This is a genuine ENFORCED delta: the journal accommodates footnote-based legal citation as an equally valid alternative to the parenthetical author-year style FIELD_SKILL treats as universal. An alphabetical reference list is required regardless of which format is chosen.

### Headings
No explicit heading convention stated. Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (Arabic numbering) in the finance-style papers sampled; law-style contributions may use section headings without numbering, consistent with legal-writing convention.

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific rules beyond the general formatting note above (single-spaced notes, ≥10pt font).

### Explicit Prohibitions
None stated beyond standard scope/fit requirements. Editors explicitly desk-reject papers that do not fit the two-of-three-discipline scope test, and authors are invited to informally query fit before submitting.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
This journal publishes short theoretical/critique essays alongside standard empirical papers (e.g., Aivazian & Callen's ~20-page Coaseian critique of Modigliani-Miller/DeAngelo/Myers results). These essays do not follow the canonical empirical-finance structure (Data, Empirical Analysis, Robustness) — they are argumentative theory pieces closer to a law-review comment. GUIDELINE ONLY / PRACTICE: the journal's stated interdisciplinary scope licenses this, and it is confirmed in practice by at least one sampled article, but the majority of sampled articles (Gelter, Roe & Wang, Jiang, Ball, Riddiough, Steuer, Lee/Liu/Romano, Litov et al.) still follow standard empirical-finance structure per FIELD_SKILL.

### Citation Density and Style
PRACTICE / ENFORCED (per guidelines): law-oriented submissions (e.g., Gelter, Steuer) lean toward heavier footnote use than is typical of FIELD_SKILL's parenthetical author-year default, reflecting the permitted "law format" option. This is a genuine journal-level accommodation, not present in the ABS 4*/4 finance baseline.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Cover letters must explicitly state which two (or more) of the journal's three constituent disciplines (accounting, finance, law) the paper speaks to — a submission-process requirement with no FIELD_SKILL analog.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a paper that fits only one of the three disciplines (pure doctrinal law, or pure accounting-standards technical work with no finance/law angle) risks desk rejection; the two-of-three scope test is real and enforced.
- Assuming footnote-only legal citation is disallowed — it is explicitly permitted as an alternative to parenthetical author-year style, but a full alphabetical reference list is still expected.
- Treating the journal as strictly empirical-finance in structure; theoretical/critique essays without a Data/Results architecture are an accepted submission type here, unlike at ABS 4*/4 empirical-finance outlets.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
