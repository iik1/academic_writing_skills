# Journal Writing Skill: Review of Development Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Established:** 1997
- **Frequency:** Quarterly (February, May, August, November)
- **Scope:** Theoretical and empirical research on development economics; growth theory, natural resources, productivity, technological change, and contemporary challenges in low- and middle-income countries
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679361/homepage/forauthors.html (paywall; public information extracted from guideline summaries and SciSpace)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 (Amougou & Fouopi 2025 fiscal decentralization; Drèze 2024 embedded experiments; Afolabi & Adeosun 2025; abstracts/issue metadata for Feb 2025, May 2025, Aug 2025, Nov 2025 issues from IDEAS/RePEc)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
The journal accepts two submission types:
- **Full articles:** Standard empirical, theoretical, or applied papers
- **Notes:** Shorter contributions presenting concise insights, methodological refinements, or constructive comments on published work

No mandatory section-naming convention is enforced beyond the expectation of clear sections (introduction, methods/data, results, conclusion). Authors may structure papers flexibly according to paper type (empirical vs. theory). Unlike the field baseline's enumerated "canonical" structures, RDE allows variation.

### Length
- **Full articles:** Maximum 9,000 words (inclusive of tables, figures, and references)
- **Notes:** Maximum 2,500 words (inclusive of references, figures, and table captions)
- **Abstract:** Maximum 200 words

ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, confirmed in recent submissions).

### Abstract
- Structured or unstructured prose; no field-specific format mandated
- 200 words maximum
- Should include readable summary of research issues, methodology, and principal findings/contributions

ENFORCED.

### Citations
- Author-date (Harvard) format: (Jones, 1998)
- In-text citations use parenthetical author-year or in-text author with year in parentheses
- No numbered footnote references for citations
- References may be submitted in any consistent format, though Harvard is standard per Wiley guidance
- Footnotes carry supplementary material, not primary citations (FIELD_SKILL norm; no delta)

GUIDELINE ONLY (stated as preferred but "any consistent format" accepted).

### Keywords and Classification
- JEL classification codes required; authors should specify relevant codes
- Keywords beyond JEL codes optional
- Conflict of interest disclosure required

ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).

### Headings
No specific naming or numbering convention stated. Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable (FIELD_SKILL norm; no delta). Heading hierarchy should be clear and consistent.

### Tables and Figures
- Preferred formats: .doc, .rtf, .ppt, .xls
- Tables and figures may be submitted together with text or in supporting information
- Must include legends/captions; variable definitions and sample restrictions should be noted
- (Consistent with FIELD_SKILL; no substantive delta)

### Language and Style
- Either American or British English acceptable, provided spelling and punctuation are internally consistent
- No explicit hedging or tone requirements stated; follows FIELD_SKILL norm

### Peer Review
- Double-blind (double-anonymous) peer review process
- Authors must anonymize manuscripts to conceal identity from referees
- More than 50% of submissions are desk-rejected within three weeks

ENFORCED (stated in editorial policy, 2015-2022 data cited).

### Data Availability
- Data availability statement required for all accepted manuscripts
- Authors must confirm adherence to data-sharing policy
- If data cannot be shared (legal/ethical reasons), authors must provide appropriate data availability statement explaining why

ENFORCED.

### Submission Portal and File Formats
- Manuscripts submitted through Wiley Authors portal
- Preferred file types: .doc, .rtf (also .ppt, .xls for tables/figures as needed)
- Single document containing main text, tables, and figures preferred for submission
- Authors whose first language is not English encouraged to use English-language editing service

GUIDELINE ONLY.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Word Limits
ENFORCED, RELAXATION. The field baseline does not specify word/page limits (those belong in journal-specific skills). Review of Development Economics enforces a hard cap of 9,000 words for full articles and 2,500 for notes. This is substantially shorter than typical ABS 4*/4 papers (which often run 25,000–40,000 words including appendix). The shorter limit reflects the journal's ABS 2 tier and its role as a venue for concise, focused research in development economics.

**Impact:** Empirical papers may omit detailed robustness checks; theory papers must distill results. Appendices are less common; online supplements, if used, must stay within word-count constraints.

### Notes Format
ENFORCED. The field baseline assumes full-length articles as the standard. Review of Development Economics actively solicits shorter "notes" (2,500 words) for empirical insights, methodological refinements, or comments on published work. This signals acceptance of smaller-scale contributions that would not be publishable in ABS 4*/4 outlets.

**Impact:** A partial replication, a novel estimator applied to a narrow dataset, or a critical comment on a landmark paper are legitimate submissions.

### Double-Blind Peer Review
ENFORCED. The field baseline does not specify review anonymity (it varies across journals). Review of Development Economics requires double-blind review, with author anonymization at submission and referee anonymity throughout. This is standard at Wiley but bears stating because it shapes anonymization burden during revision.

**Impact:** Author self-citations must be rephrased to avoid identity leakage ("Smith et al. [forthcoming] show..." is inadequate; must use third person or cite working papers only by date/region).

### JEL Classification Codes
ENFORCED. The field baseline does not mandate JEL codes. Review of Development Economics requires them; they serve both as manuscript classification and as guidance to referees' expertise. Authors should select 2–3 primary codes; "O" (Economic Development) is typical, combined with topical codes (e.g., "Q" for agriculture, "I" for health/education).

**Impact:** Manuscript metadata matters; incorrect JEL codes may route papers to unsuitable referees.

### Flexibility in Structure
PRACTICE ONLY (relaxation). The field baseline prescribes "near-universal" structures (Introduction, Background/Institutional Setting, Data, Empirical Strategy, Results, Robustness, Conclusion for empirical papers; similar scaffold for theory). Review of Development Economics applies these as guidelines, not rules. The instructions state "required sections should be contained in your manuscript" without specifying what those are or their order.

**Impact:** A paper can combine Results and Discussion, omit a standalone Literature section (integrating it into Introduction), or nest identification strategy within Empirical Strategy. This is more permissive than ABS 4*/4 practice.

### Submission and Anonymization Burden
FIELD DEFAULT. The journal requires clear anonymization (double-blind), consistent with broad ECON norms. However, the 9,000-word limit means fewer potential author-identifying citations or institutional details to scrub. (No delta; just a note on reduced anonymization friction.)

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** FIELD DEFAULT. Papers use first-person plural ("we find," "we estimate") as standard.
- **Active voice:** FIELD DEFAULT. Passive voice appears only when describing data generation outside authors' control.
- **Formality and register:** FIELD DEFAULT. Precise, technical, economical; no colloquialisms or contractions.
- **Sentence and paragraph density:** FIELD DEFAULT. Medium-to-long sentences; high-density paragraphs (4–10 sentences typical).
- **Technical vocabulary:** FIELD DEFAULT. Discipline-specific terms used without definition; acronyms freely deployed.
- **Argumentation and gap framing:** FIELD DEFAULT. Introduction announces results quantitatively; contributions enumerated; gaps framed as theoretical insufficiencies or evidence gaps, not mere "lack of research."
- **Methods tense:** FIELD DEFAULT. Past tense for data/estimation; present tense for model properties and identification logic.
- **Hedging norms:** FIELD DEFAULT. Clear claims preferred; hedging used only for external validity, causal limits, and data caveats.
- **Citation density by section:** FIELD DEFAULT. High in Introduction, medium in Methods, low-to-medium in Results, low in Conclusion.
- **Paraphrase vs. quotation:** FIELD DEFAULT. Direct quotation rare; paraphrase + citation is norm.
- **Robustness and identification:** FIELD DEFAULT. Identification strategy explicitly stated; robustness checks expected and common.
- **Results reporting:** FIELD DEFAULT. Quantitative specificity; tables primary vehicle; economic significance reported alongside statistical significance.
- **Discussion and conclusion:** FIELD DEFAULT. Restates findings in plain language; draws out implications; acknowledges limitations; suggests future directions. Conclusions shorter than introductions.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding word limits.** Many authors trained in ABS 4* outlets submit 12,000–15,000-word drafts. Desk rejection is swift for overage. Ruthlessly cut methods, appendix, and secondary robustness; prioritize main results and identification.

2. **Poor anonymization.** Casual self-citations ("as we show in Smith et al. [2024]") leak identity to referees familiar with authors' recent work. Rephrase as "recent work (Smith et al. 2024) suggests..." or cite only working paper versions without author names.

3. **Inadequate or missing JEL codes.** Submitting without JEL codes or choosing codes unrelated to paper topic (e.g., "C" Econometric Methods for a purely conceptual paper) frustrates editors and may cause routing to off-topic referees. Consult the JEL codebook; check codes of similar RDE papers.

4. **Omitting or vague data availability statement.** Desk rejection may result if authors do not explicitly state data availability status or provide insufficient detail on access restrictions. If data are restricted, explain why (privacy, commercial, legal) and whether synthetic replication code is available.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Additional Notes

Review of Development Economics occupies a distinct position in the development economics landscape: it publishes peer-reviewed research of genuine quality but at the ABS 2 tier, making it more accessible to researchers in lower-income-country institutions and early-career scholars than ABS 4*/4 outlets. The strict word limits and explicit welcome for shorter notes reflect this positioning. The double-blind review and desk-rejection (>50%) signal editorial rigor nonetheless. Authors should view this journal as a credible alternative to top-tier outlets when their work is narrowly scoped, policy-focused, or regionally grounded rather than methodologically novel.

The new editorial team (Sanghamitra Bandyopadhyay, Katsushi Imai, Wanglin Ma, alongside Andy McKay, effective May 2025) signals continuity with econometric and applied-econometrics traditions; submissions with strong identification and clear policy relevance are well-received.
