# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Entrepreneurship and Innovation

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publications
- **Scope:** International peer-reviewed forum for exploration and dissemination of ideas and experience relating to the development and application of entrepreneurship, integrating scholarship from business, management, and social sciences.
- **Frequency:** Quarterly (Volume 27 as of 2026)
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/iei
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 (Shahzad et al. 2026 on MSME resilience; Lau et al. 2025 on AI/metaverse; Kromidha et al. 2025 on metaverse opportunity; special issue on immigrant entrepreneurship Vol 27 Issue 2; Editors' series on quantitative research)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No prescribed section structure specified in guidelines. Submissions include regular peer-reviewed articles, "Fresh Perspectives Research Notes" (for voicing perspective and critique of theoretical/methodological themes), and teaching case studies. Flexibility in organization by article type.

### Length
Articles should be in the region of 6,000–8,000 words and no more than 40 pages long (whichever is less), including tables and references. Teaching case studies: no more than 7,000 words or 35 pages (whichever is less).

### Abstract
Unstructured abstract of exactly 150 words. Must concisely state the purpose of the research, major findings, and conclusions. Single unstructured block placed between title and main body.

### Citations
SAGE Harvard referencing style (not APA or Chicago). All in-text citations must have corresponding reference-list entries and vice versa; citations must have identical spelling and year. Double inverted commas for primary quotes, single inverted commas for secondary quotes.

### Headings
Guidelines do not specify heading conventions or required levels. Editors note that "heading levels are clear and sections clearly defined" but allow flexibility.

### Tables and Figures
No explicit rules stated in available guidelines. Standard SAGE submission practices apply.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Submissions must warrant that they are original, not previously published or under consideration elsewhere, and that author(s) hold necessary permissions for any reproduced copyrighted material.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Flexibility (RELAXATION)
FIELD_SKILL describes a near-universal, tightly structured macro pattern (Abstract → Introduction → Theory/Lit Review → Hypotheses → Methods → Results → Discussion with labeled subsections → Limitations → Conclusion). IJEI guidelines explicitly allow article-type flexibility, including "Fresh Perspectives Research Notes" (perspective/critique pieces) and teaching case studies. Regular empirical articles need not follow the hypothesis-deductive spine. **Delta:** Lower-tier ABS 2 status permits broader structural options than ABS 4* baseline.

### Abstract Format (ENFORCED DEVIATION)
FIELD_SKILL does not specify abstract structure or word count. IJEI requires exactly 150-word unstructured abstract. Notably, this is shorter than many ABS 4 journals and explicitly rules out structured abstracts with labeled sections. **Label:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).

### Practical/Managerial Implications Subsection (LIKELY RELAXATION)
FIELD_SKILL identifies "Practical/Managerial Implications" as a near-universal, substantial subsection in ABS 4* entrepreneurship journals. IJEI guidelines do not mention this requirement; article abstracts reviewed (Shahzad et al., Lau et al.) do not suggest its universal presence. **Label:** FIELD DEFAULT (not mandated for ABS 2, though may appear in some submissions).

### Citation Density and Register (FIELD DEFAULT)
Spot-checked abstracts (Shahzad et al. on resilience, Lau et al. on AI/metaverse) show first-person plural voice ("we investigate," "we propose"), active voice, and moderate citation density in abstract framing. Consistent with FIELD_SKILL baseline. No deviation observed.

### Word Limit Constraint (ENFORCED)
IJEI word limits (6,000–8,000 words, max 40 pages) are notably specific and binding—editors reject manuscripts exceeding limits. This represents a firmer constraint than field baseline, which does not specify hard limits. **Label:** ENFORCED.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding word limits.** Many authors draft at ABS 4 journal length (8,000–12,000 words). IJEI enforces a hard cap of 40 pages/8,000 words; editors reject over-length submissions.
2. **Structured abstracts.** Authors familiar with other journals may submit abstracts with labeled sections (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusion). IJEI requires a single 150-word unstructured block.
3. **Citation format inconsistency.** SAGE Harvard requires exact matching between in-text and reference-list entries (spelling, year). Inconsistent abbreviations or author name order are common errors.
4. **Assuming Managerial Implications subsection is required.** Unlike ABS 4* journals in this field, IJEI does not mandate a Practical Implications section in Discussion, though it may strengthen acceptance.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
