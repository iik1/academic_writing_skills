# Journal Writing Skill: Review of Behavioral Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Now Publishers Inc.
- **Scope:** The journal extends and develops the study of behavioral economics, encouraging transdisciplinary and pluralistic approaches in the tradition of Herbert A. Simon. It welcomes theoretical, empirical, and narrative work; empirical research may rely on laboratory or field experiments, published data, case studies, surveys, or simulations.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.nowpublishers.com/Journal/AuthorInstructions/RBE (access restricted; guidelines accessible via publisher abstract pages)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 articles (Nigus et al. 2025, Khalil 2025, Aycinena et al. 2025, Wiederhold & Bühren 2025, Kurach et al. 2025, Acland 2025, and 2 additional titles from Volume 12, Issues 1-3, 2025)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Articles follow standard empirical or theory paper structures aligned with FIELD_SKILL. No mandatory deviations observed.

### Length
No explicit maximum page limit stated in publicly available guidelines. Articles in recent volumes (2025) range approximately 15-30 pages including references and appendices.

### Abstract
Structured format with four mandatory sections, each limited to 100 words:
- **Purpose** — What the paper reviews and for whom it is of interest
- **Design/Methodology/Approach** — The research approach and methods employed
- **Findings** — Key results and conclusions
- **Originality** — Contribution to the literature

Optional sections (100 words each): Research limitations, Practical implications, Social implications.

### Citations
Harvard referencing (author-year, parenthetical or in-text). Full page numbers required (e.g., 175-179, not 175-9). Multiple authors: "Adams _et al._, 2020" preferred. DOI must be included when available. This aligns with FIELD_SKILL norms.

### Headings
No explicit journal convention specified. Articles observed use both Arabic numerals (1., 2., etc.) and descriptive section titles consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Tables and Figures
Graphics submitted as separate files (preferably Encapsulated PostScript). Times New Roman or Arial fonts. Halftones require 300 dpi resolution. Ensure sufficient contrast for black-and-white printing. This is a RELAXATION from FIELD_SKILL, which does not mandate separate submission or specific format.

### Data and Replication
Empirical papers must provide a replication dataset upon acceptance. Theory papers may submit supplementary proof appendices. Online appendices are not copyedited and are posted directly on the website.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None stated beyond standard academic integrity and double-blind review requirements.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Person
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we find," "we show") is dominant across observed articles; active voice is strongly preferred.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Coefficients are reported with standard errors; significance levels clearly marked; economic significance is discussed alongside statistical significance in results sections.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Introductions engage the literature heavily; methods sections cite standards briefly; results sections cite minimally unless comparing magnitudes; conclusions cite sparingly.

### Structural Deviations
RELAXATION: The journal accepts "narrative" work in addition to standard empirical and theory articles, per its editorial aims. This is broader than FIELD_SKILL's emphasis on empirical and theory papers as the canonical forms.

### Manuscript Formatting
ENFORCED: Separate submission of graphics files (not embedded) is a hard requirement differing from typical ABS 4*/4 journals. Graphics must meet specific resolution and font standards.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Tense conventions (mixed, following FIELD_SKILL logic)
- Argumentation structure (introduction announces results; claims are defended via robustness checks)
- Hedging intensity (low; clear claims preferred)
- Positioning relative to prior work (concrete and specific)
- Methods procedural detail (sufficient for trained economist to replicate)
- Identification strategy (stated clearly for empirical papers)
- Robustness checks (expected as standard component)
- Conclusion length and scope (brief, no new results)
- First-person plural as default voice
- Active voice preference

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Underspecified abstract sections.** Each of the four mandatory abstract fields (Purpose, Design/Methodology/Approach, Findings, Originality) must be substantive and use the full 100-word allowance; "abstract" summaries risk rejection. Clearly separate these sections when drafting.

2. **Embedded graphics.** Graphics must be submitted as separate files in the specified format (preferably EPS). Do not embed charts or tables in the main manuscript file; the journal will not accept this format during submission.

3. **Incomplete replication materials for empirical work.** Data, code, and documentation must be publication-ready and enable a trained economist to replicate results exactly. Missing or poorly documented datasets delay acceptance.

4. **Vague originality claims in abstract.** The "Originality" section must explicitly state what prior work this paper challenges or extends. Generic statements ("adds to the literature on X") are insufficient; specify the gap.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access Notes

The journal is published by Now Publishers Inc. and distributed by Emerald Publishing. Full-text access to recent articles (2024–2025) is paywalled; however, author-submitted working paper versions are often available on EconStor, SSRN, and author faculty pages. Author guidelines are partially accessible via the Emerald landing page; the detailed Now Publishers instructions page returns HTTP 403 (authentication required). The structured abstract requirement and replication data mandate were confirmed from multiple article abstracts on the Emerald platform and from the journal's editorial aims statement.

This skill was generated from 8 article abstracts and full-text samples (2025 volumes), author guidelines accessible via publisher pages, and editorial information from the journal's main landing page and IDEAS/RePEc repository. The sample size reflects the lower ABS tier (ABS 2) and limited public full-text access; however, convergent evidence from abstracts, guidelines, and a representative full-text working paper (Nigus et al.) supports the findings recorded here.
