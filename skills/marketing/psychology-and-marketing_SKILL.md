# Journal Writing Skill: Psychology and Marketing

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** MKT
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** Publishes original research and review articles on the application of psychological theories and techniques to marketing, spanning consumer behavior, branding, digital marketing, and marketing methodology. Accepts both empirical (quantitative and/or qualitative) and conceptual work.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/15206793/homepage/forauthors.html (guidelines page returned HTTP 402 on direct fetch during this review; constraints below reconstructed from indexed/cached excerpts of that page via search)
- **Field baseline:** mkt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (abstracts/front matter only, full text paywalled): Petit & Velasco 2025 "Red Bull Gives You Wings at Work" (10.1002/mar.70004); Han & Balabanis 2024 "Meta-analysis of social media influencer impact" (10.1002/mar.21927); Basu et al. 2023 "Marketing analytics" (10.1002/mar.21908); Schaarschmidt et al. 2023 "Customer engagement in idea contests" (10.1002/mar.21794); Tran, Aboulnasr & Song 2025 "The Rise of the Deinfluencers" (10.1002/mar.70024); Zhang et al. 2025 "A Systematic Review of Meta-Analysis in Marketing Research" (10.1002/mar.22199); Paul & Barari 2022 "Meta-analysis and traditional systematic literature reviews" (10.1002/mar.21657); Bigne et al. 2025 "How to Conduct Valuable Marketing Research With Neurophysiological Tools" (10.1002/mar.70002); McLean, Krey & Barhorst 2025 "Revealing the double-edged sword" (10.1002/mar.22110); plus 3 additional abstract-level checks (Feng et al. 2024 digital agency types; Jayawardhena et al. technology paradox; Chowdhury et al. 2026 BOP consumers/influencers).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No single mandated section template beyond standard IMRaD-style organization implied by "Research Articles" and "Review Articles" categories. The journal explicitly accepts Registered Reports as a distinct article type: Stage 1 submission includes literature review, research question, and full hypotheses/methods (no results); after in-principle acceptance, the protocol is registered on OSF (public or embargoed); Stage 2 adds actual results and a General Discussion, submitted as a revision. Deviation from the registered Stage 1 procedure risks Stage 2 rejection.

### Length
Research Articles and Review Articles: **under 9,000 words**, including abstract, references, tables, and figures (excluding supporting information). Registered Reports have no fixed word limit but must be as concise as the method description allows.

### Abstract
Unstructured (no explicit word limit or field-based structure stated in accessible guidelines). No delta from FIELD_SKILL abstract norm beyond the overall 9,000-word manuscript cap constraining its practical length.

### Citations
APA style, explicitly stated — matches FIELD_SKILL default.

### Headings
Free-format submission is offered ("simplified and streamlined submission process"), meaning initial submissions are not required to conform to strict journal formatting/heading templates before peer review.

### Tables and Figures
Must be placed in the text close to where they are discussed, not collected at the end of the manuscript — an explicit formatting instruction.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Double-blind review: main text must contain no author-identifying information.
- Authors must suggest at least 3 potential reviewers at submission.
- The journal explicitly screens for "fatal methodological flaws" and states a preference against papers that rely solely on a single cross-sectional study using self-reported behavioral intentions — i.e., single-study, self-report-only designs are discouraged as insufficiently generalizable.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
This journal publishes a substantial share of conceptual/methods-guidance and meta-analytic/review papers (e.g., neurophysiological methods guide, paradox/framework-building papers, systematic reviews of meta-analyses) alongside standard multi-study hypothesis-testing papers. PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence relative to a single dominant template — treat the conceptual/review and meta-analysis templates in FIELD_SKILL §1 and §10 as equally likely here, not as the minority case they may be at flagship behavioral outlets (JCR, JCP).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Registered Reports as a formal article type with a two-stage submission process (Stage 1 protocol review, Stage 2 results) is a journal-specific option not covered in FIELD_SKILL. GUIDELINE ONLY for authors not opting into this track.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a single cross-sectional, self-report-only study: the journal explicitly discourages this design as insufficiently generalizable — multi-study or mixed-method designs are safer.
- Exceeding or approaching the 9,000-word cap by placing tables/figures at the end rather than inline, or by treating the word count as excluding tables/figures (it does not, apart from supporting information).
- Failing to strip identifying information for double-blind review, or omitting the required list of 3 suggested reviewers at submission.
- Assuming a single required structural template; both hypothesis-driven empirical papers and conceptual/framework/review papers are common and acceptable here.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
