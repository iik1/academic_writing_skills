# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Management Reviews

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell, on behalf of the British Academy of Management
- **Scope:** The leading review journal in organisation and management studies; publishes literature reviews, debate essays, and review-methodology papers across all management sub-disciplines that make significant conceptual contributions.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14682370/homepage/forauthors.html (accessible via search snippets; full page paywalled to direct fetch)
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 13 (titles/abstracts via search snippets and aggregators): Marzi et al. 2025 "Guidelines for Bibliometric-Systematic Literature Reviews" (ijmr.12381); Tedone 2026 "See something, say something: A meta-analytic review of safety voice" (ijmr.12401); Kunisch et al. 2023 "Using review articles to address societal grand challenges" (ijmr.12335); Cacciotti 2015 "Fear and Entrepreneurship: A Review and Research Agenda"; Garengo et al. 2005 "Performance measurement systems in SMEs: A review for a research agenda"; Ryan & Gatrell 2024 (middle/older-age women employees at work); Xiao, Cooke & Wang 2024 (employee assistance programmes); Crawford & Jabbour 2023 "Enterprise risk management and managerial judgement: A systematic literature review"; Xia et al. 2023 "Big Data Analytics Capability... A Systematic Literature Review"; two 2023 titles on alliance management capabilities in sustainability-oriented collaboration, and relational dynamics in IT outsourcing; plus BAM issue listings (Oct 2024, Jan 2024 issues) surveyed for title-pattern confirmation.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
IJMR is a **review-only journal**: it does not accept empirical papers (no primary data) and explicitly rejects purely descriptive/numeric syntheses. Every accepted paper must be a literature review, debate essay, or review-methodology piece with a genuine conceptual contribution. This eliminates three of FIELD_SKILL's four templates (quantitative deductive, qualitative inductive, and most theoretical/conceptual variants) — the "Integrative review" template is the only one that applies by default.

### Length
Maximum 10,000 words, excluding title, abstract, references, tables, figures, and online-only supplementary information. Maximum six tables/figures combined. This is shorter than FIELD_SKILL's stated integrative-review range of 15,000–25,000 words — a clear RELAXATION/tightening downward, not the field default.

### Abstract
Unstructured summary, maximum 250 words, must summarize the whole paper (not just conclusions). No stated requirement for explicit gap/method/contribution fields, consistent with FIELD_SKILL's general abstract guidance but with a firm, lower word cap (250 vs. FIELD_SKILL's 150–250 upper range — this journal sits at the top of that range as a hard maximum).

### Citations
Author–year style: (May and Phillips 1992) or May and Phillips (1992) — note "and" rather than "&", and no comma before the year, unlike standard APA. Multiple citations ordered chronologically then alphabetically (not FIELD_SKILL's alphabetical-or-chronological convention).

### Headings
A4 page size, double line spacing, minimum 1-inch margins, 12-point Times New Roman. Numbered headings/paragraphs are discouraged; bracketed numbers may indicate heading priority instead. This is more prescriptive on physical formatting than FIELD_SKILL specifies.

### Tables and Figures
Maximum six combined. Mathematical/statistical content should be placed in appendices where possible.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No empirical (primary-data) papers.
- No purely descriptive or numeric-only literature syntheses — must be critical/analytical with a stated theoretical contribution.
- Author anonymity required in the manuscript text for double-blind review.
- ORCID iD required for the submitting author at submission.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Because every accepted article is a review, the "Search Strategy and Inclusion Criteria" section and thematic/chronological synthesis sections from FIELD_SKILL's Integrative Review template are the norm here, not one option among four. Titles overwhelmingly follow a "[Topic]: A Review and Research Agenda" or "[Topic]: A Systematic Literature Review" pattern — a distinctive, near-universal naming convention not noted in FIELD_SKILL. A dedicated "Future Research Agenda" section (or equivalent) is consistently present, matching FIELD_SKILL's Annals-style expectation but here applied journal-wide rather than to one outlet.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Methodology-focused papers (e.g., papers proposing new review techniques, such as bibliometric-systematic review guidelines) are a recognized article type distinct from topical reviews — this is a category not present in FIELD_SKILL's four-template scheme.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a paper with any primary/empirical data component — automatic desk rejection risk given the journal's explicit review-only remit.
- Writing a descriptive "summary of the literature" without a critical, theory-building contribution — explicitly flagged as insufficient in guidelines.
- Exceeding the 10,000-word cap or six-table/figure limit, which is markedly tighter than FIELD_SKILL's integrative-review length norm.
- Using "&" or comma-before-year citation punctuation instead of the journal's "and... year" (no comma) convention.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
