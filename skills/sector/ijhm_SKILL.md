# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Hospitality Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** SECTOR (Tourism, Transport and Hospitality)
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** Research papers, state-of-the-art reviews, and analyses of business practices in the hospitality industry, covering human resources, consumer behaviour, operations, strategic and financial management, technology, and related disciplines.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/international-journal-of-hospitality-management/publish/guide-for-authors

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for full research articles. No additional required section ordering is specified beyond standard Elsevier manuscript components (title page, abstract, keywords, highlights, body, references).

### Length
- **Full articles:** Maximum **9,000 words in total**, including tables, figures, and references. Label: ENFORCED.
- **Research Notes:** Maximum **2,500 words in total**, including tables, figures, and references. Label: ENFORCED.
- Only these two article types are accepted. Commentary, viewpoint, and forum pieces — mentioned in FIELD_SKILL as accepted by leading journals in this field — are **not accepted** at IJHM. Label: ENFORCED.

### Abstract
Format appears unstructured (single paragraph) in all spot-checked articles, ranging approximately 120–180 words. No word limit is specified in the accessible portion of the guidelines. No structured fields (e.g., Purpose, Findings) are required, unlike the Emerald-style structured abstract used by IJCHM. Label: GUIDELINE ONLY (format) / no confirmed word limit.

### Citations
**Numbered references in square brackets** (e.g., [1], [2], [3]) are used throughout all spot-checked articles. This is a departure from the FIELD_SKILL baseline, which states that author–year (APA) is used across all journals in this field.

The in-text citation format places bracket numbers at the point of citation; the reference list is ordered by citation sequence, not alphabetically. This citation system could not be confirmed from the accessible portion of the author guidelines due to page truncation, but it was consistent across all three spot-checked articles. Label: PRACTICE ONLY.

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. No journal-specific heading conventions confirmed.

### Tables and Figures
Tables and figures count toward the total word limit. No additional formatting constraints confirmed beyond standard Elsevier requirements. Label: ENFORCED (word-count inclusion).

### Explicit Prohibitions
- AI tools may not be listed as authors or co-authors.
- Accepted work may not be republished in the same form in any language.
- Inclusive language is required throughout: avoid gender-specific pronouns (prefer plurals) and descriptors implying superiority based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, culture, sexual orientation, or disability. Label: ENFORCED.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

> Only dimensions where this journal deviates from the field baseline.

### Citations

**FIELD_SKILL norm:** Author–year (APA) is used across all journals in this field, with alphabetically ordered reference lists.

**IJHM practice:** Numbered citation format [1], [2] with sequentially ordered reference list. Confirmed across all three spot-checked articles spanning different methodological approaches (mixed-methods, multi-criteria modelling, quantitative survey). Label: **PRACTICE ONLY** (consistent across all observed articles; could not confirm from guidelines text due to access limitations).

**Practical consequence:** In-text citations are brackets not parenthetical author–year. The reference list is ordered by first appearance, not alphabetically. A manuscript formatted in APA is formatted incorrectly for IJHM.

### Structural Deviations

**FIELD_SKILL norm:** Commentary, viewpoint, and forum pieces are accepted by leading journals in this field.

**IJHM constraint:** Only full-length research articles and Research Notes are accepted. No other contribution types are considered. Label: **ENFORCED.**

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Using APA (author–year) citations instead of numbered references.** The field baseline specifies APA, but IJHM uses numbered [1] [2] format throughout. Submitting a manuscript with parenthetical author–year in-text citations and an alphabetical reference list is inconsistent with observed journal practice and will require reformatting before or after acceptance.

2. **Exceeding the 9,000-word all-in limit.** The word cap includes tables, figures, and references — not just the body text. Authors who mentally exempt tables or references will undershoot the available word budget for the main text. A 9,000-word manuscript with 60 references and three tables is likely over limit.

3. **Attempting to submit a commentary or viewpoint piece.** IJHM does not accept these formats. Only full articles (≤9,000 words) and Research Notes (≤2,500 words) are considered.

4. **Writing a structured abstract with labeled fields.** Unlike Emerald journals in this field, IJHM uses an unstructured single-paragraph abstract. A Purpose / Findings / Originality-value structure is inconsistent with observed practice.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
