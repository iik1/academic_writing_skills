# Journal Writing Skill: British Journal of Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell, on behalf of the British Academy of Management (BAM)
- **Scope:** Publishes empirical, conceptual, and methodological articles across the full range of business and management disciplines; explicitly does not publish stand-alone review papers.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14678551/homepage/forauthors.html
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 2 full/near-full text (Temerak et al. 2024 "Nuances of Sales Service Ambidexterity across Varied Sales Job Types"; Wright et al., "Disrupting Management Research? Critical Reflections on British Journal of Management COVID-19 Research", PMC9878093) plus abstract/metadata-level review of ~4-5 additional 2024-2025 articles surfaced via search (e.g., Kutaula et al. 2024; McPhail et al. 2024; Jia et al. 2025). Full-text access was blocked by Wiley paywall (HTTP 402) for most items; findings below rely on the accessible guidelines page, the two full-text sources, and search-engine-extracted abstract/metadata snippets. Document this as a genuine access limitation.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Title page (separate from manuscript body) with title, author names, affiliations, full postal addresses, and email for each author; corresponding author indicated.
- First page of the manuscript text begins with the title only (no author names/details) followed by the abstract, to support anonymous (double-blind) review.
- Authors must not identify themselves anywhere in the manuscript body except the cover page and author biographies.
- A short author biography of 50-75 words is required for each author (submitted separately from the anonymized manuscript).
- Does NOT accept stand-alone review papers or systematic-review-based conceptual papers (redirected to International Journal of Management Reviews). Papers based on student surveys are only considered for the "Management Knowledge and Education" section.
- Submission system requires authors to state explicitly how the paper advances ongoing debates *in the journal itself*, with reference to earlier BJM-published work — this must also appear in the manuscript text, not only the submission form.
- AI-tool use in manuscript preparation must be disclosed transparently and in detail in the Methods or Acknowledgements section; AI tools cannot be listed as authors.

### Length
- 8,000 words maximum for almost all submission types, excluding abstract, references, figures, tables, and supporting information — described as an absolute limit without exception (tighter than FIELD_SKILL's 10,000-18,000+ word norms for empirical/qualitative papers).

### Abstract
- Unstructured, maximum 200 words (FIELD_SKILL allows 150-250 and either structured or unstructured; BJM caps lower and does not use structured fields).
- No author-identifying information in the abstract (anonymized review).

### Citations
- Harvard (author-date) referencing system: (Name, Date) in text, alphabetical reference list — not the generic APA format assumed as one option in FIELD_SKILL. Footnotes are reserved for authorial/explanatory notes, not references.

### Headings
- Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (no distinct heading convention documented beyond standard levels).

### Tables and Figures
- Follows FIELD_SKILL norm; no BJM-specific table/figure rule found beyond exclusion from the word count.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No review papers or systematic-review-style conceptual papers.
- No self-identification anywhere in the body text or reference list phrasing that would break anonymity.
- English-language editing services may be used but must be arranged and paid for by authors; use does not affect editorial decisions.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
- The required "how this paper advances ongoing debates in BJM" statement, with explicit citation to prior BJM articles, is a distinctive journal-level framing move not present in FIELD_SKILL's CARS-model description of introductions. In practice this shows up as introductions/contribution statements that cite BJM's own back-catalogue alongside the broader literature, positioning the paper within the journal's own conversation, not just the field's. (ENFORCED — stated in submission requirements and visible in the "Disrupting Management Research?" reflective piece, which explicitly reviews and extends prior BJM-published COVID-19 research.)
- Author biography and strict anonymization requirements (no self-identification in-text) are more procedural/formal than FIELD_SKILL documents, reflecting BJM's double-blind review process. (ENFORCED — stated in guidelines.)

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- BJM explicitly welcomes methodological plurality within a single empirical remit (quantitative, qualitative, mixed) at ABS-4 level without a strong house preference for one paradigm, consistent with — not exceeding — FIELD_SKILL's general methods menu. (FIELD DEFAULT; noted only because reviewers described this as journal-encouraged variety, not a deviation.)

No deltas were found in voice/register, hedging, statistical reporting conventions, or citation density in the accessible material — the spot-checked articles read consistent with FIELD_SKILL's CARS introductions, "we"-voiced argumentation, and moderate hedging.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a manuscript over 8,000 words (excluding abstract/references/tables/figures) without checking — BJM enforces this as an absolute limit.
- Writing a structured or >200-word abstract, or including author-identifying details in the abstract/body text, which breaks the double-blind review requirement.
- Omitting the required statement of how the paper advances debates specifically published in BJM (a distinct ask from a general literature-gap statement) and failing to cite prior BJM articles as evidence of that fit.
- Using APA-style referencing instead of the Harvard (author-date) system BJM specifies.
- Submitting a stand-alone literature review or systematic-review-based conceptual paper, which BJM does not accept (redirect to IJMR).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
