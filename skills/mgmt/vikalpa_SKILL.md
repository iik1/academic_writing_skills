# Journal Writing Skill: Vikalpa

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** SAGE (on behalf of Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad)
- **Scope:** Broad-view, practitioner-oriented management journal (quarterly) covering empirical research, conceptual/perspective pieces, and teaching-style management cases across all management sub-fields; explicit focus on "decision makers" (managers and policymakers), not a narrow ethics/CSR specialty outlet.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/vik (and SAGE PDF submission guidelines)
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 13 (abstract/title-page level, via RePEc/IDEAS listings): "Nurturing Job Crafting Behaviour and Thriving at Work" (Zahoor, 2026); "Distinct Dimensions of Human Capital in Distressed Firms" (Baxi & Saradhi, 2026); "Bridging the Digital Divide" (Mohapatra et al., 2026); "Innovation Adoption by Small Healthcare Firms" (Jayakar & Mishra, 2025); "High-risk, Critical Decision-making under Time Pressure" (Bangari, 2024); "The Future of Fixed-term Employment in India" (Jain et al., 2023); plus title/section review of the full 2018–2026 back-catalog (issue-level tables of contents) for structural/format patterns.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Article types: Research Articles (empirical or theoretical), Perspectives (synthesis pieces for practitioners/policymakers), Management Cases (teaching cases with separate teaching note), plus shorter Colloquium pieces and Book Reviews.
- Manuscript must be fully anonymized: no identifying information in the main file, header, footer, or file name; identifying details go on a separate Title Page (not sent to reviewers). Title Page includes a "Statements and Declarations" section (after Acknowledgements/Author Contributions).

### Length
- Research articles: up to 6,000 words.
- Other article types (Perspectives, etc.): 10,000–14,000 words guideline range per SAGE's general instructions (verify per current call, as this is unusually wide for a 6,000-word research-article cap).
- Management Cases: approx. 3,000 words, excluding the teaching note.

### Abstract
- **Unstructured**, 80–100 words — substantially shorter than FIELD_SKILL's 150–250-word norm.
- States purpose, major findings, and conclusions only; no labeled sub-sections.
- Clinical trial registrations (if applicable) noted at end of abstract.

### Citations
- APA reference style (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).

### Headings
- Not specified beyond APA conventions; no journal-specific heading labels found.

### Tables and Figures
- No journal-specific rules found beyond standard SAGE/APA formatting.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts that do not meet the abstract word-count/format requirement "will not be considered."
- No author-identifying information anywhere in the main manuscript file or file name.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for in-text argumentation, but article abstracts and titles skew toward direct, practitioner-facing framing ("Implications for Firms and Research," "Some Parallels and Learning from...") rather than the more theory-first framing typical of top-tier MGMT outlets. This is PRACTICE ONLY, consistent with the journal's explicit "for Decision Makers" positioning, not a stated rule.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm where quantitative studies appear; no journal-specific deviation observed in the sampled abstracts.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Structural Deviations
- ENFORCED: Abstract length (80–100 words unstructured) is far shorter than FIELD_SKILL's 150–250-word default — a clear RELAXATION appropriate to the shorter 6,000-word research-article cap.
- PRACTICE ONLY: The journal regularly publishes non-empirical, practitioner-reflective pieces (e.g., a self-reflective "critical decision-making" analysis of adventure-sports incidents; conceptual/framework papers without hypotheses or propositions) alongside standard empirical articles, and short-form Case Analyses, Colloquium notes, and Book Reviews appear in most issues. This is a wider tolerance for applied/reflective and short-form content than the FIELD_SKILL empirical/qualitative/theoretical/review typology assumes, consistent with ABS-1 tier RELAXATION rather than a redefinition of field norms.
- ENFORCED: Mandatory full-anonymization with a separate Title Page carrying identifying info and a "Statements and Declarations" section — more explicit/formal than FIELD_SKILL, which does not specify anonymization procedure.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
None beyond the above.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a 150–250-word FIELD_SKILL-style abstract instead of the required 80–100-word unstructured abstract — guidelines state such submissions "will not be considered."
- Leaving author-identifying information in the manuscript file, filename, header, or footer instead of moving it to the separate Title Page.
- Writing a purely theory-driven empirical paper without regard for the journal's stated practitioner/policymaker audience; Vikalpa favors clear managerial or policy implications even in empirical work.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
