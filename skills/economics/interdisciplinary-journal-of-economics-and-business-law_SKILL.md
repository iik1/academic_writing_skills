# Journal Writing Skill: Interdisciplinary Journal of Economics and Business Law

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** CJEAS Ltd
- **Scope:** A broad, deliberately interdisciplinary forum spanning economics, business law, and intellectual property, explicitly positioned against narrow econometric approaches. Covers corporate/business law, IP and intangible asset valuation, innovation and entrepreneurship, environmental sustainability, and cultural/practitioner-relevant topics, aimed at an international academic-and-practitioner readership rather than a technical-economist readership.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.ijebl.co.uk/ijebl_submission_guidelines.html (accessible)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 (abstract/front-matter level only — see Sources). This is a thin-access ABS 1 journal with no full-text repository presence found (not on RePEc/IDEAS, no NBER/SSRN working-paper mirrors located); the publisher's own "Abstracts" page was the only accessible article-level source.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
First page must contain, in order: title, author name(s), abstract, keywords beneath the abstract. Author emails and affiliations go in footnotes. A corresponding author must be designated. No mandated body-section list (no required Data/Empirical Strategy/Results headings) — reflects the journal's non-technical, cross-disciplinary readership rather than the standard empirical-economics template in FIELD_SKILL.

### Length
Maximum 30 A5 pages including references. Papers must be typeset/submitted in A5 page format. Book reviews: 1,000–1,500 words. This is substantially shorter than typical ABS 4/4* empirical papers.

### Abstract
Unstructured, approximately 200 words maximum ("no more words than 200 if possible"). Single-spaced.

### Citations
Author-year in-text, e.g., "(Smith 2026)"; multiple same-year works by an author distinguished as 2026a/2026b/2026c. References listed alphabetically at the end with full bibliographic detail and page numbers. Verbatim quotations require exact page references. Consistent with FIELD_SKILL's author-year norm; the added requirement is page numbers in references and for quotations.

### Headings
Titles/subtitles on separate lines, not indented, mixed case (no all-caps), no underlining. Avoid lengthy or over-capitalized titles.

### Tables and Figures
Figures/graphs supplied as separate JPEG files, each with a caption, and additionally supplied on a separate sheet as a Word document with one to two explanatory lines beneath each — a redundant-format accessibility requirement not found in standard economics journal guidelines. Illustrations must be grayscale/black-and-white only (no color).

### Explicit Prohibitions
No papers under review or submitted elsewhere. Purely mathematical/statistical argumentation should be minimized or relegated to an appendix — "should be banished or put into an appropriate appendix" — a direct and unusually explicit rejection of formal/technical exposition in the main text. Minimal footnotes; incorporate information into the main text where possible. Manuscripts should arrive close to final form ("not... more than a few minor corrections"). Author-requested alterations after typesetting exceeding 10% of composition cost are charged to the author. All copyright is assigned to the publisher (CJEAS Ltd) on acceptance, including waiver of moral rights; manuscripts must be in English only.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Consistent with the anti-technical guideline, the sampled abstracts are written in accessible, non-technical prose aimed at a practitioner/legal/cross-disciplinary reader rather than a trained economist — case-study and legal-analysis framing (e.g., competition-law case studies, legal-managerial cross-analysis) is common alongside conventional economic/policy topics. This is a genuine register shift from FIELD_SKILL's technical-economist assumption, consistent with both the guidelines and the sample. ENFORCED.

### Statistical Reporting
No econometric or statistical apparatus was evident in any sampled abstract; framing is qualitative, institutional, or case-based even where the topic (ESG risk, productivity) would typically invite quantitative treatment at an ABS 4/4* outlet. Consistent with the guideline's instruction to banish purely statistical argumentation from the main text. ENFORCED / RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's quantitative-specificity norm.

### Structural Deviations
Article types are heterogeneous by design: the journal explicitly publishes articles, comments, letters, and book reviews as distinct categories (e.g., a "Comment Article" format observed in the sample), not just standard research articles. This is a structural feature not present in FIELD_SKILL, which assumes a single research-article template. ENFORCED.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Topical range extends well beyond conventional economics subject matter into corporate/IP law, surrogacy and family law, and consumer/dietary culture — several sampled articles are legal case analyses or socio-cultural commentary rather than economics research per se, reflecting the journal's stated interdisciplinary breadth. ## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a technically dense, equation-heavy manuscript: the guidelines explicitly require mathematical/statistical argument to be minimized or moved to an appendix, which is a much stronger constraint than typical ECON journals.
- Formatting for standard A4/Letter layout instead of the required A5 page format and 30-page cap.
- Omitting the redundant Word-document figure format alongside JPEGs, or submitting figures in color rather than grayscale.
- Treating this as a narrow economics outlet: submissions in pure law, business ethics, or cultural/social commentary are explicitly within scope and observed in practice.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
