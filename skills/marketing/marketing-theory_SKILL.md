# Journal Writing Skill: Marketing Theory

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** MKT
- **Publisher:** SAGE
- **Scope:** A theory-driven, critical, and interdisciplinary outlet for marketing scholarship. Not limited to any marketing sub-area; explicitly favors alternative and critical perspectives over mainstream managerial/quantitative work. Publishes theory papers, speculative essays, "think pieces," theoretically grounded review and methodology articles, book reviews, and thematic symposia.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/mtq
- **Field baseline:** mkt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 (McVey, Gurrieri & Tyler 2025 "Moral market compliance" [full text], Roux 2025 "Marketing (for) a post-growth future," Cheetham et al. 2025 "wild atmospheres," Bertilsson & Egan-Wyer 2025 "Envisioning post-growth marketing," Sherry 2025 flash-ethnography piece, Fitchett 2026 "Marketing theory and critique," Varman/Kerrigan/Chatzidakis 2024 editorial "Markets are dying, long live Marketing Theory," Spotswood et al. 2024 "teleoaffective configuration," Anker 2024 "Meaningful choice: Existential consumer theory" — abstracts/front matter for all except the McVey et al. full text)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed template required; heading levels must be clear and sections clearly defined. Word file preferred (LaTeX also accepted). Distinct recognized article types: theory papers/speculative essays, review articles (must be theory-building or problematizing, not integrative/systematic — see below), theoretically grounded methodology/empirical articles, "think pieces," book reviews, and periodic editorials/thematic symposia (3–4 papers).

### Length
- Full papers: 8,000 words including references.
- "Think pieces": 2,000–3,000 words — an alternative critical viewpoint on an existing debate, or a new (possibly cross-disciplinary) way to theorize marketplace activity, aimed at sparking further conversation/debate.
- Book reviews: ~1,500–3,000 words.
- These are firm, stated limits, notably shorter than the FIELD_SKILL norm of 8,000–16,000 words for conceptual/review work in MKT generally.

### Abstract
Unstructured, 100–150 words (narrower than FIELD_SKILL's 150–250), stating purpose, major findings, and conclusions. 5–8 keywords required (FIELD_SKILL does not specify a count). Abstract typed on a separate sheet at the start of the manuscript.

### Citations
Sage Harvard reference style (author-date), not APA. Every in-text citation must have an exact-match reference-list entry (identical spelling and year). Preprint citations discouraged once a peer-reviewed version exists.

### Headings
No prescribed heading scheme; author-defined but must be clear and consistently leveled. No requirement for numbered hypotheses or a "Study 1/Study 2" convention (see Structural Deviations below).

### Tables and Figures
Figures numbered consecutively in order of appearance; 300dpi resolution. Color figures appear in color online regardless of print reproduction. Standard SAGE permissions/copyright rules for reproduced material.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Reviews that are mainly systematic/integrative syntheses with "future research" suggestions are explicitly NOT wanted; reviews must be theory-building (using prior research to develop new theory) or problematizing (challenging existing theory), per Alvesson & Sandberg's problematizing-review framework.
- AI chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT) must not be listed as authors.
- Third-party submissions (e.g., by a commercial writing service rather than an author) may be refused by the editor.
- Double-anonymized review is strictly enforced; manuscripts must be fully anonymized, including a separate Title Page (not sent to reviewers) carrying all identifying information, acknowledgments, funding, ethics, and data-availability statements.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
The journal's dominant register is critical/theory-building rather than the hypothesis-testing empirical voice FIELD_SKILL treats as default for MKT. Argumentation proceeds as a sustained conceptual argument organized around explicit, numbered contributions ("First, ... Second, ...") rather than sequentially numbered hypotheses (H1, H2). Confident, assertive claims about theoretical stakes are the norm even in purely conceptual pieces; the tone is markedly more polemical/reflexive than the FIELD_SKILL description of "academic but direct" — pieces explicitly aim to provoke debate and can foreground normative/political argument (e.g., critiques of neoliberalism, capitalism, patriarchy) as legitimate scholarly content rather than something to be hedged away. ENFORCED (aims and scope state the journal is "open, reflexive, imaginative and critical"; confirmed across all spot-checked pieces).

### Structural Deviations
- No "Study 1/Study 2" behavioral template and no numbered hypotheses (H1a, H1b …) appear in the sampled qualitative/conceptual articles — contribution statements substitute for hypotheses as the paper's logical backbone. RELAXATION of the FIELD_SKILL behavioral template (expected at ABS 3, since MT's qualitative/critical orientation differs from JCR/JMR-style experimental work).
- The journal has a distinct, recurring "think piece" format (2,000–3,000 words, no fixed IMRaD structure) not addressed in FIELD_SKILL at all. PRACTICE + GUIDELINE (stated in guidelines; confirmed via the Varman/Kerrigan/Chatzidakis and Fitchett pieces, which read as short, essayistic, first-person editorial/critical commentary rather than full empirical papers).
- A distinct Book Review section (not mentioned in FIELD_SKILL) invites extended critical exposition of texts (not necessarily marketing texts) for a marketing-theory audience. GUIDELINE ONLY for this skill's purposes (not spot-checked directly, but explicitly specified in guidelines).
- Annual editorial pieces reporting journal metrics (submission counts, desk-reject rate, impact factor) appear periodically — a journal-specific genre with no FIELD_SKILL analogue. PRACTICE ONLY (observed once).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- Methodologically, qualitative/archival/institutional-logics analyses (coding in NVivo, institutional orders framework) are common and are reported in continuous prose rather than FIELD_SKILL's quantitative-table-heavy or Study-by-Study behavioral conventions. PRACTICE ONLY, but recurrent enough across the sample to note.
- Reference lists in the spot-checked full-text article ran well over 100 entries (McVey et al., ~115 references for a single empirical paper), denser than the FIELD_SKILL norm of 40–80 for empirical papers. PRACTICE ONLY (single full-text observation; consistent with the journal's citation-heavy critical-theory style, but not confirmed across multiple full texts due to paywall access).
- Author bios are printed at the end of the article (name, affiliation, brief research description) — not addressed in FIELD_SKILL. PRACTICE ONLY.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Citation format mechanics (in-text author-date, alphabetical multi-citations): FIELD DEFAULT, aside from the Harvard-vs-APA style substitution noted above.
- Contribution statements as an organizing device in the introduction/discussion: FIELD DEFAULT.
- Use of theory as scaffolding, extended/challenged rather than merely applied: FIELD DEFAULT (and especially pronounced here, consistent with the journal's critical mandate).
- Discussion/conclusion structure (synthesis, limitations, future research): FIELD DEFAULT.
- Table/figure formatting mechanics beyond the numbering/DPI specifics above: FIELD DEFAULT.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a systematic or integrative literature review expecting it to be treated as a standard review paper — Marketing Theory will not consider these; reviews must build or problematize theory (see Explicit Prohibitions).
- Writing a "think piece" as a scaled-down full empirical paper — it should read as a critical/speculative essay, not a compressed IMRaD article.
- Using APA-style referencing instead of Sage Harvard.
- Applying the FIELD_SKILL behavioral "Study 1/Study 2 + numbered hypotheses" template to a submission aimed at this journal's dominant critical/qualitative readership, when a contribution-driven conceptual argument is more consistent with what the journal publishes.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
