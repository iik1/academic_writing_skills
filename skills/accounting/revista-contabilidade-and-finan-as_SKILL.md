# Journal Writing Skill: Revista Contabilidade & Finanças

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Universidade de São Paulo (FEA/USP), via SciELO
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed, open-access journal publishing research in Accountancy, Controllership, Actuarial Science, and Finance. Quarterly publication with triple-blind review process.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://revistas.usp.br/rcf/en/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (Tavares et al. artisanal fishing; M&A enforcement study; public procurement study; Machado & Galdi intangible assets; Panosso et al. debt maturity & culture; Santos et al. environmental slack)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscripts must follow: Introduction → Development (subdivided as appropriate to research question) → Conclusion. Articles may include a separate Literature Review or Theoretical Framework section between Introduction and Methods. Data/Methods sections are always present in empirical work.

### Length
Maximum 32 pages including references (ENFORCED). This is significantly shorter than typical ABS 4* papers. Plan content accordingly.

### Abstract
Exactly 250–300 words (ENFORCED). Structured format: the guidelines specify expected content in order as: Results and contributions, Purpose, Gap, Relevance, Impact, and Methodology. However, articles observed do not always follow this exact sequence rigidly—treat as guidance rather than strict rule. No charts, lists, tables, graphs, illustrations, equations, formulas, abbreviations, acronyms, symbols, or citations allowed in the abstract (ENFORCED).

### Keywords
Up to 5 keywords (ENFORCED), separated by commas, concluding with a period. Keywords should be in the language of the manuscript (Portuguese or English).

### Formatting
- **Font:** Times New Roman
- **Size:** 12pt for body text; 10pt for tables and figures (ENFORCED)
- **Spacing:** Double spacing for text including formulas; single spacing for tables/figures (ENFORCED)
- **Alignment:** Justified, with 1cm indent on first line of paragraphs (ENFORCED)
- **Margins:** 2.5cm (upper, lower, left, right)
- **Paper:** A4 (21.0cm × 29.7cm)

### Tables and Figures
Tables carry sequential headers explaining what is represented, when it occurred, and where. Only the term "table" should be used for both quantitative and qualitative data presented in rows/columns format. Figures are permitted but subordinate to tables in this journal's practice.

### Submission Requirements
- Manuscripts must be unpublished nationally and internationally
- No simultaneous submission to other outlets
- Triple-blind review process (author identifying information removed from all review copies)
- No submission fee
- Submitted through ScholarOne Manuscripts System hosted on SciELO platform

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Do not include author identification or acknowledgments in the manuscript body
- Do not use abbreviations, acronyms, or symbols in the abstract
- Do not use direct quotations from prior scholarly literature except in qualitative/interpretive papers using primary fieldwork data

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Bilingual Publication
RC&F publishes each article in both Portuguese and English (or Portuguese with English translation). Authors are responsible for providing or arranging translation. This is distinct from the ABS 4* baseline which typically publishes in English only. Plan timeline and budget accordingly if submitting non-Portuguese work.

### Abstract Structure Prescription
The guidelines prescribe a specific abstract content sequence (Results → Purpose → Gap → Relevance → Impact → Methodology), which is more detailed than FIELD_SKILL's general "structured summary." However, observed articles sometimes deviate from this order. Treat as a template to guide organization rather than an absolute requirement. Ensure all elements are present; sequence flexibility appears acceptable in practice.

### Higher Prevalence of Qualitative and Interpretive Research
While FIELD_SKILL notes AOS as a qualitative outlier, RC&F shows a notably higher proportion of qualitative interpretive papers (1 of 6 sampled articles) relative to ABS 4* journals. Qualitative papers in this journal follow the AOS interpretive tradition: extended theoretical framing (often drawing on social theory), a Methodology/Research Context section, and Findings/Themes sections. If submitting qualitative work, expect this tradition to be standard, not exceptional.

### Citation Density in Results Sections
FIELD_SKILL indicates medium to low citation density in results sections. Observed RC&F articles show slightly lower citation density in results—more tables with minimal interpretive citations, more reliance on parenthetical coefficient/statistic reporting. This may reflect the journal's style or tighter page constraints relative to ABS 4* journals.

### Page/Word Limit Enforcement
The 32-page maximum is strictly enforced and substantially shorter than ABS 4* baseline papers (typically 40–60 pages). This constraint shapes manuscript organization: abbreviate hypothesis development, compress robustness sections, and prioritize core findings. Plan your structure to fit comfortably within this limit. Essays (literature reviews, position papers) have a separate 15-page limit.

- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (we-narrative in quantitative work, this-study language in some papers; both acceptable)

- Formality level: FIELD DEFAULT (high throughout)
- Methods section convention (past tense, procedural detail): FIELD DEFAULT

- Hedging and epistemic caution (well-identified results declarative; speculative claims softened): FIELD DEFAULT

- Citation format (author-date in-text): FIELD DEFAULT (consistent with FIELD_SKILL; some articles use numbered citations in brackets as variant, likely journal-house-style flexibility)

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding 32-page limit:** Plan ruthlessly. Tighten hypothesis development and robustness sections. A paper that would be 45 pages at ABS 4* journals needs severe cuts for RC&F.

2. **Overloading the abstract:** The 250–300-word constraint is tight. Do not attempt to summarize all robustness checks or secondary findings. Stick to research question, primary method, main findings, and contribution. Avoid citations and equations.

3. **Ignoring the abstract content order guidance:** Although observed articles show some flexibility, the prescribed order (Results → Purpose → Gap → Relevance → Impact → Methodology) is a reasonable template. Deviating entirely may disadvantage review.

4. **Assuming quantitative-only field:** If submitting qualitative interpretive work, align with AOS-style theoretical framing and thematic findings presentation. Qualitative papers in this journal are not exceptions but a recognized research mode.

5. **Submitting Portuguese text without translation:** RC&F is bilingual. If you write in Portuguese, provide an English translation or arrange for professional translation. Ensure both versions read naturally (not mechanical translation).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
