# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Comparative Labour Law and Industrial Relations

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Kluwer Law International
- **Scope:** Original articles in labour law and industrial relations with required comparative or international (regional/EU) analysis. Requires scholarly engagement across jurisdictions; single-jurisdiction articles must reference international labour law systems.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://labourlawjournals.com/journals/international-journal-of-comparative-labour-law-industrial-relations/
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 accessible articles from 2024 (Kramer worker-to-worker relations; Aloisi algorithmic management; editors' introductions to special issues on domestic work regulation and labour law methodologies)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows standard academic IMRaD-with-theory structure expected in HRM field. Explicit requirement for comparative or international dimension throughout (not merely cited but integrated into analysis). Manuscript must engage multiple jurisdictions or labour law systems; single-jurisdiction analysis must explicitly reference international comparative context.

### Length
**Maximum 12,000 words including footnotes.** Longer articles considered only if editors believe length justified and space permits. This is a tighter constraint than many HRM journals.

### Abstract
Required. Word limit not specified in accessible guidelines; standard academic abstract format assumed (objective, methods/scope, findings, implications). Accessibility did not yield detailed specifications beyond requirement for abstract presence.

### Citations
**Footnote style only.** Citations do not appear in running text; all citations and references appear as consecutively numbered footnotes. This is a HARD CONSTRAINT that deviates from many HRM journals which permit parenthetical in-text citation.

### Headings
No explicit specification provided in accessible guidelines. Likely follows standard hierarchical structure (inferred from article samples). Journal-specific deviations not documented.

### Tables and Figures
No explicit specification in accessible guidelines. Standard academic conventions assumed based on field practice.

### Language
**English only.** Manuscripts must use standard English with American spelling conventions: 'ize' and 'ization' endings required (not 'ise'/'isation'). Only articles in English considered for publication.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Non-English manuscripts rejected outright.
- Articles lacking comparative or international dimension (single-jurisdiction analysis without engagement with labour law systems elsewhere) do not fit scope.

### Submission and Review Process
Double-blind peer review. Authors must submit two versions: one with author identifying information and one anonymized. Submission by email to Editor-in-Chief Prof. Mia Rönnmar (mia.ronnmar@jur.lu.se) and Dr. Olga Rymkevich (rymkevitch@unimo.it). Quarterly publication (four issues annually).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

Only dimensions where this journal deviates from the HRM field baseline.

### Citation Placement and Density
**ENFORCED DELTA - requires footnote-only citation style.** FIELD_SKILL permits and increasingly normalizes in-text parenthetical citation (especially in results sections). This journal mandates footnotes exclusively, a practice more typical of legal scholarship than HRM field norms. This is a structural requirement that will affect reading flow and argumentation presentation, as all citation support must be deferred to end-of-page or end-of-section footnotes rather than integrated parenthetically into running prose.

### Manuscript Length Constraint
**ENFORCED DELTA - tighter word limit.** 12,000-word limit (including footnotes) is more restrictive than many ABS 4/4* journals in HRM field, which often permit 15,000–18,000 words. Tighter constraint will compress literature review and/or results/discussion sections. Authors from economics-style backgrounds may find this generous; those accustomed to HRM practitioner journals may find it tight.

### Language Register: Legal Scholarship Convention
**PRACTICE DELTA - legal scholarship conventions more prominent than HRM field norms.** Articles observed (Kramer, Aloisi) employ language and conceptual framing closer to legal scholarship tradition (case-law engagement, regulatory analysis, doctrinal development) than to organizational or occupational sociology or psychological framing more typical of top-tier HRM journals. Voice remains formal and academic (consistent with FIELD_SKILL), but conceptual orientation leans toward law and institutional regulation rather than human capital, motivation, or organizational behaviour theories. This is less a requirement than an observational pattern—papers adopting management/psychology frames are not rejected, but they compete in a journal whose modal voice is legal-scholarly.

### Comparative Dimension as Structural Requirement
**ENFORCED DELTA - uniqueness in field.** Most HRM journals publish both comparative and single-context studies. This journal requires comparative or international (or regional/EU) analysis explicitly. Single-jurisdiction articles are in-scope only if they substantively engage labour law systems in other countries, not merely cite international sources. This reshapes introduction and literature engagement: authors must establish a gap not just within one country's literature but across multiple labour law systems, and must frame contribution in terms of comparative understanding.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Formal impersonal register with first-person plural permitted (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Medium-to-long sentences with subordination and topic-sentence paragraph structure consistent with HRM norms.
- **Argumentation and theoretical framing:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Introductions follow CARS-like structure (problem, gap, contribution) as in FIELD_SKILL. Theoretical contribution framed relative to existing literature. Practical implications discussed.
- **Methods conventions:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Quantitative papers report sample, measures, estimation strategy; qualitative papers report sampling, data collection, analytic approach and reflexivity consistent with FIELD_SKILL.
- **Results and reporting norms:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Quantitative results narrated with reference to tables; qualitative findings organized thematically with illustrative quotations and interpretation.
- **Discussion and conclusion norms:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Discussion revisits contributions, distinguishes theoretical from practical implications, closes with limitations and future research.
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** FIELD_DEFAULT. Moderate-to-high hedging; causal claims qualified in observational designs; qualitative papers hedge generalizability.
- **Structural flexibility:** FIELD_DEFAULT. No journal-specific mandates on integration vs. separation of literature review and introduction, or on combined vs. separate discussion/conclusion sections.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Footnote compliance failure.** Submitting with in-text parenthetical citations instead of footnotes. All citations must be numbered consecutively in footnotes; in-text citation will trigger desk reject or major revision request. Check that all references (author-date, author-page, or other parenthetical format) are converted to footnote format before submission.

2. **Insufficient comparative framing.** Single-country case studies without explicit comparative context or engagement with labour law systems in other jurisdictions. The journal's scope requirement is distinctive: it is not enough to cite international sources; the research question and analysis must be framed comparatively. Articles that read as "here's labour law in Country X" rather than "how does labour law in Country X compare to..." are at risk of scope desk-reject.

3. **Word-count miscalculation (footnotes included).** Authors accustomed to field conventions where abstracts and keywords are excluded from word count, or where footnotes don't count, will exceed the 12,000-word limit. Word count includes footnotes; verify final length carefully.

4. **Spelling conventions.** Using British English conventions (labour, organize/organisation) without standardizing to American 'ize'/'ization' forms. The guideline explicitly requires 'ize'/'ization' endings; copyediting may catch this, but compliance before submission is expected.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access and Scope Limitations

Full-text access to this journal is restricted to institutional subscribers via Kluwer Law Online and Law-Store. Author guidelines PDF (2012 version) is publicly available but is dated; some specifications may have evolved. Recent article access was limited to abstracts and partial text via Kluwer Law Online; full texts were not accessible. Spot-check sample of 5 articles is below the ideal 10–15; this reflects a niche, low-tier (ABS 1) journal with paywalled full-text access and limited open-access preprints. Deltas reported are based on accessible guidelines and partial article text rather than systematic full-text reading. Findings are robust for Hard Constraints (taken directly from stated guidelines) and citation placement (confirmed through all accessible article excerpts); deltas on register and comparative framing are based on article samples and are likely representative but not exhaustively verified.
