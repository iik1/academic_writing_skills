# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Investment Strategies

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Infopro Digital Risk Ltd (Risk.net journals)
- **Scope:** Publishes academic, buy-side, and sell-side research on modern investment strategies (fundamental, relative value, tactical, event-driven, algorithmic trading, portfolio management/asset allocation, and econometric/statistical methods), plus a practitioner-facing Forum section for discussion articles.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.risk.net/static/risk-journals-submission-guidelines (house-wide Risk Journals guidelines, applicable to this title); https://www.risk.net/static/about-journal-investment-strategies (scope)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 15 (front-matter/abstracts only, full text paywalled): "Option strategies based on semiparametric implied volatility surface prediction" (Audrino & Colangelo); "Constructing the best trading strategy" (Maymin & Maymin); "On a multi-timescale statistical feedback model for volatility fluctuations" (Borland & Bouchaud); "Perspectives on systemic risk" (Curnutt & Lam); "Understanding risk-based portfolios" (Taliaferro); "Two centuries of trend following" (Lempérière et al); "Risk-return-efficient target-volatility strategies" (Giese); "Mean reversion in stock prices" (Spierdijk & Bikker); "Intertemporal risk parity" (Roncalli et al); "Efficient trading in taxable portfolios" (Das et al); "Portfolio concentration and equity market contagion" (Kaiser); "Tail protection for long investors: trend convexity at work" (Dao et al); "Speed and dimensions of trading" (Gnedenko & Yelnik); "Should we invest more in multinational companies when domestic markets decline?" (O'Hagan-Luff, Berrill, Lucey); "A consistent investment strategy" (Chen & Tian); "Factor-based tactical bond allocation and interest rate risk management" (Thomann) — 16 articles total across 2011-2025 issues.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Required order: separate title page (author details, running header ≤50 characters, word count, figure/table count) → figure/table legends with one-sentence captions → Abstract → Keywords (4-6) → Key messages (3-4 bullet points, max 85 characters each) → main text (blinded, no author names/affiliations) → Acknowledgements and Declarations of Interest → References. Recommended main-text sections: Introduction, Literature Review, Data, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Conclusion.

### Length
Research papers: max 10,000 words. Forum (discussion) papers: max 7,000 words. Not strictly enforced; final length decisions rest with the publisher.

### Abstract
Single paragraph, 150-200 words (narrower than FIELD_SKILL's 150-250 range). Must state purpose, principal results, and major conclusions. No references permitted in the abstract.

### Citations
In-text citations: author-date per The Chicago Manual of Style. Reference list: APA format, alphabetical by author then chronological, with DOIs where available.

### Headings
No explicit heading-style rule beyond the recommended section list above; follows FIELD_SKILL norm otherwise.

### Tables and Figures
Minimum number of tables/figures required. Submit as separate editable files plus embedded in the main PDF. Vector formats preferred (EPS, SVG, PDF); minimum 300dpi if raster. Figures appear in color online. One-sentence captions required for each.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No author names/affiliations in the main text (blind review). No previously published or simultaneously submitted papers. Footnotes used sparingly, max two sentences each. Generative AI use restricted to data extraction, background research, and editing assistance only — not drafting. Published papers must not be posted online afterward.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
This journal publishes a distinct **Forum** article type (practitioner discussion pieces, e.g. "Perspectives on systemic risk") alongside standard research papers. Forum pieces run shorter (7,000-word cap), are more applied/practitioner-oriented in framing, and do not consistently follow the full Introduction→Literature Review→Data→Methodology→Results→Discussion→Conclusion structure FIELD_SKILL assumes. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, confirmed by sampled Forum-style pieces). When writing a research paper (not Forum), structure follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

The required "Key messages" block (3-4 bullet points, max 85 characters each, placed before the main text) is a journal-specific addition not present in FIELD_SKILL or typical ABS 4*/4 finance journals. ENFORCED.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Author pool mixes academic, buy-side, and sell-side contributors by design (per journal scope). Sampled abstracts skew toward direct, applied framing of practical strategy performance/implementation questions (e.g., tax efficiency, trading speed metrics, tail-risk hedging) rather than the identification-heavy empirical framing typical of ABS 4*/4 finance papers. PRACTICE ONLY / consistent with ABS 1 tier — a RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's identification-strategy emphasis, not a redefinition of it.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 200-word abstract ceiling (FIELD_SKILL allows up to 250; this journal caps at 200) or including references in the abstract.
- Omitting the required "Key messages" bullet block, which has no FIELD_SKILL analog.
- Leaving author names/affiliations in the main text file rather than isolating them to the separate title page (blind review requirement).
- Using MLA/numbered citation style instead of the required Chicago author-date + APA reference list combination.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
