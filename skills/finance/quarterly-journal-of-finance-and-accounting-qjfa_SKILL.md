# Journal Writing Skill: Quarterly Journal of Finance and Accounting (QJFA)

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Creighton University, Heider College of Business
- **Scope:** General interest journal publishing original finance, economics, and accounting research papers. Also publishes high-quality survey articles. Emphasis on finance, economics, accounting topics including capital markets, banking, corporate finance, investments, and international finance.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.creighton.edu/business/community-connection/qjfa/submission-manuscripts
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** Limited access (journal is behind JSTOR paywall; full-text access unavailable in public domain. Review based on author guidelines, editorial policy, and available metadata from Creighton University and JSTOR.)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Standard sections (Introduction, Methods/Data, Results, Conclusion) apply. No journal-specific structural requirements stated beyond standard norms.

### Length
**ENFORCED:** Manuscripts must not exceed 40 pages (double-spaced). This is substantially shorter than the typical length range in higher-tier finance journals and enforces conciseness. Abstract maximum 150 words.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED:** Abstract limited to 150 words or less. Must be unstructured (single paragraph). This is tighter than the 150–250 word range stated in FIELD_SKILL.

### Citations
**ENFORCED:** Parenthetical author-date format in text (e.g., "Hassan (2007)"). Parentheses required, not brackets. References section at end. Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for author-date in-text citation but explicitly specifies parentheses over brackets.

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. No explicit heading conventions stated in guidelines beyond standard Arabic numeral section numbering.

### Tables and Figures
**ENFORCED:** All tables and figures must be incorporated within the text body (not deferred to appendix as supplement). Sequentially numbered with Arabic numerals. Figures must not exceed 4.75 inches by 6.75 inches. Labels in 8-point Arial or similar sans-serif. This is more restrictive than FIELD_SKILL, which allows figures to be placed after references and does not specify strict size limits.

### File Format
**ENFORCED:** Accepted manuscripts must be submitted in Microsoft Word format exclusively. No other formats permitted.

### Footnotes
**ENFORCED:** Only explanatory footnotes are numbered in text. Footnotes should be minimal and used only to avoid breaking argument flow. Citations use parenthetical author-date in text, not footnotes.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Journal explicitly discourages replication studies as the primary focus (per editorial policy). Original research is strongly preferred. No other explicit language or tone prohibitions stated.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Length and Scope
**RELAXATION, ENFORCED:** The 40-page limit is substantially shorter than typical for ABS 4*/4 journals in finance, which often run 50+ pages. This is a genuine lower-tier constraint. Authors must compress methodological detail and results presentation compared to top-tier norms. Standard-error clustering choices and variable definitions may be abbreviated or moved to appendix more readily than in higher-tier journals.

### Abstract Specifications
**ENFORCED:** The 150-word cap is a hard upper limit (vs. "typically 150–250" in FIELD_SKILL). No flexibility. This may require authors to be more aggressive in trimming methods preview and results preview from the abstract.

### Table and Figure Placement
**ENFORCED:** Figures and tables must be embedded in-text, not placed at end of manuscript as is common in some top-tier journals. Size constraints (4.75 x 6.75 inches) force selectivity about visual presentation. Appendices may be less commonly used for supplemental figures compared to ABS 4*/4 norms.

### Footnote Practice
**ENFORCED:** Minimal footnote use (explanatory only, no citations). This aligns with FIELD_SKILL but is explicitly stated as mandatory in QJFA guidelines, suggesting it may be enforced more strictly than in some peers.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding page limit:** 40 pages (double-spaced) is significantly shorter than reviewers accustomed to top-tier finance journals may expect. Manuscripts over 40 pages will face immediate desk rejection. Prioritize results over extended literature review.

2. **Figure size and placement:** Failing to embed figures in-text or exceeding size constraints (4.75 x 6.75 inches) will require revision before review. Do not place figures in appendix; do not use oversized illustrations.

3. **Overlong abstracts:** Abstract of 150+ words will violate guidelines. Authors often struggle to compress abstract to 150 words while previewing method and results; prioritize research question and key findings over methodological detail.

4. **Using non-Word format:** Submissions in PDF, LaTeX, or other formats will not be accepted. Convert all manuscripts to .docx before submission.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
