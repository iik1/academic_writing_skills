# Journal Writing Skill: Quarterly Journal of Austrian Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Ludwig von Mises Institute
- **Scope:** A refereed heterodox-economics journal promoting the development and extension of Austrian School economics, including praxeological method, monetary and business-cycle theory, entrepreneurship theory, and history of Austrian economic thought.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://mises.org/quarterly-journal-austrian-economics/qjae-submission-guidelines (submission portal: https://qjae.scholasticahq.com/for-authors)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (Matarán 2024, "The Essential Jesús Huerta de Soto..."; Burr 2024, "Ludwig von Mises's Outstanding Position in Economic Research"; D'Andrea 2024, "Man of Action: Murray N. Rothbard's Contributions to the Theory of Entrepreneurship"; Yen, Turnipseed & Borodkin 2026, "The Development and Application of the Disutility of Labor in Marginalist Theory"; Mulligan 2006, "An Empirical Examination of Austrian Business Cycle Theory"; Hébert 2024, "Whither Goeth the Entrepreneur?" — a published Mises Memorial Lecture transcript rather than a standard research article)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section architecture is specified beyond a title page (with author name/email, removed from the review copy for double-blind refereeing) and a bibliography limited to sources actually cited.

### Length
Manuscripts: maximum 10,000 words, exclusive of references. Comments/replies to prior articles: generally no more than 5 double-spaced pages. Maximum two articles per author under simultaneous review; maximum two published per volume.

### Abstract
No more than 250 words, unstructured.

### Citations
The Chicago Manual of Style, 14th ed. Footnotes are explanatory only, never used for citation purposes — citations belong in-text with a bibliography of cited sources only.

### Headings
No fixed convention specified; observed articles use descriptive, topic-named headings rather than generic labels (see Structural Deviations below).

### Tables and Figures
Not addressed in the guidelines; no journal-specific rule identified.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No simultaneous submission to other journals. No previously published work. If a manuscript is submitted elsewhere while under QJAE review, the author must withdraw it from QJAE or from the other venue. Manuscript files carrying identifying information are not reviewed (double-blind enforcement).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
FIELD_SKILL describes a near-universal empirical/theory skeleton (Introduction → Data/Model → Results → Conclusion) with numbered generic section labels. QJAE articles instead consistently use descriptive, content-specific headings tied to the argument's substance (e.g., "The Austrian School of Madrid," "Praxeology as the Method of Austrian Economics," "Jevons's Theory," "Controversies: Kirzner, Lachmann, Schumpeter, and More") rather than generic "Literature Review," "Data," or "Empirical Strategy" labels. A standalone numbered Introduction/Conclusion pair is not consistently present; several sampled pieces have no separately labeled introduction section at all. PRACTICE ONLY, but consistent across the sample — treat as the journal's default heading style. This is a RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's structural expectations, consistent with the journal's ABS 1 tier and its history-of-thought/theoretical emphasis rather than a reduced-form empirical template.

### Genre Range
FIELD_SKILL assumes standard research-article format throughout. QJAE also publishes non-standard genres not addressed by FIELD_SKILL at all: published memorial-lecture transcripts (e.g., the annual Ludwig von Mises Memorial Lecture) and short comments/replies capped at 5 pages. PRACTICE ONLY / journal-specific genre, not a deviation from an article-format norm FIELD_SKILL never claimed to cover.

### Topical Emphasis
A large share of sampled output is intellectual history and exegesis of specific Austrian economists (Mises, Rothbard, Huerta de Soto) rather than original empirical or model-based contributions in the FIELD_SKILL sense. Papers of this type substitute biographical/bibliographic analysis and textual interpretation for the Data/Empirical-Strategy/Results skeleton; "contribution" is framed as systematizing or reassessing a thinker's scattered ideas rather than an empirical or theoretical result. PRACTICE ONLY, high-prevalence in the sample — flag as a common paper type at this journal, not a universal requirement.

### Citation Style
FIELD_SKILL does not specify a citation format (explicitly out of scope for the field baseline). QJAE mandates Chicago 14th ed. with footnotes restricted to explanatory content only — this is a HARD_CONSTRAINT with no FIELD_SKILL analog to compare against, not a delta from stated field practice.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural, active voice, formal register — no counter-evidence observed in abstracts/headings sampled)
- Hedging and epistemic caution: FIELD DEFAULT (no deviation observable at abstract/heading level)
- Argumentation moves (motivation, positioning, contribution framing): FIELD DEFAULT where observable — abstracts state contributions directly (e.g., "systematically organizes Rothbard's scattered contributions," "argues that ... his position was actually unique rather than marginal")
- Results/statistical reporting norms: Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for the one empirical article sampled (Mulligan 2006 reports theory-consistent predictions and cites specific mechanisms; too thin a sample to detect a delta)

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Do not force a generic Introduction/Data/Results/Conclusion skeleton onto history-of-thought or exegetical submissions to this journal — descriptive, argument-specific headings are the observed norm for that paper type.
- Citing in footnotes instead of in-text with a Chicago-style bibliography will conflict with the explicit guideline that footnotes are explanatory only.
- Exceeding the 250-word abstract cap or the 10,000-word body limit (exclusive of references) violates a stated hard constraint.
- Leaving author-identifying information in the manuscript file defeats the double-blind process and will block review.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
