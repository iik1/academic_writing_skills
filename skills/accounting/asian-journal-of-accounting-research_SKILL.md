# Journal Writing Skill: Asian Journal of Accounting Research

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing (on behalf of Universitas Airlangga)
- **Scope:** Double-anonymous peer-reviewed journal on accounting and finance in developing countries. Published three times annually by Universitas Airlangga. Diamond open access (no author fees).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/ajar (full guidelines behind access wall)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 across Vol. 9–10 (2024–2025)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows canonical ACCOUNT structure: Introduction → Literature Review & Hypothesis Development → Methodology → Data Analysis/Results → Discussion & Conclusion → References. All empirical papers examined follow this sequence without exception.

### Heading Numbering
Arabic numerals with title-case headings (e.g., "2. Theoretical Framework and Hypothesis Development", "3.1 Sample and Data Collection"). Subsection levels use decimal notation (2.1, 2.2, 3.1.1).

### Abstract
**Structured abstract required.** Five-part framework: (1) Purpose, (2) Design/Methodology/Approach, (3) Findings, (4) Practical Implications, (5) Originality/Value. All eight articles examined use this mandatory structure. Stated as separate field components in the abstract presentation.

### Citations
Author-date in-text format throughout (confirmed via inspection). "Author (Year)" or "(Author Year)" depending on grammatical role. Matches FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Double-Blind Review
Manuscript must contain no author identifying information anywhere, including in figures, captions, and acknowledgments. Acknowledgments submitted as separate file.

### Other Formatting Notes
- AI copy-editing for grammar/clarity permitted (Emerald policy)
- Explicit instruction: for self-citations, use wording like "previous research has demonstrated" rather than "our previous research"
- No specific word/page limits published in accessible guidelines

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates.

### Abstract Format
**Delta: STRUCTURED MANDATORY.** FIELD_SKILL describes "short, structured summary" but does not specify five-part framework. AJAR requires explicit Purpose–Design–Findings–Implications–Originality structure. This is more prescriptive than typical ABS 4* journals and should be followed strictly.

### Heading Conventions
**Delta: ARABIC NUMERALS STANDARD.** FIELD_SKILL notes field variation between Roman numerals (TAR style) and Arabic numerals (JAE/JAR style). AJAR exclusively uses Arabic numerals with decimal subsection notation (1., 2.1, 2.1.1). This is consistent with field practice but standardized within AJAR.

### Section Naming
**Practice: "Data Analysis" or "Findings" used interchangeably with "Results".** Three of eight articles use "Data Analysis," one uses "Findings," others use "Results" or "Empirical Analysis." All convey the same content. FIELD_SKILL assumes "Results" as the standard section title; AJAR accepts variation here. No editorial preference detected in the guidelines or articles.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- **Voice and Register:** FIELD DEFAULT. First-person plural "we" dominates; active voice standard; high formality maintained throughout. Matches FIELD_SKILL exactly.
- **Confidence Calibration:** FIELD DEFAULT. Medium hedging appropriate to identification strength; "suggest," "indicate," "consistent with" used for mechanistic claims; direct declarative claims for well-identified results.
- **Citation Density:** FIELD DEFAULT. High in Introduction/Hypothesis Development; low to medium in Results; medium in Discussion.
- **Paraphrase vs. Quotation:** FIELD DEFAULT. Paraphrase is universal norm; direct quotation of prior scholarly work absent in all eight articles.
- **Statistical Reporting:** FIELD DEFAULT. Coefficients, t-statistics, and significance levels reported in prose alongside tables. Tables are primary vehicle; prose interprets.
- **Robustness and Sensitivity:** FIELD DEFAULT. All empirical papers examined include robustness checks (alternative specifications, lagged models, alternative estimators). Expected field norm.
- **Discussion and Conclusion:** FIELD DEFAULT. Merged format; restates findings, states contributions, acknowledges limitations explicitly.
- **Limitations Acknowledgment:** FIELD DEFAULT. Explicit limitations paragraph standard; honest naming of threats to identification expected.
- **Methodology Tense:** FIELD DEFAULT. Past tense throughout Methods/Data section. Sample construction and estimation described in past tense.

## Common Pitfalls
> Submission mistakes specific to THIS journal.

1. **Author Identification in Manuscript:** Most critical. Any author names, self-citations in first-person ("our previous work"), or identifiable institutional affiliation in text, figures, or captions will violate double-blind policy. Rewrite all self-references to passive or third-person, place acknowledgments in separate file.

2. **Abstract Not Structured:** Do not submit a prose abstract. Must follow explicit Purpose–Design–Findings–Implications–Originality framework with each component clearly delineated.

3. **Heading Numbering Inconsistency:** Use Arabic numerals throughout. Inconsistently switching between Roman and Arabic numerals, or omitting subsection numbers, signals unfamiliarity with journal style.

4. **Omitting Practical Implications Component:** Abstract must include Practical Implications or Originality/Value statement. This is mandatory in AJAR abstracts and signals contribution to practice/policy/stakeholder decision-making.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
