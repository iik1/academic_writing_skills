# Journal Writing Skill: Contemporary Economic Policy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell (on behalf of Western Economic Association International)
- **Scope:** Publishes empirical economic research and analysis on policy issues facing business, government, and policy-makers. Emphasis on accessible, evidence-based solutions to contemporary economic problems.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.weai.org/submit-a-manuscript
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10+ recent articles from vol. 42–43 (2024–2025)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- **Review process:** Double-blind; author identity must be masked throughout manuscript.
- **Section headings:** Roman numerals (I, II, III, etc.), upper case, centered.
- **Subheadings:** Upper and lower case, underlined, flush left.
- **Further subheadings:** Flush left, underlined, upper and lower case, followed by a period; text begins on same line.
- **Title page:** Submitted separately from masked manuscript; contains author affiliations, contact details.
- **Manuscripts:** Word or LaTeX formats acceptable.

### Length
- **Maximum:** 35 pages including references, footnotes, tables, figures; longer papers considered if warranted.
- **Title:** Maximum 75 characters and spaces.
- **Abstract:** Maximum 100 words (exact, enforced).

### Abstract
- **Word limit:** 100 words maximum.
- **Content:** Must clearly convey main conclusions, methods employed, and reasoning. Opening sentence should indicate the major conclusion.
- **Prohibitions:** No equations, diagrams, or footnotes in abstract.
- **Required elements:** Minimum 3 keywords and 3 JEL classification codes (principal code listed first).

### Citations
- **Format:** Author-year in-text citations (inherited from FIELD_SKILL).
- **Table/figure captions:** Provide full source information.
- **Copyright permissions:** Authors must obtain and provide copyright permission letters for copyrighted material.
- **Organization abbreviations:** Spell out fully on first mention with abbreviation in parentheses; use abbreviation only thereafter.

### Headings
- See Structure section above (Roman numerals, centered for main headings; specific formatting for sub-levels).

### Tables and Figures
- Full source information required in captions.
- Both common in recent articles; figures illustrate key policy outcomes and distributional results.

### Additional Requirements
- **Submission fees:** $90 for WEAI members, $100 for non-members.
- **Disclosure Statement:** All submissions (including revisions) must include Original Article Disclosure Statement; all coauthors provide separate statement.
- **Data availability:** Required statement describing data availability or absence of shared data. When data shared, include repository link and cite the shared data.
- **Appendices:** Supplementary material (3+ pages) posted online; main text appendices may be included within 35-page limit.
- **Readability:** Journal explicitly endorses Deirdre McCloskey's "Economical Writing" (1985, Economic Inquiry 23(2):187–222) as writing standard. Papers must be "readable and of broad interest."

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No equations, diagrams, or footnotes in abstract.
- Author identity must not appear in manuscript body during peer review.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Format and Length
**ENFORCED:** Contemporary Economic Policy mandates a strict 100-word abstract with no equations, diagrams, or footnotes. FIELD_SKILL does not specify abstract length or format; this is a hard constraint unique to this journal.

### Keywords and Classification
**ENFORCED:** Minimum 3 keywords and 3 JEL codes required on title page (principal code first). FIELD_SKILL does not address keyword or JEL code requirements.

### Section Heading Format
**ENFORCED:** Roman numerals (I, II, III) for main headings, centered; precise subheading formatting (centered, underlined, flush left hierarchy). FIELD_SKILL does not prescribe specific heading conventions.

### Review Process
**ENFORCED:** Double-blind review with mandatory author identity masking. FIELD_SKILL does not specify blind vs. single-blind convention.

### Emphasis on Readability and Accessibility
**GUIDELINE ONLY:** Journal explicitly endorses McCloskey's "Economical Writing" principles and requires papers to be "readable and of broad interest." This reflects a broader accessibility mandate than FIELD_SKILL, which emphasizes technical precision and density. While FIELD_SKILL specifies "high formality" and "technical vocabulary density," Contemporary Economic Policy weights readability and policy-maker accessibility more heavily—a potential tension. Authors should balance technical rigor with clearer exposition.

### Title Character Limit
**ENFORCED:** 75 characters and spaces (including spaces). This is a strict hard constraint not addressed in FIELD_SKILL.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (first-person plural "we" expected across empirical and analytical sentences).
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium-to-long sentences, high paragraph density, technical vocabulary appropriate to policy domain).
- **Argumentation pattern:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (research question, approach, preview of results, contributions to literature, roadmap expected).
- **Citation density:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (high in introduction, medium in methods, lower in results).
- **Hedging intensity:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (clear claims preferred; hedging used for external validity, causal interpretation caveats, and data limitations).
- **Statistical reporting:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (quantitative specificity, economic significance alongside statistical significance expected).
- **Conclusion length:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (shorter than introduction; synthesis of findings, implications, limitations, future directions).

---

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Abstract overrun or equation inclusion:** Manuscripts with abstracts exceeding 100 words or containing equations/diagrams/footnotes will be rejected at desk level. Count carefully; this is a hard stop.
2. **Missing or incomplete JEL codes and keywords:** Title page must include minimum 3 keywords and 3 JEL codes. Incomplete submissions will be returned without review.
3. **Unmasked author identity:** Even coauthor names in the manuscript body, acknowledgments acknowledging a specific grant or institution uniquely identifying the author(s), or self-citations without appropriate anonymization will violate double-blind review and cause desk rejection.
4. **Title exceeds character limit:** Titles over 75 characters (including spaces) are rejected; plan for this limit in early drafting.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
