# Journal Writing Skill: Industrielle Beziehungen

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Verlag Barbara Budrich / Nomos Verlag (Baden-Baden, since 2023)
- **Scope:** German industrial relations (Arbeitsbeziehungen), labour organization, workplace management, unions, works councils, collective bargaining, employment policy
- **Guidelines URL:** https://ojs.nomos-journals.de/index.php/IndBez/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 articles from Volume 30, Issue 2 (2023)
  - Holst & Niehoff: "Varianten der Mitbestimmung in der Corona-Krise" (mixed-methods, 5 typologies identified)
  - Flemming: "Mobiles Arbeiten im Spannungsfeld von Autonomie und Schutz" (qualitative, works council perspectives)
  - Menz & Rowitz: Care work and labour conflict (qualitative)
  - Huke: "Rassismus als verdecktes Strukturprinzip der Arbeitswelt" (theoretical/critical)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Must follow standard IMRaD-like structure: Introduction, Literature Review / Theoretical Foundation, Methods, Results/Analysis, Discussion, Conclusion. Sections should be clearly headed and logically organized. A separate introduction and literature review are expected (not merged).

### Length
**Manuscript:** Maximum 70,000 characters (including spaces), equivalent to approximately 10,000–12,000 words depending on spacing and paragraph formatting.

**Abstracts:** Maximum 1,500 characters each in German and English (separate abstracts required in both languages).

### Abstract
Dual-language abstracts required: separate abstracts in German and English, each maximum 1,500 characters. Must include **five keywords in each language** and **JEL classification descriptors** (for economic/labour relation topics). Abstracts should summarize the research question, methods, key findings, and contributions.

### Citations
**Style:** APA (author-date system). Examples:
- Narrative: "Freiling und Fichtner (2010) zeigen..."
- Parenthetical: "(Freiling & Fichtner, 2010; Hornung, Glaser, & Rousseau, 2010, S. 12)"

**Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs):** Mandatory for all sources where available. Use Crossref's reference lookup tool to obtain DOIs. Every reference in the bibliography must include a DOI if obtainable.

### Headings
Headings should be used for major section divisions (Introduction, Literature Review/Theoretical Framework, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion). Subheadings are acceptable for subsections within major sections (e.g., "Data Collection" under Methods). Use left-aligned formatting; avoid excessive nesting.

### Tables and Figures
- Must be submitted in editable formats (Word, Excel) with resolution of 300+ dpi for graphics
- Include numbered captions with clear textual references (e.g., "As shown in Table 1...")
- Figure captions must be legible when reduced to 13 cm column width
- Tables in Word format to allow journal layout adaptation
- All figures must be scalable

### Formatting Rules
- **Text alignment:** Left-aligned only; no justified text
- **Spacing:** 1.5 or 2-line spacing required
- **Emphasis:** Italics permitted for emphasis; avoid excessive use
- **Quotation marks:** Use typographic quotation marks (curved), not straight quotes
- **Em-dashes:** Use proper em-dashes, not hyphens
- **Automatic hyphenation:** Disable; no automatic word-breaking
- **Spacing for layout:** Prohibited; use paragraph formatting or tabs instead
- **Abbreviations:** Capitalize only for acronyms and specific terms; avoid generic abbreviations

### Anonymization (Double-Blind Peer Review)
Manuscripts must be **completely anonymized** for double-blind review. Submit two separate files:
1. **Anonymized manuscript:** Remove all author identification from content, footnotes, and metadata (file properties). No author name, institution, identifying details in acknowledgments.
2. **Title page (separate file):** Contains author name(s), affiliation(s), author contribution statement, and any conflict-of-interest declarations.

Failure to anonymize completely may result in desk rejection.

### Language and Gender-Sensitive Language
Manuscripts must be in **very good German or very good English** (native-level fluency expected). Journals value **gender-equal, gender-sensitive language treatment** (Geschlechtssensible Sprache). Avoid generic masculine forms (e.g., "Arbeiter" for mixed-gender groups). Use gender-neutral expressions or the **gender asterisk** (e.g., "Arbeitgeber*innen") to indicate inclusion of all genders. This is an explicit editorial requirement, not optional.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **No automatic hyphenation**
- **No spacing for layout purposes**
- **No generic masculine language** (non-negotiable for editorial approval)
- **No unacknowledged borrowing of text or ideas**
- **Articles already published or under review elsewhere** will be desk-rejected

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Character Limit Instead of Word Limit
Unlike FIELD_SKILL (which does not specify word limits), Industrielle Beziehungen uses a **strict character limit of 70,000 characters** rather than a page count or word count. This is measurably tighter than many economics/HRM journals (typically 8,000–10,000 words) and may require more concise abstract writing and tighter results sections.

**Delta classification:** ENFORCED (stated explicitly in guidelines)

### Mandatory Dual-Language Abstracts with JEL Codes
FIELD_SKILL does not specify abstract language or format. Industrielle Beziehungen **requires separate abstracts in German and English** (each 1,500 characters max) plus JEL classification descriptors. This is unusual for non-economics journals and reflects the journal's hybrid economics-labour relations positioning.

**Delta classification:** ENFORCED (stated explicitly; standard practice observed in spot-checked articles)

### Explicit Gender-Sensitive Language Requirement
FIELD_SKILL mentions no explicit gender language policy. Industrielle Beziehungen **explicitly and non-negotiably requires gender-sensitive language**, including avoidance of generic masculine forms and use of the gender asterisk (*). This is an editorial requirement that affects acceptability at review stage, not merely a stylistic suggestion.

**Delta classification:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines and reflected in journal policy; high editorial emphasis)

### Mandatory DOI Inclusion
FIELD_SKILL does not specify DOI requirements. Industrielle Beziehungen **mandates DOI inclusion for all sources where available**, requiring authors to verify DOIs via Crossref. This is stricter than field norms and reflects a shift toward linked, discoverable scholarship.

**Delta classification:** ENFORCED (stated explicitly in guidelines)

### Double-Blind Anonymization Requirement
FIELD_SKILL notes no explicit anonymization protocol. Industrielle Beziehungen uses **strict double-blind peer review with complete anonymization**. Authors must submit two separate files (anonymized manuscript + separate title page), and removal of all identifying metadata is mandatory. Failure to anonymize completely results in desk rejection.

**Delta classification:** ENFORCED (stated explicitly; desk-reject consequence noted)

### Tighter Abstract Length
FIELD_SKILL does not specify abstract word counts. Industrielle Beziehungen limits abstracts to **1,500 characters each language** (approximately 250–300 words in English, depending on German–English asymmetry). This is tighter than ABS 4*/4 journal norms, which often permit 250–350 words.

**Delta classification:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines)

## Common Pitfalls
> Submission mistakes specific to THIS journal, evidenced in guidelines and practice.

1. **Incomplete anonymization or failure to submit separate title page.** Many first-time submitters forget to remove author names from acknowledgments, remove metadata, or submit the title page separately. This triggers desk rejection. Always use a fresh copy of your manuscript for anonymization; have a colleague check for identifying details.

2. **Generic masculine language or gender-insensitive phrasing.** The journal's explicit gender policy means submitted articles using only masculine nouns ("Arbeiter," "Manager," "Arbeitgeber") without gender-inclusive modifications may be rejected outright by editors as not meeting journal standards. Use the gender asterisk or neutral plurals consistently throughout.

3. **Missing or incomplete DOIs in references.** Authors who have not verified each reference for a DOI (especially older sources) may be asked to provide corrected reference lists. Check Crossref or your library database before submitting.

4. **Exceeding character limits.** The 70,000-character limit is strict. Exceedances of more than 5% may trigger desk rejection or forced revision. Use tools to count characters including spaces; budget generously for figures and tables if embedded.

5. **English submissions with non-native fluency.** While the journal publishes in English, submissions must meet "very good English" standard. Articles with grammatical errors, awkward phrasing, or unclear expression are more likely to be desk-rejected or given a revise-and-resubmit with language concerns flagged. Non-native speakers should have manuscripts reviewed by a professional editor or native speaker before submission.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
