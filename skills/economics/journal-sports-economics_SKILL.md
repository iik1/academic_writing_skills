# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Sports Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publications (official journal of the North American Association of Sports Economists, NAASE)
- **Scope:** Quantitative research on the economics of sport — professional and amateur leagues, labour markets for athletes, competitive balance, attendance and TV demand, betting markets, sports facilities and mega-events, gender and discrimination in sport, and the economic impact of teams and events.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/jse
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 15 in depth, plus full TOC enumeration Vol 22–27 (2021–2026). See Sources.

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Required components in order: Title page, Abstract, Keywords, Text, **Notes**, References, Tables, Figures, Appendices. The standalone **Notes** block before References implies **endnotes rather than footnotes** — a delta from the field norm of footnotes for supplementary material. Body section order is not mandated; observed papers follow the field empirical IMRaD norm (Introduction → Data/Background → Empirical Strategy → Results → Conclusion). **ENFORCED** (component list / endnotes); body order is FIELD DEFAULT.

### Length
**Manuscripts normally should not exceed 30 single-sided, double-spaced typewritten pages** (12-pt Times New Roman, 1-inch margins, all pages numbered). This is a page-based cap (~7,500–9,000 words including everything), generous but real. The field baseline does not specify length. **ENFORCED.**

### Abstract
Unstructured plain prose, **maximum 150 words**, on a separate page carrying the title but **no author names** (anonymous review). **ENFORCED.** Observed practice runs ~90–180 words; write to the 150-word cap.

**4–5 keywords** required on the same page. **ENFORCED.** **JEL codes are not mandated by the guidelines** but appear in roughly half the sample (see Deltas).

### Citations
**APA 7th edition** — author-year in text, reference list alphabetical by author surname then oldest-to-newest by year. This is a specific style delta: the field default is Chicago-style author-year. Functionally still parenthetical author-year, so citation *density and voice* inherit the field norm; only the *formatting* differs. **ENFORCED** (APA 7 is the stated house style).

### Headings
APA leveled-heading scheme specified (five levels: L1 centred bold, L2 flush-left bold, L3 indented bold run-in, L4 indented bold italic run-in, L5 indented italic run-in). **Headings are not numbered** under APA — a delta from the field norm of Roman/Arabic numbered sections. In practice some authors still number sections. **GUIDELINE ONLY** (APA scheme stated; numbering practice varies).

### Tables and Figures
Each table needs a clear, concise title; figures at **300 dpi** at submission; authors secure reproduction permissions. Otherwise FIELD DEFAULT.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No simultaneous submission elsewhere; no prior publication.
- MS Word required (not the 2007 format).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

> Only dimensions where this journal deviates from the field baseline.

### Voice and Register
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** First-person plural ("we find", "we estimate") is the near-universal default; even sole-authored papers use "we"; impersonal voice is dispreferred.

**This journal:** "We"/"our" is the majority voice (~10 of 15 read — e.g. NBA salary distribution 27/5, NBA bargaining 27/3, skills-vs-luck 26/8, EPL incentives 26/7, Iron Curtain referees 25/1, Taylor Swift/Chiefs 27/3), but **impersonal third-person is clearly accepted in practice** — "This paper examines…", "This study finds…", "The research examines…" (StarCraft peer effects 27/1, boxing reinjury 26/2, sports-broadcasting theory 27/5, football-betting 27/5). Both registers coexist without penalty.

**Classification:** RELAXATION (ABS 2). The field "we" norm is not enforced here; impersonal exposition is fine. Do not mix registers within one paper. As an ABS 2 outlet, JSE relaxes the ABS 4*/4 baseline but does not redefine it — aim for "we" if in doubt, but impersonal is safe.

### Abstract Length
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** Length left to the journal (~150–250 words typical in econ).
**This journal:** Hard **150-word cap**, unstructured. **ENFORCED.**

### JEL Classification (Practice)
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** JEL codes are a journal-specific matter, not a field requirement.
**This journal:** JEL codes are **not required by the guidelines** but appear in roughly half the articles read (e.g. 27/5 NBA salaries: J52/J31/Z22/Z21/C32; 26/7 EPL; 26/5 RAE; 25/1 referees; 25/3 injury policy; 24/8 second-tier stars). When present, the sports codes **Z20/Z21/Z22/Z28** are near-ubiquitous as the primary classification. **PRACTICE ONLY** (low-to-moderate prevalence; include them, lead with a Z2x code, but their absence is not a rejection trigger).

### Structural Deviations
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** Full-length research articles with appendix for proofs/robustness.
**This journal:** Routinely publishes **short formats** — research notes ("A Note on…"), comments and replies (e.g. the 2022 UFC comment/reply exchange, 23/3), obituaries/In Memoriam, and themed **special issues** (e.g. the James Quirk combat-sport issue 26/2, the Title IX issue 25/8, the 20th-anniversary issue 23/6). Short empirical notes (e.g. Taylor Swift/Chiefs 27/3, Caitlin Clark superstar 27/3) sit comfortably alongside full articles. **PRACTICE ONLY** — a tightly-scoped short paper is a legitimate submission type here, unlike at the longer-form ABS 4*/4 outlets.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- Section architecture (empirical IMRaD, results previewed in intro): FIELD DEFAULT
- Citation density by section (high in intro, lower in results): FIELD DEFAULT
- Statistical reporting (quantitative, tables primary, magnitudes + significance): FIELD DEFAULT
- Identification/robustness expectations: FIELD DEFAULT
- Hedging intensity (low; clear claims preferred): FIELD DEFAULT
- Roadmap paragraph and results preview in introduction: FIELD DEFAULT
- Paraphrase over direct quotation: FIELD DEFAULT
- Spelling/dialect: no journal mandate — FIELD DEFAULT (US spelling dominates the sample)

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Wrong reference style.** JSE is **APA 7th**, not the Chicago/AER-style author-year economists default to. Convert the reference list (ampersands, sentence-case article titles, DOI formatting) before submitting.
2. **Over-long abstract.** The 150-word cap is firm and the abstract page must be anonymous (title only, no names) — strip author identifiers for review.
3. **Footnotes vs endnotes.** The guidelines route notes to a "Notes" block before the references; supply endnotes, not bottom-of-page footnotes.
4. **Blowing the page cap.** 30 double-spaced pages is the working limit; a long working paper with extensive appendices must be trimmed or moved online.
5. **Forgetting the sports JEL code.** Not required, but reviewers and indexers expect a Z2x (Z20/Z21/Z22/Z28) lead code when JEL codes are included; cap keywords at 4–5.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
