# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Sustainable Finance and Investment

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis
- **ISSN:** 2043-0795
- **Scope:** Empirical and conceptual research on sustainable investment, ESG integration, green finance, climate finance, carbon markets, sustainable development, and socially responsible investment. Bridges finance theory with environmental, social, and governance considerations. Welcomes interdisciplinary perspectives from finance, economics, accounting, policy, and management scholarship.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> Based on Taylor & Francis standard practices for finance journals and observable publication patterns.

### Structure
Follows standard FIELD_SKILL article structure. Common sections: Introduction, Literature Review (integrated or standalone), Methodology/Data, Results/Findings, Discussion/Conclusion, References. The journal publishes both empirical and conceptual (theoretical/review) articles, so structure varies slightly by article type.

### Length
No explicit word limit found in accessible guidelines. Taylor & Francis finance journals typically allow 8,000–15,000 words (text, tables, figures, notes, appendices combined). Some variation permitted for conceptual vs. empirical work.

### Abstract
No explicit abstract word limit found. Following Taylor & Francis standard: 150–250 words is typical for this field baseline. Structured as a single paragraph (not explicitly required by journal, but is field norm). Keywords and JEL codes listed separately in metadata on published page, not in abstract body itself.

### Citations
Author-year parenthetical format (FIELD_SKILL norm). Confirmed via EndNote output style documentation: "Journal of Sustainable Finance & Investment" uses author-year style based on T&F Standard.

### Headings
Follow FIELD_SKILL conventions: Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3, 2.1, 2.2). No explicit deviation observed. Headings should be clear and not excessively numbered.

### Tables and Figures
Follow FIELD_SKILL conventions: Extensive footnotes defining variables, samples, and standard-error clustering (for empirical tables). Tables are primary vehicle for results presentation.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None found in accessible guidelines. Journal welcomes interdisciplinary work and diverse methodologies (quantitative, qualitative, mixed, bibliometric).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Scope and Topic Emphasis
This journal is more narrowly scoped than the broader FINANCE field baseline. The field baseline is derived from top-tier general finance journals (JF, JFE, RFS). JSFI is lower-ranked (ABS 1 vs. ABS 4*/4 for the baseline) and explicitly themed around sustainable finance, ESG, and environmental/social/governance dimensions of financial decision-making. Articles that do not address sustainability themes, ESG integration, green finance, climate finance, or socially responsible investment are less likely to be accepted, even if methodologically rigorous. This is not a *stylistic* delta but a *topical* filter upstream of submission.

### Methodological Diversity
The baseline emphasizes rigorous empirical identification in leading finance journals. JSFI publishes empirical, conceptual (theoretical), literature review, and bibliometric studies with equal standing. Review articles and conceptual pieces are given substantial space (not relegated to special issues). This represents a broader methodological scope than the ABS 4*/4 baseline, which skews heavily toward novel empirical findings with strong identification.

### Citation Density
No systematic deviation observed. However, given the journal's interdisciplinary scope (finance, economics, accounting, policy, management, geography, sociology), citation density may include references outside core finance journals. The baseline is derived from journals with dense, inward-focused citation patterns; JSFI authors cite more liberally across adjacent fields (environmental policy, law, geography, sociology).

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Topical mismatch.** Submitting empirically rigorous finance papers that do not engage with sustainability/ESG dimensions risks desk rejection. Ensure the paper's contribution is framed in terms of sustainable investment, climate finance, ESG integration, or related sustainability themes.
2. **Narrow methodological scope.** The journal values diverse methodologies (empirical, conceptual, literature review, bibliometric). Do not assume the journal only publishes econometric studies; conceptual or policy-focused papers are welcome if they advance understanding of sustainable finance practice or theory.
3. **Insufficient interdisciplinary perspective.** The editorial board includes scholars from finance, geography, law, policy, accountancy, sociology, and ethics. Papers that engage with only finance literature and ignore relevant policy, legal, or social science perspectives may appear narrow. Broaden the conceptual framing.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
