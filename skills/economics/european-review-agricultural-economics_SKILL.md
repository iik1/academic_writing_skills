# Journal Writing Skill: European Review of Agricultural Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press (for the European Agricultural and Applied Economics Publications Foundation / EAAE)
- **Scope:** Innovative theoretical and applied research in agricultural, food, and resource economics with a European focus — farm production, agri-food markets and trade, rural development, agri-environmental and CAP policy, consumer behaviour, and sustainable resource use.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://academic.oup.com/erae/pages/General_Instructions
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 16 (Vol 52–53, 2025–2026). See Sources.

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Numbered sections with headings; **maximum three grades of headings**. No fixed section order mandated. Observed structures follow the field empirical norm, often with a named conceptual section: e.g. Introduction → Conceptual/Theoretical Framework (or Background/Previous Studies) → Data/Methodology → Results → Discussion → Conclusion(s). "Concluding remarks" and "Policy implications and conclusions" are common closings. **ENFORCED** (numbering); otherwise FIELD DEFAULT.

Author names/affiliations on the **first page only** (anonymous review); acknowledgements and funding as a first-page footnote. **ENFORCED.**

### Length
**Maximum 8,000 words or word-equivalents, INCLUDING abstract, tables, figures, and graphs** (≈475 words per printed page for display items). Shorter articles explicitly welcomed. Tighter than ABS 4*/4 econ outlets, and the inclusive count penalises table/figure-heavy papers. **ENFORCED.**

### Abstract
Unstructured plain prose, **maximum 100 words**, conveying main conclusions, methods, and line of reasoning. Substantially shorter than the field-typical 150–250. **ENFORCED (guideline cap).** Observed practice across the sample ran ~80–150 words — write to the 100-word target.

**Maximum 5 keywords** below the abstract. **JEL classification codes required** — present in 15 of 16 articles read (e.g. Q12, Q15, Q18, C-series). **ENFORCED** (JEL/keywords are a journal requirement; FIELD_SKILL does not mandate them).

### Citations
Author-year, **with page number for direct/specific references**: `(Swinnen, 1997: 12)`, `(Zeller et al., 1997)`. The colon-page form is a journal convention. References collected at manuscript end. **ENFORCED** (page-number form: **GUIDELINE ONLY** — inconsistently applied in practice).

### Language and Spelling
**British English throughout** — confirmed in 16/16 articles ("behaviour", "organisation", "optimisation", "recognised", "favour", "per cent"). **Single quotation marks**. Foreign words need parenthetical translation; acronyms spelled out at first use. **ENFORCED** (dialect: a real delta — FIELD_SKILL is American-leaning).

### Headings
Numbered; table column headings and figure captions in **sentence case (initial capital only)**. **ENFORCED.**

### Tables and Figures
- **No vertical lines** in tables; computer printouts unacceptable.
- Consecutive numbering; positioned near first citation.
- Figures: TIFF preferred, 600 dpi (line) / 300 dpi (colour/halftone).
- **Alt-text required** for every figure, placed directly under the legend (prefixed "Alt text:"). Uncommon in economics; an OUP accessibility requirement. **ENFORCED.**

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Computer-printout tables; vertical table lines; >3 heading grades; abstracts >100 words; >5 keywords.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

> Only dimensions where this journal deviates from the field baseline.

### Voice and Register
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** First-person plural ("we find", "we estimate") is near-universal; even sole-authored papers use "we"; impersonal voice is dispreferred.

**This journal:** "We" is the majority voice (~12 of 16 read), but two other registers are clearly accepted in practice:
- **Impersonal third-person** — "This study investigates…", "This article contributes…", "Results revealed…" (seen in several consumer-behaviour and applied-method papers, e.g. organic health halo 53/1/30, fish-auction 52/4/759, pesticide-taxation 52/4/818).
- **First-person singular "I"** in sole-authored papers ("I provide…", "my identification strategy" — Fertile Grounds 52/4/617), where FIELD_SKILL would expect "we".

**Classification:** RELAXATION (ABS 3). All three registers are acceptable; impersonal/"I" are not penalised here as they would be at ABS 4*/4 econ outlets. Do not mix registers within one paper. Authors from agronomy/consumer-science backgrounds need not adopt "we".

### Abstract Length
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** ~150–250 words, unstructured.
**This journal:** ~100-word cap. Authors must compress the contribution/method/results preview aggressively. **ENFORCED.**

### British English
**FIELD_SKILL norm:** No dialect consensus; American dominates top US journals.
**This journal:** British spelling and single quotes mandatory; confirmed throughout the sample. **ENFORCED.**

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Over-long abstract.** The 100-word cap is roughly half the field norm; abstracts ported from a working paper will be far too long. Rewrite to ~100 words covering conclusion + method + reasoning.
2. **Blowing the inclusive word cap.** 8,000 words counts the abstract, tables, *and* figures (~475 words/printed page). A table-heavy paper that fits a 10,000-word exclusive limit elsewhere must be trimmed.
3. **American spelling / double quotes.** Run a British-English and single-quote pass; this is an explicit, consistently observed requirement.
4. **Missing JEL codes / >5 keywords / vertical table lines / figure alt-text.** Easy mechanical rejections — include JEL codes, cap keywords at 5, strip vertical lines, add "Alt text:" under each figure.
5. **Forgetting anonymity.** Keep author identity to the first page only; scrub self-identifying references for review.
6. **Submitting a review article cold.** Review Articles require a pre-submission inquiry to the editorial office.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
