# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Economic Cooperation and Development

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Statistical, Economic and Social Research and Training Centre for Islamic Countries (SESRIC)
- **Scope:** Applied development economics for OIC (Organisation of Islamic Cooperation) member states and other developing countries, with special interest in papers on economic/technical cooperation among developing countries.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sesric.org/publications-jecd.php (author guidelines page); submission portal at https://jecd.sesric.org/author/
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 24 titles/abstracts across 3 issues (Vol. 42 Nos. 1-3, 2021); 2 full abstracts read in detail (Shababi et al. on cultural diversity and export complexity, Zenodo 17051648; Al Samman & Akkas on COVID-19 and GCC stock markets, Zenodo 17052978). No full-text articles were accessible — publisher site and Zenodo mirrors provide only abstracts/metadata; DOI links resolve to Zenodo record pages, not full PDFs. This is a good-faith sample for an ABS 1 journal with a thin public access chain.

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section list beyond a title page and abstract; guidelines do not prescribe an Introduction/Data/Methods/Results/Conclusion sequence explicitly, but observed titles/abstracts are consistent with FIELD_SKILL's empirical-paper structure.

### Length
Maximum 8,000 words. Manuscript must be double-spaced with generous margins and consecutively numbered pages. This is a firm cap well below what top-tier ECON outlets tolerate — FIELD_SKILL carries no length ceiling.

### Abstract
Single unstructured abstract of maximum 200 words, placed on the first page alongside title, author names, affiliations, and corresponding-author contact details. No mention of required JEL codes in the guidelines, and none were observed on sampled article records — see Practice Only note below.

### Citations
Author-year reference list required, double-spaced, at manuscript end — consistent with FIELD_SKILL. Journal specifies exact reference formatting patterns:
- Books: `Author (Year), Title, Publisher, Location`
- Chapters: `Author (Year), "Chapter Title," in Editor(s) (eds), Book Title, Publisher; Location`
- Articles: `Author (Year), "Article Title," Journal Name, Volume(Number), pages`
Article and chapter titles are quoted; book/journal titles are italicized.

### Headings
Not specified beyond general structure; no explicit numbering convention given.

### Tables and Figures
All diagrams, charts, and graphs must be black and white (no color), labeled as "Figures" with consecutive Arabic numerals. Tables must be kept minimal, containing only essential data, each with an Arabic numeral, heading, and in-text reference. This is stricter than FIELD_SKILL, which has no color/minimalism restriction.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No simultaneous multi-journal submission. All relevant prior publications must be cited. A signed Copyright Transfer Form (original signatures) is required from all authors before publication, submitted by email, fax, or mail.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm on the abstracts sampled (concise, direct claims, e.g., "cultural diversity in the MENA countries has a positive and significant effect on export complexity").

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm where visible — findings stated with named methods and directional/magnitude claims (e.g., phase-specific effects by country in the GCC stock market study).

### Citation Density
Not assessable from abstracts alone; no delta identified.

### Structural Deviations
None beyond the Hard Constraints above (8,000-word cap, 200-word unstructured abstract, black-and-white-only figures). PRACTICE ONLY: sampled abstracts do not display JEL classification codes, unlike common practice at higher-ABS-tier ECON journals; this is not stated in the guidelines as a prohibition, so authors should follow submission-portal instructions if JEL codes are requested there.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY: Papers in the sample are near-uniformly single-country or small-cross-country applied panel/time-series studies (e.g., MENA, GCC, D-8, Sub-Saharan Africa, ECO member states) using standard applied econometric techniques (panel regression, GARCH, bootstrap panel causality, AHP-TOPSIS) rather than novel identification strategies or theoretical contributions. This reflects the journal's development-economics/OIC-cooperation scope rather than a stated stylistic rule — RELAXATION of the frontier-identification emphasis in FIELD_SKILL, consistent with its ABS 1 tier, not a redefinition of the baseline.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 8,000-word cap or writing a structured/long abstract — the journal requires a single unstructured abstract of 200 words maximum.
- Submitting color figures — all diagrams, charts, and graphs must be black and white.
- Overloaded tables — the journal explicitly asks for minimal tables containing only essential data, a stricter norm than typical FIELD_SKILL practice of comprehensive results tables.
- Omitting the Copyright Transfer Form with original signatures, required before publication.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
