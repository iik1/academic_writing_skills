# Journal Writing Skill: American Journal of Health Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** University of Chicago Press
- **Scope:** Forum for in-depth analysis of institutional health care systems and individual health behaviors; publishes theoretical and applied research
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/ajhe/instruct (paywalled; details below from accessible sources)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** Limited accessibility (see Sources section); primary data from author guidelines and journal metadata

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscripts must be arranged in the following order:
1. Title page
2. Abstract (strict limit: 200 words)
3. Keywords
4. Classification codes
5. Text (with numbered or lettered section headings)
6. Footnotes
7. References
8. Appendixes
9. Tables & Figures (as separate files)

**Note:** FIELD_SKILL specifies empirical papers typically follow Introduction → Background → Data → Methods → Results → Robustness → Conclusion. AJHE does not mandate this structure explicitly; section organization should follow your paper's logic but must be clearly defined with heading hierarchy.

### Manuscript Format
- **Required format:** Microsoft Word (.doc/.docx) OR LaTeX (.tex). PDFs are NOT acceptable.
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced throughout, including footnotes, tables, and figure legends.
- **Submission:** Via Editorial Manager (https://www.editorialmanager.com/ajhe/default.aspx).
- **Submission fee:** None.

### Length
- **Abstract:** Strict limit of 200 words.
- **Overall manuscript length:** Not specified in accessible guidelines (typical economic journals: 10,000–15,000 words including text and tables; confirm with instructions before submission).

### Abstract
- **Word limit:** 200 words (strict).
- **Format:** Not specified as structured or unstructured; standard practice in health economics is a single paragraph covering motivation, methods, key findings, and implications.

### Citations
- **Style:** Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition, Author-Date system (parenthetical citations in text: Author-Year).
- **Footnotes:** Permitted for substantive content only. Footnotes must not contain purely bibliographic material (citations belong in the reference list, not footnotes).
- **Reference list:** Chicago author-date style; use bibliographic software with "Chicago Manual Author-Date" selected for consistency.

### Headings
Three levels of headings are permitted:
- **Level One:** Headline Style (e.g., "Introduction" or "Methods and Data")
- **Level Two:** ALL CAPS (e.g., "DATA SOURCES")
- **Level Three:** Run-in Heading in ALL CAPS (embedded in paragraph text, followed by period)

No explicit guidance on numbering; standard practice is Arabic numerals (1. Introduction, 2. Methods, etc.) or the section names without numbers.

### Tables and Figures
- **Submission format:** Tables and figures must be provided as separate files, not embedded in the main manuscript.
- **Table notes:** Should explain variable definitions, standard error clustering, and sample restrictions (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).
- **Figure captions:** Must be descriptive and self-contained.

### Copyright and Disclosure
- **Copyright assignment:** Upon acceptance, authors must sign a publication agreement assigning copyright to the American Society of Health Economics (ASHE).
- **Disclosure forms:** A complete Disclosure Statement Form is required for each author.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Do NOT submit as PDF; only Word or LaTeX accepted.
- Do NOT embed tables and figures in the main document; provide as separate files.
- Do NOT use endnotes; only footnotes allowed, and only for substantive material.
- Do NOT include purely bibliographic content in footnotes.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates from the ECON field baseline.

### Manuscript Format Constraint
**ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL makes no specification about submission file format (Word vs. PDF vs. other). AJHE explicitly rejects PDF and accepts only Word (.doc/.docx) or LaTeX (.tex). This is a hard constraint not typical of all ECON outlets, which often accept PDFs or are flexible.

### Double Spacing
**ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL does not specify spacing requirements (these are typically publisher/submission-system matters, outside the field norm). AJHE requires double spacing throughout the document, including footnotes, tables, and figure legends. This is stricter than typical top-tier practice.

### Footnote Restrictions
**ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL notes that footnotes carry supplementary material and qualifications, not primary citations. AJHE goes further: footnotes must be substantive and cannot contain purely bibliographic material. This is a tighter restriction on footnote use than FIELD_SKILL describes.

### Heading Levels
**ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL makes no specification about the number of heading levels. AJHE permits exactly three levels with specific formatting conventions (Headline Style, ALL CAPS, Run-in ALL CAPS). This is a concrete, journal-specific constraint.

### Abstract Length
**ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL does not specify abstract word limits. AJHE enforces a strict 200-word limit on abstracts. This is shorter than many economics journals (which often allow 150–250 words) but represents a clear constraint.

### Structural Flexibility
**RELAXATION.** FIELD_SKILL specifies near-universal empirical paper structure (Introduction → Background → Data → Methods → Results → Robustness → Conclusion) as the expected norm at ABS 4*/4. AJHE does not mandate this structure; it only requires clearly defined sections with proper heading hierarchy. Papers may deviate from the canonical structure if organization is transparent. This is a relaxation of the field norm, consistent with AJHE's interdisciplinary focus (it publishes theoretical, applied, and policy-focused work alongside empirical studies).

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Submitting in PDF format.** AJHE accepts only Word or LaTeX. Submissions in PDF will be desk-rejected.
2. **Embedding tables and figures in the main document.** AJHE requires these as separate files. Non-compliance may trigger a revision request before review.
3. **Using single spacing or inconsistent spacing.** Double spacing is required throughout, including footnotes and table legends. Failure to comply may result in a request to revise formatting before review.
4. **Placing citations in footnotes instead of using parenthetical citations.** Chicago author-date system expects citations in the text with a reference list. Purely bibliographic footnotes (e.g., "See also Smith [Year]") violate AJHE policy.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
