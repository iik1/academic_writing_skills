# Journal Writing Skill: Critical Finance Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Now Publishers Inc.
- **Scope:** Publishes research across all areas of financial economics. Distinctive editorial identity: explicitly positions itself to "take more risks" and attract "more controversial and provocative papers" than peer journals, and runs recurring special-volume calls soliciting direct replications/reanalyses of seminal finance studies.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.nowpublishers.com/Journal/AuthorInstructions/CFR ; https://cfr.ivo-welch.org/submit/ (editor's own submission page, partially accessible)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (front matter/abstracts + 1 full-text editorial): "Do Factors Matter?" (Loughran, 2025); "Bidder and Target Size Effects in M&A Are Not Driven by Overconfidence or Agency Problems" (Schneider & Spalt, 2025); "Intangible Value" (Eisfeldt, Kim & Papanikolaou, 2022); "Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing" (Chen & Zimmermann, 2022); "Liquidity Risk?" (Pontiff & Singla, 2019, title/abstract level); and full text of Welch's "Introduction" editorial to the CFR liquidity reanalysis special volume (2019).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No rigid required-section template is published; the journal instead publishes an explicit "initial style guidelines" note described as "intentionally modest." Title page must separately list title, author name(s), and affiliation(s), with acknowledgments placed as the article's first footnote on the title page (not embedded in the main text).

### Length
No hard word/page cap is stated on the primary instructions page beyond generic submission-system bounds (up to ~100 pages accepted by the system). No explicit target length is enforced editorially.

### Abstract
Abstract capped at approximately 150 words — noticeably shorter than the general finance-journal norm and worth enforcing explicitly when drafting for this outlet.

### Citations
Author-year, consistent with FIELD_SKILL. One CFR-specific footnote convention: give each author's first name at first mention (in text or footnote); use last name only on subsequent mentions unless a last-name collision requires disambiguation. References list alphabetically by last name, then by year.

### Headings
No journal-specific heading convention beyond FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Tables and Figures
Number tables and figures consecutively across the whole paper (Table 1, Table 2, ...), NOT restarted or keyed by section number. Do not embed figures/tables inline in the text; instead insert a text placeholder/tag immediately after the paragraph that first references the item, with the actual figure/table to follow.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Replicability is a stated, hard editorial policy, not a generic aspiration:** "It is the policy of the CFR not to publish papers that cannot be replicated anywhere else in the world." The paper itself, not just a supplementary appendix, must contain enough descriptive detail for independent replication.
- **Data "snippets" requirement:** authors must include the first few observations that entered each reported statistic/regression, so a reader can sanity-check inputs directly from the paper.
- **$400 submission fee, "no exceptions."**
- Submissions go through a suitability screening step before being sent to formal peer review.
- No formal appeals process; a rejected paper may only be resubmitted if substantially improved.
- Editor explicitly states CFR is "not beholden to a goal of minimizing turnaround time" — standard review targets ~4-6 months, but the dedicated "Critique" track (non-anonymous author response plus anonymous referee evaluation) can run 9-12 months.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
The editor's own editorial prose (Welch, 2019 special-volume introduction) is markedly more informal and personal than typical ABS 4*/4 finance writing: first-person singular commentary ("My own (current) perspectives are as follows..."), asides in parentheses, and even a self-deprecating footnote ("pardon thy self-citation"). This reflects the journal's stated identity as more willing to host candid, opinionated scientific debate. This is PRACTICE ONLY at the editorial/commentary level (introductions to special issues), not confirmed as a norm for standard empirical articles, whose abstracts read as standard, unhedged FIELD_SKILL-consistent prose. Flag as low-prevalence outside editorial matter.

### Structural Deviations
CFR runs a distinctive, named article type not covered by FIELD_SKILL: direct **replication/reanalysis papers** and paired **"Critique" + original-author "Response"** pairs, published as linked pieces (e.g., Kazumori et al. 2019 replication alongside Acharya and Pedersen's 2019 response in the same special volume). Authors targeting CFR for a replication or critique piece should expect this two-sided, non-anonymous response structure rather than the standard single-manuscript research-article format. GUIDELINE ONLY / journal-specific article type, not applicable to standard empirical submissions.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Replicability is operationalized more literally here than the generic "replication package expected" norm in FIELD_SKILL: CFR asks for embedded data snippets inside the paper itself, and its explicit rejection criterion is non-replicability, not merely incomplete disclosure. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; consistent with the journal's founding rationale as articulated in the Welch 2019 editorial, which centers entirely on reproducibility of prior published results).

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing a 200-250 word abstract as if for an ABS 4 journal — CFR's ~150-word cap is stricter than the field norm.
- Numbering tables/figures by section (e.g., Table 2.1) instead of sequentially through the whole paper.
- Treating the replication package as sufficient for the replicability requirement — CFR wants the replication information (including data snippets) inside the paper text itself.
- Omitting first names of cited authors on first mention in text/footnotes.
- Assuming a fast turnaround; CFR explicitly deprioritizes speed relative to thoroughness, especially for Critique submissions.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
