# Journal Writing Skill: Cross Cultural and Strategic Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** IB&AREA
- **Publisher:** Emerald Group Publishing
- **Scope:** High-quality cross-cultural and strategic management research in the global context, with special interest in theoretical, empirical, and multidisciplinary papers investigating new ideas at the micro-meso-macro levels.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/ccsm (public site; full author guidelines behind access barrier)
- **Field baseline:** international-business-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 (Kumar et al. 2021 bibliometric review; Lee 2016 author version; editorial examples; structural examples from search results)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Empirical and conceptual papers follow the canonical IB&AREA order (introduction, theory, methods/design, results/findings, discussion, conclusion) but with **mandatory structured abstract** required before the introduction. All papers must include an abstract formatted per Emerald's structured template (see Abstract section below).

### Length
Manuscripts must be **7,000–12,000 words in total length**, including references and appendices. This is a firm constraint stated in submission guidelines.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED.** Abstracts must use Emerald's structured format, not FIELD_SKILL's unstructured 150–250 word model. 

**Structure (4–7 mandatory/optional headings):**
- **Purpose:** State the research question or contribution (required).
- **Design/Methodology/Approach:** Describe study design, sample, methods (required).
- **Findings:** Report results, key outcomes, or main contributions (required).
- **Research Limitations/Implications:** Discuss boundaries of scope and implications for future research (optional; may omit if not applicable).
- **Practical Implications:** Discuss business, managerial, or policy relevance (optional).
- **Social Implications:** Discuss broader societal impact (optional).
- **Originality/Value:** Summarize the novel contribution and academic or practical value (required).

**Total word limit: 250 words maximum (including all sections and keywords).** No bullets or direct references in abstract. Keywords list (typically 4–6 terms) may be presented as a separate line after the abstract.

### Citations
Author-date parenthetical style (Harvard/APA standard). No deviation from FIELD_SKILL on citation mechanics; Emerald's structured abstract is the main delta.

### Headings
**ENFORCED.** First-level headings must be in **bold**. Second-level (and lower) headings in **medium italics**. This is Emerald's formal requirement across all journals, including CCSM. FIELD_SKILL does not specify heading formatting, so this is a journal-level addition.

### Tables and Figures
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (tables and figures essential for results reporting, with narrative prose interpretation). Emerald journals do not impose distinctive table/figure rules beyond standard academic formatting.

### Explicit Prohibitions
**AI policy (ENFORCED).** Copywriting using generative AI tools to generate new material is **not permitted**. Copy-editing using AI to improve structure and clarity **is permitted**. Any breach detected during review may result in desk rejection or request to revise before review.

**Ethical requirements (ENFORCED).** Any work submitted without a suitable ethical statement and details of informed consent for all participants (where required) will be returned unreviewed.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Format
**MAJOR DELTA.** FIELD_SKILL describes a "front-loaded abstract (roughly 150–250 words) that states the research question, the gap being addressed, the empirical or theoretical approach, and the core contribution in compressed form." This is a **fluid, narrative abstract**. CCSM requires a **structured abstract** with seven labeled sections (Purpose, Design/Methodology/Approach, Findings, etc.), each serving a distinct rhetorical function. Abstracts must fit all seven sections in ≤250 words total. This is not a stylistic preference; it is a mandatory format enforced by Emerald and monitored during peer review.

### Manuscript Length
**FIELD_SKILL does not specify word limits.** CCSM enforces **7,000–12,000 words** (including references). This is a hard constraint; submissions outside this range are typically desk-rejected.

### Heading Formatting
**FIELD_SKILL does not specify.** CCSM requires first-level headings in **bold**, second-level in **medium italics**. This is a minor but explicitly stated formatting convention specific to Emerald journals.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we argue," "we find") is standard and expected for authors' own claims; impersonal/passive registers remain acceptable for literature synthesis and procedural description.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High citation density in introduction, theory, and discussion sections; multiple citations per paragraph standard.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Results conveyed through tables/figures and narrative prose; both expected. Robustness checks standard and expected.

### Argumentation and Rhetorical Moves
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (CARS logic for introductions; explicit contribution statements; respectful repositioning rather than direct refutation).

### Methods Conventions
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Procedural and replicability detail expected; sample construction, operationalization, model specification, and filtering/screening steps to be described with sufficient detail for reconstruction.

### Discussion and Conclusion Norms
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Looping back to gap and contributions; theoretical implications before practical/managerial ones; limitations statement paired with concrete future research directions; conclusion brief and avoiding new claims.

### Hedging and Epistemic Caution
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Moderate hedging standard; strong language reserved for describing gaps in literature rather than authors' own claims.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Submitting an unstructured abstract.** CCSM requires all seven Emerald structured sections labeled explicitly. Generic or narrative abstracts (even if 200–250 words) will be returned for revision before review begins.

2. **Exceeding 12,000 words.** Manuscripts >12,000 words (including references) are typically desk-rejected without review, regardless of content quality. Plan carefully.

3. **Failing to include ethical statement and informed consent details.** If your work involves human participants or data, the ethical statement is mandatory. Submissions lacking it are returned unreviewed.

4. **Using generative AI for content generation.** The journal explicitly prohibits use of ChatGPT, Claude, or similar tools to generate substantive text. AI-assisted copy-editing (grammar, clarity) is permitted, but generating new paragraphs, arguments, or data interpretations is grounds for rejection or retraction. Document your AI use if any.

5. **Inconsistent heading formatting.** First-level headings must be bold; second-level medium italic. Failure to follow this convention may delay copyediting and signals unfamiliarity with the journal's standards.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
