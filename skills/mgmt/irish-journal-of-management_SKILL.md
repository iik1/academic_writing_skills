# Journal Writing Skill: Irish Journal of Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Sciendo (De Gruyter imprint); originally "Irish Business and Administrative Review" (1979), published online by De Gruyter since March 2015. Journal co-owned by the Irish Academy of Management.
- **Scope:** Management-related issues in Irish and international contexts; explicitly welcomes inter-disciplinary/multi-disciplinary work alongside traditional functional areas (accounting/finance, CSR/ethics, education, entrepreneurship, healthcare/public sector, leadership/OB, marketing, strategy/IB, technology/innovation, HR/employment).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://sciendo-parsed-data-feed.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/IJM/Instructions_for_Authors.pdf (Author Information Pack, fetched directly)
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 abstracts/front-matter read in full (Gyepi-Garbrah & Preko 2025, "Building a better tomorrow" Kelliher/Hynes/Mottiar 2023, plus front-matter for Heffernan 2025 Foreword) and titles/section listing surveyed across ~25 articles in Volumes 40-44 (2021-2025) via EconPapers/RePEc. Note: the journal ceased publication after Vol. 44, Issue 2 (2025), so this skill describes its historical/final conventions.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Three submission types: Research Papers (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-methods empirical research, literature reviews — traditional/narrative, systematic, meta-analysis, meta-synthesis — and conceptual papers); Teaching Submissions (case studies, pedagogical critiques/retrospectives); Book Reviews. Double-blind review, minimum two anonymous reviewers. Submission package requires three separate documents: anonymised manuscript, title page (with author names/affiliations/bios), and cover letter (confirms originality, notes if revision/special issue, discloses funding — NOT disclosed in the manuscript itself).

### Length
- Research Papers: under 40 pages, double-spaced, including references, tables, figures, and appendices.
- Teaching Submissions: 30-40 pages, double-spaced, including references and appendices.
- Book Reviews: 1,000-2,000 words.
- No stated overall word count for Research Papers beyond the page cap — notably looser/shorter than FIELD_SKILL's 10,000-18,000 word norm for empirical papers.

### Abstract
Unstructured, maximum 150 words (tighter than FIELD_SKILL's 150-250 word range). Must state the aim(s)/research question(s) and key contribution(s). Followed by 3-6 keywords, semicolon-separated, lowercase except accepted acronyms (CEO, CSR, HR), no terminal full stop.

### Citations
APA style. Distinctive in-text rules that diverge from generic APA/FIELD_SKILL default:
- Multiple citations in one parenthetical are ordered by **most recently published first**, not alphabetically (contrast FIELD_SKILL's "alphabetically or chronologically by convention").
- Works with more than two authors: list all authors in full at first mention; shorten to "et al." only thereafter (i.e., first-instance-full-list is mandatory, not just permitted).
- Page numbers cited only when directly quoting.
- Journal names spelled out in full in the reference list, never abbreviated.
- No "ibid." or "op. cit." permitted.
- Multiple works by the same author/year distinguished with letters (a, b, c), separated by commas.

### Headings
Distinctive formatting rules not covered by FIELD_SKILL:
- Section headings: bold, NOT numbered, capitalised (all-caps) and centred (e.g., INTRODUCTION, METHOD, RESULTS, DISCUSSION, CONCLUSION).
- Sub-headings: bold, left-aligned, sentence case (only first letter capitalised, except acronyms).
- Footnotes should be avoided; essential endnotes are numbered consecutively and grouped at the end of the paper immediately before References.

### Tables and Figures
- Tables embedded in the manuscript body at point of use (not collected at the end); captions above the table in sentence/title case matching heading style, e.g. "Table 1. Significant development of CSR themes and its pivotal articles"; source line goes under the table.
- Figures submitted as separate, high-resolution, editable jpg/png files, with "Insert Figure X here" placeholder in text; caption below the figure in the format "Figure 1: Dominant themes by period."
- Author is responsible for securing and uploading copyright/usage permission for any adapted/reproduced tables or figures.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No author-identifying information anywhere in the manuscript body (including funding/acknowledgment details, which must go only in the cover letter pre-acceptance, then move to the manuscript end before References post-acceptance).
- Standard British English required (-ise not -ize endings; Collins Dictionary as spelling guide).
- Poorly written manuscripts (grammar, spelling, comprehension issues) are returned without review; non-native English authors may be required to obtain paid copy-editing before review.
- No picture elements, text boxes, tabs, or hard returns inside tables.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Sections are bold/centred/all-caps and unnumbered rather than the numbered-heading convention common in many management outlets — a purely typographic delta not addressed in FIELD_SKILL. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; consistent with visible front-matter in spot-checked articles).

### Citation Density
No evidence of deviation from FIELD_SKILL's density norms in the abstracts/front-matter reviewed; the ordering-by-recency rule (rather than alphabetical) for multi-citation parentheticals is an ENFORCED mechanical convention, not a density difference.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- Abstract cap of 150 words (unstructured) is tighter than FIELD_SKILL's 150-250 word range. ENFORCED.
- Overall paper length capped at ~40 pages double-spaced is shorter than FIELD_SKILL's typical 10,000-18,000-word empirical article — a RELAXATION consistent with this being an ABS 1 outlet.
- Journal accepts a broader mix of article types at shorter length (including 1-2 page editorial/foreword pieces and short "retrospective/perspective" pieces, observed in the 2025 final-issue front-matter) than FIELD_SKILL's four core templates. ## Common Pitfalls
- Including funding/conflict-of-interest details in the manuscript body at initial submission — this journal requires them in the cover letter only, moved to the manuscript (before References) solely after acceptance.
- Alphabetising multiple in-text citations — this journal orders them by most recent publication year first.
- Submitting a title page or biography embedded in the main manuscript file rather than as a fully separate document (breaks the double-blind review).
- Using numbered section headings — this journal wants bold, unnumbered, centred, all-caps main headings.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
