# Journal Writing Skill: Review of Political Economy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed journal welcoming constructive and critical contributions in all areas of heterodox political economy, including Post-Keynesian, Sraffian, Marxian, Institutionalist, Feminist, and Kaleckian traditions. Publishes theoretical and empirical research, plus methodology, economic history, and history of economic thought articles that address contemporary political economy issues.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://files.taylorandfrancis.com/style_crpe.pdf (PDF; also see journal home at https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/crpe20)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (from IDEAS/RePEc, March 2026 and January 2026 issues; symposium pieces and research articles)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows standard economics sections (Introduction, substantive body sections, Conclusion). Journal does not prescribe strict empirical vs. theory division. Accepts standard research articles, symposium pieces, tributes, and review essays—more diverse formats than field baseline.

### Length
No explicit word or page limit mentioned in publicly available guidelines.

### Abstract
Required; indicated with a heading or by reducing font size. Unstructured format (not structured abstract with mandatory fields). Must be accompanied by **5 keywords** and **JEL classification codes**. Abstract word limit not specified.

### Citations
Author-date system (Chicago Manual of Style). Parenthetical citations: (Author Year). Author-year format matches FIELD_SKILL; not numbered footnotes. Footnotes used for supplementary material and qualifications, not primary citations (aligns with FIELD_SKILL).

### Headings
**ENFORCED DETAIL (journal-specific):**
- **First-level headings** (Introduction, Conclusion, etc.): Bold, left-aligned, main words capitalized.
- **Second-level headings:** Italics, left-aligned, main words capitalized.
- **Third-level headings:** Indented from left margin, italics, sentence case.

This is more prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL (which notes Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable with no naming convention mandated). Follow journal format strictly.

### Tables and Figures
Supplied in separate files (.png format). Indicated in text with notation like [Table 1 near here]. Table notes explain variable definitions, standard error clustering, and sample restrictions (standard practice).

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Double-blind review:** Ensure author identity and co-author identities are not revealed in the manuscript text or file metadata during submission.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Article Types and Formats
**DELTA: More diverse publication types than top-tier outlets.** FIELD_SKILL discusses empirical papers and theory papers as the canonical types. Review of Political Economy accepts these plus:
- Symposium pieces and editorial introductions to symposia
- Tributes to deceased scholars or thematic retrospectives
- Review essays and methodological contributions
- History of economic thought articles

Recent issues (2026) include multiple symposium pieces (Pasinetti, STOREP conference). This is not unusual for the journal.

**Classification:** PRACTICE ONLY (observed in recent issues, not stated in submission guidelines).

### Scope and Tier Effects: Theory without Strict Empirical Validation
**DELTA: RELAXATION from top-tier expectations.** FIELD_SKILL (derived from ABS 4*/4 journals) emphasizes novel empirical findings and robust identification. Review of Political Economy (ABS 2) equally welcomes theoretical contributions without requiring empirical case studies or validation. The journal's scope explicitly includes "theory" submissions on equal footing with empirical research.

Pure theoretical papers on monetary circuits, capital accumulation, distribution, or value theory do not face the same replicability and robustness-check expectations as mainstream top-tier journals. Non-empirical methodological or historical articles are publishable.

**Classification:** RELAXATION (lower tier and stated journal scope).

### Identification Strategy and Methods Specification
**DELTA: RELAXATION.** FIELD_SKILL states identification strategy is a required element for empirical papers. Review of Political Economy accepts "methodology" and "history of economic thought" submissions that are non-empirical. These do not require formal causal identification.

For non-empirical papers, the standards of FIELD_SKILL on identification, robustness checks, and statistical reporting do not apply.

**Classification:** RELAXATION (journal scope).

### Voice, Passion, and Editorial Engagement
**DELTA: Possibly MORE first-person and passionate tone acceptable.** Journal description notes papers are "written with vigour and some passion" (from review of a Pasinetti work). FIELD_SKILL emphasizes clear claims and "we" as standard but does not encourage passionate tone. Review of Political Economy, as a heterodox outlet, may tolerate or even welcome more engaged, critical, or passionate argumentation—particularly in theory papers and contributions to ongoing debates.

This is not stated explicitly in submission guidelines. Inferred from journal character and recent publications.

**Classification:** PRACTICE ONLY (inferred, not stated in guidelines; observed tone in published work).

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Forgetting keywords and JEL codes.** These are mandatory and must accompany every submission. Missing them will delay review process.
2. **Incorrect heading format.** Use bold for first-level, italics for second-level, indented italics for third-level. Incorrect formatting will be flagged in revision requests.
3. **Revealing author identity during double-blind review.** Remove author names, institutional affiliations, and identifying phrases from text and file metadata before submission. Self-citations in first person ("in our prior work," "as the author") compromise anonymity.
4. **Treating as if it follows top-tier empirical standards.** This is an ABS 2 journal with heterodox scope. Theory papers, methodology papers, and historical accounts are publishable without empirical validation or formal identification strategies. Do not burden non-empirical submissions with the apparatus of FIELD_SKILL's empirical-paper requirements.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
