# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Applied Accounting Research

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing Limited
- **Scope:** Forum for robust academic research that discusses and proposes field-driven solutions with potential to change accounting practice. Accepts conceptual or empirical work, qualitative or quantitative methods, and interdisciplinary approaches. Emphasis on practical implications for industry, policymakers, and scholars.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jaar (public submission info available; detailed guidelines require submission system access)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (2022–2025; multiple sources: IDEAS/RePEc abstracts and Emerald metadata)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Length
- **Maximum word count:** 8,000 words (stated constraint in author guidelines)
- This is substantially shorter than typical ABS 4*/4 papers, indicating an expectation for concise, focused contributions.

### Abstract
- **Format:** Structured abstract with 4–7 subheadings
- **Word limit:** Maximum 250 words total (Emerald standard)
- **Required subheadings (typical):** Purpose, Design/methodology/approach, Findings, Research limitations/implications, Practical implications, Originality/value
- This is a DELTA from FIELD_SKILL: ABS 4* journals use short, unstructured abstracts. JAAR requires explicit sub-sections.

### Article Types
- Accepts conceptual and empirical work equally
- Accepts qualitative and quantitative methods
- Explicitly invites interdisciplinary, multidisciplinary, transdisciplinary approaches
- Welcomes industry-academia collaboration outputs

### Anonymity
- Double-blind review required
- Authors must remove all identifying information including author names
- Cannot self-cite by name; use "previous research has demonstrated" rather than "our previous research"
- Unpublished own work should not appear in references

### Quality Requirements
- Scientific rigour
- Clear structure with good flow of arguments
- Solid command of English
- Clear relevance for target audiences
- Well-explained contextualisation
- Carefully derived practical implications

### Citation Style
- Not explicitly stated in publicly accessible guidelines
- Emerald journals typically use author-date in-text citations (inferred from article abstracts on IDEAS/RePEc)
- APA 7th Edition is Emerald's preferred style, though subject-area conventions may vary

### Headings and Formatting
- No explicit constraints found in public guidelines
- Emerald standard is flexible; follow journal's template or most recent articles as model

### Tables and Figures
- Standard practice: place after references or in appendix
- No explicit constraints found

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No duplicate submissions (same or similar form)
- Must be original and not under consideration elsewhere
- AI tool usage: Copy-editing (correction, editing, formatting, modifying, refining) of one's own work using generative AI is permitted
- Fraudulent data or results

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Article Structure and Sections
**ENFORCED & PRACTICE ONLY:** JAAR articles observed follow a Purpose-Design-Findings-Implications pattern rather than the canonical Introduction-Hypothesis-Methods-Results-Discussion structure described in FIELD_SKILL.

The structured abstract (with explicit subheadings) effectively replaces the narrative introduction structure of ABS 4* papers. Observed articles map as follows:
- **Purpose** (replaces motivation and research question of FIELD_SKILL Introduction)
- **Design/methodology/approach** (replaces Methods section)
- **Findings** (replaces Results)
- **Research limitations/implications** (combines Discussion and limitations)
- **Practical implications** (explicit in JAAR; secondary in FIELD_SKILL)
- **Originality/value** (explicit positioning of contribution)

This is not a relaxation but a different organizational convention. Articles still contain all necessary elements (hypothesis development, methods, results), but the flow is flatter and more results-forward.

### Practical Implications Emphasis
**ENFORCED:** JAAR explicitly requires "carefully derived practical implications" in both the abstract and the article body. This is a delta from FIELD_SKILL, where practical implications are optional and often underdeveloped.

Practical implications should address: how findings change practice, implications for practitioners, policymakers, or industry; actionable insights derived from the research.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. "We" dominant, active voice, high formality, confidence calibrated to identification strength.

### Hedging and Caution
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Well-identified results stated directly; mechanistic interpretations use "suggest," "indicate," "consistent with."

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Precise reporting of coefficients, significance levels, t-statistics; economic significance increasingly expected.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High in Purpose and Design/methodology sections, lower in Findings, medium in Limitations/Implications.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding 8,000 words.** JAAR is a lower-tier venue with strict word limits. Submissions exceeding this will face desk rejection or requirement to trim. Plan for conciseness from the start.

2. **Weak or absent practical implications.** The journal explicitly emphasizes practical impact. Purely theoretical contributions without actionable implications for practice or policy are less competitive. Ensure Section 6 (or equivalent) articulates how practitioners, policymakers, or industry stakeholders benefit.

3. **Failing to structure the abstract correctly.** The required 4–7 subheadings (Purpose, Design/methodology/approach, Findings, etc.) are not optional. Reviewers and editors expect this format. Use it even in non-blind versions of your manuscript.

4. **Not clarifying contribution upfront.** While FIELD_SKILL emphasizes explicit contribution statements, JAAR requires it earlier and more prominently (in the abstract "Originality/value" heading and in the article's opening). Bury it at your peril.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Notes on Access and Limitations

- **Full-text access limitations:** Emerald Publishing implements paywalls and CAPTCHA verification on direct URLs. Full-text PDFs are not publicly accessible for most articles. Abstract and structured summary information was extracted from IDEAS/RePEc (an open bibliography service) and publisher metadata pages.
- **Article sample:** 6 articles across 2022–2025 provide broad structural confirmation but do not represent exhaustive stylistic analysis of the full journal. However, convergence on abstract structure, word limit, and practical-implications emphasis is high across all sampled articles.
- **Guidelines completeness:** Emerald's detailed author guidelines page (structure-your-journal-submission, write-article-abstract) is not publicly accessible without institutional login. The hard constraints listed above are extracted from: (1) public Emerald summary pages, (2) direct author statements found in search results, and (3) inference from observed articles.
- **Citation style:** Not explicitly stated in publicly available materials. Assumed to follow Emerald standard (author-date, inferred APA 7th) but not confirmed from the journal's own style guide.
