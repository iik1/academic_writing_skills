# Journal Writing Skill: Cognitive Science

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell (on behalf of the Cognitive Science Society)
- **Scope:** Official journal of the Cognitive Science Society; explicitly
  interdisciplinary across psychology, linguistics, computer science,
  philosophy, neuroscience, and anthropology. Welcomes theoretical or
  philosophical analyses of knowledge representation and cognitive
  processes, experimental or ethnographic studies, descriptions of
  intelligent programs that model human abilities, design proposals for
  cognitive models, and protocol/discourse analysis — a wider article-type
  net than most PSYCH (GENERAL) empirical outlets.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/15516709/homepage/forauthors.html (Wiley); https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/cognitive-science-journal/ (society scope page)
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 (Portelance, Frank & Jurafsky 2024, "Learning the Meanings of Function Words From Grounded Language Using a Visual Question Answering Model," doi:10.1111/cogs.13448; Van Kerrebroeck et al. 2025, "Virtual Partners Improve Synchronization in Human−Machine Trios," doi:10.1111/cogs.70040; Cohen, White & Luo 2026, "Psychological Value Theory," doi:10.1111/cogs.70181; Westbury 2024, "A Constant Error, Revisited: A New Explanation of the Halo Effect," doi:10.1111/cogs.13463-series; author(s) 2025, "Individualization Without Internalization," doi:10.1111/cogs.70132; authors 2025, "With or Without a System: How Category-Specific and System-Wide Cognitive Biases Shape Word Order," doi:10.1111/cogs.70139; authors 2022, "Is the Mind Inherently Predicting? Exploring Forward and Backward Looking in Language Processing," PMC9786242)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No single mandated structure across all article types. Empirical reports
follow standard IMRaD/multi-study conventions consistent with FIELD_SKILL.
Distinctively, the journal also regularly accepts and publishes purely
theoretical/synthetic articles with free-form, researcher-defined thematic
headings (no Method/Results at all) — see Structural Deviations below.
Cover letter must argue why the submission fits the journal and, for
"extended articles" (a recognized longer article type), justify why the
extended length/format is warranted.

### Length
No explicit word/page limit found on the guidelines page for the main
journal (word-limit language present but exact figures not accessible in
this environment — likely paywalled/JS-rendered). An "extended article"
category exists for papers exceeding normal length, requiring
justification in the cover letter. Do not confuse this with the Cognitive
Science Society's separate 6-page conference proceedings limit, which
applies to the CogSci conference, not this journal.

### Abstract
No distinct delta from FIELD_SKILL observed; abstracts in spot-checked
articles are single-paragraph, unstructured, and follow standard framing
(motivation, approach, key result, implication).

### Citations
Free-format submission: authors need not format references in-text or in
the reference list to any specific style at initial submission — the
typesetter applies journal style post-acceptance. This relaxes the
practical burden relative to most journals but does not change what should
appear in the final published text (APA-based, per Cognitive Science
Society convention). RELAXATION of formatting effort only, not of citation
practice itself.

### Headings
No fixed heading taxonomy is mandated; theoretical articles use
researcher-defined thematic headings even at first level (see Structural
Deviations).

### Tables and Figures
Figures, supporting information, and appendices are submitted as separate
files (not embedded near first reference at submission stage, unlike
FIELD_SKILL's typical expectation — this is a submission-mechanics
requirement rather than a stylistic one).

### Explicit Prohibitions
Double-anonymized peer review: authors must anonymize the manuscript body
and submit a separate title page with identifying information and funding
detail. Poorly formatted manuscripts or manuscripts with substandard
English are explicitly stated to be returned before review. ORCID iD is
required for all authors. Authors must share code/data/stimuli sufficient
for reviewers to understand how a study was conducted (Open Materials
requirement) and select a data availability statement at submission.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
This journal publishes a meaningful share of purely theoretical/integrative
articles using free-form, thematic section headings with no Method/Results
sections at all (e.g., "Internalization as process and outcome," "A
relational world," "Hearing one's self in terms of the other"), closing
with "Concluding remarks" rather than a Discussion. FIELD_SKILL treats
this pattern as characteristic mainly of dedicated review journals
(Psychological Bulletin, Annual Review of Psychology); at Cognitive
Science it coexists routinely alongside standard empirical IMRaD papers in
the same issues. PRACTICE ONLY, but well-evidenced (1 of 7 spot-checked
articles was of this type, and the journal's stated scope explicitly
invites "theoretical or philosophical analyses" and "design proposals for
cognitive models" as first-class article types, not exceptions). Authors
targeting this journal should not assume IMRaD is mandatory — a
theory/model paper with thematic headings is an accepted norm here, not a
deviation requiring special justification (beyond the standard cover-letter
note for extended articles).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Computational/formal modeling papers (agent models, cognitive architectures,
value-based choice models, NLP/neural-network models of language
acquisition) are a routine, well-represented article type here, more so
than typical PSYCH (GENERAL) empirical venues. These papers commonly
report model-fit statistics (BIC, cross-validated r², AIC-style
comparisons) alongside or instead of conventional NHST effect sizes —
consistent with, not contradicting, FIELD_SKILL's effect-size expectation,
but the metrics used skew toward model-comparison statistics. FIELD DEFAULT
governs when the paper is a standard behavioral experiment; PRACTICE ONLY
for the prevalence of computational-modeling papers as a category.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming every submission must fit IMRaD: a theory/model paper with
  thematic headings and no Method/Results section is an accepted, routine
  format at this journal, not a special case.
- Treating the Cognitive Science Society's 6-page conference proceedings
  limit as applying to the journal — it does not; that limit is for the
  separate CogSci annual conference submission track.
- Over-formatting references before submission — the journal uses free
  format at submission and defers reference styling to the typesetter.
- Failing to anonymize the manuscript body for double-anonymized review, or
  omitting the separate title page with author/funding details.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
