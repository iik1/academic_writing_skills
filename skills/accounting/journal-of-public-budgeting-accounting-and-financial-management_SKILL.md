# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Public Budgeting, Accounting and Financial Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing
- **Scope:** Double-blind peer-reviewed journal publishing empirical and conceptual research on public budgeting, accounting, auditing, and financial and performance management. Interdisciplinary, welcoming quantitative, qualitative, and comparative analyses from accounting, public management, and public finance perspectives.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jpbafm (author guidelines accessible via ScholarOne submission portal)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 (Almqvist & Wällstedt 2025; Canestrini, Galizzi & Siboni 2025; Roberto, Grossi, Guthrie & Mattei 2025; Bertheussen 2025)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Manuscripts must follow a standard academic structure with numbered or unnumbered sections.
- No explicit fixed section requirements, but all examined articles contained: Introduction, Literature/Theoretical Framework, Methods/Design, Findings/Results, Discussion, and Conclusion/Implications sections.
- Ethical statement required for research involving human subjects, with name and location of ethics reviewing committee, approval number, and date.

### Length
- **Manuscript length:** 1,000–4,000 words (including abstract, body, references).
- This is a RELAXATION from FIELD_SKILL baseline (which does not specify limits and typically ranges 6,000–10,000 words at ABS 4*/4 level).

### Abstract
- **Structured abstract mandatory:** Must include 4–7 labeled sub-headings.
- **Required sub-headings (in order):**
  1. Purpose — research question or objective
  2. Design/methodology/approach — study design and methods
  3. Findings — main results
  4. Research limitations/implications — constraints and broader relevance
  5. Practical implications (optional but common)
  6. Social implications (optional)
  7. Originality/value — contribution statement
- **Word limit:** Maximum 250 words total for entire abstract (including all sub-headings and text).
- **Keywords:** Maximum 12 keywords (as separate metadata field, not embedded in abstract).

### Citations
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm: author-date, in-text citations, all sources cited in references.

### Headings
Varies by article; section numbering is optional (Roman numerals, Arabic numerals, or unnumbered all observed). Subsections follow parallel convention within each article. Title-case or ALL-CAPS not strictly enforced; consistency within manuscript is expected.

### Tables and Figures
- Tables include column headers, row labels, and explanatory notes.
- Figures/tables placed after references or at end of manuscript (per FIELD_SKILL norm).
- All tables should include variable definitions and statistical methods (e.g., clustering, significance tests) in notes.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Generative AI use:** Copy-editing of author's own existing work using generative AI tools is PERMITTED if it improves structure and clarity of language and grammar. However, creating, drafting, or writing any part of the submission using generative AI is NOT permitted.
- **Conflict of interest:** Must be declared upon submission; omission or misrepresentation is a serious breach.
- **Data and code citation:** All data, program code, and other methods must be fully referenced and cited with persistent identifiers (DOI) where available.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Abstract Format
**Delta: ENFORCED (stated in guidelines and confirmed across all examined articles)**

FIELD_SKILL states "short, structured summary" but does not prescribe the exact labeling convention. This journal *requires* a highly structured abstract with 4–7 explicitly labeled sub-headings in fixed order. The "Design/methodology/approach" sub-heading is mandatory and appears as a distinct labeled section in all examined articles, visually separating method from purpose and findings. This is more prescriptive than the field baseline.

The 250-word limit is tighter than many ABS 4*/4 journals, which typically allow 300–400-word abstracts. This represents a RELAXATION in permissible length.

### Practical Implications Section
**Delta: PRACTICE ONLY**

All four examined articles included an explicit "Practical implications" section within their conclusions or a standalone discussion subsection. FIELD_SKILL notes this as "common but not universal" at the ABS 4*/4 level. At this lower tier, the norm appears stronger: practical implications are universally stated, often as a labeled subsection or conclusion paragraph. This is consistent with the journal's stated scope ("relevant to practitioners, consultants, advisors") and may reflect the ABS 2 tier's emphasis on actionable research.

### Manuscript Length
**Delta: RELAXATION (stated in guidelines)**

The 1,000–4,000 word limit is substantially shorter than the FIELD_SKILL baseline of 6,000–10,000+ words typical at ABS 4*/4 level. This accommodates shorter empirical studies, case studies, and commentary pieces, and reflects Emerald's editorial model for a lower-tier journal.

### Research Design Diversity
**Delta: PRACTICE ONLY**

Examined articles included longitudinal case studies, multi-site case studies, document analysis, and structured literature reviews—methodologies less common in the ABS 4* quantitative/archival/experimental mainstream. FIELD_SKILL's discussion of methods centers on archival, experimental, and survey designs; this journal prominently publishes qualitative and case-based studies. The presentation of findings and discussion of limitations are adapted accordingly (e.g., internal vs. external validity, generalizability bounds, thick description rather than statistical power).

### Structured Abstract Sub-Heading "Design/Methodology/Approach"
**Delta: ENFORCED (stated and observed)**

FIELD_SKILL notes that methods are described in a dedicated section but does not mandate a separate "Design/methodology/approach" sub-heading in the abstract. This journal requires it as a labeled abstract element, making the methodological frame explicit and prominent from the abstract.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT (we/we find conventions consistent with FIELD_SKILL; active voice dominant; formality high)
- Citation density: FIELD DEFAULT (high in introduction/hypothesis development; low in results; medium in conclusion)
- Hedging and epistemic caution: FIELD DEFAULT (well-identified claims stated directly; mechanistic interpretations softened; limitations explicitly acknowledged)
- Paraphrase vs. quotation: FIELD DEFAULT (paraphrase dominant except in fieldwork/interview-based papers)
- Null results reporting: FIELD DEFAULT (not suppressed; reported straightforwardly)
- Discussion and Conclusion structure: FIELD DEFAULT (merged Discussion & Conclusion typical; restatement of findings, contributions, limitations, future directions)

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Abstract sub-heading misalignment:** Omitting or mislabeling the "Design/methodology/approach" sub-heading, or exceeding the 250-word limit. Reviewers expect this structure as stated in guidelines; deviations are flagged immediately.

2. **Word limit overreach:** Submitting 5,000+ words when the hard limit is 4,000. This journal is more space-constrained than the ABS 4* baseline; tightening prose and ruthlessly cutting tangential literature review is essential.

3. **Missing practical implications:** Concluding with only theoretical contributions or limitations without explicitly addressing "so what?" for practitioners, auditors, or policy-makers. This journal expects all papers to articulate actionable insights.

4. **Generative AI drafting:** Submitting work where sections were drafted (not merely edited) using ChatGPT or similar tools. Emerald's submission checklist asks authors to confirm no generative AI was used to create content; false certification triggers desk rejection.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
