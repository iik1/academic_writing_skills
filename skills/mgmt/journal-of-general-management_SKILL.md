# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of General Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publishing
- **Scope:** Cross-functional, practitioner-oriented management journal. Publishes theory-building/theory-testing papers and contributions to current management debates across strategy, leadership, governance, international business, decision-making, and change management, with an explicit goal of bridging academic and practitioner/policymaker audiences.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/gem (JS-rendered; content retrieved via search-engine snippets, not direct fetch)
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 (titles/abstracts confirmed via search-indexed snippets and one open-repository PDF landing page; full text largely paywalled): "Managing the healthcare revolution" (Schiavone & Leone, 2023, DOI 10.1177/03063070231184031); "Reframing talent acquisition, retention practices for organisational commitment in Malaysian SMEs" (Abraham et al., DOI 10.1177/03063070231184336); "Organizational structure and organizational learning: The moderating role of organizational defensive routines" (Yang, Secchi & Homberg, 2022, DOI 10.1177/03063070211038922); "The determinants of environmental performance and its effect on the financial performance of European-listed companies" (Djoutsa Wamba, 2022, DOI 10.1177/03063070211021050); "Employer branding: Confirmation of a measurement model and its implication for managing the workforce" (Sarabdeen et al., 2023, DOI 10.1177/03063070221079574). Access-limited good-faith sample per ABS 1 tier allowance — see Sources.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed mandatory section template stated in the guidelines beyond standard journal-article norms; accepts quantitative, qualitative, and conceptual papers per FIELD_SKILL's article-type variation table.

### Length
**Normal article length is 4,000–6,000 words** (both shorter and longer are considered). This is well below FIELD_SKILL's typical 10,000–18,000-word range for quantitative/qualitative management papers — a substantial compression.

### Abstract
**A "synopsis" of no more than 100 words is required** (unstructured), placed between the title and main body, stating purpose, method, and major findings/conclusions. This is far shorter than FIELD_SKILL's 150–250-word norm.
**Minimum 5 keywords** required after the abstract.

### Citations
**SAGE Harvard style** (author-date), not APA. Key differences from FIELD_SKILL's APA convention: up to three authors listed before "et al." is used (APA uses two); "et al." set upright/roman, never italic; no ibid./op. cit./infra/supra — repeat the same author-date citation format on subsequent mentions; multiple works by the same author cited together are ordered oldest to newest, separated by commas.

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Tables and Figures
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Journal supports hosting supplementary online materials (datasets, podcasts, videos, images) alongside the article — not covered by FIELD_SKILL.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Double-blind peer review: author-identifying details must be removable to a separate title page (standard blinding requirement, not otherwise emphasized in FIELD_SKILL).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in the sampled abstracts (third-person/passive descriptive framing typical of short synopses; sample too brief to assess first-person "we" usage in body text).

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm where visible — quantitative papers in the sample use standard techniques (hierarchical regression, structural equation/PLS modeling) consistent with FIELD_SKILL's methods section expectations, just compressed into a shorter overall article.

### Citation Density
Not assessable from abstract-only access; no evidence of deviation from FIELD_SKILL.

### Structural Deviations
The compressed word limit (4,000–6,000 words vs. FIELD_SKILL's 10,000+ norm) implies proportionally shorter Theory and Discussion sections than top-tier outlets, though no explicit section-by-section guidance was found. Treat as PRACTICE-level inference (RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL length norms), not a confirmed structural template change.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
The journal explicitly frames itself as bridging academic and practitioner audiences ("thought leadership," "knowledge exchange between academics and policymakers") more strongly than the FIELD_SKILL baseline's general note that management journals "expect some managerial relevance." Authors should weight practical/managerial implications more heavily relative to pure theoretical contribution, consistent with an ABS 1 practitioner-adjacent outlet.

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing a FIELD_SKILL-length (150–250 word) structured abstract instead of the required ≤100-word unstructured synopsis.
- Drafting a full 10,000+ word manuscript on the top-tier template; this journal expects 4,000–6,000 words.
- Using APA-style citation conventions (two authors before et al.; ibid.) instead of SAGE Harvard rules (three authors before et al.; no ibid./op. cit.).
- Underweighting practitioner/managerial relevance relative to theoretical contribution, given the journal's explicit cross-functional/practitioner mission.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
