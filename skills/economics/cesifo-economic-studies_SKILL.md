# Journal Writing Skill: CESifo Economic Studies

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Scope:** High-quality papers in economics with emphasis on policy issues; combines theory and empirical research in style accessible to economists across all specialisations.
- **Status:** Journal ceased publishing after Volume 70 (December 2024); no longer accepting submissions. Archive maintained by OUP in perpetuity.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://academic.oup.com/cesifo/pages/General_Instructions
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 1 abstract from Volume 70, Issue 4 (2024) + guidelines and metadata from 10+ recent articles (2023–2024)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
**No mandatory section sequence specified.** Authors structure content as appropriate to their paper type. This is a **RELAXATION** from FIELD_SKILL's disciplined section architecture norm. While FIELD_SKILL expects near-universal ordering (Intro → Methods → Results → Conclusion for empirical papers), CESifo places no explicit requirement on section order or naming.

### Length
**Approximately 20–25 pages, double-spaced, excluding tables and figures.** This is a **RELAXATION** from ABS 4*/4 norms (which are typically longer and more rigorous). The 20–25 page range accommodates shorter, more policy-focused contributions typical of ABS 2 journals.

### Abstract
**Maximum 100 words.** Format: Unstructured (no mandatory sections like "Background | Methods | Results"). Must include:
- Abstract text (≤100 words)
- Up to five keywords
- Journal of Economic Literature (JEL) classification codes

This is **more explicit than FIELD_SKILL**, which does not specify abstract word limits or the keyword/JEL requirement.

### Citations
**Harvard (author-date) style.** In-text: "(Author Year, pp. X–Y)" or similar. Bibliography ordered alphabetically by surname. This aligns with FIELD_SKILL (author-year is near-universal). **No delta.**

### Language and Spelling
**British English spelling required throughout.** This is an **EXPLICIT REQUIREMENT** not stated in FIELD_SKILL. Authors may retain American spelling "where accustomed," but British is the stated preference. Non-native speakers encouraged to use language editing services (author-funded).

### Headings
No specific convention mandated beyond standard academic hierarchy. FIELD_SKILL norm (Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable) applies.

### Tables and Figures
- Submit tables in MS Word format
- Provide graph data in MS Excel files
- Journal will redesign all figures in house
- Each table/figure requires: number, title, explanatory notes (if needed), source attribution
- Alt-text descriptions required for accessibility
- **ENFORCED CONSTRAINT**: Separate from main text during submission; will be typeset by publisher

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No block text formatting
- No ligatures (fi, fl, etc.)
- Avoid excessive footnotes; use only for supplementary material

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Structure and Conventions
CESifo specifies an **unstructured abstract** (no mandatory divisions), whereas FIELD_SKILL does not address abstract format. The explicit 100-word limit and keyword/JEL requirement are more prescriptive than field baseline. Observed practice in "Electoral Cycles in Fiscal Rules" (Jung & Kim, 2024): abstract follows standard empirical structure (Data → Findings → Context → Implications), announces quantitative results upfront ("we find"), uses first-person plural ("we"), consistent with FIELD_SKILL voice norms.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (Hard constraint in guidelines; confirmed in spot-checked abstract).

### Language Requirement
CESifo mandates British spelling; FIELD_SKILL does not specify. This is a **localized journal preference**, not a field-wide norm.

**Classification:** ENFORCED.

### Section Structure Flexibility
CESifo imposes **no mandatory section sequence or naming**, whereas FIELD_SKILL emphasizes disciplined, near-universal ordering (Intro → Methods → Results → Conclusion for empirical papers). This reflects the ABS 2 tier: more permissive than ABS 4*/4. Authors are free to structure as appropriate to paper type without adhering to the canonical sequence.

**Classification:** RELAXATION (journal permits what FIELD_SKILL treats as strongly expected).

### Manuscript Length
20–25 pages (double-spaced, excluding tables/figures) is **shorter than typical ABS 4*/4 papers** and reflects ABS 2 standards. This is **less stringent than FIELD_SKILL**, which does not specify page limits but implies longer, more comprehensive treatment.

**Classification:** RELAXATION.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** First-person plural ("we") standard; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Citation density and style:** Author-date (parenthetical), high in Introduction, medium in Methods/Results, low in Conclusion; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Hedging intensity:** Low; clear claims preferred; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Active vs. passive voice:** Active preferred; passive acceptable for data generation processes; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Statistical reporting:** Quantitative specificity expected; economic significance alongside statistical significance; FIELD DEFAULT.
- **Conclusion structure:** Restates main findings, draws implications, acknowledges limitations, brief; FIELD DEFAULT.

## Common Pitfalls Specific to CESifo Economic Studies

1. **Exceeding 100-word abstract limit:** Many economists accustomed to longer abstracts (150+ words) overshoot the constraint. Keep abstracts tight; cut to essentials.

2. **Omitting JEL codes and keywords:** Required on submission; absence causes desk rejection or revision request. Include all five if possible; minimum three recommended.

3. **Using American spelling throughout:** While "American spelling acceptable where accustomed," British is the stated preference. Consistency with British conventions strengthens submission likelihood.

4. **Specifying rigid section order:** Unlike ABS 4* journals, CESifo does not enforce a standard section sequence. Flexibility is allowed and expected; structure your paper for clarity and policy relevance, not canonical ordering.

5. **Misformatting tables and figures:** Submit tables as editable MS Word, graph data as Excel. Figures will be redesigned; provide clean source files. Poorly formatted submissions may be desk-rejected.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access Limitations and Notes

Full-text articles from CESifo Economic Studies are paywalled via Oxford University Press (except open-access selections). This skill was derived from:
- Official author guidelines (General Instructions and General Information pages, both publicly accessible)
- Abstract and metadata from spot-checked 2023–2024 articles
- Table-of-contents examination for Volume 69 (2023) and Volume 70 (2024)

Due to paywall restrictions, full-text analysis was limited to abstracts, guidelines, and observable metadata. However, the guidelines are comprehensive and explicit. The single full abstract examined ("Electoral Cycles in Fiscal Rules," Jung & Kim, 2024) confirmed alignment with FIELD_SKILL voice and argumentation norms, with no additional deltas observed.

Journal ceased accepting submissions December 2024. This skill remains valid for historical reference and for authors submitting to sister publications in the CESifo network.
