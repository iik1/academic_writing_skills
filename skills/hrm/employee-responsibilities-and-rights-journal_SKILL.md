# Journal Writing Skill: Employee Responsibilities and Rights Journal

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature
- **Scope:** Interdisciplinary forum for employee relations research covering ethics, organizational behavior, law, economics, sociology, social psychology, and employment relations. Emphasizes connection between practitioner concerns and academic rigor.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/10672/submission-guidelines (requires authentication)
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** Limited to abstract/title level due to Springer paywall; 6+ recent article titles and abstracts analyzed (2025)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscripts should follow a logical, recognizable structure appropriate to article type. The journal accepts empirical studies (qualitative and quantitative), case studies, critical commentaries, conceptual/theoretical articles, and viewpoint pieces (symposiums, roundtable discussions, interviews, book reviews).

### Length
No explicit word limit found in publicly available guidelines. Follows standard Springer journal format expectations.

### Abstract
- Must not contain undefined abbreviations or unspecified references
- For trial-based research: include trial registration number (TRN), registration date, and "retrospectively registered" as final line
- Exact word limit not publicly specified

### Keywords
- Authors must provide **4 to 6 keywords** for indexing purposes

### Citations
- Uses **author-year (APA-style) in-text citations** with parenthetical format (Author Year)
- Multiple references: (Author Year; Author Year)
- Three or more authors: (First Author et al. Year)
- Reference list format follows standard Springer/APA conventions

### Headings
Springer standard: use decimal numbering system (1, 1.1, 1.2, etc.) if headings are numbered; never skip a heading level. Ensure unique and consistent formatting for each level.

### Tables and Figures
- All tables numbered with Arabic numerals
- Must be cited consecutively in text (Table 1, Table 2, etc.)
- Each table requires a caption/title explaining components
- Figures similarly numbered and cited

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Undefined abbreviations in abstract prohibited
- Unspecified references in abstract prohibited
- Lack of required conflict-of-interest disclosure statements leads to desk rejection
- Plagiarism screening applied; COPE guidelines followed if detected

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Broader Interdisciplinary Framing
This journal explicitly bridges law, ethics, philosophy, and economics alongside HRM and organizational behavior. Where FIELD_SKILL emphasizes psychological/managerial framing or sociological/institutional framing as a binary choice, this journal integrates multiple disciplinary lenses simultaneously. Articles may foreground legal, ethical, or economic analysis as primary contribution rather than purely managerial or organizational relevance.

**Classification:** PRACTICE ONLY / STRUCTURAL DELTA. The journal's scope statement explicitly lists law, ethics, and philosophy as core disciplines, not secondary. This represents a genuine departure from HRM-field defaults, which typically weight organizational behavior and management first.

### Non-Traditional Article Formats
Unlike the FIELD_SKILL baseline (dominated by empirical IMRaD structure), this journal has an explicit **"Perspectives" or "Viewpoints" section** accepting symposiums, roundtable discussions, interviews, and book reviews. These do not follow traditional structure and may omit methods/results sections entirely.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (stated in scope and editorial policies).

### Critical Commentaries and Conceptual Articles
The journal explicitly publishes "critical commentaries" and "conceptual and dialectic presentations" as core offerings, not peripheral. FIELD_SKILL describes conceptual framing but does not emphasize critical commentary as a standalone article type. This journal treats critical engagement with existing scholarship, policy, and legal frameworks as a valued contribution in its own right.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (stated in scope).

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Failing to disclose conflicts of interest:** The journal requires explicit "Statements and Declarations" section including financial and non-financial interest disclosures for the last 3 years. Omission results in desk rejection as incomplete submission.

2. **Abbreviations in abstract:** Undefined abbreviations are explicitly prohibited in abstracts. Spell out all acronyms on first mention or avoid them entirely in the abstract.

3. **Underestimating interdisciplinary scope:** Submissions that frame the issue purely as HR management risk misalignment with journal values. The journal expects engagement with legal, ethical, or policy dimensions of employee relations, not solely managerial implications.

4. **Misunderstanding article types:** Attempting to force critical commentary or viewpoint pieces into traditional IMRaD format. The journal actively encourages non-traditional formats; if your contribution is primarily critical or dialogical, frame it explicitly as a commentary or roundtable piece, not a standard research article.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
