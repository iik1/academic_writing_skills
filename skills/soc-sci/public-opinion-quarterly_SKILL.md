# Journal Writing Skill: Public Opinion Quarterly

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** SOC SCI
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press (on behalf of AAPOR)
- **Scope:** Theoretical contributions to opinion and communication research, analyses of current public opinion, and methodological studies of survey validity (questionnaire construction, interviewing, sampling, mode effects).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://academic.oup.com/poq/pages/general_instructions
- **Field baseline:** soc-sci-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (abstracts/metadata only, full text paywalled) — "Not So Sexually Modern After All" (89(4):1167, López Ortega & Blanco Bosco); "Whose Party Is This? Explaining Perceptions of US Party Ideology" (89(4):1185, Warner); "Public Opinion Quarterly: Past, Present, and Future" (89(1):1, Holbrook & Jones, editorial); "Scaling Open-Ended Survey Responses Using LLM-Paired Comparisons" (90(3):630, DiGiuseppe & Flynn); plus Vol. 90 Issue 3 table of contents (5 Research Articles, 5 Research Notes, 1 Research Synthesis, 1 Book Review) reviewed for structural/labeling patterns.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
POQ publishes five distinct submission types, each with its own explicit label used in the table of contents: **Original/Research Articles**, **Research Notes**, **Polls in Context** (formerly "Polls—Trends"), **Research Syntheses**, and **Book Reviews**. No mandated internal section-heading set beyond an **Appendix A: Disclosure Elements** (AAPOR Code compliance) for empirical submissions.

### Length
- Original Articles: max 6,500 words (text + notes, excluding figures/tables/references)
- Research Notes: max 3,000 words (text + notes, excluding figures/tables/references/appendices) — for single findings, replications/extensions, or minor additions
- Polls in Context: max 2,500 words (text + notes)
- Research Syntheses: max 6,500 words (including footnotes)
- Book Reviews: ~1,200 words

### Abstract
No structured-abstract format or explicit word limit specified in guidelines.

### Citations
Any conventional format (MLA, APA, Chicago/Turabian) accepted at **initial submission**; upon revision invitation, must convert to POQ house style (adapted from Chicago Manual of Style, 18th ed.) with footnotes required in the final version (footnotes or endnotes acceptable pre-revision). Self-citations must be phrased in third person (blinding requirement).

### Headings
No fixed heading taxonomy mandated; "current best practices" for methodological reporting govern content, not headings.

### Tables and Figures
Alt-text descriptions required for all images, placed directly under figure legends. Must consider colorblind readers and black-and-white print legibility. 12-pt font, double-spaced, one-inch margins, ragged-right (not justified), 8.5×11 page setup for the manuscript overall.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Simultaneous multi-journal submission
- Prior publication (unless explicitly framed as an extension)
- Fabricated/manipulated data
- Unattributed material from other sources
- Resubmission of previously declined POQ manuscripts without substantial change
- Identifying information in the blinded manuscript: grant/funding numbers, identifiable author-linked archive URLs (OSF, Dataverse), identifiable data-collection organizations or locations

### Other Hard Requirements
- Mandatory AAPOR Code disclosure elements for any survey-based submission: funding source, exact question wording, population/sampling frame, sample design and recruitment, data collection method/dates, response rate with calculation method, sample sizes/precision estimates, design-effect adjustments.
- Replication materials must be deposited in POQ's Harvard Dataverse archive before accepted manuscripts advance to typesetting (or an exception secured).
- Double-blind review, typically 2–3 reviewers, ~3-month target turnaround.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Structural Deviations
POQ's five-way article-type taxonomy (Original Article / Research Note / Polls in Context / Research Synthesis / Book Review) is an explicit, labeled submission category system that determines length and expected content — this is more granular than the generic FIELD_SKILL structure and is ENFORCED (stated in guidelines and consistently visible in tables of contents across issues). Authors should identify and write to the target category from the outset, since format (e.g., Research Note vs. full article) governs permissible length and depth of literature engagement, not just word count.

### Citation Density
FIELD_SKILL's high citation density in Introduction/Theory sections is consistent with what's observable in POQ abstracts, but POQ Research Notes (3,000-word cap) necessarily compress literature engagement to a tighter, single-contribution frame rather than the fuller CARS-model gap-establishment typical of full ABS-4 articles — RELAXATION appropriate to the shorter format, not a distinct journal norm.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a full-length manuscript that is actually better scoped as a Research Note (single finding/replication) — POQ enforces the category distinction and reviewers expect content scope to match the declared type.
- Leaving identifying funding/archive-link details in the initial blinded submission, which violates POQ's explicit blinding rule.
- Using final Chicago-style footnote citations at initial submission instead of waiting for the revision-stage format requirement (wastes effort, but also signals unfamiliarity with process if done incorrectly in the other direction — omitting required AAPOR disclosure elements at submission).
- Failing to pre-register a Dataverse replication-archive plan before acceptance, causing delays at the typesetting stage.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
