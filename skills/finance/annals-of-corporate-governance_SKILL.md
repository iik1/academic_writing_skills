# Journal Writing Skill: Annals of Corporate Governance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** now publishers Inc. (Foundations and Trends-style monograph series; distributed/indexed via Emerald as of recent issues)
- **Scope:** Publishes under the auspices of the International Corporate Governance Society. Each issue is a single long-form survey or tutorial monograph on a corporate governance topic — not a collection of short original-research articles. Scope includes tutorial coverage of a subfield, research retrospectives, state-of-the-art literature reviews, and advanced theoretical/debate pieces.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.nowpublishers.com/Journal/AuthorInstructions/ACG (fetched via search-engine cache; direct WebFetch returned 403)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 (abstracts/metadata only, full text paywalled): "The Corporate Governance of Business Groups: What We Know and What Lies Ahead" (Aguilera et al. 2023, DOI 10.1561/109.00000040); "New Directions for Corporate Governance: A Comparative Capitalisms Perspective" (Allen 2024, DOI 10.1561/109.00000037); "Corporate Governance and Human Resource Management" (Wood & Brewster 2016, Vol 1(4)); "Shareholder Primacy as an Untenable Corporate Norm" (Lee 2023, Vol 8(1)); plus the RePEc/IDEAS full title/author/date index of all ~32 issues published 2016–2025 was reviewed for structural pattern (single-authored or small-team monographs, quarterly cadence).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Two-stage submission: authors first submit an abstract and proposed table of contents to the editor-in-chief/publisher for initial review; only after preliminary acceptance is the full monograph drafted and peer-reviewed. This pre-approval-of-scope stage has no analogue in standard FIELD_SKILL journals, which accept complete manuscripts directly.

### Length
Monograph format: observed published page ranges run roughly 50–100+ pages (e.g., pp. 152–251, pp. 158–249, pp. 249–319, pp. 1–50), far exceeding the standard FIELD_SKILL empirical article length. This is a survey/tutorial monograph series, not a short-form article journal.

### Abstract
No structured abstract format specified in guidelines; observed abstracts are single unstructured paragraphs, consistent with FIELD_SKILL default, but longer/more synthesis-oriented given the survey nature of the work (summarizing a literature and its gaps rather than a single study's findings).

### Citations
Reference style is **alphabetical**, not the numbered/chronological style used in some other now publishers "Foundations and Trends" titles. In-text citation format matches FIELD_SKILL (author-year parenthetical: "Reis and Stocken [2007]" or "[Reis and Stocken, 2007]"), except that now publishers house style uses **square brackets**, not parentheses, around the year/citation — a deviation from the parenthetical "(Fama and French, 1993)" format FIELD_SKILL describes. Three-plus authors use "et al." (matches FIELD_SKILL).

### Headings
No explicit heading rules stated in the guidelines beyond standard sectioning; not distinguishable from FIELD_SKILL norm in the accessible material.

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific rules found in accessible guidelines.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscript must not have been previously published or be under consideration elsewhere; publication must be approved by all co-authors and any relevant institutional authorities; if accepted, the work may not be republished elsewhere in similar form. These are standard originality/exclusivity declarations, not content-style prohibitions.

### Statistical Reporting
Not applicable in the FIELD_SKILL sense: sampled monographs are literature syntheses ("review applicable governance theories," "identify current knowledge gaps," "synthesis of current knowledge") rather than papers reporting original regression results. Where a monograph does contain original empirical work, FIELD_SKILL statistical-reporting norms presumably still apply, but the dominant article type in this journal does not feature a results/tables section of the kind FIELD_SKILL describes. STRUCTURAL DEVIATION (see below), not a reporting-norm change.

### Citation Density
Given the survey/tutorial nature of nearly every sampled piece, citation density is almost certainly higher and more sustained throughout the paper (not concentrated mainly in the introduction as FIELD_SKILL describes for empirical articles), since the entire monograph is a literature synthesis. PRACTICE ONLY, inferred from genre rather than directly observed in full text — flag as likely but unconfirmed.

### Structural Deviations
This is the dominant, ENFORCED delta. The journal's fundamental unit is a single-topic survey/tutorial/retrospective monograph (50–100+ pages), not a short original-research article with the canonical Introduction/Data/Results/Conclusion arc FIELD_SKILL describes. Typical structure observed across sampled titles: framing of the topic and its governance-theory foundations, thematic review sections organized by sub-topic (e.g., ownership structure, board composition, external controls), a synthesis of the state of knowledge, and a forward-looking research-gaps/future-directions section. There is no "Data" section, no empirical "Results" section with regression tables, and no "Robustness" section in the sampled titles — these are literature-synthesis works, not hypothesis-testing empirical papers. Authors targeting this journal should structure the piece as a field survey with a strong closing "future research directions" section, not as a standard empirical FINANCE paper.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Two-stage submission (abstract + TOC pre-approval before full draft) is unusual relative to the direct full-manuscript submission process assumed by FIELD_SKILL norms — ENFORCED per guidelines.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a standard short-form empirical article structure (Introduction/Data/Results/Robustness/Conclusion) instead of a long-form thematic survey/tutorial monograph — this journal's format is fundamentally different from the FIELD_SKILL default article shape.
- Skipping the required abstract-plus-table-of-contents pre-approval stage and sending a complete draft directly.
- Using parenthetical (Author, Year) citations instead of the house bracket style [Author, Year].

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
