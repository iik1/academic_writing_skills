DELTAS FROM FIELD_SKILL (account-field-writing-style)
================================================================================

Delta 1: Methodological Scope (RELAXATION)
  Issue:    FIELD_SKILL baseline derives from ABS 4*/4 quantitative archival/experimental
            tradition. AFGR explicitly welcomes broader range: empirical research,
            analytical papers, systematic reviews, qualitative studies, pedagogical work,
            critical essays.
  Evidence: 7 of 12 sampled articles are systematic/scoping reviews; 2 are qualitative
            participatory; only 2 are traditional empirical archival. This breadth is
            not reflected in FIELD_SKILL norms.
  Status:   PRACTICE ONLY (observed; not stated in guidelines, but overwhelming in
            publication record)
  Action:   Acknowledged in Skill; authors should expect reviews and qualitative work
            to be valued.

Delta 2: Citation Density in Review and Qualitative Articles (PRACTICE ONLY)
  Issue:    FIELD_SKILL specifies variable citation density by section (high intro/theory,
            low results) for archival papers. Reviews and qualitative work show high
            density throughout because methodology integrates literature throughout.
  Evidence: Systematic review articles (Batt 2025, Hasan & Miah 2024, Oliveira 2025,
            Smith & Hayden 2025) all maintain high citation throughout. Empirical archival
            papers (Miller 2024, Hammami & Sioud 2026) follow FIELD_SKILL pattern.
  Status:   PRACTICE ONLY (methodological necessity, not a deviation in style proper)
  Action:   Noted; no override needed; authors following different methods will
            naturally follow different citation patterns.

Delta 3: Hedging in Qualitative and Review Work (PRACTICE ONLY)
  Issue:    FIELD_SKILL specifies medium hedging calibrated to identification strength.
            Qualitative and review papers use more tentative language throughout
            (e.g., "suggest," "may indicate," "appears to") because systematic reviews
            frame findings as patterns across studies, not definitive claims.
  Evidence: Oliveira et al. (2025): "findings suggest organizations must adopt...",
            "accounts that matter may reflect...". Hasan & Miah (2024): "may enhance",
            "could potentially". These show higher baseline hedging than FIELD_SKILL
            predicts.
  Status:   PRACTICE ONLY (methodological necessity for review work)
  Action:   Noted in Skill; not a violation but a genre convention.

Delta 4: Discussion and Conclusion Structure (PRACTICE ONLY)
  Issue:    FIELD_SKILL notes Discussion and Conclusion merged is "more common" in
            accounting. AFGR systematic reviews and qualitative work more often separate:
            Conclusion, Limitations (or Research Gaps), Future Research Directions.
  Evidence: Smith & Hayden (2025): separate "Conclusion and Future Research" section.
            Hasan & Miah (2024): separate "Research Gaps" and "Future Research Directions"
            sections. Qualitative participatory work (O'Donohoe & Sheenan 2025) includes
            standalone "Limitations" and "Contributions & Implications" sections.
  Status:   PRACTICE ONLY (review/qualitative convention, not empirical archival)
  Action:   Noted; authors using these methods should expect separated sections to be
            valued.

Delta 5: Research Gaps and Limitations Emphasis (PRACTICE ONLY)
  Issue:    FIELD_SKILL mentions limitations as standard in conclusions. AFGR articles,
            especially reviews and qualitative, dedicate full sections (e.g., "Research
            Gaps," "Future Research Directions") to gaps and limitations even in
            shorter papers.
  Evidence: Almost all 12 articles include explicit research gaps or future research
            sections. Empirical archival (Miller 2024, Hammami & Sioud 2026) follow
            FIELD_SKILL pattern; reviews and qualitative work go further.
  Status:   PRACTICE ONLY (emphasis, not reversal)
  Action:   Noted; reflects journal's openness to exploratory/agenda-setting work.

CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The Accounting, Finance & Governance Review is a journal that adheres closely to
FIELD_SKILL norms for voice, register, argumentation, and reporting conventions.
Its primary delta from the ABS 4*/4 baseline is methodological: it welcomes systematic
reviews, qualitative studies, and pedagogical work alongside empirical archival
research. This reflects its ABS 1 status and its institutional role as the journal
of the Irish Accounting and Finance Association, serving a broader audience than
purely quantitative research communities.

Hard constraints on word/abstract length and cover page requirements are non-negotiable
and must be observed. Beyond these, authors can be confident that FIELD_SKILL provides
reliable guidance, with the understanding that review and qualitative methodologies
will naturally follow their own convention patterns (higher hedging, integrated citations,
separated conclusion sections, explicit gap identification).
