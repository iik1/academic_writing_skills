# Journal Writing Skill: Foundations and Trends in Finance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Now Publishers Inc. (distributed/hosted via Emerald Publishing)
- **Scope:** Publishes long-form survey, tutorial, and research-retrospective monographs (not original empirical research papers) that provide state-of-the-art, accessible reviews of established or emerging finance topics for both specialists and newcomers to the area.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.nowpublishers.com/Journal/AuthorInstructions/FIN (author instructions page; fetched via cached search results, direct fetch returned 403)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6 (front-matter/abstract level; full text paywalled) — "Financial Machine Learning" (Kelly & Xiu, 2023); "Currency Risk Premiums: A Multi-Horizon Perspective" (Chernov & Dahlquist, 2023); "Asset Allocation with Private Equity" (Korteweg & Westerfield, 2022); "Behavioralizing Finance" (Shefrin, 2010); "Corporate Governance Meets Data and Technology" (Jiang & Li, 2024); "What is Forensic Finance?" ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
This is NOT a standard empirical-article journal. The journal publishes only long review/tutorial/survey monographs and explicitly does not accept original stand-alone empirical research papers in the FIELD_SKILL sense. Internal organization is a numbered sequence of topical sections (survey-style), not the canonical Introduction/Data/Results/Conclusion arc assumed by FIELD_SKILL. Contributions are by invitation only: authors submit an abstract and table of contents for initial editorial review before a full draft is solicited.

### Length
Each monograph runs approximately 50–100 pages (observed published lengths range roughly 60–230+ pages, e.g. "Corporate Governance Meets Data and Technology" pp. 61–136; "What is Forensic Finance?" pp. 137–243).

### Abstract
Approximately 200 words (roughly the same order of magnitude as FIELD_SKILL's 150–250 word norm, so treat as FIELD DEFAULT with a slightly looser target).

### Citations
Alphabetical reference-list style (not numbered). Every reference must be cited in text. For three or more authors, in-text citation uses "et al." — consistent with FIELD_SKILL's author-year convention but explicitly codified in guidelines.

### Headings
Use "section" terminology throughout, not "chapter."

### Tables and Figures
Accepted formats are LaTeX (FnT LaTeX style files encouraged) or Microsoft Word. On acceptance, authors must supply manuscript source files, a PDF, and separate source files for figures and tables.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Authors must avoid format-specific terms — "chapter," "article," "book," or "paper" — when referring to the work itself. Instead use "monograph," "tutorial," "review," or "survey." This reflects the fact that the same content is later repackaged as a standalone book.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Genre shifts from primary empirical research to synthesis/survey writing. Framing centers on organizing and evaluating an existing literature for a mixed audience of specialists and newcomers ("designed for both financial economists interested in grasping machine learning tools, as well as for statisticians and machine learners...") rather than establishing a single paper's novel identification and contribution. Tone remains formal and authoritative, consistent with FIELD_SKILL, but self-positioning is expository ("this survey covers...", "we survey the literature on...") rather than the FIELD_SKILL contribution-enumeration pattern ("First, we show...Second..."). PRACTICE ONLY, evidenced across all sampled abstracts.

### Structural Deviations
Sections are organized by topic/theme rather than by the empirical-paper arc (Intro/Data/Results/Conclusion). Example: "Asset Allocation with Private Equity" organizes by institutional background, portfolio fit, performance measurement, performance persistence, and the LP's commitment-management problem; "Behavioralizing Finance" organizes by the five domains of the field (beliefs/preferences, portfolio selection, asset pricing, corporate finance, regulation). ENFORCED (matches guidelines' survey/tutorial framing and is confirmed structurally across all sampled monographs).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Because acceptance is by invitation following an abstract/TOC pre-review, there is no cold open-submission pipeline as assumed generically by FIELD_SKILL-adjacent journals. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting as if this were a standard empirical-paper venue: unsolicited original-research submissions are out of scope — the journal only publishes invited surveys/tutorials/retrospectives.
- Using "paper," "article," or "chapter" to refer to the manuscript in the text itself; use "monograph," "survey," "tutorial," or "section" instead.
- Underestimating length: entries average well beyond a standard journal-article page count (50–100+ pages), so content must be scoped as a field-level review, not a single-study report.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
