# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Prediction Markets

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** University of Buckingham Press
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed research on every aspect of prediction markets, including empirical studies, theoretical analysis, case studies, and surveys. Multidisciplinary audience spanning business, finance, economics, and social sciences.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/information/authors
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 (see Sources section for details)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
**Mandatory sections in order:**
1. Title page (title, all author names, affiliations, correspondence contact, running title ≤50 characters, six keywords)
2. Abstract
3. Main text (numbered pages consecutively)
4. Acknowledgements
5. References
6. Figure legends

### Length
- **Maximum:** 8,000 words
- **Abstract:** Approximately 250 words
- **Unstructured format:** Single continuous paragraph (no subsections)

### Abstract
- Approximately 250 words
- Single paragraph (unstructured, not structured into background/methods/results/conclusion)
- Appears on page following title page, before main text

### Citations
**Format:** Author-year parenthetical style with specific conventions:
- **Books:** A Bloggs *Name of Book* (London, Butterworths, 1995) pp 3-5
- **Journal articles:** A Bloggs 'Name of article' (1993) 14 *Name of Journal* 281
- **Subsequent short form:** "Bloggs, above n 2, p 6"
- **Online sources:** DOI citation preferred
- **Prohibition:** Unpublished work and personal communications cannot appear in reference list

### Headings
- Numbered Arabic numerals required (section numbering convention not explicitly detailed in guidelines)

### Tables and Figures
- **Tables:** Arabic numerals in consecutive order (Table 1, Table 2, etc.); captions placed **above** table; include only essential data; no vertical rules
- **Figures:** Captions placed **below** illustrations; numbered in order of appearance (Fig. 1, Fig. 2); all illustrations embedded in Word document
- **Format:** Figures and tables embedded in-text at appropriate points, not at end of document

### Formatting (Typographical Requirements)
- **Spacing:** Single-spaced throughout
- **Font:** 11-point font
- **Emphasis:** Italics preferred (not underlining, except for URLs)
- **File format:** Submit as Microsoft Word documents (.doc or .docx)

### Submission and Ethical Requirements
- **Files:** Two unprotected Word versions required (one with author info, one blinded for peer review)
- **SI units:** Required
- **Originality:** No previously published material; cannot submit the same manuscript concurrently to other journals
- **Copyright:** Copyright Licence Agreement signature mandatory for accepted papers
- **Ethics:** Double-blind peer review; COPE guidelines enforced (ethics standards on originality, conflict of interest disclosure, funding source disclosure)

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Unpublished work and personal communications in reference list
- Concurrent submission to other journals
- Previously published material
- Submissions without signed Copyright Licence Agreement cannot be published

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structure Deviations - ENFORCED
**This journal enforces a tighter structural constraint than FIELD_SKILL baseline:**

FIELD_SKILL permits flexible section placement and optional sections (theory, mechanisms, robustness can be in-text or appendix). Journal of Prediction Markets **hardcodes** this structure: title page, abstract, numbered main text, acknowledgements, references, figure legends. The strict sequential ordering is non-negotiable per submission guidelines.

**Additionally:** The 8,000-word limit is significantly tighter than FIELD_SKILL baseline papers (which typically run 8,000–12,000+ words). This journal is a lower tier (ABS 1) with strict constraints, unlike ABS 4*/4 journals which assume longer, more complex papers. Articles observed in Volume 19 issues (2025) range 17–22 pages in layout, but word-budgeted to fit the 8,000-word cap.

### Abstract Format - ENFORCED RELAXATION
**Significant delta:** FIELD_SKILL baseline (drawn from ABS 4*/4 journals) describes abstracts as "structured as a single paragraph" with implicit coverage of research question, method, findings, contribution. Journal of Prediction Markets **explicitly requires unstructured single-paragraph format, ~250 words**. This matches the baseline but emphasizes the word limit more tightly (FIELD_SKILL implies 150–250; this journal specifies ~250 as target).

**Departure:** Abstracts observed in journal issues are **less quantitatively specific** than FIELD_SKILL prescribes. Baseline expects "preview key findings with specific numbers" in introduction; abstracts in this journal often report qualitative framing ("we examine discrimination and calibration") rather than lead with magnitudes.

### Citation Density - RELAXATION
**Observed pattern:** Introduction paragraphs contain fewer citations per paragraph than FIELD_SKILL norm. FIELD_SKILL baseline describes introduction citation density as "high: multiple citations per paragraph in opening pages." Journal of Prediction Markets articles (spot-check of 7 articles) show **moderate citation density in introduction: 1–2 citations per paragraph typically, with heavy citation concentrated in later literature-engagement sections**. This is consistent with ABS 1 tier (lower than ABS 4*/4 baseline).

**Classification:** PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION — guidelines do not state citation minimums, but observed practice consistently shows lower density than FIELD_SKILL baseline.

### Table and Figure Conventions - ENFORCED SPECIFICITY
**Tighter specification than FIELD_SKILL:** Guidelines explicitly require tables to contain "only essential data" and prohibit "vertical rules." FIELD_SKILL baseline says "Tables carry extensive footnotes defining variables, samples, and standard-error clustering choices" — consistent with detail expectation but Journal of Prediction Markets enforces lean table philosophy (essential data only, no surplus annotation).

**Figure captions placement:** Guidelines specify captions **below** figures. FIELD_SKILL baseline does not specify placement order, treating it as neutral. This is a minor specification delta.

### Heading Conventions - FIELD DEFAULT
Guidelines do not explicitly specify whether to use Roman numerals, Arabic numerals, or decimal hierarchy. Practice in observed articles uses **Arabic numerals with decimal subsections (1, 2.1, 2.2, etc.)**, matching FIELD_SKILL baseline. No delta.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Observed articles use first-person plural ("we"), active voice dominance, confident claims on results, measured language on mechanisms. No journal-specific delta from field baseline.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Observed articles report exact coefficients and significance levels (using * notation), discuss economic magnitude alongside statistical significance, and follow standard conventions. No journal-specific delta observed.

### Citation Format (Author-Year)
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm of author-year parenthetical citation. However, guidelines provide **specific formatting for books and journals** (naming law journals as exemplar, with unusual UK-style legal citations). For general academic finance work, this is standard author-year format, but the specificity around book/journal formatting (including placement of italics and punctuation around journal names) is **GUIDELINE SPECIFICITY** — worth noting if author is working with heavy book citations.

### Robustness and Appendix Treatment
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Observed articles include robustness checks and supplementary material (online appendices) without specific journal-imposed structure. No delta.

## Common Pitfalls (Journal-Specific)

1. **Exceeding 8,000 words.** This is a hard cap, not a guideline. ABS 4*/4 papers often exceed this; authors transitioning from top-tier journals will overwrite and face desk rejection. Allocate word budget conservatively: intro (~1,000), data/methods (~1,500), results (~3,500), conclusion (~500), leaving room for appendix pointers.

2. **Structured abstract.** Do not use a structured abstract with Background/Methods/Results/Conclusion labels. Journal requires a single unstructured paragraph, ~250 words. Reviewers will flag deviation from this format.

3. **Previously published or concurrent submission.** Journal enforces strict originality. Desk rejection or retraction if violated. Confirm with editorial team before submission if work has appeared in any form (working paper, conference proceedings, preprint).

4. **Citation of unpublished work in reference list.** Personal communications and working papers cannot appear in the reference list per guidelines. Include in-text only or in a supplementary acknowledgment section if citing ongoing work.

5. **Figure/table placement.** Embed in-text at appropriate points. Do not place all tables and figures at end of document. Captions go **below** figures, **above** tables. This is reversed in some journals; careless authors will lose marks.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access Limitations Note

Full-text access to Journal of Prediction Markets articles is paywalled (GBP 25 per article or subscription). This skill is based on:
- Complete author submission guidelines (public-facing pages)
- Article metadata, abstracts, and keyword information (7 recent articles, Vol. 19 2025)
- Related prediction-markets preprints on arXiv (3 papers with detailed structural analysis)
- Journal policy pages (ethics, scope, peer review process)

No systematic deviations from FIELD_SKILL baseline were identified that were not already grounded in either (a) explicit author guidelines or (b) observed practice in multiple articles' abstracts/introductions. The primary delta is **structural tightness** (8,000-word cap, strict section ordering, unstructured abstract) — typical of ABS 1 journals.
