# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Business and Economic Statistics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis (on behalf of the American Statistical Association)
- **Scope:** Publishes high-quality methodological contributions in econometrics and statistics oriented toward applications in microeconomics, macroeconomics, business, and finance; theoretical contributions are expected to have clear empirical relevance and typically include a substantive empirical application.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/ubes20 (Instructions for Authors page found via search-engine snippets; full guidelines page itself returned 403 on direct fetch — accessed via cached/indexed content)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 direct (1 full-text working-paper version via arXiv: Clark, Huber, and Koop, "A Flexible Approach to Augmenting a Bayesian VAR with Nonlinear Factors," forthcoming/2026; abstracts/metadata for Kolesár and Plagborg-Møller (2025), "Dynamic Causal Effects in a Nonlinear World," Vol 43(4); and Hauzenberger et al. (2025), "Gaussian Process Vector Autoregressions," Vol 43) plus a broader abstract/title survey of ~10 additional recent JBES articles (2011–2025 issues) via IDEAS/RePEc and Taylor & Francis search snippets to confirm topical scope and structural conventions.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section structure beyond the field-standard empirical/methodological format; JBES does not impose a fixed section list. One structural feature specific to this journal: JBES routinely publishes **Comment** and **Rejoinder** pieces attached to a lead article (a discussion-paper format), a recognizable recurring category not addressed in FIELD_SKILL.

### Length
Manuscripts should normally be 15–30 pages, not exceeding 40 pages (including references, tables, and figures), in 12-point Times, Helvetica, or Courier, double-spaced. Submissions over 40 pages require prior discussion with the Managing Editor; reviewers will expect a contribution commensurate with the extra length.

### Abstract
No distinct structured-abstract format found; standard single-paragraph unstructured abstract, consistent with FIELD_SKILL. JEL codes and keywords are included below the abstract (confirmed in spot-checked paper).

### Citations
References follow APA 7th edition style — a deviation from the general author-year-parenthetical norm described only generically in FIELD_SKILL; APA 7th imposes specific formatting (e.g., DOIs included where available, specific punctuation/ordering conventions) that authors should follow precisely rather than using ad hoc author-year formatting. Only cited references are listed.

### Headings
No journal-specific heading convention beyond field default.

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific rule beyond field default found.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscripts must be double-blind: stripped of all author-identifying information in the main file. A separate cover/title page must be uploaded containing author names, affiliations, contact information, and a brief (50-word) biographical sketch of each author — a submission-mechanics requirement not found in FIELD_SKILL or typical of ABS 4* econ journals' double-blind norms (which usually require only omission of identifying detail, not a biographical sketch).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm — first-person plural, active voice, high but economical formality, confirmed in full-text spot-check.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. As expected for a methods-oriented statistics/econometrics journal, papers place heavier emphasis on Monte Carlo simulation evidence (data-generating-process design, coverage rates, relative MAE/log-scores) alongside a real-data application, more so than the typical ABS 4 empirical paper — this is a matter of degree consistent with the journal's methodological scope rather than a stated or enforced norm, so it is treated as PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence rather than elevated to a stated dimension.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Structural Deviations
PRACTICE ONLY: many JBES papers place a substantial "Priors"/"Identification" or algorithmic subsection (e.g., MCMC sampler details, prior specification) either in the main text or relegated to an online technical appendix, more extensively than typical ABS 4 empirical papers. This reflects the journal's Bayesian/methodological article mix rather than a stated requirement, and is common but not universal across ECON generally — labelled PRACTICE ONLY given the journal's ABS 4 (not 4*) status.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
GUIDELINE ONLY: the Comment/Rejoinder discussion format (noted under Structure) recurs across volumes (confirmed via multiple Rejoinder entries in the archive) but applies only to a minority of lead articles selected by the editors, not something an ordinary submitting author designs for.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding 40 pages without first contacting the Managing Editor.
- Formatting references in generic author-year style rather than strict APA 7th (missing DOIs, incorrect punctuation).
- Leaving author-identifying information in the main manuscript file instead of the separate cover page (breaks double-blind review).
- Omitting the required 50-word biographical sketch from the cover page.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
