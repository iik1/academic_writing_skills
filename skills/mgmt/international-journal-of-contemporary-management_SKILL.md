# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Contemporary Management

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN (general management/business journal; publishes across strategic management, operations, marketing, finance, HR)
- **Publisher:** Faculty of Management and Social Communication, Jagiellonian University (Kraków); distributed via Sciendo/De Gruyter
- **Scope:** Publishes empirical and theoretical research on contemporary management challenges, requiring every submission to include an organizational/management dimension. Accepts conceptual papers, empirical (qualitative or quantitative) studies, case studies, and systematic literature reviews.
- **Guidelines URL:** Submission Guidance PDF via https://sciendo-parsed-data-feed.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/IJCM/Submission_Guidance.pdf (linked from https://reference-global.com/journal/IJCM); submission portal https://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/qijcm
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 13 (abstracts/metadata via IDEAS/RePEc and search snippets; full text not accessible — see Sources). Titles/DOIs: "The Theory of Entrepreneurship Becoming as an Analytical Framework" (v61i1, 2025); "Interdisciplinary Approach to Sustainable Employee Development" (v61i1, 2025); "Human Resource Differentiation and Labor Market Segmentation: A Systematic Review" (v61i1, 2025); "Foreign ownership and gender differences in pay" (v60i1, 2024); "Trust in Managers in SME Organizations Undergoing Digitization" (v59i4, 2023); plus 8 additional titles surveyed for structural pattern only (2021-2026 issues).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Mandatory main-text section order: **Introduction → Theoretical Background → Research Methodology → Results → Discussion → Conclusions.** This is a fixed, prescribed sequence (not a template that varies by article type as in FIELD_SKILL). Manuscript-level element order: title, authors/affiliations/ORCID, abstract, keywords, main text, acknowledgments, references, figure/table caption list.

### Length
Entire manuscript (including tables, figures, references) should be approximately **7,000–9,000 words**. Longer papers accepted only if they make a substantial theoretical/knowledge contribution. Title must not exceed 15 words.

### Abstract
**Structured abstract required, max 250 words**, using a fixed labeled scheme: **Purpose → Design/methodology/approach → Findings → Practical implications (optional) → Social implications (optional) → Originality/value.** Placed below the title, above keywords. Followed by **5-8 keywords**.

### Citations
APA (2017 edition / APA 7th) throughout.

### Headings
Concise, with clear hierarchy signaling required (no further detail specified beyond this).

### Tables and Figures
Numbered consecutively with Arabic numerals; highest available quality; numbers/text must be clearly legible.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **No footnotes or endnotes** except where "absolutely necessary" — information judged important must go in the main text instead.
- No active hyperlinks in the submitted file.
- Author-identifying data (name, affiliation, ORCID) must be stripped from the main document before system upload (double-blind review).
- Formal ethics-committee approval statement required for any research involving human participants (Helsinki Declaration compliance), submitted as a separate signed supplemental file.
- Ghostwriting-procedure statement required at submission.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
FIELD_SKILL describes four possible structural templates depending on article type (quantitative deductive, qualitative inductive, theoretical/conceptual, integrative review) with section labels varying accordingly (e.g., "Findings" for qualitative, "Results" for quantitative). IJCM collapses this into a **single mandatory section sequence** applied uniformly — "Results" and "Discussion" are used as fixed labels even in qualitative/SLR papers, rather than the FIELD_SKILL norm of qualitative work preferring "Findings." ENFORCED (stated in guidelines; spot-checked abstracts consistently reference "Findings" as an abstract-scheme label, but the guidance's main-text heading list uses "Results," suggesting authors follow the prescribed template over field convention). Classification: ENFORCED / RELAXATION-adjacent departure — journal imposes a simpler one-size-fits-all structure appropriate to its ABS 1 tier, rather than the tiered structural sophistication of FIELD_SKILL's top-tier outlets.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
- The structured abstract format (Purpose/Design-methodology-approach/Findings/Practical implications/Social implications/Originality-value) is an Emerald-style structured abstract, not mentioned anywhere in FIELD_SKILL (which only specifies unstructured 150-250 word abstracts with no citations). ENFORCED — confirmed present in all spot-checked abstracts.
- Systematic literature reviews are unusually prevalent in the sampled issues (multiple SLRs per issue across 2021-2026), a higher proportion than FIELD_SKILL's "Integrative review" category would suggest is typical for a management journal's overall mix. PRACTICE ONLY / low confidence given small sample — flagged as a pattern, not a stated norm.
- Papers frequently center on Polish/Central European organizational contexts even when not required by guidelines. PRACTICE ONLY — reflects author base, not a stated editorial requirement; do not treat as mandatory framing for submissions.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting an unstructured abstract or omitting the Purpose/Design-methodology-approach/Findings/Originality-value labels — this journal requires the labeled Emerald-style scheme, not free-text.
- Using footnotes for substantive content instead of folding it into the main text — footnotes/endnotes are discouraged except when unavoidable.
- Leaving author-identifying metadata (name, affiliation, ORCID) in the submitted Word file, which breaks the double-blind process.
- Exceeding ~9,000 words without a clear, substantial theoretical contribution justifying the length.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
