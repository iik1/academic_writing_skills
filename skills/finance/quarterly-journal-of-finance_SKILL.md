# Journal Writing Skill: Quarterly Journal of Finance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** World Scientific Publishing (on behalf of Midwest Finance Association)
- **Scope:** Quarterly peer-reviewed journal publishing research across all areas of finance, including corporate finance, asset pricing, financial econometrics, international finance, macro-finance, behavioral finance, banking and financial intermediation, capital markets, risk management, derivatives, and quantitative finance.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.worldscientific.com/page/qjf/submission-guidelines (limited publicly accessible content)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 articles sampled from 2024-2025 issues via IDEAS/RePEc abstract listings: "Videogame Attributes as Alternative Investments" (Vol. 15, No. 04); "The Treasury–SOFR Swap Spread Puzzle Explained" (Vol. 15, No. 04); "Trading Behavior of Multi-Firm Insiders" (Vol. 15, No. 04); "Behind the Curtain: Analysis of TDFs' Holdings" (Vol. 15, No. 03); "Reporting Quality and Stock Returns of de-SPAC Mergers" (Vol. 15, No. 03)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
First page includes: manuscript title, list of authors with affiliations and email addresses, and designated corresponding author. Manuscript body followed by references. Tables placed on separate pages after references with placement instructions in text. [ENFORCED]

### Length
No explicit word or page limit identified in publicly accessible guidelines. World Scientific standard practice across journals: manuscript, abstract, keywords, references, tables, and figures are expected; no documented maximum identified for QJF specifically. [FIELD DEFAULT]

### Abstract
Maximum 100 words. Single paragraph format (unstructured). Separate from the body of the paper. Keywords (three to five) listed on a separate page. Do not include references, footnotes, or abbreviations in the abstract. [ENFORCED]

### Manuscript Formatting
Double-spaced throughout with minimum 11-point font. Submit as PDF initially; accepted papers require final version in .doc or LaTeX format. [ENFORCED]

### Citations
Author-year parenthetical system employed throughout (e.g., "Smith and Jones (1995)" inline; "(Smith and Jones, 1995)" parenthetical). References listed alphabetically by author's last name at end of text, arranged chronologically for each author. [ENFORCED]

### Tables and Figures
- Tables: Center the word "Table" followed by an Arabic numeral above the body; separate headings from title and body with solid lines.
- Figures: Numbered with Arabic numerals.
- Placement: Tables and figures appear at end of manuscript after references, with placement instructions in text ("INSERT TABLE 1 HERE" style).
- All tables and figures must be numbered and include legends.
[ENFORCED]

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Papers under simultaneous consideration at another journal: prohibited
- Non-original work: prohibited
- Copyright: Upon acceptance, authors sign agreement transferring copyright to Midwest Finance Association, Quarterly Journal of Finance, and Publisher (World Scientific)
[ENFORCED]

### Structural Deviations
**Field norm (FIELD_SKILL):** Almost all papers include a "Data / Data and Institutional Background" section, often merged with Methods. Robustness sections vary (dedicated section or embedded in results/appendix). The introduction "rarely given an explicit heading numbered '1' in isolation."

**QJF practice:** No deviations from FIELD_SKILL baseline observed in sample articles. Papers follow canonical structure (Introduction, Data, Empirical Analysis, Conclusion) consistent with field norms. No evidence of systematic structural divergence at ABS 1 tier. [FIELD DEFAULT]

**Field norm (FIELD_SKILL):** Structured single paragraph, typically 150–250 words, states research question, method, key findings, and contribution.

**QJF guideline:** Maximum 100 words. This is a tighter constraint than the field baseline but does not fundamentally alter the paragraph structure. Abstract must be concise and omit references, footnotes, and abbreviations. [RELAXATION — shorter word limit but same single-paragraph format and content expectations]

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Abstract exceeding 100 words** — QJF enforces strict adherence to the 100-word limit. Reviewers flag overlong abstracts. Ensure abstract is concise; remove any non-essential detail.

2. **Including references, footnotes, or abbreviations in the abstract** — Explicitly prohibited. Strip the abstract of any parenthetical citations, footnote markers, or undefined terms before submission.

3. **Manuscript formatting (double-spacing, font size)** — Violations of double-spacing or use of fonts smaller than 11 point trigger desk rejection. Verify formatting before submission.

4. **Table placement and numbering inconsistency** — Tables must appear after references with clear placement instructions in text. Inconsistent or inline table placement violates journal format, leading to revision requests.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
