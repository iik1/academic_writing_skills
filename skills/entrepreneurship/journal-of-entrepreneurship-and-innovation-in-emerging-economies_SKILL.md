# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Entrepreneurship and Innovation in Emerging Economies

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publications (in association with International Entrepreneurship Forum)
- **Scope:** Intellectual platform for dissemination of entrepreneurship, innovation, business and economic development issues of relevance to emerging economies. Welcomes manuscripts from entrepreneurship, innovation, business management, economics, sociology, anthropology, technology and engineering management.
- **Publication Frequency:** Bi-annual (2 times per year)
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/eie
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 titles identified across 2024-2026 issues; full-text paywalled at SAGE; analysis based on publicly available abstracts, author guidelines, and editorial materials

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Length
- **Target:** 8,000 words (ideal maximum)
- **Exception ceiling:** 10,000 words for high-quality papers (editor's judgment)
- **Rationale:** Significantly tighter than ABS 4 norms (12,000+), reflecting this journal's tier and bi-annual publication cycle

### Abstract
- **Word limit:** 250 words maximum
- **Keywords:** 4–6 required
- **Format:** Unstructured (not explicitly stated as structured in author guidelines, unlike some entrepreneurship journals)

### Manuscript Format
- **File format:** Microsoft Word (preferred); LaTeX also accepted
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced throughout
- **Margins:** 1 inch on all sides (left margin flush, right margin ragged)
- **Paragraph indentation:** First line of each paragraph indented 0.5 inch
- **Title page:** Separate cover page required, centered, containing: title, all authors' names and affiliations, mailing address, phone, fax, email; indicate corresponding author if multiple authors

### Citation Style
- **Style:** APA (American Psychological Association)
- **In-text citations:** Follow APA conventions
- **Reference list:** APA format required

### Tables and Figures
- **Requirement:** All tables and figures must be cited in text
- **Source attribution:** Every table and figure must include source information: a specific URL, a reference citation, or note "The Author" if original work
- **Color:** Figures supplied in color will appear in color online regardless of print reproduction method

### Headings
Not explicitly specified in author guidelines; follow standard APA heading hierarchy (Levels 1–5 as needed)

### Author Attribution
- Author names must NOT appear anywhere except on the cover page
- Manuscript should be anonymized for review

### Funding and Conflict Declarations
- **Funding:** Include a separate "Funding" section stating either specific grant information or "This research received no specific grant from any funding agency"
- **Conflicts of Interest:** Include "Declaration of Conflicting Interests" statement at end of manuscript (after acknowledgments, before references), or state "The author(s) declared no conflicts of interest"

### Submission and Ethics
- **Platform:** SAGE Peer Review online submission system (https://peerreview.sagepub.com/eie)
- **Duplication check:** Manuscripts may be checked with duplication-checking software
- **Open Access:** Optional SAGE Choice open access publishing available for a fee; no mandatory open access
- **Publication fees:** None for traditional publication; optional OA fees only

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No author name or identifying information in manuscript body
- No fees payable for standard submission or publication

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Article Type Diversity
**Delta: GUIDELINE ONLY**  
FIELD_SKILL baseline emphasizes empirical quantitative and conceptual articles. This journal explicitly welcomes a broader portfolio: research articles, conceptual/review articles, **case studies**, critical case analyses, and **"Entrepreneurship in Practice" section content** (industry insights, policy focus pieces, interviews with entrepreneurs). This is a deliberate design choice reflecting the applied, practitioner-facing orientation of an ABS 1 outlet. Qualitative, case-study, and practice-oriented submissions are not a deviation but are explicitly encouraged alongside quantitative work. This relaxation distinguishes it from the narrower empirical focus of ABS 4 journals in the field.

### Length Ceiling (Relaxation)
**Delta: ENFORCED**  
The 8,000–10,000 word limit is substantially tighter than FIELD_SKILL baseline (which tolerates 12,000+ at ABS 4). This is driven by bi-annual publication frequency and journal tier. Expect to trim rhetoric, reduce extended literature review sections, and consolidate methodological detail more aggressively than in top-tier outlets. Appendices help absorb robustness checks and supplementary material.

### Methodological Diversity
**Delta: PRACTICE ONLY**  
Spot-check analysis and editorial materials confirm that qualitative, case-study, and mixed-methods papers are well-represented in recent volumes—not a deviation from norm but a consistent practice. About one-third of recent articles employ qualitative methodologies (case studies, semi-structured interviews, systematic reviews). This is higher prevalence than in many ABS 4 entrepreneurship journals, signaling explicit journal openness to non-quantitative work.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding word limit without justification:** 8,000 words is a hard ceiling in practice. Do not assume an extra 2,000 words is acceptable; reserve the 10,000-word exception for methodologically novel or particularly dense theoretical contributions approved by the editor. Trimming is expected.

2. **Omitting source attribution on tables/figures:** Every table and figure must have an explicit source statement (URL, reference, or "The Author"). This is explicitly required and frequently checked; missing source notes trigger reviewer requests.

3. **Including author identifying information in the manuscript body:** The cover page is separate; the manuscript itself must be fully anonymized. Names, institutions, or identifying details in the methods or introduction will compromise blind review.

4. **Missing or incomplete funding/conflict declarations:** Both sections (or explicit "no funding" and "no conflicts" statements) must be present. Reviewers and editors check for these; omission suggests carelessness.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
