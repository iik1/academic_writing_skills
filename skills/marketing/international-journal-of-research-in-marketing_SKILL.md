# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Research in Marketing

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** MKT
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** General marketing journal covering marketing management, consumer behavior, marketing strategy, marketing research methodology, and the marketing mix, with a strong European/international editorial base; publishes conceptual, quantitative/econometric, and behavioral/experimental work side by side.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/international-journal-of-research-in-marketing/publish/guide-for-authors (partially accessible; ScienceDirect blocked full fetch, guidance reconstructed from search-indexed excerpts)
- **Field baseline:** mkt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 substantive articles + 2 co-editor/editorial perspective pieces (Claus, Eelen & Warlop 2025 "This tree in the forest is mine"; Vomberg & von Gegerfelt 2025 NFT buyer segmentation; Miller, Lukic & Skiera 2025/2026 GDPR online tracking; Edeling & Wies 2024 "creatrepreneurs"; Bernritter, Danatzis, Möller-Herm & Sotgiu 2025 brand safety in multiplayer games; Proserpio et al. 2025 sustainability programs on Amazon; Brandes & Dölp 2025 NFTs as brand extensions; Reinartz 2026 CRM past/present/future; Welden, Johnston & Hasford 2025 gender expectations in game livestreaming; Tang, Huang & Zhang 2026 logo complexity; plus Pauwels et al. 2026 "How to publish in a top marketing journal" and Stremersch et al. 2026 "The Bible of publishing" editorials). Full text mostly paywalled; abstracts, editorial content, and search-engine-extracted summaries used.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No rigid mandated section template at submission; Elsevier's general "your paper your way" flexibility applies. IJRM accepts a wider mix of article types than FIELD_SKILL's three templates imply — bibliometric/text-mining reviews (LDA topic modeling), conceptual/taxonomy papers built from expert interviews, econometric field studies, multi-study behavioral papers, and segmentation studies all appear as first-class formats, not niche exceptions.

### Length
Not specified in the accessible guidelines excerpt; no explicit word cap surfaced. Follow FIELD_SKILL norm pending confirmation.

### Abstract
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (unstructured, no citations).

### Citations
GUIDELINE ONLY / RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL default: the guide for authors explicitly states the journal "does not set strict requirements on reference formatting at submission" — references may be in any consistent style, provided author names, titles, year, volume, and pagination/DOI are present. Do not assume strict APA is enforced pre-acceptance; match the target style only if instructed otherwise or preparing a final production file.

### Headings
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Tables and Figures
Graphical abstracts, if submitted, must be a minimum of 531 × 1328 px and readable at 5×13 cm; generative AI/AI-assisted tools are explicitly **not permitted** for producing graphical abstract artwork.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Authors must declare any use of generative AI or AI-assisted technologies in manuscript preparation upon submission (disclosure requirement, not a ban on use of AI for text).
- AI tools may not be used to generate graphical abstract artwork.
- The journal explicitly states that "replications and minor methodological or theoretical improvements will generally not be publishable" — incremental contributions are discouraged at the submission-fit stage, not just in review.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Voice and Register
PRACTICE ONLY: across the sampled abstracts, framing leans more toward a real-world managerial hook than FIELD_SKILL's theory-first default even in econometric/quantitative pieces — e.g., GDPR tracking, NFT brand extensions, toxic gaming environments, mobile payment friction are all introduced through a business/managerial problem rather than a theoretical gap first. This matches the co-editors' own stated criteria (see Structural Deviations below) rather than being a stylistic quirk of a few papers.

### Structural Deviations
ENFORCED (stated by co-editors in a 2026 editorial, consistent with observed articles): Pauwels et al.'s "How to publish in a top marketing journal" (IJRM 2026), written from the co-editors' first year handling submissions, states that accepted papers across *all* subfields (quantitative marketing, inductive scholarship, consumer behavior, marketing strategy) are evaluated on five shared qualities: an intellectually intriguing, stakeholder-relevant problem; well-executed appropriate methods; actionable insight for a specific audience; a clear path for future research; and precise, low-effort-to-read communication. This is a stronger and more explicit emphasis on managerial/practitioner actionability than FIELD_SKILL describes for JCR/JCP-style behavioral papers, where theory is primary and managerial relevance secondary. For IJRM, treat practical/managerial payoff as co-equal to theoretical contribution regardless of methodological tradition.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY: article-type diversity is unusually high for a single ABS-4 marketing outlet — accepted formats observed include large-scale bibliometric/topic-modeling literature reviews (Reinartz 2026, using LDA on 1,025 articles), qualitative/expert-interview-based taxonomy and framework papers (Edeling & Wies 2024, eight expert interviews), and empirical segmentation studies with named consumer segments (Vomberg & von Gegerfelt 2025), alongside the standard experimental and econometric formats FIELD_SKILL already covers. When drafting for IJRM, do not default only to the Study 1/Study 2 or Data/Model/Results templates — a review/synthesis or qualitative-taxonomy structure is an equally legitimate and evidently welcomed format.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming APA reference formatting is mandatory pre-acceptance — IJRM's guide for authors explicitly relaxes this; do not spend drafting effort forcing strict APA at submission stage.
- Framing a quantitative/econometric paper purely around identification strategy without an explicit managerial or stakeholder-relevant hook — the co-editors' stated criteria weight practical actionability heavily across all subfields, not just behavioral work.
- Pitching incremental replications or minor theoretical/methodological extensions — the journal's own guidelines flag these as generally unpublishable, more explicitly than the typical ABS-4 marketing outlet.
- Defaulting to a single rigid template (e.g., always Study 1/Study 2/General Discussion) — IJRM visibly publishes bibliometric reviews, qualitative taxonomy papers, and large-panel econometric studies as equally standard formats.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
