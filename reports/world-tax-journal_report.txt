DELTAS FROM FIELD BASELINE (account-field-writing-style)
================================================================================

Critical Deltas (ENFORCED, HIGH-IMPACT):
1. CITATION FORMAT: Chicago Manual 18th ed., Notes & Bibliography (footnotes)
   vs. FIELD_SKILL's author-date parenthetical. Fundamental restructuring
   required.
2. MANUSCRIPT LENGTH: Capped at 28,000 words (preferred ≤28,000; ENFORCED).
3. TABLE OF CONTENTS: Required at submission (non-standard for ACCOUNT field).
4. ABSTRACT: 150-word maximum, strictly enforced; no undefined abbreviations.
5. HEADING NUMBERS: Fixed Arabic numerals (1, 2, 2.1, 2.1.1); max 4 levels;
   manual numbering required.

Moderate Deltas (GUIDELINE):
6. INTRODUCTION & CONCLUSION: Must appear as explicit, labeled sections
   (FIELD_SKILL permits merged sections).
7. BRITISH SPELLING: Oxford 11th edition, mandatory.
8. EMPHASIS: Italics only, no bold (FIELD_SKILL does not restrict).
9. LISTS: Numbered (1), (2), (3) or hyphens, no bullets (FIELD_SKILL unspecified).

Relaxations (ABS 1 practice):
10. SCOPE: Single-jurisdiction submissions permitted if demonstrating
    international relevance (more permissive than higher-tier journals).

================================================================================
FIELD_SKILL OVERRIDES
================================================================================

The following FIELD_SKILL conventions are OVERRIDDEN by WTJ:

1. Citation style → Chicago Notes & Bibliography instead of author-date
2. Citation density section patterns → All sections now use footnotes instead
   of varying parenthetical density
3. Formatting conventions → Mandatory British spelling, italics-only emphasis,
   specific list format (not specified in FIELD_SKILL)
4. Structure → Introduction and Conclusion must be explicit sections with
   numbered headings

All other FIELD_SKILL dimensions (voice, register, hedging, methods conventions,
argumentation structure, results reporting norms) remain applicable.

================================================================================
COMMON PITFALLS (DOCUMENTED)
================================================================================

1. Citation format inertia — Author-date citations are field-standard in
   ACCOUNT; WTJ's Chicago Notes-Bib requires complete rewriting.

2. Abstract violations — 150-word limit is firm; abstracts with abbreviations
   or forward footnote references are desk-rejected.

3. Missing table of contents — This is a mandatory submission element for WTJ;
   omission triggers desk rejection.

4. Word count inflation — 28,000-word cap is tighter than some ACCOUNT journals
   (which allow 35,000+).

5. Ambiguous section numbering — Auto-numbered headings in Word drift during
   editing; WTJ requires fixed, manually-entered numbers; violations flagged as
   manuscript non-compliance.

================================================================================
CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The World Tax Journal's most significant deviations from the ACCOUNT field
baseline are: (1) citation format (Chicago Notes & Bibliography vs. author-date),
(2) mandatory table of contents, (3) explicit Introduction/Conclusion section
structure, (4) strict 150-word abstract limit, and (5) British spelling
requirement.

The journal's substantive writing conventions (voice, argument structure,
hedging, methods reporting) align closely with FIELD_SKILL, indicating that
the journal follows broader ACCOUNT field norms despite lower ABS ranking.

Authors from ACCOUNT background should prioritize learning Chicago Notes &
Bibliography format before submission.
