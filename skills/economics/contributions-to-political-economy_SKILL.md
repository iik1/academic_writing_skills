# Journal Writing Skill: Contributions to Political Economy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Scope:** Forum for academic discussion within critical traditions in economic analysis: Classical political economists, Marx, Keynes, and Sraffa. Heterodox and non-mainstream perspectives.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://academic.oup.com/cpe/pages/General_Instructions
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 11 articles from volumes 43–44 (2024–2026): Dutt & Rada (appreciation); Hornborg, Crawford (critical surveys); Smith & Thompson, Royle, Pratten, Hinshelwood (review articles); Forget, Zappia (book reviews); Shipman (retrospective)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
No mandatory section order prescribed beyond "suitability" for the journal's scope. Articles must be anonymised before submission.

### Length
**ENFORCED:** Manuscripts must not normally exceed 10,000 words including tables and references. Book reviews: 800–2000 words.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED:** Maximum 250 words. Separate title page required (title, abstract, author names, keywords, JEL codes, corresponding author email).

### Keywords and Classification
**ENFORCED:** Up to five keywords; three JEL classifications (required).

### Citations
Author-year format (Keynes 1937 or (Keynes, 1937, p.250)). Full reference list alphabetically ordered by author surname. Cite by original publication date; full reprint details in references.

### Headings
No prescribed naming convention. Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable for section numbers (per FIELD_SKILL). Practice varies in sampled articles: most use Roman numerals with substantive section titles (e.g., "I. INTRODUCTION"; "II. THE ECOLOGICAL MARX").

### Footnotes
**ENFORCED:** Keep to an absolute minimum. Straightforward references belong in text, not in footnote form. Do not use automatic footnote features in word processors.

### Tables and Figures
Provide on separate sheets with position indicated in text. Minimum 300 DPI for black-and-white conversions. Alt text descriptions required for accessibility. Color figures available online at no cost; print color £350 per figure.

### Mathematics
Follow Marshall's principle: use mathematics as shorthand, then translate into English prose with real-world examples.

### Formatting
Double-spaced throughout (including footnotes and references). Journal style format (consult recent issues).

### Data and AI Disclosure
**ENFORCED:** Use of AI tools must be disclosed in cover letters and in Methods or Acknowledgments section. Accepted papers may include datasets in Word, PDF, PowerPoint, Excel, TIFF, JPEG, or EPS formats.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Manuscript Length: RELAXATION
This journal enforces a strict 10,000-word cap including references, significantly shorter than typical ABS 4* papers (which average 12,000–15,000+ words in working paper form). This is a constraint-driven relaxation; papers must compress arguments more tightly. Implications:
- Literature coverage is necessarily lighter than FIELD_SKILL norm.
- Robustness checks and extensions are often omitted in favor of main results.
- Methods sections are more concise, relying on citations to standard procedures rather than full procedural detail expected at ABS 4*.

### Article Types: PRACTICE ONLY
This journal publishes a broader mix of article types than FIELD_SKILL assumes:
- **Original research** (critical surveys, theoretical articles)
- **Review articles** (sustained engagement with a single argument, book, or theory)
- **Retrospectives** (historical appraisals of influential works, authors, or debates)
- **Book reviews**
- **Appreciations** (obituary-style tributes to scholars)

FIELD_SKILL describes original empirical and theoretical papers. Most review articles, retrospectives, and appreciations follow FIELD_SKILL conventions (Introduction, substantive sections, Conclusion) but with different argumentative goals: they critique, contextualize, or synthesize rather than present novel findings.

### Section Numbering: PRACTICE ONLY
Sampled articles use Roman numerals (I, II, III...) consistently throughout, rather than mixing Roman and Arabic numerals. This is a consistent local practice in recent issues but not an enforced requirement.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural used throughout; active voice preferred)
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** FIELD DEFAULT (medium-to-long sentences; high paragraph density; technical vocabulary assumed)
- **Argumentation structure:** FIELD DEFAULT (Introduction announces research question and approach; explicit positioning relative to prior work; roadmap provided; results stated directly)
- **Hedging intensity:** FIELD DEFAULT (low hedging overall; claims defended through argument and evidence, not verbal qualification; appropriate hedging in external validity, mechanism vs. reduced-form, and data limitation contexts)
- **Citation density and format:** FIELD DEFAULT (author-year parenthetical; high density in Introduction, medium in Methods/Results, low in Conclusion; paraphrase over direct quotation)
- **Discussion and conclusion length:** FIELD DEFAULT (conclusions shorter than introductions; synthesis rather than new results)

## Common Pitfalls Specific to This Journal

1. **Exceeding the 10,000-word limit.** Reviewers and editors strictly enforce this constraint. Every word must earn its place; unlike ABS 4* journals where additional robustness or extensions are expected, here they are often impossible.

2. **Inadequate engagement with heterodox traditions.** This journal explicitly values Classical, Marxist, Keynesian, and Sraffian economics. Submissions framed purely in mainstream terms, even if heterodox in substance, risk rejection for poor rhetorical fit. Position your work explicitly within these traditions if applicable.

3. **Insufficient anonymisation.** Authors must remove identifying information before submission. Reviewers take anonymity seriously; failing to anonymise costs credibility.

4. **Missing JEL classifications or keywords.** All three classifications and five keywords are required fields, not optional. Provide them at submission.

5. **Inadequate disclosure of AI tools.** If you used ChatGPT, Claude, or similar tools in writing, revision, or data analysis, you must disclose this in the cover letter and in the manuscript. Non-disclosure is a matter of research integrity.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
