# Journal Writing Skill: RAE Revista de Administracao de Empresas

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Fundação Getulio Vargas (FGV), São Paulo School of Business Administration (EAESP)
- **Scope:** Generalist management journal (marketing, strategy, organizational behavior, public administration) focused on theoretical and societal impact, with an explicit mandate to serve scholars from developing countries and Global South contexts. Publishes theoretical articles, empirical studies (qualitative/quantitative/mixed), literature reviews, and essays.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://periodicos.fgv.br/rae/autor-rae ; scope at https://periodicos.fgv.br/rae/linha-editorial
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 read in detail (front matter + structure), 5 additional titles/abstracts scanned from Vol. 65 (2025) tables of contents. DOIs/titles: "The Entrepreneurial University in the Global South" (65(3), 2025); "Transição para Sistemas Alimentares Circulares" (10.1590/S0034-759020250404, 65(4) 2025); "Doing Research on Real-World Challenges" (65(2) 2025, editorial/essay); plus abstracts scanned for "Da conduta ética à ciência responsável na pesquisa em administração," "Comportamento de investidores agressivos na era das mídias sociais," "Efeitos mediadores da orientação para o mercado," "Estudo dos aspectos econômicos da RSC," "O impacto das capacidades gerenciais dinâmicas" (all Vol. 65, 2025). Access limited to abstracts/section headings for most; full text read for 3.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Articles: Title → Abstract → 5 Keywords → body sections → References → Tables/Figures (placed after references, positioned in text via callouts). Essays follow the same front matter but a lighter body. Both are bilingual in title/abstract/keywords (English + Portuguese); full text is published in English only per current guidelines (Portuguese/Spanish accepted at submission).

### Length
Articles: maximum 8,000 words all-inclusive (title, abstract, keywords, tables, figures, references); 8,800 words for revised versions. Essays: maximum 5,000 words. This is far shorter than the FIELD_SKILL norm of 10,000-18,000 words for quantitative/qualitative empirical papers.

### Abstract
Unstructured, single paragraph, maximum 150 words (FIELD_SKILL allows 150-250). Must explicitly state theoretical AND societal impact/contribution — not just theoretical contribution as in FIELD_SKILL. No citations (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).

### Citations
APA 7th edition (consistent with FIELD_SKILL's general APA convention; note edition is specified as 7th).

### Headings
No numbered headings (H1/H2 numbering not used); avoid abbreviations in section titles. Specific formatting: section headings uppercase+bold+justified; first-level subheadings mixed-case+bold+justified; second-level subheadings mixed-case+bold+gray text.

### Tables and Figures
Formatted per APA 7th edition; inserted after the reference list rather than inline, with in-text placement callouts. Must be submitted in editable formats (Excel, EPS/AI/PDF/SVG vector, or editable Word) — no embedded flattened images. Color images are converted to grayscale for print. Explicit caution against "excessive" tables/figures/exhibits/graphs.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No footnotes or endnotes anywhere in the manuscript.
- No appendices or attachments.
- No diagrams in essays specifically.
- No author-identifying information in the manuscript body (double-blind review; identity confined to the submission system).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm where quantitative work appears; sample skews qualitative/mixed and essayistic, consistent with the journal's stated preference for theoretical/societal contribution over confirmatory quantitative testing — RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's expectation of extensive robustness-check reporting, not a redefinition (ABS 1 tier).

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Structural Deviations
The no-footnotes/no-appendices rule (HARD_CONSTRAINT, ENFORCED) forces definitional and methodological asides that FIELD_SKILL would place in footnotes to be integrated directly into body text or omitted. Tables/figures placed after references rather than embedded near first mention in text — ENFORCED, confirmed in the food-systems and entrepreneurial-university articles.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Several sampled pieces are editorials, invited essays, or special-issue introductions rather than standard empirical articles (PRACTICE ONLY, reflects journal's broader acceptance of essay/perspective formats at ABS 1 tier) — essays use a distinct, looser heading scheme (numbered strategy/argument headings rather than Theory/Methods/Results) that is a RELAXATION of the standard empirical template, permitted explicitly by the guidelines' "Essays" manuscript type.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 8,000-word all-inclusive cap by treating it as body-text-only (word count includes abstract, keywords, tables, figures, and references).
- Including footnotes or an appendix, both of which RAE does not publish — content must be integrated into the main text or cut.
- Writing an abstract that states only theoretical contribution; RAE guidelines require explicit societal-impact framing as well.
- Leaving author names, affiliations, or self-identifying citations in the manuscript body, violating the double-blind anonymity requirement.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
