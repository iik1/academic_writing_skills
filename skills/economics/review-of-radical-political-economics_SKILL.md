# Journal Writing Skill: Review of Radical Political Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** SAGE (for the Union for Radical Political Economics, URPE)
- **Scope:** Publishes radical, Marxist, and heterodox political-economic analysis of capitalism, exploitation, class, imperialism, financialization, and related topics; explicitly outside the mainstream/neoclassical paradigm.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/rrp (mirrored at https://urpe.org/the-journal/contribute/submissions/ and https://urpe.org/wp-content/uploads/RRPE-Instructions-To-Authors.pdf)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 with confirmed abstract/keywords/JEL, plus table-of-contents review of ~15 additional titles across Vol. 57-58 (2025-2026) for structural/topical pattern. Full text was paywalled for all articles; abstracts, keywords, and JEL codes sourced via IDEAS/RePEc and SAGE abstract pages. Checked: Charitou/Maniatis/Passas "Net Social Wage Ratio in Nine European Countries" (2026); Andrade Neto "Financialization in Peripheral Countries" (2025); Desai "Imperialism in Marx, the Marxist Tradition, and Today" (2025); Wachtel "RRPE at Its Half Century: A Retrospective" (2018); plus TOC listings for Vol. 58 Iss. 1-2 (2026) and Vol. 57 Iss. 4 (2025).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No mandated section architecture beyond standard IMRAD-style expectations; the journal accepts theoretical, historical, and empirical papers without a fixed template. Distinct manuscript types: regular articles, book reviews, and review essays, each with its own length band (see Length).

### Length
- Regular manuscripts: maximum 10,000 words.
- Book reviews: 1,500-2,000 words.
- Review essays: 2,500-3,500 words.
This is shorter than typical ABS 4*/4 empirical papers and caps discursive theoretical exposition more tightly than FIELD_SKILL assumes.

### Abstract
Required, maximum 125 words (unstructured). Minimum two JEL codes and minimum three keywords also required — FIELD_SKILL does not specify these as mandatory metadata.

### Citations
Chicago Manual of Style Author-Date system, not the generic author-year format assumed by FIELD_SKILL. Specific mechanical rules: in-text form is (Author Date: page) with no comma before the page number and no "p."/"pp." abbreviation; en dash (not hyphen) for page ranges; full first names (not initials) required in the reference list; multiple works by the same author listed chronologically.

### Headings
No specific heading-numbering convention mandated beyond standard practice; not otherwise specified.

### Tables and Figures
All tables/figures must be referenced in running text and numbered consecutively in Arabic numerals. Image resolution: grayscale minimum 300 dpi, color preferred 600 dpi; .tif or .eps file formats preferred. Authors are responsible for copyright permissions on any reproduced material.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No simultaneous submission elsewhere.
- Spell out "and"; do not use "&".
- No endnotes — footnotes only, and only for substantive comments, not for citations.
- No Latin citation abbreviations (ibid., op. cit., supra).
- No single quotation marks except when nested inside a double-quoted quotation.
- American (not British) spelling, orthography, grammar, and punctuation required throughout.
- Single-anonymized peer review: authors must strip all self-identifying references from the manuscript before submission.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm on active voice and clear claim-making, but the underlying theoretical commitments differ: articles write from an explicit Marxist/heterodox standpoint rather than a value-neutral positivist stance assumed by FIELD_SKILL. Terms like "exploitation," "surplus value," "dependency," and "class" are used as analytical categories, not merely descriptive labels, and the register openly signals a critical political-economic orientation rather than the studied neutrality typical of ABS 4*/4 mainstream journals. PRACTICE ONLY / FIELD DEFAULT elsewhere — sentence construction and person conventions otherwise unremarkable.

### Statistical Reporting
Not assessed directly (full text inaccessible for all sampled articles); abstracts suggest a mix of empirical (e.g., net social wage estimation) and purely theoretical/historical papers, a wider mix of paper types than the empirical-dominant ABS 4*/4 sample underlying FIELD_SKILL. GUIDELINE ONLY / low confidence given access limits.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm as far as observable from abstracts; no delta detected.

### Structural Deviations
Shorter maximum length (10,000 words) than typical for empirical or theory papers at the ABS 4*/4 level; this compresses literature review and robustness sections relative to FIELD_SKILL's expectations. RELAXATION, consistent with ABS 1 tier — not a new norm to imitate for higher-tier submissions.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Frequent special issues on thematic topics (e.g., "Climate Change and Capitalism," "Anti-Capitalist Pedagogies and Teaching Radical Economics") observed across recent volumes — a higher proportion of guest-edited thematic issues than is typical at ABS 4*/4 general-interest journals. PRACTICE ONLY.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 125-word abstract cap or omitting the required minimum of two JEL codes and three keywords — both are enforced submission-system requirements, not soft suggestions.
- Using "&" instead of spelling out "and," or using endnotes instead of footnotes — both explicitly prohibited.
- Leaving author-identifying material in the manuscript body; review is single-anonymized and self-identification must be scrubbed.
- Writing in a tone of positivist neutrality inconsistent with the journal's explicit radical/Marxist analytical framework — reviewers expect engagement with class, exploitation, or dependency as substantive categories, not just as topics described from the outside.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
