# Journal Writing Skill: Economic Systems

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **ISSN:** 0939-3625
- **Scope:** Refereed journal for the analysis of causes and consequences of institutional variety among developed, developing, emerging, and transition economies. Publishes rigorous, policy-relevant empirical research using robust quantitative methods (panel/time-series econometrics, causal identification strategies, empirically validated structural models) with emphasis on comparative, international, and system-level analysis of economic systems undergoing structural change, integration, development, or institutional reform.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.elsevier.com/journals/economic-systems/09393625/guide-for-authors (paywalled; also indexed at https://ideas.repec.org/s/eee/ecosys.html)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 30+ recent articles across 2025–2026 (volumes 49–50, issues 1–4) via IDEAS/RePEc, ScienceDirect abstracts, and working paper versions; includes empirical papers on government spending, financial development, FDI, institutional analysis, inequality, and policy effects.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
**Section numbering:** Articles must divide into clearly defined, numbered sections. Use Arabic numerals: 1, 1.1, 1.1.2, 1.2, 2, etc. Subsection headings appear on their own line. The abstract is not numbered. **[ENFORCED]**

**Required elements:** Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, body sections, Conclusion. Appendices for proofs, robustness, data description are standard. **[ENFORCED]**

### Length
No explicit maximum word or page limit stated in publicly accessible guidelines. **[FIELD DEFAULT]**

### Abstract
Required. Format not specified in accessible sources; standard Elsevier format presumed. **[GUIDELINE ONLY]**

### Keywords and Classification
**Keywords:** Maximum 6; placed after abstract. American spelling; avoid plural forms and general/multi-concept terms. **[ENFORCED]**

**JEL codes:** Up to 6 standard JEL classification codes. **[ENFORCED]**

### Citations
Author-year parenthetical format. Single reference: (Author, Year). Multiple references: (Egnatchik and DeBerardinis, 2015; Jorgensen, 2004) with semicolon separation. Three or more authors: (Author et al., Year). At submission, any consistent reference style is acceptable; Elsevier applies journal house style at proof stage. **[ENFORCED]**

### Headings
Section and subsection headings use Arabic numerals; either decimal notation (1.1.1) or cascading (1, 1.1, 1.1.1) acceptable. Each heading on a separate line. No explicit constraint on heading style (imperative vs. noun phrase vs. statement). **[FIELD DEFAULT]**

### Tables and Figures
Both are common. Tables and figures must have captions. Table notes explain variable definitions, standard error clustering, sample restrictions, and data sources. **[FIELD DEFAULT]**

### Explicit Prohibitions
No prohibitions found in accessible guidelines beyond standard research integrity requirements. **[FIELD DEFAULT]**

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

None. Economic Systems follows the FIELD_SKILL baseline in voice, register, citation density, hedging, argumentation, results reporting, and article structure. ABS 2 tier does not imply relaxation of these norms; rather, papers are typically shorter and may cover narrower questions than top-tier work, but stylistic conventions remain aligned.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** FIELD DEFAULT (first-person plural "we"; active voice preferred; high formality; no archaic language)
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** FIELD DEFAULT (medium-to-long sentences, high paragraph density, high technical vocabulary density)
- **Argumentation and rhetorical moves:** FIELD DEFAULT (introduction announces results; contributions explicit; positioning vs. prior work required; conclusion is synthesis, not verbatim summary)
- **Methods conventions:** FIELD DEFAULT (mixed tense following principled logic; sufficient procedural detail for replication; identification strategy stated clearly for empirical papers; robustness checks standard; data description explicit)
- **Results and reporting norms:** FIELD DEFAULT (integration of results and interpretation; tables as primary vehicle; claims reported with quantitative specificity; economic significance expected alongside statistical significance; null results reported directly)
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** FIELD DEFAULT (low overall hedging intensity; clear claims preferred; hedging reserved for external validity, causal limits, data limitations, and mechanism vs. reduced-form claims)

## Common Pitfalls Specific to Economic Systems

1. **Insufficient institutional contextualization:** Papers submitted to this journal should explicitly connect empirical findings or theoretical models to institutional or governance structures. Generic "comparative economics" without institutional grounding is less competitive. Papers must articulate how institutional variety explains or shapes the phenomenon under study.

2. **Underspecified policy relevance:** While the journal welcomes theory and structural estimation, all papers should address real-world policy or governance questions. A purely methodological contribution without policy context is less aligned with the journal's stated mission.

3. **Overly narrow geographic scope:** The journal emphasizes comparative, international, and system-level analysis. Papers focused on a single country or narrow regional sample should either explicitly frame findings as case studies informative to broader questions or extend analysis across multiple institutional contexts.

4. **Weak section numbering or organizational clarity:** Because the journal enforces strict section numbering (1, 1.1, 1.1.2, etc.), manuscripts with inconsistent or ad-hoc heading structures are flagged at desk review. Ensure all headings follow the required decimal notation and are on separate lines.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
