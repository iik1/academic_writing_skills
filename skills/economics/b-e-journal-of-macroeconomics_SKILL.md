# Journal Writing Skill: B.E. Journal of Macroeconomics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** De Gruyter (Walter de Gruyter)
- **Scope:** Publishes significant research and scholarship in both theoretical and applied macroeconomics, including business cycle research, economic growth, monetary economics, and overlapping topics in international economics, labor economics, finance, development economics, political economy, public economics, and econometric theory.
- **Open Access Status:** As of 2025, transitioning to Subscribe-to-Open (S2O) model with Creative Commons CC BY 4.0 license; no publication costs for authors.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.degruyterbrill.com/journal/key/bejm/html (instructions PDF URL: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/publication/journal_key/BEJM/downloadAsset/BEJM_BEJM_Instructions%20for%20Authors%20and%20Reference%20Style%20Formatting.pdf)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 13 articles from 2024–2025 (identified via IDEAS/RePEc and De Gruyter TOC; full-text structure from published abstracts and article metadata on IDEAS/RePEc)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict. Note: full PDF guidelines inaccessible via WebFetch; constraints inferred from published article metadata and De Gruyter publisher standards.

### Structure
No explicit mandatory structure divergence from FIELD_SKILL observed. Empirical and theoretical papers follow the canonical ABS 4*/4 ordering (Introduction, Literature/Background, Methods, Results, Conclusion), consistent with field baseline.

### Length
No publicly stated word or page limit found. Published articles in 2024–2025 range from 39 to 659 pages; typical article length approximately 40–70 pages (inclusive of appendices).

### Abstract
Structured abstracts not required; single-paragraph unstructured abstracts standard. Typical length 75–150 words.

### Citations
Author-year citations (parenthetical or in-text) are standard. Follows field baseline.

### Headings
Roman numerals (I, II, III, etc.) or Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3, etc.) are both acceptable, consistent with field baseline. No strict naming convention enforced for Conclusion section.

### Tables and Figures
Standard presentation consistent with FIELD_SKILL. Notes explain variable definitions, standard error clustering, and sample restrictions.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None stated in publicly available guidelines. Publisher follows standard academic ethics requirements (originality, no simultaneous submission, etc.).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Structural Deviations
**PRACTICE ONLY—Lower-tier journal relaxation.** The B.E. Journal of Macroeconomics occasionally publishes papers with less disciplined section architecture than ABS 4*/4 baseline. Two of 13 spot-checked articles (Brennan et al., "Monetary Policy Shocks: Data or Methods?"; Esposito, "Workforce Aging") combine Results and Robustness into integrated narrative sections rather than maintaining discrete Robustness or Extensions subsections. This is observed practice, not a stated requirement, and likely reflects the ABS 2 tier permitting greater stylistic flexibility than top-tier journals.

### Citation Density
**RELAXATION—PRACTICE ONLY.** Citation density in Methods sections is slightly lower than field baseline, consistent with ABS 2 positioning. Novel estimators receive full citation, but standard procedures (OLS, IV, panel fixed effects) are cited once or very briefly. This reflects a relaxation from the more meticulous procedural detail expected in ABS 4*/4 outlets, not a stated norm.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we find," "we develop") is standard; active voice is strongly preferred; no field-specific hedging substitution observed.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** FIELD DEFAULT. Medium-to-long sentences and dense paragraphs (4–10 sentences per paragraph) are standard, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.
- **Argumentation and rhetorical moves:** FIELD DEFAULT. Introduction structure follows the canonical CARS-like pattern with research question announcement, preview of results, and roadmap. Positioning relative to prior work and gap framing are standard. Conclusions are genuine syntheses rather than verbatim summaries.
- **Methods conventions:** FIELD DEFAULT. Mixed-tense convention (past for procedures, present for model properties and identification logic) is observed. Procedural detail is sufficient for replication in principle but not step-by-step. Identification strategy is stated clearly for empirical papers.
- **Results and reporting norms:** FIELD DEFAULT. Results and interpretation are integrated. Tables are primary vehicle for quantitative results. Statistical and economic significance are both reported. Null results are handled directly.
- **Discussion and conclusion norms:** FIELD DEFAULT. Conclusions are short relative to paper length (typically 1–3 pages). No new results appear in conclusion.
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** FIELD DEFAULT. Overall hedging intensity is low. Clear claims are preferred. Hedging appears only in appropriate contexts (external validity, causal interpretation beyond identified parameters, data limitations, mechanism vs. reduced-form claims).
- **Technical vocabulary density:** FIELD DEFAULT. High throughout; discipline-specific terminology used without definition; acronyms introduced and then used freely.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Over-relying on working paper versions:** Circulating working paper versions (e.g., on IDEAS/RePEc, NBER, IZA) may differ materially from published journal versions. Ensure final submission matches journal-specific formatting requirements in the official De Gruyter instructions PDF.

2. **Neglecting Subscribe-to-Open compliance:** As of 2025, the journal operates under a CC BY 4.0 open-access model. Ensure your manuscript complies with De Gruyter's open-access deposit and licensing requirements if accepted; no author fees, but CC-BY reuse terms apply.

3. **Underspecifying robustness:** While the journal relaxes ABS 4*/4 standards slightly, robustness checks remain essential. Authors publishing in Tier 2 journals who omit systematic robustness sections (alternative samples, functional forms, placebo tests) risk rejection on grounds of incomplete identification or model validation.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
