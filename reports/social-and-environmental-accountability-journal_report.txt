PHASE 3 - DELTA CLASSIFICATION
===============================

DELTAS FOUND: 3 major deviations from FIELD_SKILL baseline

1. ARTICLE STRUCTURE FLEXIBILITY
   Classification: PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION
   Observation: ~40-50% of sampled articles deviate significantly from hypothesis-testing structure.
   - "Reclaiming Resistance" (commentary): narrative argumentation, no hypotheses
   - "Bringing Nature Into Decision-Making" (thematic review): structured around literature synthesis, not empirical testing
   - Special issue editorial: framing and positioning, no methods/results
   Evidence: 9 articles examined; 4 non-empirical or commentary pieces; 5 empirical standard structure
   Impact on writing: Authors of non-empirical work should not force hypothesis structure. FIELD_SKILL assumes canonical structure near-universal; SEAJ explicitly rejects this assumption.

2. MANUSCRIPT LENGTH (RELAXATION)
   Classification: ENFORCED RELAXATION
   Observation: Deliberate, stated policy that articles are shorter than field baseline.
   - FIELD_SKILL baseline (ABS 4*/4 journals): typically 40-60 pages
   - SEAJ observed range: 8-33 pages; average ~18 pages
   - Explicit journal statement: "papers are shorter than the word length typically anticipated in academic journals"
   Evidence: All 9 sampled articles fall within 8-33 page range; none exceed 33 pages
   Impact on writing: Do NOT write to ABS 4 length norms. Target shorter manuscripts (2,400-9,900 words depending on article type).

3. ARTICLE TYPES ACCEPTED (ENFORCED RELAXATION)
   Classification: ENFORCED
   Observation: SEAJ accepts multiple article types; not all follow standard empirical structure.
   - Accepted types: conceptual papers, empirical papers, essays, engagement papers, commentaries, polemics, reviews, thematic reviews
   - FIELD_SKILL assumes empirical/archival/experimental default; SEAJ is intentionally pluralistic
   Evidence: Sample includes editorials, commentaries, thematic reviews, and empirical papers; all treated as legitimate articles
   Impact on writing: Declare article type clearly; structure accordingly. Non-empirical work has different expectations.
