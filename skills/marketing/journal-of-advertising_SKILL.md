# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Advertising

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** MKT
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis (in cooperation with the American Academy of Advertising)
- **Scope:** The premier journal devoted to the development of advertising theory and its relationship to practice; covers advertising effectiveness, advertising ethics, public policy/regulation, social responsibility, global advertising issues, and methodological advances (including computational/AI methods) across all forms of advertising.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=ujoa20 (tandfonline.com blocks automated fetches; guidelines cross-checked via AAA-hosted page and search-indexed snippets). Legacy guidelines page journalofadvertising.org no longer resolves.
- **Field baseline:** mkt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (titles/abstracts/scope confirmed via search-indexed snippets and abstract pages; full text paywalled): "Unraveling the Ozempic Craze: A Content and Thematic Analysis of Prescription Weight Loss Medication Advertisements on Meta" (DOI 10.1080/00913367.2025.2593673); "Simple is Eco-Friendly but Complex is Effective: Inferences from Visual Complexity in Package Design" (Ryu, Affonso & Sela, DOI 10.1080/00913367.2025.2593659); "Network Analysis in Advertising Research, Reimagined" (Zhou et al., Vol 55(3)); "Too Sensitive, Too Much? Triggers and Consequences of Perceived Surveillance in Digital Data-Driven Advertising" (Zhang et al., Vol 55(3)); "Computational Content Analysis in Advertising Research" (DOI 10.1080/00913367.2024.2407642); "A Meta-Analysis of the Antecedents and Consequences of Advertising Value" (DOI 10.1080/00913367.2024.2309923); "Advancing a Transformative Agenda Amid Institutional Complexity in Advertising" (special issue intro, DOI 10.1080/00913367.2026.2617048); "Advancing Computational Advertising: Conceptualization of the Field and Future Directions" (DOI 10.1080/00913367.2020.1795759).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No structured/mandatory section template beyond standard journal-article convention. Article types: Original Research Articles, Literature Reviews, and Research Notes (a shorter format).

### Length
Original Research Articles and Literature Reviews: maximum 12,000 words (including references, tables, figures, appendices). Research Notes: maximum 6,000 words (same inclusions). This is a firm word-count ceiling not typically stated with this precision at other MKT-field outlets in FIELD_SKILL.

### Abstract
Unstructured; AAA-hosted guidance indicates a brief abstract (~100 words) walking the reader through the article's major aspects — shorter than the 150–250 word norm in FIELD_SKILL. Treat as GUIDELINE ONLY given inconsistent enforcement seen across search-indexed abstracts of varying length; err toward concision (100–150 words) for this journal.

### Citations
APA style, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
No journal-specific deviation found.

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific deviation found; standard APA formatting expected.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None identified beyond standard AAA ethical-standards guidance (originality, no simultaneous submission, standard research-ethics disclosures).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Recognizes a distinct "Research Note" article type (max 6,000 words) for shorter empirical or methodological contributions — this shorter-format category is not called out in FIELD_SKILL. GUIDELINE ONLY / journal-specific format option.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Strong and recurring editorial emphasis on public policy, advertising ethics/regulation, and social responsibility as a distinguishing thematic scope relative to more consumer-psychology-centric MKT outlets — evident across special issues (e.g., inclusive advertising, advertising regulation) and article topics sampled (health-advertising ethics, surveillance/privacy). Authors targeting this journal should foreground societal/policy relevance in framing contributions, more so than the generic managerial-relevance framing described in FIELD_SKILL. PRACTICE-level pattern, consistent with journal's stated scope.

Growing share of computational/content-analysis and network-analysis methodology among recent articles (alongside standard experimental and survey work), reflecting an active journal push (special issue, methods papers) into computational advertising research. Not a structural requirement, but a visible topical/methodological trend worth noting when methods framing is being pitched to this journal. PRACTICE ONLY.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 12,000-word all-inclusive cap (references/tables/figures/appendices count toward the limit, unlike some journals that exclude them).
- Framing contributions purely in managerial/consumer-psychology terms without any public-policy, ethics, or societal-relevance angle, which is a recurring emphasis at this journal relative to peer MKT outlets.
- Submitting a short methodological or empirical contribution as a full Research Article when the Research Note category (≤6,000 words) may better fit scope and length.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
