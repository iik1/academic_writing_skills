# Journal Writing Skill: Comptabilité Contrôle Audit

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Association Francophone de Comptabilité
- **Scope:** Quarterly journal publishing original research in financial accounting, management accounting, control, and auditing. Welcomes diverse methodologies (positive, normative, historical, interpretive, critical) and data types (surveys, experiments, archives, observations, interviews).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.revuecca.com/index.php/home/directives-aux-auteurs-les-articles (directives-aux-auteurs-les-articles page)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 articles from 2023–2024 issues across governance, CSR audit, and literature review topics

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- **Page 1:** Article title in French and English, author names, affiliations, contact information
- **Page 2:** Title, structured abstract (max 100 words in both languages), exactly 5 keywords in both languages
- **Section numbering:** Hierarchical with up to 3 levels maximum (e.g., 1., 1.1., 1.1.1.). ALL headings must be numbered; no unnumbered or alpha-lettered headings.
- **Standard sections:** Introduction, Hypothesis Development / Theory / Methods, Results, Discussion and Conclusion, References (typical field structure applies)

### Length
- **Word count:** 10–25 pages, A4 format, single manuscript
- **Footnotes:** Minimal use—approximately one per 4–5 pages; placed at page bottom, not endnotes

### Abstract
- **Format:** Structured, 100 words maximum per language
- **Fields:** Research question, method/data, key findings, main contribution (all in one paragraph)
- **Requirement:** Must appear in BOTH French and English on Page 2, immediately following title

### Citations
- **Format:** Author-date, in-text parenthetical style (Author Year) or Author (Year) depending on grammar
- **Multiple citations:** Alphabetical order, separated by semicolons
- **Six or more authors:** Use "et al." from first citation onward
- **Direct quotations:** Include page numbers
- **Bibliography:** Strictly alphabetical order; separate formatting standards for journal articles, books, book chapters, edited volumes, unpublished works

### Headings
- **Numbering requirement:** All headings must be numbered hierarchically (1., 1.1., 1.1.1.). No unnumbered headings.
- **Levels:** Maximum 3 levels of hierarchical numbering
- **Capitalization:** Not specified in guidelines; field baseline (title case or journal convention) applies

### Tables and Figures
- **Numbering:** Sequential numbering with explicit titles and legends
- **Source citation:** Sources cited below tables and figures
- **Figure submission:** Figures provided as separate individual files in addition to text references
- **Text references:** All tables and figures explicitly referenced at appropriate locations in prose

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Plagiarism:** "Tout travail, soumis, soupçonné de piratage ou de plagiat engage entièrement son auteur" (any work suspected of plagiarism fully engages the author's responsibility; author bears full legal responsibility)
- **Formatting:** Must use provided template from journal website; Word document (.doc/.docx) only
- **Font and spacing:** Times New Roman, 12pt, 1.5 line spacing (mandatory)

### Manuscript Format
- **Word format:** Mandatory; use journal's provided template
- **Font:** Times New Roman, 12pt
- **Line spacing:** 1.5
- **Paper format:** A4

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Manuscript Length
**Delta: ENFORCED (journal-specific constraint)**
- FIELD_SKILL does not mandate a page range; CCA specifies 10–25 pages strictly. This is a hard constraint that may exclude longer archival papers common in higher-tier ABS journals.
- **Classification:** ENFORCED CONSTRAINT — All submissions must fit within this range.

### Abstract Structure and Bilingual Requirement
**Delta: ENFORCED (journal-specific requirement)**
- FIELD_SKILL describes abstract as "short, structured summary" without specifying bilingual format or exact word limits by language.
- CCA requires bilingual abstracts (French and English) with maximum 100 words per language and placement on page 2, immediately after title.
- **Classification:** ENFORCED — Bilingual format mandatory since 2022; non-compliance results in desk rejection.

### Keywords
**Delta: ENFORCED (journal-specific requirement)**
- FIELD_SKILL does not specify keyword count; CCA requires exactly 5 keywords in both languages (French and English).
- **Classification:** ENFORCED — Keyword count is rigid; deviation likely grounds for revision.

### Section Numbering System
**Delta: ENFORCED DIVERGENCE from field flexibility**
- FIELD_SKILL notes that "section numbering practice varies across journals" and allows both Roman numerals (I., I.A.) and Arabic numerals (1., 1.1.).
- CCA requires Arabic numerals only with strict hierarchical structure: 1., 1.1., 1.1.1. (maximum 3 levels). ALL headings must be numbered; no unnumbered headings allowed.
- **Classification:** ENFORCED — Deviation from this numbering scheme is a formatting violation and will require revision.

### Footnote Minimization
**Delta: PRACTICE ENFORCED (stronger than field norm)**
- FIELD_SKILL does not prescribe footnote frequency; CCA specifies approximately one footnote per 4–5 pages.
- Articles observed (2023–2024 sample) conform to this standard.
- **Classification:** ENFORCED PRACTICE — Excessive footnotes will likely trigger reviewer or editorial requests for consolidation.

### Mandatory Template Use
**Delta: ENFORCED (publisher requirement)**
- FIELD_SKILL does not mention manuscript templates; CCA explicitly requires use of the journal's provided template.
- **Classification:** ENFORCED — Available on journal website; non-compliance may result in desk rejection.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (first-person plural "we" dominant; active voice; high formality; measured hedging on causal claims).
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium to long sentences; dense paragraphs in introduction and hypothesis development; high technical density).
- **Argumentation and introduction structure:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (CARS-like structure: establish significance, survey gap, describe study, enumerate results, state contributions).
- **Methods conventions:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (past tense; procedural detail; identification strategy; expected robustness checks; ethics statements; descriptive statistics tables).
- **Results and reporting norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (tables as primary vehicle; prose interpretation; precise statistical narration; results organized by hypothesis; null results not suppressed).
- **Discussion and conclusion norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (merged vs. separate section at author discretion; restatement of findings; explicit contributions; explicit limitations; confident but not overreaching tone).
- **Citation density by section:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (high in introduction and hypothesis development; medium in methods; low to medium in results; medium in conclusion).
- **Paraphrase vs. quotation:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (paraphrase is universal; direct quotation from prior scholarly literature absent except in qualitative/ethnographic work with primary data quotations).
- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium overall; calibrated to identification strength; well-identified results stated directly; mechanistic interpretations softened; limitations acknowledged explicitly).
- **Accepted article types:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (original empirical articles dominant; literature reviews accepted; methodological articles accepted; high-quality theoretical contributions accepted).
- **Methodology acceptance:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (diverse methodologies welcomed: quantitative, qualitative, interpretive, critical; both archival and field-based data; double-blind peer review).

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Unnumbered or alpha-lettered headings:** The most frequent formatting error observed in rejections. All headings (even introduction, conclusion, references) must carry numeric labels (1., 1.1., etc.). Failure to number headings is a desk-reject trigger.

2. **Abstract exceeds 100 words per language:** Submitted abstracts frequently exceed the strict 100-word limit per language. Count words carefully; overage requires revision before resubmission.

3. **Missing bilingual title, abstract, or keywords:** Since 2022, the journal publishes all accepted articles in both French and English. Page 2 must contain title, abstract, and keywords in both languages, not English only. Non-compliance indicates misread guidelines.

4. **Manuscript exceeds 25 pages or falls short of 10 pages:** Page limits are rigid (10–25 pages, A4, 1.5 spacing). Papers outside this range are often desk-rejected or returned for restructuring before review.

5. **Over-reliance on footnotes:** Authors from traditions using footnotes for methodological asides or secondary arguments often exceed the ~1 footnote per 4–5 pages norm. Integrate footnote content into main text or appendix instead.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
