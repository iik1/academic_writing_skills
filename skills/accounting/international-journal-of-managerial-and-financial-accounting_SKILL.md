# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Managerial and Financial Accounting

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Inderscience Publishers (primary); also IAEME Publication
- **Scope:** Managerial and financial accounting research, corporate governance, audit, accounting information systems, and financial reporting standards (IFRS, mark-to-market, activity-based costing, knowledge management in accounting)
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.inderscience.com/jhome.php?jcode=ijmfa (public guidelines limited); https://iaeme.com/Home/journal/IJMFA (detailed guidelines available)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 (Bhavna Thawani et al. Integrated Reporting adoption; Behavioral Impacts of Managerial Myopia; Forensic Accounting Techniques)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- **Required sections in order:** Introduction, Body (with subheadings), Conclusion, Acknowledgements (optional), References
- **Heading numbering:** Main headings numbered as Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3, etc.); subheadings as 1.1, 1.2; further divisions as 1.1.1
- **Flush left alignment** for all headings and subsequent paragraphs (ENFORCED)
- **First paragraph after heading** begins flush left; **subsequent paragraphs indented five spaces** (ENFORCED)

### Length
- **Abstract:** Maximum 350 words (ENFORCED)
- **Keywords:** Approximately five keywords in alphabetical order, comma-separated (ENFORCED)
- **No word limit or page limit stated** in public guidelines; practice varies widely across articles

### Abstract
- **Structured format not explicitly required**, but abstracts in spot-checked articles follow standard academic model (research question/motivation, methods, key findings, implications)
- **No references or equations permitted in abstract** (ENFORCED)
- Summarizes paper content in under 350 words

### Citations
- **Numbered bracketed style [1], [2], [3]** — references numbered in order of first appearance in text, NOT alphabetically (ENFORCED; CRITICAL DELTA)
- Each reference is inserted as a number in square brackets
- This diverges significantly from FIELD_SKILL's author-date convention
- Examples provided in guidelines for journal papers, books, book chapters, theses, proceedings

### Headings
- **Main headings:** 12pt bold, numbered (1, 2, 3), flush left (ENFORCED)
- **Subheadings:** Numbered 1.1, 1.2; further divisions use 1.1.1 (ENFORCED)
- **No underlining, dashes, or colons in heading text** (ENFORCED)
- No ALL-CAPS convention (differs from TAR/AAA style)

### Tables and Figures
- **Computer-drafted or India ink illustrations required** (ENFORCED; archaic language suggests legacy guideline)
- **Figure captions:** Placed below figure, lowercase, flush left (ENFORCED)
- **Table captions:** Centered above table, upper/lowercase (ENFORCED)
- **Inclusion:** Tables and figures embedded within running text, numbered sequentially (PRACTICE: articles follow this)

### Font and Formatting
- **Font:** Times New Roman, 12pt (ENFORCED)
- **Text formatting:** Standard; no special emphasis conventions stated
- **Paper format:** Implied to be single-spaced or standard academic spacing (specific requirements not detailed in public guidelines)

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Conference papers only if completely re-written and permissions cleared** (ENFORCED)
- **Briefs and research notes not accepted** — full original articles only (ENFORCED)
- **Ethical compliance required:** Authors must declare they have "read and agreed to the content of the submitted article" and comply with journal's Ethical Guidelines for Authors (ENFORCED)
- **No duplicate publication:** Articles must be original and unpublished elsewhere (ENFORCED)

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Citation Style: NUMBERED BRACKETED SYSTEM
**CRITICAL DELTA** — ENFORCED in journal guidelines and articles

The journal uses **numbered citations [1], [2], [3]** ordered by first appearance in the text, NOT alphabetically and NOT author-date. This is a major departure from FIELD_SKILL's author-date convention (e.g., "(Smith 2020)" or "Smith (2020)"). 

- References appear in-text as bracketed numbers: "This finding was established in prior work [5]."
- The reference list at the end is numbered to match.
- Authors accustomed to author-date style (standard in ABS 4*/4 accounting journals) must convert entirely to this numbered system.
- Implication: No "Smith (2020) argues…" constructions; instead, "Prior work argues [5]…"

### Structural Numbering: ARABIC NUMERALS, NOT ROMAN
**ENFORCED**

FIELD_SKILL documents two heading conventions: Roman numerals (TAR/AAA journals) and Arabic numerals (JAE/JAR journals). IJMFA enforces **Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) for main headings**, not Roman numerals. This is consistent with modern practice but explicit here.

### Headings: NO ALL-CAPS CONVENTION
**ENFORCED**

Unlike TAR and other AAA journals (which use "I. INTRODUCTION," "II. HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT"), IJMFA uses **title-case or sentence-case headings**, not ALL-CAPS. Main headings are flush left, bold, numbered. No prescriptive capitalization beyond standard title case.

### Heading Substructure: EXPLICIT PROHIBITION ON COLONS AND DASHES
**ENFORCED**

Headings may **not include colons or dashes** — e.g., "3.1 Methodology: Data Sources" would be non-compliant. Headings must be simple noun phrases or gerunds. This is a rigid constraint not typical of FIELD_SKILL journals.

### Manuscript Length: NO EXPLICIT LIMIT
**FIELD DEFAULT / RELAXATION**

Unlike many ABS 4*/4 journals which have implicit or explicit 40-50 page norms, IJMFA provides no word or page limit in its public guidelines. Practice-observed articles span a wide range; this is a relaxation relative to FIELD_SKILL baseline expectations for tightly-scoped submissions.

### Abstract Structure: UNSTRUCTURED PERMITTED
**RELAXATION**

FIELD_SKILL describes structured abstracts as "short, structured summary" that "states the research question, primary method or data, key findings, and main contribution in a single paragraph." IJMFA allows up to 350 words with no requirement for explicit labeled sections (Background | Methods | Results | Conclusions). Articles in the spot-check used prose-form abstracts covering key elements but not formally partitioned. This is a relaxation of field norm.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. "We" voice for multi-authored; clinical register; active voice dominant; high formality. No deviation observed.
- **Confidence calibration:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Well-identified findings stated directly; mechanistic interpretations hedged ("suggest," "indicate").
- **Hedging conventions:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Medium-level hedging, calibrated to identification strength.
- **Sentence and paragraph density:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Medium-to-long sentences with embedded clauses; dense paragraphs in intro and results.
- **Citation density by section:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (format differs, but density is high in intro/theory, medium in methods, low in results). Introduction cites 15–40+ prior works; hypothesis development cites extensively; results sections include citations when interpreting against prior findings.
- **Methodology conventions:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Past tense; procedural detail high; identification strategy stated explicitly; robustness checks expected.
- **Results and reporting:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Tables are primary vehicle; prose narrates. Coefficient estimates, significance levels, and economic significance reported. No systematic deviation observed.
- **Discussion and Conclusion:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Conclusions often merged with discussion. Limitations acknowledged explicitly. Tone confident but not overreaching.
- **Argumentation structure:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Introduction follows CARS model: establish significance, survey gap, describe study, enumerate results, state contributions. Gap framing explicit. Contributions stated clearly.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Citation Format Conversion:** Authors from ABS 4*/4 journals (who use author-date style) often submit with in-text author-date citations (e.g., "Smith (2020)") instead of numbered brackets [1]. This is a near-certain desk reject. Convert all citations to [1], [2], [3] order of appearance; rebuild reference list accordingly.

2. **Heading Colons and Dashes:** Avoid constructions like "2. Methodology: Data Collection" or "3.1 Theory—Hypotheses." Headings must be simple noun phrases. Instead, use "2. Methodology," then introduce subsections as "2.1 Data Collection," "2.2 Variables," etc.

3. **Abstract Word Limit Breach:** 350-word limit is strictly enforced. Abstracts that exceed this (which happens when authors port abstracts from longer ABS 4* submissions) will be rejected. Condense to ~250–300 words typical of well-drafted abstracts.

4. **Alfhabetical Keywords Not Alphabetical:** The guideline says keywords should be "about five key words in alphabetical order." Authors sometimes overlook alphabetization. Always sort alphabetically (e.g., "accounting," "corporate governance," "earnings management," "financial reporting," "sustainability").

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
