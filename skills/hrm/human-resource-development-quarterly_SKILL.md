# Journal Writing Skill: Human Resource Development Quarterly

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** The first peer-reviewed journal focused directly on the evolving field of Human Resource Development (HRD). Official publication of the Academy of Human Resource Development (AHRD). Publishes empirical (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method), methodological, and conceptual work on HRD theory, research, and intervention evaluation.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/15321096/homepage/forauthors.html (summary details; full PDF requires authentication)
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** Limited accessibility; examined journal structure and author guidance documents via Wiley and AHRD sources

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows IMRaD convention (Introduction, Methods, Results/Findings, Discussion) standard to HRM&EMP field. Title page must be separate and contain author names, affiliations, and contact details. Manuscript must be anonymized (no author identification in text). Running head on all subsequent pages.

Acceptable article types: empirical studies (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method); methodological papers demonstrating quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-method approaches relevant to HRD; conceptual papers only when proposing an empirical research agenda (not permitted as standalone theoretical essays).

### Length
Manuscript total approximately 35 pages (all-inclusive: text, tables, figures, and references). Double-spaced with margins at least one inch wide throughout.

### Abstract
Unstructured (narrative) format, no more than 250 words. Should include problem statement, research approach, key findings, and implications for HRD practice or theory. Appears on separate page following title page.

### Keywords
Keywords page required following abstract (Wiley standard for HRDQ); typically 4–6 keywords listed after abstract.

### Citations
APA style (Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, 6th edition). Full reference list at end, double-spaced. Paraphrase strongly preferred over direct quotation of other scholars (per FIELD_SKILL norm).

### Headings
Follows APA heading hierarchy: no explicit prohibition, but structured with numbered or hierarchical levels for clarity. Section headings centre or left-aligned depending on level (APA 6th edition conventions).

### Tables and Figures
Include captions. Tables and figures placed at end of manuscript following reference section, separated by page breaks. Figures should be submitted in highest resolution possible. Legends required for all figures.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts must not have been published elsewhere or be under consideration by another journal (standard provenance requirement).
- Author identifying information must not appear in text body or acknowledgements on the main manuscript (blind review).
- No data-sharing waivers; authors expected to comply with Wiley data-sharing policy and select appropriate data availability statement on submission.

### Submission Process
Free Format submission via Research Exchange portal (https://authors.wiley.com/journal/HRDQ). Editable files (text + figures/tables together, or separate files accepted). No submission fees. Ethics approval statement required for primary data collection; funding sources listed in Acknowledgments.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Methodological Expectations
FIELD_SKILL notes that econometric sophistication is lower and less uniform in HRM&EMP than in economics, with varying estimation approaches accepted. HRDQ reinforces this by explicitly welcoming methodological papers and mixed-methods work alongside traditional regression designs. Qualitative work is treated as equally rigorous; no preference for quantitative over qualitative is signalled in guidelines. Reflexivity and ethics statements are framed as expectations, not optional.

### Conceptual and Theory-Building Boundaries
Unlike some ABS 4/4* HRM journals that permit standalone conceptual papers, HRDQ restricts these to papers that propose and justify a specific empirical research agenda. This is ENFORCED and represents a tighter gate on pure theory than FIELD_SKILL implies.

### Interdisciplinary Framing
HRDQ emphasizes recognition of the interdisciplinary nature of HRD explicitly in author guidelines, welcoming research from economics, management, education, sociology, psychology, and technology. This broadens the citation and framing norms beyond the managerial/psychological focus common in narrower HRM journals and aligns with a more sociological tolerance (per FIELD_SKILL), but is made explicit as a journal norm rather than implicit in field practice.

### Data Sharing and Reproducibility
HRDQ (as a Wiley journal) enforces current data-sharing policy with required statements on submission. This is a GUIDELINE ENFORCED requirement not explicitly mentioned in FIELD_SKILL but consistent with contemporary HRM journal practice.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and Register: FIELD DEFAULT. HRM field norms (formal, impersonal with permitted first-person plural in methods/discussion, narrative register) apply.
- Citation Density: FIELD DEFAULT. Highest in introduction/literature review, lowest in results, rising in discussion.
- Hedging and Epistemic Caution: FIELD DEFAULT. Moderate to high, with appropriate caution on causal claims in observational designs.
- Discussion and Conclusion Structure: FIELD DEFAULT. Revisit contributions, distinguish theoretical from practical implications, close with limitations and future directions.
- Acknowledgements and Ethics Statements: FIELD DEFAULT. Treated as standard back matter; ethics approval required for primary data.

## Common Pitfalls
(Based on Managing Editor guidance and field practice observations)

1. **Weak or Unclear Contribution Statement**: Submissions that do not explicitly state how the research extends, qualifies, or challenges existing HRD theory or practice. HRDQ expects clear positioning relative to prior work, not just empirical novelty.

2. **Conceptual Papers Without Research Agenda**: Submitting papers that theorize without proposing or justifying a specific empirical program. HRDQ will desk-reject pure theory; connect to actionable research questions.

3. **Insufficient Methodological Transparency**: Qualitative work submitted without explicit statement of analytic approach (e.g., thematic analysis, grounded theory, template analysis) or reflexivity. Mixed-methods papers must be clear about how integration occurs, not just report both data types separately.

4. **Over-Claiming Generalizability**: Qualitative and case-study submissions that generalize beyond what sample size and design support. HRDQ reviewers are sensitive to overclaiming. Be explicit about contextual and sample limits.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Access and Limitation Notes

Full-text access to HRDQ articles and the detailed HRDQ_Contributors_Document PDF is restricted to institutional or subscription holders. Most author guidelines were gathered from Wiley public-facing pages, official journal metadata, and guidance documents referenced in search results. The Managing Editor's 2014 editorial "Strengthening Your Submissions to HRDQ" is a key source for detailed expectations but is paywalled. This skill captures the constraints and norms stated publicly and inferred from available abstracts and structural metadata; minor updating may be warranted when the full contributors document becomes accessible or when journal policies are formally updated by Wiley or AHRD.
