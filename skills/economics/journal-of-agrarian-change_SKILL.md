# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Agrarian Change

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** Agrarian political economy. Publishes historical and contemporary studies of social relations and dynamics of production, power relations in agrarian formations, and ownership structures. Covers farm productivity, land reform, capitalist transition, rural movements, agricultural ecology, global value chains, and labor dynamics. Multidisciplinary (development studies, agricultural studies, political economy).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14710366/homepage/forauthors.html (publisher site, paywalled; details extracted via search)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 recent articles sampled via abstracts and institutional repository metadata (Paprocki 2025, Mezzadri et al. 2024, White 2025, Karaçimen & Değirmenci 2025, Barbesgaard 2025); full-text access limited by Wiley paywall

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Free Format submission permitted. Manuscripts must include abstract, introduction, methods, results, and conclusions, but section ordering is flexible and not strictly enforced. Unlike the FIELD_SKILL emphasis on canonical empirical/theory structures, the journal accepts research articles, review articles, essays, and book reviews—each may have distinct internal organization.

### Length
**Manuscript word count:** 8,000–10,000 words excluding references and notes. Longer articles considered where justified. **Total word count required at submission:** Authors must provide total word count for text, abstract, notes, and references (excluding figures and tables).

### Abstract
**Format:** Structured (intro/methods/results/conclusion) or unstructured; author choice.
**Length:** 100–150 words.

### Citations
**Style:** Harvard referencing (author-date), standard for Wiley journals. References may be submitted in any consistent style initially, but will be converted to Harvard format upon publication.
**Format flexibility:** Free format submission allows references in any internally consistent style during submission; Wiley handles conversion.

### Headings
No explicit conventions stated. Section numbering and heading hierarchies follow journal typesetting standards post-acceptance.

### Tables and Figures
Figures and tables must include legends/captions. Figures should be submitted in highest resolution possible; low-quality figures may delay publication. Both are permitted as primary research vehicles.

### Manuscript Format
- Double-spaced with ample margins throughout (including quotations, notes, references)
- All pages numbered consecutively, preferably centered at bottom
- Fully anonymized for peer review
- Editable file format (Word, LaTeX, or other) with text, figures, tables in one file or separate files

### Author Information and Acknowledgements
Author institutional affiliation and full address (or home address where appropriate) plus email appear as an unumbered footnote. Acknowledgements in separate "Acknowledgements" section (note British spelling with "e"), including thanks to anonymous reviewers.

### Submission Method
Research Exchange portal: https://authors.wiley.com/journal/JOAC

### Explicit Prohibitions
No prohibition of first-person language stated. No explicit prohibition of hedging. No restriction on article types or theoretical approaches. No data availability or replication-package mandate mentioned in searched guidelines.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Article Type Diversity
**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL baseline focuses on empirical and theory papers with canonical structures. JAC explicitly publishes research articles, review articles, essays, and book reviews. Essays and reviews do not follow the Introduction→Methods→Results→Conclusion structure. This is a **PRACTICE ONLY** delta—the journal accepts diverse genres beyond the ECON field baseline, reflecting its interdisciplinary scope (development studies + agricultural studies) rather than pure economics.

### Section Ordering Flexibility
**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL emphasizes canonical ordering: Introduction, optional Background/Literature, substantive body sections, Conclusion. JAC permits "free format submission" with flexible section ordering, provided that abstract, introduction, methods, results, and conclusions are present (not necessarily in that order). This is a **RELAXATION**—lower-tier journals often permit more flexibility than ABS 4*/4 venues.

### Word and Length Limits (Stated)
**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL does not specify word limits or page constraints for ABS 4*/4 journals. JAC explicitly mandates 8,000–10,000 words, with author declaration of total word count at submission. This is **ENFORCED** in guidelines.

### Submission Format Flexibility
**Delta:** FIELD_SKILL assumes camera-ready manuscripts with specific formatting. JAC accepts "free format submission"—any internally consistent reference style, flexible file organization, and Wiley handles conversion. This is a **RELAXATION** and reflects Wiley's streamlined submission process, not a deviation from ECON norms per se, but a publisher-level convenience that reduces friction for submissions.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Assuming rigid section order:** JAC permits flexible section ordering under free format submission, but authors may inadvertently follow ABS 4* canonical structures and create inflexible, overly formalized manuscripts unsuitable for essays or review articles. Confirm article type with journal before committing to strict section order.

2. **Exceeding word limits:** 8,000–10,000 words is a strict boundary. Many ABS 4* ECON papers run 12,000+ words. JAC rejects or requests significant cuts for longer papers. Authors familiar with top-tier journals should budget carefully.

3. **Over-formatting references:** Free format submission means authors can use any consistent reference style initially. Avoid using an idiosyncratic style; Harvard or another standard (APA, Chicago) will be accepted, but consistency matters. Wiley will convert to Harvard at proofs.

4. **Inadequate figure resolution:** The guidelines explicitly warn that low-quality figures delay publication. This is not typical in ECON papers (where figures are often simple line plots). Ensure all visual materials meet publication standards.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
