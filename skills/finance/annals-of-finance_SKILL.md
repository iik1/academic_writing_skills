# Journal Writing Skill: Annals of Finance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature
- **Scope:** Original research across all areas of finance and its applications, with an explicit welcome for work that is "motivated by the analysis of financial problems" from adjacent disciplines (math, physics, computer science). Strong presence of mathematical/theoretical finance (derivative pricing, stochastic analysis, dynamical/chaotic systems in finance) alongside empirical banking, corporate finance, and asset pricing work.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/10436/submission-guidelines (accessible via redirect-follow; standard Springer Nature author portal)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 with abstract/keyword/JEL detail, plus ~12 additional titles/author lists for scope triangulation (2023–2026): "Bank risk in flux: policy interplay under uncertainty" (Lucchetta, 2025); "An interval-valued extension of the internal rate of return" (Sokolov & Polyakova, 2025); "Foreign bank entry and performance of domestic SMEs: evidence from Korea" (Lee & Oh, 2025); plus titles-only from Madan & Wang, Jarrow, Hainaut & Casas, Sokolov, Jasso-Fuentes et al., Stolbov & Shchepeleva, Turinici & Brugiere (2023–2026).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Title page (title, author info, affiliations, corresponding author email, ORCID if available) → Abstract → Keywords → Main text (decimal heading system, max 3 levels) → References → Statements and Declarations section. JEL classification codes are a required separate field. Standard Springer Nature template structure — matches FIELD_SKILL's near-universal section list with no journal-specific reordering.

### Length
No stated overall word/page limit for standard research articles. Distinct short-form category: **"finance notes"** — short articles up to 10 pages, for contributions "of substantial interest" that don't warrant full-length treatment. This is a journal-specific article type not covered by FIELD_SKILL.

### Abstract
150–250 words, single paragraph, unstructured. No undefined abbreviations or unspecified references permitted in the abstract itself. Matches FIELD_SKILL range.

### Citations
Author-year parenthetical, e.g., "(Thompson 1990)" — no comma before year (note: differs slightly from the comma-inclusive "(Fama and French, 1993)" example in FIELD_SKILL, but this is a copyediting-level formatting variant, not a substantive delta). Reference list alphabetized by first author surname; DOIs included as full links; journal names abbreviated per the ISSN List of Title Word Abbreviations.

### Headings
Decimal numbering system, maximum three levels (e.g., 2.1.1 is the deepest permitted). Matches FIELD_SKILL's Arabic-numeral norm but caps depth explicitly.

### Tables and Figures
Tables and figures numbered with Arabic numerals, cited consecutively in text, descriptive captions required; figure sub-parts labeled with lowercase letters (a, b, c). Specific resolution requirements: line art 1200 dpi, halftone 300 dpi, combination 600 dpi. Figures placed within text body where possible.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No simultaneous submission to multiple journals; no plagiarism or self-plagiarism; no fabrication/falsification of results; no "salami-slicing" (splitting one study into multiple submissions); all conflicts of interest must be disclosed in the Statements and Declarations section. Standard Springer Nature research-integrity boilerplate, not journal-specific.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for empirical papers in the sample (Lucchetta 2025; Lee & Oh 2025) — confident declarative claims, measured interpretive language. The mathematical-finance papers in the sample (Sokolov & Polyakova 2025, and titles from Madan, Jarrow, Hainaut) lean more axiomatic/propositional than FIELD_SKILL's empirical-paper voice — abstracts state formal results ("we propose an interval-valued extension... consistent with a natural set of axioms") rather than empirical findings with magnitudes. This is PRACTICE ONLY, driven by the journal's mixed empirical/theoretical scope rather than a stated journal norm, and is consistent with — not a deviation from — FIELD_SKILL's existing allowance for theory/model papers.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in the empirical articles sampled (economic-magnitude framing present, e.g., Lee & Oh's credit-access-to-performance channel). No delta observed.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Structural Deviations
The "finance notes" short-format category (≤10 pages) is a journal-specific structural option with no FIELD_SKILL analogue — GUIDELINE ONLY / journal-specific (not confirmed against a sampled finance-notes article, since none appeared in the accessible sample).

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a manuscript with headings nested beyond three decimal levels (e.g., 2.1.1.1) — the journal's template caps depth at three.
- Treating "finance notes" as a lesser proofreading pass rather than a genuinely short (≤10-page) self-contained contribution — it is a distinct submission track, not a length waiver on a full paper.
- Omitting the separate "Statements and Declarations" section, which Springer Nature requires distinct from the acknowledgments/conflict-of-interest text some authors fold into the main body.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
