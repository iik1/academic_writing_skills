# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** American Psychological Association (APA)
- **Scope:** Publishes original experimental and theoretical research on human cognition, with emphasis on learning, memory, language, and higher cognition; also accepts literature reviews, meta-analyses, direct replications, Registered Reports, theoretical notes, and commentaries. Editorial priority is explicitly on methodological soundness, analytic rigor, and reproducibility/replicability (per 2025 incoming editorial by Jeffrey Starns).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/xlm/submit (accessed via WebFetch; ScienceDirect-style paywall not applicable, page rendered)
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 full-text (Isbilen et al., 2025, xlm0001412; Gronau, Wolfe, Nartker, Yakim, Utochkin, 2024, xlm0001377; Lee, Holyoak, & Lu, 2025, xlm0001514) plus abstracts/titles/editorial content for ~10 additional articles (e.g., Hu & Worthy 2026; Hong et al. 2025; Davies et al. 2017; Hendrix & Sun 2021; Siegelman et al. 2022; incoming editorial by Starns 2025) identified via search and journal sample-articles page.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Title page, unstructured abstract, keywords (up to 5), Method (including a mandatory "Transparency and Openness" subsection), Results, Discussion, References, tables, figures. Multi-experiment papers nest Method/Results/Discussion (or combined "Results and Discussion") per experiment under a shared Introduction, closing with a General Discussion — consistent with FIELD_SKILL's multi-study variant.

### Length
Theoretical notes capped at ~25 pages of text. Commentaries capped at half the length of the target article. No stated page limit for standard empirical articles.

### Abstract
Unstructured, maximum 250 words, submitted as plain text (Word formatting is stripped on submission).

### Citations
APA Style 7th edition; references alphabetical; every in-text citation must appear in the reference list and vice versa; DOIs required where available.

### Headings
No journal-specific heading conventions beyond standard APA Style levels.

### Tables and Figures
Tables must use Word's native table tool (no tab/space alignment). Figures: TIFF/JPG (raster) or EPS (vector); 300 DPI color/halftone, 600 DPI black-and-white; multipanel figures merged into one file. Print color figures incur a fee ($900 first figure, $600 second, $450 each additional); online color is free.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No dual/concurrent submission.
- Data must remain available for at least 5 years post-publication.
- Previously published data cannot be resubmitted as original.
- Generative AI use must be disclosed in the Method section and cited; AI cannot be listed as an author.
- Undisclosed post hoc exclusions are treated as a serious violation (this is a FIELD_SKILL norm the journal explicitly operationalizes via mandatory disclosure fields).

### Open Science / Transparency Requirements (journal-specific, beyond FIELD_SKILL)
A mandatory "Transparency and Openness" subsection (within Method) must disclose: sample-size determination, all data exclusions, all manipulations and measures, data/code/materials availability with persistent identifiers (or a stated legal/ethical reason for unavailability), and preregistration status with a stable link. The journal follows APA JARS reporting standards, requiring hypotheses/analyses to be labeled primary, secondary, or exploratory. Author Contribution Statements using the CRediT taxonomy are required. Optional Open Science Badges (Open Data, Open Materials, Preregistered, Preregistered+Analysis Plan) are available on request with a signed disclosure form.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Structural Deviations
ENFORCED / PRACTICE ONLY (confirmed in all 3 full-text articles). The Transparency and Openness subsection and explicit preregistration statement ("preregistered at OSF osf.io/...") are not merely encouraged as in FIELD_SKILL but appear as a formal, labeled subsection in every article checked, typically at the start of each experiment's Method. Some multi-experiment articles combine Results and Discussion into one labeled subsection per experiment ("Results and Discussion:") rather than keeping them fully separate as FIELD_SKILL's strict separation norm implies — PRACTICE ONLY, observed in 1 of 3 full papers (Gronau et al.), not stated in guidelines, so treat as acceptable variant rather than a required deviation.

### Statistical Reporting
PRACTICE ONLY. Beyond FIELD_SKILL's baseline of exact statistics + effect size + CI, articles in this journal commonly report Bayes Factors (BF10, BFincl) alongside frequentist tests as a routine practice for both significant and null results, with an explicit footnote defining Bayes Factor interpretation thresholds (e.g., Gronau et al.). This is not stated in guidelines but appeared in 2 of 3 full-text articles and reflects the 2025 incoming editorial's stated push toward "more sophisticated…modern approaches to statistical inference." Treat as a strong practice-level norm for this journal, not yet a hard requirement.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
PRACTICE ONLY. All three full-text articles include an "Action Editor" credit line and detailed CRediT-taxonomy author contribution statements as standard front matter — this level of contribution granularity exceeds what FIELD_SKILL specifies (FIELD_SKILL is silent on contribution statements). Preregistration links (OSF) are cited inline in Method sections as formal citations (e.g., "Wolfe et al., 2020") rather than mentioned only in prose, and appear in the reference list.

## Common Pitfalls
- Omitting or burying the Transparency and Openness subsection: this journal expects it as a distinct, labeled Method subsection, not folded into general procedural prose.
- Reporting only p-values without accompanying Bayes Factors or effect sizes in papers involving null-result interpretation; reviewers at this journal appear to expect Bayesian evidence for null claims given the stated editorial emphasis on rigorous statistical inference.
- Missing CRediT-style author contribution statements, which appear standard in accepted articles even though FIELD_SKILL does not require them.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
