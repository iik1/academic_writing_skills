# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Gambling Business and Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** University of Buckingham Press (UBP), hosted on OJS (ubplj.org)
- **Scope:** Interdisciplinary outlet for the economic and business aspects of the international gambling and gaming industry, explicitly welcoming contributions "from academics to policy-makers to those involved in the business and commercial side of betting and gaming." Accepts technical/theoretical papers alongside accessible policy surveys and practitioner-oriented pieces.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jgbe/about/submissions (accessed; also https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jgbe/about)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 5 (front-matter/abstract level only; full text paywalled). "Tennis Serve Clock Started Ticking and Betting Market Inefficiencies Followed" (McFall & Cutter, Vol.19(1) 2026); "From Juice to KPIs: Institutional Change and Occupational Identity in Las Vegas Casino Management" (Lantz, Vol.19(1) 2026, open access); "A Review of Regulatory Theory and the U.S. Casino Industry" (Calcagno & Walker, Vol.10(1) 2016); "Introduction" to special issue on gaming-machine consumer behaviour (Etches, Vol.7(3) 2013); table-of-contents scan of Vol.19(1) 2026 (4 articles).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Title page (with author/institutional info) → running title (max 50 characters) → six keywords → abstract (~250 words) → body text (consecutively numbered pages) → acknowledgements → references → figure legends. No mandated internal section-heading scheme for the body beyond this front/back matter.

### Length
Target ~6,000 words; "generally shouldn't be more than 6,000 words," with up to 10,000 permitted "in certain circumstances." This is materially shorter than typical ABS 4/4* empirical papers.

### Abstract
Unstructured, approximately 250 words, preceding the main text.

### Citations
Legal-style citation convention (OSCOLA-like), not economics author-year: books cited as "Author, *Title* (Place, Publisher, Year) pp X–X"; journal articles as "Author 'Article Title' (Year) Volume *Journal Name* Page"; subsequent references use short form ("Bloggs, above n 2, p 6") or "Ibid, p 6"; unpublished work cited in-text only as "unpubl. observ." or "pers comm." (not in reference list); DOI citation preferred for online material. This is a substantial departure from the field-standard parenthetical author-year format.

### Headings
Not explicitly specified in guidelines.

### Tables and Figures
Tables numbered consecutively with Arabic numerals; no vertical rules; units in column headings (parenthetical), not in the table body; captions above tables, below figures; figures embedded in the Word file and referenced as "Fig. 1," "Fig. 2," etc.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No previously published material; no simultaneous submission elsewhere without disclosure; blind-review Word files must not contain author-identifying metadata (double-blind review, two referees, third referee on split verdict).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Scope explicitly spans rigorous technical/theoretical papers and practitioner-facing policy surveys within the same journal; register is correspondingly more heterogeneous than the uniform high-formality FIELD_SKILL register, ranging from standard quantitative-economics prose (e.g., Calcagno & Walker) to qualitative, narrative-driven prose with extended direct quotation from interview subjects (Lantz). PRACTICE ONLY / GUIDELINE-consistent — the "practical concerns of decision-makers" framing in the aims-and-scope is stated, and the spot-check confirms wide register variance in practice.

### Structural Deviations
At least one spot-checked article (Lantz, 2026) is a qualitative oral-history study organized around emergent themes rather than the Data/Empirical Strategy/Results architecture FIELD_SKILL describes as near-universal. PRACTICE ONLY, low-prevalence in this small sample — do not assume standard quantitative empirical structure is required; check whether the specific paper is quantitative or qualitative/policy-survey before applying FIELD_SKILL's empirical-paper template.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Direct quotation is used more freely in at least one qualitative article (extended quotes from interviewees, e.g. "a lived negotiation of meaning, authority, and professional identity"), contrasting with FIELD_SKILL's norm that direct quotation is "extremely rare." RELAXATION, PRACTICE ONLY — applies specifically to qualitative/case-study submissions, not the norm for quantitative papers in this journal.

## Common Pitfalls
- Using economics-standard author-year parenthetical citations instead of the journal's required legal-style citation format (Author 'Title' (Year) Volume *Journal* Page; "above n X" short forms).
- Writing to a 10,000-word ABS-4-style length by default; this journal's norm is ~6,000 words.
- Assuming a strict quantitative empirical structure (Data/Identification/Results) is mandatory — the journal explicitly accepts qualitative, practitioner-oriented, and policy-survey papers with different architectures.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
