# Journal Writing Skill: World Review of Entrepreneurship, Management and Sustainable Development

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ENT-SBM
- **Publisher:** Inderscience Publishers
- **Scope:** Multidisciplinary refereed journal on entrepreneurship management and sustainable development, focusing on business creation, growth, and challenges of global competitiveness and sustainable development.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.inderscience.com/jhome.php?jcode=wremsd
- **Field baseline:** entrepreneurship-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (from 2024-2025 publications via IDEAS/RePEc abstracts and article metadata)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
**Acceptable article types:** Original papers, review papers, conceptual papers, business and management case studies, conference reports, management reports, book reviews, notes, commentaries, and news. Briefs and research notes are NOT accepted.

**Manuscript components (in order):** Title, Abstract, Keywords, Main text, References/Notes, Tables with captions, Figures with captions. Author names, biographical notes, and acknowledgements must NOT appear in the main document (author information is submitted separately).

### Length
- **Article length:** 5,000–7,000 words excluding figures, tables, and references. High-quality articles exceeding this range receive consideration.
- **Page format:** A4 size, single or double spacing acceptable.
- **Font:** Preferably Times New Roman, size 12.

### Abstract
- **Format:** Unstructured (single paragraph)
- **Length:** Maximum 150 words
- **Content requirements:** Must briefly cover research aim, methodologies used, and conclusions. Minimize references; avoid uncommon abbreviations.
- **Keywords:** Approximately 10–15 words or phrases to improve searchability in indexes and online search engines.

### Citations
- **Style:** Harvard (name–date) reference system
- **In-text format:** "Author (Year)" or "Author and Author (Year)" or "multiple authors' study (Year)"
- **Reference list:** Full, detailed alphabetical list at end of article with all authors' names and initials, date, title of article, title of publication (italicized), volume and issue number, page numbers, and DOI if available.
- **Notes/Footnotes:** Avoided, but short, succinct notes making specific points may be placed in numbered order following the reference list.

### Headings
Not explicitly specified in guidelines. Inderscience publisher guidelines suggest a standard hierarchical structure but do not mandate specific heading formats in WREMSD-specific guidance.

### Tables and Figures
- **Placement:** Figures should be placed at the end of the article, not interspersed with text.
- **Captions:** Required for all tables and figures.
- **Citations in text:** Articles must reference tables and figures; they are integral to the narrative.

### Language and Tone
- **Language:** All articles must be written in English using UK spelling and terminology.
- **Proofreading:** If English is not your first language, articles should be proofread by a native English speaker.
- **Tone requirements:** Not explicitly specified in guidelines (follows field baseline).

### Review Process and Publication Status
- **Peer review:** Double-blind review process.
- **Publication requirements:** Submitted articles must be original work, not previously published, and not currently under consideration for publication elsewhere.
- **Conference papers:** Must be completely rewritten and copyright permissions cleared from original venue.
- **AI disclosure:** If AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, generative AI) were used, authors must describe the technologies and purposes in the Acknowledgements section (excluding standard grammar checkers or reference managers).

### Explicit Prohibitions
- No briefs, research notes, or standalone literature review articles without novel contribution (implied from accepted types).
- Author names, biographical information, and acknowledgements must not appear in the submitted manuscript document (submitted separately).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Simplicity
**FIELD_SKILL expectation:** Detailed, standardized section structure with named subsections (Theory/Hypothesis Development, Methods, Results, Discussion, Limitations, Conclusion) and explicit Practical/Managerial Implications subsection.

**WREMSD practice:** Guideline does not mandate explicit subsections within the main text. Articles spanning review papers, conceptual papers, and case studies are accepted alongside empirical work, suggesting greater structural flexibility than the ABS 4*/4 baseline. The field baseline's near-universal Practical Implications subsection is recommended but not enforced at this tier.

**Classification:** RELAXATION. Lower-tier journal permits more varied structures while maintaining coherent argumentation.

### Voice and Person
**FIELD_SKILL consensus:** Strong expectation of first-person plural voice ("we") throughout.

**WREMSD practice:** Guideline does not specify voice/person requirements. Given the journal's lower tier and broader article types (including case studies, commentaries, notes), voice requirements are likely relaxed to match the diversity of contribution types.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT. No explicit delta; assume FIELD_SKILL applies unless contradicted in article samples.

### Citation Density and Style
**FIELD_SKILL consensus:** High citation density in introduction and theory sections; lower in methods and results; moderate in discussion. Paraphrase dominates; direct quotation sparse.

**WREMSD practice:** Harvard referencing (consistent with FIELD_SKILL norms) is mandatory. Density expectations are not specified and likely vary by article type (review papers would typically have higher citation density than case studies or commentaries).

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT. Citation *style* is enforced (Harvard); citation *density* follows FIELD_SKILL by default.

### Abstract Format
**FIELD_SKILL baseline:** Not explicitly addressed at ABS 4*/4 level.

**WREMSD enforcement:** Unstructured abstract (single paragraph), less than 150 words, must cover aim, methodology, and conclusions. This is more prescriptive and restrictive than typical ABS 4 journals and represents an explicit ENFORCED constraint.

**Classification:** ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, confirmed across multiple publisher resources).

### Practical/Managerial Implications
**FIELD_SKILL consensus:** Near-universal, substantial subsection expected in discussion.

**WREMSD practice:** Not mentioned in guidelines. Accepted article types include conceptual papers, commentaries, and notes, which may not require practical implications. At ABS 1 tier, this near-universal requirement of the field baseline is a relaxation.

**Classification:** RELAXATION. Not explicitly required; follows article type.

### Hedging and Epistemic Caution
**FIELD_SKILL consensus:** Moderate hedging, concentrated in Discussion and Limitations sections; Results reported in direct, unhedged language.

**WREMSD practice:** Not specified in guidelines. No delta observed.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT.

### Methods Transparency and Common-Method Bias
**FIELD_SKILL consensus:** Extensive procedural detail, explicit common-method bias discussion, and robustness checks.

**WREMSD practice:** Not specified in guidelines. Given lower ABS tier and mixed article types (case studies, commentaries), this level of methodological rigor is likely not enforced uniformly.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT with likely relaxation for non-empirical contributions.

### Length and Compression
**FIELD_SKILL baseline:** Not specified (typical ABS 4 articles range 8,000–10,000 words).

**WREMSD enforcement:** 5,000–7,000 words (excluding figures, tables, references) is a hard constraint. This is notably shorter than top-tier norms, permitting consideration of high-quality articles exceeding this range.

**Classification:** ENFORCED. Significantly shorter manuscript length compared to ABS 4*/4 baseline.

## Common Pitfalls Specific to WREMSD

1. **Exceeding word limit:** The 5,000–7,000 word target is significantly shorter than ABS 4*/4 journals. Submitting lengthy theoretical dissertations or comprehensive empirical studies without careful compression will likely result in desk rejection or major revision requests.

2. **Including author information in manuscript:** Many authors forget that Inderscience requires author names, biographical notes, and acknowledgements to be submitted separately from the main document. Submission of a document with author names embedded in headers or footnotes violates journal policy.

3. **Omitting AI tool disclosure:** If any generative AI or machine-learning tools were used in drafting, analysis, or revision, authors must explicitly declare this in the Acknowledgements section (submitted separately). Failure to disclose triggers plagiarism concern and potential desk rejection.

4. **Misjudging article-type suitability:** The journal explicitly does not accept "briefs" or "research notes." Papers framed as preliminary findings or short communications without substantial novel contribution risk rejection before peer review. Conceptual papers and case studies must demonstrate clear managerial or theoretical relevance to sustainable entrepreneurship.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
