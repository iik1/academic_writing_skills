# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Accounting and Taxation

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Academic Journals
- **ISSN:** 2141-6664
- **DOI Prefix:** 10.5897/JAT
- **Scope:** Open access, peer-reviewed journal covering all areas of accounting and taxation. Published monthly. Accepts full-length research, short communications, and reviews.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://academicjournals.org/journal/JAT/authors (author guidelines accessible)
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 15 articles from 2025-2026 publications (ESG disclosure, tax compliance, earnings management, transfer pricing, accounting technology adoption, bibliometric reviews, and corporate governance studies)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict. All constraints are ENFORCED per explicit author guidelines.

### Structure
Articles must include the following sections in order:
1. Title (with authors' full names, affiliations, emails—no academic titles like Prof. or Dr.)
2. Abstract (unstructured; max 300 words)
3. Keywords (max 10)
4. Introduction
5. Materials and Methods (or equivalent methods section)
6. Results and Discussion (may be combined or separate)
7. Conflict of Interest Disclosure
8. Acknowledgments
9. References
10. Tables and Figures (numbered consecutively, placed at end)
11. Optional: Appendices

### Length
- **Maximum:** 25 pages (single-line spacing)
- No extension requested in sampled articles

### Abstract
- **Required:** Yes
- **Format:** Unstructured single paragraph
- **Word limit:** Maximum 300 words (ENFORCED)
- Follows standard format: research question, method, key findings, contribution

### Citations
- **Style:** Author-date format (alphabetical order in references)
- **In-text:** (Author Year) or Author (Year) depending on syntax
- **Reference format (example from guidelines):** Davis MM, Heineke J (1994). Title. Journal Name 14(5):21-34. http://dx.doi.org/[DOI]
- **Requirement:** Full journal names (not abbreviated). DOI links must be included where available.
- **Alphabetical order:** References must be listed alphabetically at end of paper
- FIELD_SKILL norm: Author-date with high citation density in introduction. JAT follows this standard.

### Headings
- **Major headings (Level 1):** ALL CAPS, BOLD, no numbering (e.g., INTRODUCTION, MATERIALS AND METHODS, RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
- **Subheadings (Level 2):** Title case, normal text, no numbering
- **Level 3+:** Not discussed in guidelines; avoid deep nesting
- **Critical constraint:** NO NUMBERED HEADINGS OR SUBHEADINGS ALLOWED (differs from FIELD_SKILL variable practice)

### Tables and Figures
- **Table titles:** Brief descriptive title positioned at top-left corner
- **Table format:** Must be organized in Microsoft Word or Excel—NOT as pictures
- **Table use:** Explicitly minimize table use. This is a deliberate editorial choice distinct from FIELD_SKILL norm (which views tables as primary vehicle for results).
- **Units:** State unit of measurement in table
- **Numbering:** Tables numbered consecutively (Table 1, Table 2, etc.)
- **Figure captions:** Brief descriptive caption placed at bottom-left of figure
- **Figure formats:** GIF, TIFF, JPEG, or PowerPoint acceptable
- **Citation in text:** Tables and figures must be appropriately cited in manuscript prose

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Footnotes:** NOT accepted. All information must appear in main text.
- **Abbreviations:** Use standard abbreviations throughout. Non-standard abbreviations must be minimized, well-defined at first use, and an abbreviations section provided if extensive.
- **Formatting:** International System of Units (ISI) and accepted nomenclature must be followed
- **Plagiarism:** Submissions checked via iThenticate. Unacceptable similarity to published works results in desk rejection.
- **Duplicate submission:** Cannot submit same/similar manuscript to another journal simultaneously
- **Data fabrication:** Fabrication, manipulation, or falsification of data is prohibited; COPE guidelines apply
- **Citation manipulation:** Irrelevant citations and self-citation inflation are prohibited

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Heading Format and Numbering
**Delta (ENFORCED):** JAT explicitly prohibits numbered headings and subheadings. All major headings must be ALL CAPS and bold; subheadings must be title case and normal text. This is stricter than FIELD_SKILL, which notes that heading numbering practice varies across journals (Roman numerals vs. Arabic numerals in TAR/JAE/JAR/AOS). Submitting a JAT manuscript with numbered headings violates journal requirements.

### Footnotes
**Delta (ENFORCED):** JAT explicitly states "The use of footnotes is not accepted." FIELD_SKILL does not explicitly restrict footnotes (only notes that passive voice is used for procedural descriptions where the subject is irrelevant). Any information conveyed in a footnote must be moved to the main text.

### Page and Abstract Limits
**Delta (ENFORCED):** JAT specifies a 25-page limit (single-line spacing) and abstract must be < 300 words. FIELD_SKILL does not specify these constraints. This is a hard limit and violations will result in desk rejection or request for revision.

### Keywords
**Delta (ENFORCED):** JAT requires keywords (max 10) as a distinct field. FIELD_SKILL mentions keywords appear as separate fields in ABS 4* journals but does not impose a word count. JAT's guidance is more restrictive.

### Table Use Philosophy
**Delta (ENFORCED):** JAT's guidelines state "Table use should be kept to a minimum." This is an editorial stance distinct from FIELD_SKILL, which treats tables as the primary vehicle for communicating quantitative results and does not discourage their use. Authors should be more conservative with tabular presentation at JAT than at higher-tier journals.

### Citation Examples and Journal Name Format
**Delta (ENFORCED):** JAT examples show full journal names (not abbreviated) and must include DOI links. The example format deviates slightly from standard accounting author-date (e.g., "Davis MM, Heineke J" without "and" between final authors, and the year in parentheses after authors). Verify formatting carefully against JAT examples, not FIELD_SKILL conventions.

### Results and Discussion Section
**FIELD_SKILL default:** Discussion and Conclusion are typically merged; standalone Discussion is less common.
**JAT guideline:** "Results and discussion maybe combined into a single section or presented separately, where necessary." This explicitly allows both forms and is not a delta—JAT aligns with FIELD_SKILL norm.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (first-person plural "we" dominant; active voice primary; high formality; measured hedging).
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium to long sentences; dense paragraphs; high technical density in methods and results).
- **Argumentation:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (standard CARS introduction structure; explicit contribution statement; gap framing).
- **Methods conventions:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (past tense in methods; high procedural detail; identification strategy discussion; robustness checks expected).
- **Results reporting:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (tables + prose interpretation; statistical narration with coefficients and p-values; organization by hypothesis; null results reported; economic significance increasingly expected).
- **Discussion and Conclusion norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (merged or separate both acceptable; restates findings; contributes explicitly; includes limitations paragraph; forward-looking suggestions optional).

## Common Pitfalls for This Journal

1. **Numbering headings:** Many authors trained on other journals use numbered headings (1., 2., I., II., etc.). JAT desk editors will reject or request revision. All headings must be unnumbered.

2. **Footnotes for additional detail:** Authors often use footnotes to avoid cluttering the main text. JAT does not accept footnotes. Reorganize content to integrate footnote material into main text or move to appendix.

3. **Exceeding page limit:** Targeting 25 pages with single-line spacing is tighter than some accounting journals. Early drafts often run long; budget time for compression. Tables and figures count toward the limit.

4. **Overloading tables:** While FIELD_SKILL journals value comprehensive tabular results, JAT editorial guidance suggests minimizing tables. Present only essential tables in main text; move supplementary tables to appendix or online supplement if necessary.

5. **Non-standard abbreviations:** Do not assume readership will recognize field-specific acronyms. Define all non-standard abbreviations at first use, even if common in your subfield. Provide an abbreviations section if they are extensive.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
