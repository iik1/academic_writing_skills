HARD CONSTRAINTS IDENTIFIED
================================================================================
Structure:             Non-empirical work only. Theory, conceptual articles,
                       integrative literature reviews, theory-building methods,
                       foundations of HRM. NO empirical field-based studies
                       (quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods).

Length:                Maximum 40 pages (including abstract, keywords, references,
                       tables, figures). Hard limit.

Abstract:              Unstructured, ~150 words. Purpose, major findings/
                       contributions, conclusions.

Keywords:              Minimum 4-5, specific to topic, listed after abstract.

Citations:             APA format. Every in-text citation must have reference
                       match (identical spelling, year). Update preprints to
                       published versions; citations to preprints discouraged.

Formatting:            Word format preferred. Double-spaced text and abstract.
                       Submit via Sage Track.

Headings:              Clear, consistent levels required. No single mandatory
                       structure (varies by article type).

Explicit Prohibitions: Field-based empirical studies (data collection from
                       populations, organizations, individuals) are explicitly
                       NOT accepted. Empirical method/design papers are rejected.

Critical Delta 1: Article Type and Scope (ENFORCED)
    - FIELD_SKILL assumes diversity: empirical quantitative, qualitative,
      mixed-method studies PLUS conceptual and review articles.
    - HRDR accepts ONLY non-empirical work: theory, conceptual models,
      integrative reviews with theoretical implications, theory history,
      metatheory, taxonomy/typology development.
    - Implication: Empirical researcher must reposition work as theoretical
      synthesis or move to different journal. This is the journal's defining
      constraint.

Major Delta 2: Argument Structure (PRACTICE ONLY)
    - FIELD_SKILL describes CARS-like structure (context → gap → solution)
      typical of empirical papers.
    - HRDR integrative reviews establish CONCEPTUAL gaps (not empirical gaps),
      conduct systematic review, synthesize, conclude with theory-building
      implications. No uniform structure enforced.
    - Theory articles may skip formal "gap" entirely, instead presenting new
      model/framework.
    - Implication: Structure is flexible by article type; no single prescription.

Major Delta 3: Methods Section (N/A)
    - FIELD_SKILL details empirical methods (sample, measures, estimation,
      robustness, ethics approval).
    - HRDR has no empirical research, so traditional methods sections do NOT
      apply. Integrative reviews may include "Approach" section (databases,
      selection criteria, synthesis method), but this is methodological
      documentation of the review process, not empirical methodology.
    - Implication: Authors should not include empirical methods sections. For
      reviews, document review process explicitly and systematically.

Major Delta 4: Results/Findings (N/A)
    - FIELD_SKILL describes quantitative results tables and qualitative findings
      organized thematically with participant quotations.
    - HRDR does not report empirical findings. Integrative reviews present
      synthesized themes/patterns/insights from reviewed literature. Theory
      articles present new theoretical propositions and frameworks.
    - Implication: No results tables, no participant data, no new empirical
      findings reported.

Minor Delta 1: Voice/Register (Practice, aligned overall)
    - FIELD_SKILL notes HRM&EMP tolerates more narrative/interpretive prose
      than economics. HRDR's emphasis on theory-building and conceptual
      synthesis likely encourages slightly more interpretive, discursive tone
      than strict field norm, especially in integrative reviews framing
      synthesis arguments.
    - Implication: Slightly more interpretive framing acceptable; still formal
      and academic.

Minor Delta 2: Practical Implications (Practice)
    - FIELD_SKILL treats practical implications as required for applied field.
    - HRDR expects implications for theory-building and future HRD practice/
      policy, but emphasis is on theory advancement first. Practitioner
      implications secondary to theoretical contribution.
    - Implication: Frame practitioner implications as consequences of
      theoretical insight, not primary contribution.

COMMON PITFALLS (JOURNAL-SPECIFIC)
================================================================================
1. Submitting empirical research (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method field
   studies). This is the most common rejection reason. HRDR is a theory-only
   journal. If your work is empirical, reposition as theoretical synthesis or
   submit elsewhere.

2. Misunderstanding article type. "Literature review" is not always acceptable;
   it must be integrative, systematic, and with clear theoretical implications.
   Narrative summaries and descriptive reviews are rejected.

3. Overstating empirical claims. When building theory from other studies' data,
   distinguish between prior empirical evidence (cited) and your theoretical
   contribution (novel).

4. Exceeding 40 pages. Initial submissions over 40 pages may be desk-rejected.
   Conceptual work is dense; prioritize concision and clarity.

ASSESSMENT
================================================================================
HRDR represents a significant departure from the field baseline in scope and
article type. The FIELD_SKILL baseline assumes a healthy mix of empirical
research (the core output of HRM&EMP journals at ABS 4*/4 levels). HRDR, as
an ABS 2 journal, has carved out a specialized niche: theory development only.

This is not a "relaxation" of field norms (lower rigor), but a different
editorial philosophy—value is placed on conceptual clarity, theoretical
synthesis, and meta-theoretical contribution rather than on empirical evidence
and methodological rigor. For scholars transitioning from empirical work to
HRDR submissions, the shift is substantial.

All other conventions (voice, citation density, discussion structure, hedging,
practical implications) remain aligned with HRM&EMP field norms. The skill
captures the critical distinction (article type/scope) and flags the second-
order implications (no empirical methods, no results sections, flexible
structure).
