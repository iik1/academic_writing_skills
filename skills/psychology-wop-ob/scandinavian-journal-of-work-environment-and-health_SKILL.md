# Journal Writing Skill: Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment and Health

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** PSYCH (WOP-OB)
- **Publisher:** Nordic Association of Occupational Safety and Health (NOROSH), independent not-for-profit
- **Scope:** Occupational and environmental epidemiology, occupational medicine, psychosocial work factors, musculoskeletal disorders, work ability, occupational hygiene and safety — a multidisciplinary occupational-health journal rather than a dedicated WOP-OB psychology outlet.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sjweh.fi/instructions.php
- **Field baseline:** psychology-wop-ob-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 7 (Backes et al. 2026;52(2):79-97 [systematic review]; van Ooijen et al. 2021;47(6):435-445; Pahwa et al. [glyphosate/NHL pooled analysis]; Ryynänen et al. [cluster RCT low-back pain]; Bonde et al. 2016;42(1):17-25 [workplace bullying]; Jaspers et al. 2025;51(5):349-354 [editorial]; Pega et al. [WHO/ILO burden-of-disease study])

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Title page (separate/anonymized), then main manuscript: Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, optional "Concluding Remarks," Acknowledgements, References, Tables, Figures, appendices last. No separate "Theoretical Background/Hypotheses" heading is used or expected — theory is embedded in the Introduction. Double-blind peer review, so the main text must be anonymized.

### Length
Original research: abstract ≤250 words; body text (Introduction through Conclusion) ≤4,000 words; ≤5 tables/figures; ~40 references. Short communications: 1,500 words body, 250-word abstract, 2 tables/figures, 20 references. Systematic reviews: 5,000 words body, 250-word abstract, 5 tables/figures preferred, 40 references preferred (both expandable with justification).

### Abstract
Structured, mandatory 4-part format: Objectives, Methods, Results, Conclusions — no separate "Practical Implications" or "Originality" fields. 250-word hard cap. Key terms not already in the title should appear in the abstract for indexing.

### Citations
Vancouver style (ICMJE), not APA. In-text citations are numbered consecutively as Arabic numerals in parentheses (non-superscript) in order of first mention, not author-date. Journal titles abbreviated per Web of Science conventions. ≤6 authors: list all; >6: first six + "et al." Unpublished observations/personal communications cannot be cited. Software packages (R, Stata, SPSS, etc.) may not be cited as references.

### Headings
Standard four-part Introduction/Methods/Results/Discussion skeleton with an optional "Concluding Remarks" subsection at the end of Discussion; no field-standard "Theoretical Background," "Hypotheses," or "Practical Implications" headings are used.

### Tables and Figures
Placed at the end of the manuscript (after references), not embedded in text. One value per cell, no macros/tabs/spacebar formatting, consistent column data types, acronyms defined via alphabetical footnotes. Figures require ≥800 dpi resolution and legible reduction-scale text. Self-explanatory, non-duplicative of prose.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Field codes must be converted to plain text; unpublished data/personal communications cannot be cited; software packages cannot be referenced as sources; identifying patient/participant information requires explicit written consent. Reporting-guideline checklists (STROBE for observational studies, CONSORT for RCTs, PRISMA/MOOSE for reviews, COREQ for qualitative work, etc.) must be followed and submitted where applicable — this is enforced, not optional.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
RELAXATION: register is plainer and more concise than the ABS 4/4* field norm — the journal explicitly instructs authors to write "as concise as possible" for an applied, multidisciplinary occupational-health readership (physicians, epidemiologists, ergonomists) rather than a specialist WOP-OB psychology audience. Theoretical framing is lighter; abstracts foreground exposure, outcome, and effect size rather than theoretical contribution.

### Statistical Reporting
PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence toward epidemiological rather than psychometric reporting: many spot-checked articles report incidence/prevalence, odds ratios, and disability-adjusted measures rather than the SEM/moderated-mediation apparatus common in ABS 4/4* WOP-OB journals. This reflects the journal's broader occupational-epidemiology scope; where a submission is a classic psychosocial-survey design, FIELD_SKILL statistical norms (effect sizes, CIs, reliability coefficients) still apply and were observed (e.g., Bonde et al.).

### Structural Deviations
ENFORCED: no distinct "Theoretical Background/Hypotheses" section and no "Practical Implications" heading — both common at the ABS 4/4* level per FIELD_SKILL — are used here. Practical relevance is discussed in prose within the Discussion/Concluding Remarks rather than as a labeled subsection.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED: numbered Vancouver citation style replaces the field-default author-date convention — this is a mechanical formatting delta, not a stylistic one, but it changes in-text sentence construction (claims are followed by bare numerals in parentheses rather than "(Author, Year)" clusters).

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Hedging and epistemic caution (causal-language caution for non-experimental designs): FIELD DEFAULT
- Passive voice in Methods/Results: FIELD DEFAULT
- Results/Discussion separation of fact from interpretation: FIELD DEFAULT
- Candid, specific limitations reporting: FIELD DEFAULT
- Argumentation logic (gap identification, framing disagreement as unresolved tension): FIELD DEFAULT

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting an APA author-date reference list instead of Vancouver numbered citations.
- Including a separate "Theoretical Background" or "Hypotheses" heading, or a labeled "Practical Implications" section — neither is part of this journal's expected skeleton.
- Exceeding the 250-word structured abstract or omitting one of the four required fields (Objectives/Methods/Results/Conclusions).
- Citing software (R, SPSS, Stata) or unpublished/personal communications as numbered references.
- Failing to attach the relevant reporting-guideline checklist (STROBE/CONSORT/PRISMA/COREQ) for the study design used.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
