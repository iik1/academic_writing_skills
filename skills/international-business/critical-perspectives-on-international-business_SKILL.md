# Journal Writing Skill: Critical Perspectives on International Business

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** IB&AREA
- **Publisher:** Emerald Group Publishing
- **ISSN:** 1742-2043 | eISSN: 1758-6062
- **Scope:** Exclusively supports critically reflexive discussions of the nature and impact of international business activity around the globe from disciplinary and interdisciplinary perspectives. Emphasizes critical engagement with globalization, international business strategies, corporate social responsibility, and power relations within and between multinational firms and civil society. The journal promotes questioning of transnational corporate hegemony and managerial orthodoxy.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

**Accessibility note:** The journal's specific author guidelines page is paywalled. The following constraints are derived from Emerald Publishing's standard journal submission requirements and observable patterns from journal abstracts and editorials.

### Structure

**Empirical and conceptual papers** should follow standard academic structure: Introduction, theoretical/conceptual sections, methodology (if applicable), results/findings, discussion, and conclusion. Near-universal sections include introduction, theoretical grounding, discussion/implications, and conclusion (aligns with FIELD_SKILL).

**Editorial and special issue submissions** require introduction with literature review, critique of extant literature, identification of current debates and limitations, and a future research agenda.

**Structured Abstract (Emerald standard):** Required; must be presented under 4–7 sub-headings. Mandatory categories: Purpose, Design/methodology/approach, Findings, Originality/value. Optional categories: Research limitations/implications, Practical implications, Social implications.

### Length

**Abstract:** Maximum 250 words total (including keywords and article classification).

**Keywords:** Up to 12 keywords recommended.

**Article word count:** Specific limits not publicly stated in accessible guidelines. Word count calculation includes structured abstract, all references, and all text within tables and figures, plus 280 words per table/figure. Each Emerald journal sets its own word count parameter on its homepage.

### Abstract
Structured format mandatory (4–7 subheadings). Maximum 250 words. Keywords (up to 12) typically listed after abstract.

### Citations

Author-date parenthetical style (standard for management/IB field). No explicit journal-specific deviation documented from FIELD_SKILL norms, but given Emerald's broad portfolio, Harvard or APA style confirmation not provided in accessible guidelines.

### Headings

No explicit constraints found in publicly accessible materials beyond standard academic convention. Likely follows numbered or unnumbered hierarchical heading structure (Emerald norm).

### Tables and Figures

No explicit constraints found in accessible guidelines. Standard academic conventions apply (clear captions, source attribution, integration into text narrative).

### Explicit Prohibitions

**Generative AI use:** Copywriting (creating, drafting, or writing) any part of a submission using generative AI tools is prohibited. Copy-editing using generative AI to improve structure, clarity, language, and grammar is permitted.

**Cover letters:** Strongly encouraged (not mandatory), especially for new/early-career authors. Should explain why CPoIB is appropriate, which editorial discourses the paper engages with, and how the submission addresses journal's editorial agenda.

**Conflict of interest declaration:** Required upon submission.

**Copyright:** Authors must guarantee work is not infringing any existing copyright and have obtained written permission for any third-party created material.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Critical Reflexivity and Stance
CPoIB explicitly frames itself as the only journal exclusively supporting "critically reflexive discussions." Editorial materials emphasize the journal's mission to "creatively and critically question the hegemony of transnational corporations, managerial orthodoxy and the dominant academic discourse." This is stronger language than typical IB field norms. Authors should position work not just as contributing knowledge but as offering a critical, reflexive perspective on international business power structures, effects on individuals/communities/environment, and societal implications. The tone is explicitly engaged and argumentative rather than neutral.

**Classification:** GUIDELINE ONLY (stated in journal mission; observable in editorials and cover letter expectations; not explicitly mandated in author instructions).

### Scope Beyond Traditional IB
Beyond markets and firms, papers frequently address sustainability, environment, labor practices, gender, decolonization, and civil society engagement. This broader scope is implied in the journal's critical stance and editorial topics but is not a hard constraint on all submissions. Authors submitting empirical efficiency or strategic analyses should consider whether a critical angle on broader societal implications strengthens fit.

**Classification:** PRACTICE ONLY (visible in recent editorial selections and special issue calls; not a stated requirement).

### Inclusion of Limitations and Future Research
CPoIB editorials and special issue calls consistently emphasize identifying current debates, limitations in extant literature, and future research agendas. This aligns with FIELD_SKILL expectations but appears more explicitly foregrounded here.

**Classification:** FIELD DEFAULT (aligned with FIELD_SKILL but may be weighted more heavily in editorial decisions).

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and Person:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we argue," "our findings") is standard and expected; passive/impersonal voice acceptable for literature synthesis and procedural description.
- **Sentence and Paragraph Style:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Medium-to-long, compound sentences with qualified claims; topic-sentence-led paragraphs advancing single claims; conceptual framing devices acceptable.
- **Argumentation and Rhetorical Moves:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. CARS introduction logic expected; gap framing (empirical, theoretical, synthetic); explicit itemized contribution statements; respectful repositioning of prior work rather than refutation.
- **Methods and Replication Detail:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Substantial procedural detail expected; past tense for methods; robustness checks standard.
- **Results and Reporting:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Tables/figures plus narrative prose; present tense for results; description before interpretation.
- **Discussion and Implications:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Loop back to gap and contributions; theoretical before practical implications; distinct limitations statement; concrete future research directions.
- **Citation Density:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High throughout, especially in introduction/theory/discussion; author-date parenthetical; paraphrase over direct quotation; juxtaposition for contrast.
- **Hedging and Epistemic Caution:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Moderate hedging; unhedged language reserved for describing gaps in literature, not authors' own claims.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Ignoring the critical reflexivity requirement.** Submitting a purely descriptive or managerially-focused IB paper without engaging with power structures, societal impact, or critical questions about multinational firm legitimacy and effects. CPoIB editors expect papers to do more than add empirical findings; they should offer a reflexive position on what the findings mean for understanding international business as a social phenomenon.

2. **Overlooking the cover letter as a signaling tool.** Especially for early-career authors and those unfamiliar with the journal, failing to submit a cover letter, or submitting a generic letter without explicitly connecting the submission to CPoIB's editorial agenda and critical positioning, reduces perceived fit.

3. **Neglecting to address broader societal implications.** Papers that focus narrowly on firm strategy or market outcomes without acknowledging effects on communities, labor, environment, or power dynamics may be perceived as insufficiently aligned with the journal's scope, even if methodologically sound.

4. **Using generative AI for drafting.** Submitting a manuscript drafted or substantially written using ChatGPT, Claude, or similar tools will trigger desk rejection. Copy-editing for clarity is permitted; original composition is not.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
