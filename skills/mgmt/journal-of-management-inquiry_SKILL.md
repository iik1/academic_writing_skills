# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Management Inquiry

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** SAGE
- **Scope:** Publishes catalytic, eclectic, and creative management research across eight distinct sections (Empirical, Essay, Curated, Provocations and Provocateurs, Generative Curiosity, Meet the Person, Reflections on Experience, Six Degrees), explicitly welcoming non-traditional research and theory/practice-bridging work rather than only standard empirical papers.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/jmi (accessed via cached PDF snapshot)
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 6-7 (Berglund & Dimov, "Grounding and Developing the Design Perspective on Entrepreneurship," JMI 34(3), full text via author's site; Pearce & Pechmann essay, 2025, abstract; Johansen et al., "Theorizing Governance Paradoxes," JMI 35(3), 2025, abstract; Gioia, "Collective Stupidity," Provocations, JMI 33(1), 2023, abstract; Hannah, "A Call for Greater Humility in Management Research," Provocations, 2025, abstract; additional current-issue Provocations/Essay abstracts surfaced in search snippets)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No single mandated IMRaD template. The journal is organized into eight named sections, each with a distinct expected form (Empirical, Essay, Curated, Provocations and Provocateurs, Generative Curiosity, Meet the Person, Reflections on Experience, Six Degrees). Authors effectively select a section/genre rather than following one universal structure. "Provocations and Provocateurs" is invitation-only; unsolicited submissions to that section are not accepted. Essays and other non-empirical sections need not include Methods or Results sections at all.

### Length
No explicit overall word limit stated in guidelines. "Provocations and Provocateurs" follows an explicit "6-6" rule: 6 pages, 6 references — far shorter and far more citation-sparse than any FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Abstract
Unstructured abstract required, placed between title and body, concisely stating purpose, major findings, and conclusions. No stated word limit. No citations in the abstract (consistent with FIELD_SKILL).

### Citations
APA reference style (consistent with FIELD_SKILL). Every in-text citation must have a matching reference-list entry and vice versa, with identical spelling and year. Citations to preprints are discouraged once a peer-reviewed version exists.

### Headings
Title Page and main manuscript must be fully anonymized for double-anonymized review (no author names in file name, headers, or footers). A "Statements and Declarations" section is required near the end, with mandatory sub-headings (funding, conflicts of interest, ethics approval, consent to participate, consent for publication, data availability) even when "Not applicable."

### Tables and Figures
Standard SAGE rules: figures numbered consecutively, 300dpi, color free online. No journal-specific table/figure conventions beyond SAGE defaults.

### Explicit Prohibitions
AI chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT) must not be listed as authors. Third-party ghost-submission (submitting on behalf of someone not listed as author) is prohibited without disclosure; the editor may refuse such submissions. Author-recommended reviewers are not accepted.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
Essay-type articles (a major, non-empirical section unique to this journal) use a markedly more personal, reflective, first-person register than FIELD_SKILL's "academic but direct" norm — e.g., "we hope this essay can make such accounts easier to articulate," "our ambition has been to breathe new life into the concept of opportunity." This is closer to an argumentative op-ed voice than standard management prose. PRACTICE ONLY / journal-genre-specific (essay and provocation sections), not a norm for the Empirical section, which follows FIELD_SKILL voice.

### Structural Deviations
The journal formally offers non-empirical, argument-driven article types (Essay, Provocations and Provocateurs, Generative Curiosity) with no Methods/Results sections and no numbered hypotheses or propositions — sections are instead named after the substantive claim being advanced (e.g., "The Design Perspective," "Tradeoffs Between Simplicity and Accuracy"), consistent with FIELD_SKILL's "Theoretical/Conceptual" template but pushed further toward essayistic argument rather than formal propositions (no P1/P2 labeling observed even in a fully theory-building essay). RELAXATION of FIELD_SKILL's proposition/hypothesis convention for this journal's non-empirical sections (ABS 3, cannot redefine baseline, but journal genre permits skipping formal labeling entirely). The "Provocations and Provocateurs" section's 6-page/6-reference format is a hard structural constraint well outside anything in FIELD_SKILL. ENFORCED (stated in guidelines, confirmed by Gioia and Hannah pieces being short, invitation-only, sparsely cited).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Extensive endnotes are used in essay-type pieces to carry substantive tangential argument (not just definitional asides as in FIELD_SKILL) — the Berglund & Dimov essay carries 8 discursive endnotes engaging directly with the theoretical argument. PRACTICE ONLY, observed in the essay genre.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Empirical-section structure and voice: FIELD DEFAULT (follows FIELD_SKILL quantitative/qualitative templates; e.g., Johansen et al.'s governance paradox paper is a standard longitudinal qualitative case study with document analysis and interviews).
- Citation style (APA): FIELD DEFAULT.
- Abstract content requirements (purpose, findings, conclusions, no citations): FIELD DEFAULT.
- Table/figure formatting: FIELD DEFAULT (SAGE house style, no journal-specific deviation observed).
- Hedging and epistemic stance in empirical work: FIELD DEFAULT.

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting an unsolicited manuscript to "Provocations and Provocateurs" — this section is invitation-only.
- Treating the essay/provocation genres as requiring a Methods section or formal hypotheses/propositions; these sections are explicitly argument-driven and do not follow the empirical template.
- Exceeding the 6-page/6-reference limit for a Provocations piece.
- Failing to fully anonymize the manuscript body (author identity must appear only on the separate Title Page) given the double-anonymized review process.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
