# Journal Writing Skill: Oxford Review of Economic Policy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Scope:** Quarterly thematic journal on contemporary economic policy issues, balancing macro and microeconomics. Each issue focuses on a single economic policy topic through an editorial overview and 4-8 commissioned articles. Targets broad audience including policymakers, government, business, and academics.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://academic.oup.com/oxrep/pages/general_instructions
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (Vol. 41 Issues 1-2, 2025; selected from UK welfare policy, health economics, climate economics, competition policy themes)

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows standard economics article format (Introduction, substantive sections, Conclusion). No mandatory section-naming conventions specified beyond general academic expectations. Articles organized around policy questions rather than methodological exposition.

### Length
**Critical constraint:** Manuscripts must not exceed 9,000 words total, including diagrams, footnotes, and tables. Articles exceeding this limit may not be accepted.

### Abstract
**Format:** Unstructured single paragraph. **Word limit:** Maximum 150 words. Submitted to American Economic Association for online bibliographic service (AEA EconLit). No structured fields required (no objectives/methods/results/conclusions).

### Citations
Author-year format (standard field default confirmed). References listed alphabetically by author surname, then chronologically within each author. Numerical footnote citations are not the norm.

### Headings
No mandatory heading style or numbering convention specified. Standard hierarchical heading structure expected (main sections, subsections).

### Tables and Figures
No specific formatting rules identified in publicly available guidelines. Standard academic journal conventions apply (numbered, captioned, source attributed).

### Explicit Prohibitions
- **Technical exposition discouraged:** Articles should avoid heavy technical exposition. Target is informed general audience, not exclusively specialists.
- **Journalistic tone prohibited:** Despite accessibility requirement, articles are "definitely not journalistic in style."
- **Unsolicited submissions unlikely to be accepted:** Journal is thematic with editors typically commissioning articles rather than accepting unsolicited work. Clarify editorial interest before extended submission effort.

### Additional Formal Requirements
- **Funding disclosure:** Required in format "This work was supported by..." with full official funding agency name and grant numbers in brackets.
- **Alt text for images:** All figures, illustrations, photographs must include alt text descriptions in the main manuscript directly under the figure legend, preceded by "Alt text:".
- **Equation numbering:** All equations numbered sequentially on the right-hand side of the page.
- **Copyright licensing:** Authors must complete copyright license form online at time of acceptance; choice between standard or open access licensing offered.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
**RELAXATION:** While FIELD_SKILL describes high-formality third-person registers, Oxford Review of Economic Policy explicitly targets broader audience beyond academic economists. Examination of multiple articles (particularly policy-focused pieces on UK welfare and health economics) reveals **third-person voice is strongly preferred**, with minimal use of first-person plural ("we") that FIELD_SKILL describes as "near-universal" in ABS 4* economics. Policy brief tone permits more accessible, less technical exposition. Language remains precise but less densely technical than top-tier journals. **Classification: RELAXATION—lower formality and higher accessibility than field baseline, but without colloquialism.**

### Citation Density
**PRACTICE ONLY:** Citation density appears lower in introduction and results sections than FIELD_SKILL describes for ABS 4* journals. This reflects the journal's policy-oriented, contemporary-issue focus rather than deep literature genealogy. Positioning relative to prior work is still present but less elaborate than in top-tier outlets. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY—observed in spot-check but not stated in guidelines.**

### Structural Deviations
**ENFORCED:** The mandatory 9,000-word limit enforces **substantially compressed article structure compared to FIELD_SKILL.** This is not merely a word budget recommendation but an explicit constraint ("articles which fail to meet this limitation may not be accepted"). Consequence: introduction, methods, and results must be tighter and less leisurely than typical ABS 4* papers. Roadmaps (Section 2 describes... Section 3 develops...) are briefer or omitted. Extended literature sections are incompatible with the limit and rare in published articles. **Classification: ENFORCED.**

### Tone and Audience
**ENFORCED:** Journal explicitly requires articles to be "written with an intelligent audience in mind, who will be able to grasp new ideas rapidly, but who may not be familiar with the existing academic literature." This differs materially from FIELD_SKILL's assumption of "trained economists" as the primary audience. Discipline-specific acronyms and techniques still used, but explanation is more generous. Policy relevance and real-world applicability are foregrounded. **Classification: ENFORCED.**

### Commissioned vs. Unsolicited
**GUIDELINE ONLY / PRACTICE ENFORCED:** Journal is explicitly thematic. Editors approach scholars and commission articles rather than accept unsolicited submissions. Journal is "very unlikely to accept unsolicited articles." This is a structural and practical constraint on submission strategy, not a stylistic one, but material to understand before investing in a submission. **Classification: GUIDELINE ONLY (stated), PRACTICE ENFORCED (observed across all articles examined).**

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Hedging and epistemic caution:** FIELD DEFAULT. Journal exhibits low hedging relative to social sciences; clear claims are preferred. Uncertainty articulated through robust identification and transparency, not verbal softening.
- **Methods conventions and tense:** FIELD DEFAULT. Mixed tense (past for procedures, present for model properties) and sufficient procedural detail for trained economist to understand are standard.
- **Results reporting:** FIELD DEFAULT. Integration of results and interpretation, emphasis on economic significance alongside statistical significance, and quantitative specificity are expected.
- **Citation format (author-year):** FIELD DEFAULT. No deviation from field standard.
- **Paraphrase over quotation:** FIELD DEFAULT. Direct quotation is rare; paraphrasing with citation is the norm.
- **Conclusion structure:** FIELD DEFAULT. Conclusion restates findings, draws implications, acknowledges limitations, suggests future work; it is shorter than introduction and contains no new results.

## Common Pitfalls
> Submission mistakes specific to this journal, evidenced in author guidelines and practice:

1. **Exceeding word limit:** Submitting manuscripts longer than 9,000 words (including all text, figures, tables, footnotes) results in desk rejection. Plan structure and pruning carefully from the start. Tightness is a feature, not a constraint to resent.

2. **Overestimating technical audience:** Using heavy econometric exposition, advanced mathematical modeling, or specialized jargon without explanation will alienate readers. The journal's strength is policy relevance to non-specialist decision-makers. Simplify technical content without oversimplifying the argument.

3. **Unsolicited submission:** Submitting without editorial interest or commissioning. Editors choose articles to fit each issue's theme. Check with editors or venue conference (e.g., recent OXREP special issue calls) before investing time in a full draft.

4. **Missing formal metadata:** Forgetting funding disclosure in required format ("This work was supported by..."), omitting alt text for figures, or improper equation numbering will delay acceptance and require revisions. Complete these elements from drafting stage, not at proof.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Summary of FIELD_SKILL Overrides

This journal deviates from ABS 4* economics norms (FIELD_SKILL) in three material ways:

1. **Word limit (9,000 max):** Enforces compression; FIELD_SKILL does not specify journal-specific limits.
2. **Audience and tone:** Explicit requirement to write for intelligent non-specialist readers; FIELD_SKILL assumes "trained economists."
3. **Voice preference:** Observational data shows third-person tone is strong; FIELD_SKILL describes first-person plural as "near-universal" at ABS 4*.

All other dimensions (citation format, hedging logic, results integration, conclusion structure, tense conventions) align with FIELD_SKILL.
