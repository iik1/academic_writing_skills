# Journal Writing Skill: Pacific Economic Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** Official journal of the Hong Kong Economic Association. Publishes high-quality empirical and theoretical research in all areas of economics, with a focus on economic issues in the Asia-Pacific region.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14680106/homepage/forauthors.html (paywalled; guidelines accessed via WebSearch and public documentation)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 4 (titles and abstracts from 2026 issue information and IDEAS/RePEc repository; full-text access limited by journal paywall)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for empirical and theory papers. No journal-specific section ordering requirements beyond standard economics conventions. All manuscripts require introduction, substantive body sections, and conclusion per FIELD_SKILL.

### Length
**ENFORCED CONSTRAINT:** Manuscripts shall not exceed 50 pages. This is a strict hard limit stated in author guidelines and enforced at submission. This is notably shorter than typical ABS 4*/4 papers and may require greater selectivity in extended robustness tables and appendix material than field norms.

### Abstract
No explicit word limit stated in publicly accessible guidelines. Standard practice inferred from similar Wiley economics journals: 150–250 words, structured (objectives, methods, results, conclusions) or unstructured. Confirmed unstructured abstracts acceptable in 2026 issues.

### Citations
**ENFORCED:** Author-year citations following APA 6th edition (American Psychological Association). In-text: (Author, Year). References listed alphabetically at end. Author-date style aligns with FIELD_SKILL norm; no deviation.

### Headings
Numbered or Roman numeral headings acceptable per FIELD_SKILL baseline. No journal-specific convention observed.

### Tables and Figures
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Tables and figures integrated into main text or appendix per standard practice. Figure legends and table notes required. No journal-specific restrictions observed beyond standard economics conventions.

### Language and Spelling
**ENFORCED:** British English spelling and grammar required throughout. This is distinct from American English conventions (colour vs. color, realise vs. realize, etc.). Check consistently for American English that may creep into technical terms or citations.

### Explicit Prohibitions and Requirements
- **ORCID Required:** Authors must provide ORCID identifiers during submission.
- **Funding Acknowledgment:** All funding sources must be listed in the Acknowledgments section.
- **Review Model:** Double-anonymized peer review (author and reviewer identities hidden).
- **Submission Portal:** Use Wiley Authors (authors.wiley.com) for new submissions.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Page Limit as a Structural Constraint
The 50-page manuscript ceiling (ENFORCED) is significantly shorter than typical ABS 4*/4 economics papers and may require compression of Extended robustness checks, appendix proofs, or data documentation sections. Within this limit, follow FIELD_SKILL expectations for results, robustness, and conclusion structure, but expect to prioritize main results over exhaustive supplementary material.

### British English Standard
The explicit language requirement (ENFORCED) introduces a minor but measurable delta: all spelling, grammar, and terminology choices must reflect British English conventions. This is a formal requirement, not a stylistic preference, and deviations will be flagged in review.

### Other Dimensions
**Voice and Register:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural standard; active voice strongly preferred.

**Statistical Reporting:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Quantitative specificity expected; confidence intervals and economic significance required.

**Citation Density:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. High in introduction, medium in methods, low in results and conclusion.

**Structural Deviations:** None observed. Article structures in 2026 issues follow standard empirical and theory patterns outlined in FIELD_SKILL.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding the 50-page limit.** The journal enforces a strict ceiling well below typical ABS 4 practice. Plan robustness checks and appendix material accordingly; prioritize main results.

2. **American English creeping into drafts.** If working in North American academic contexts, double-check spelling and word choice (realise/realize, colour/color, labour/labor, etc.). Copy editors will flag this during proofs, but catching it early prevents review delays.

3. **Incomplete ORCID and funding statements.** Submission will be rejected if ORCID is not provided and funding sources are not listed in Acknowledgments. Verify these at submission stage.

4. **Underestimating author-date citation precision.** APA 6th edition author-date is standard in economics, but some authors mix it with footnote citations or numbered references. PER expects consistent, clean author-date throughout (no footnote citations of references; footnotes for supplementary material only).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
