# Journal Writing Skill: The Forum for Social Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis / Routledge
- **Scope:** Publishes stimulating, original, and clearly-written academic research on socioeconomic problems, including alternative perspectives on indicators of socioeconomic performance (well-being, quality of life, institutional functioning, human development) and policy issues. Realistic outlook, pluralistic approach, emphasis on interdependent agents and institutional/evolutionary nature of the economy. Not interested in quantitative exercises devoid of theoretical perspective.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/rfse20 (full instructions not publicly accessible; submit via Taylor & Francis platform)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
Follows the empirical paper structure standard to ECON field (Introduction, Background/Institutional Setting, Data, Methods, Results, Conclusion). No journal-specific deviations documented.

### Length
**ENFORCED:** Manuscripts should normally be 7,000–9,000 words inclusive of all notes and references. This is a tighter constraint than the FIELD_SKILL baseline, which does not specify a hard word limit.

### Abstract
Guidelines not publicly accessible. Standard practice for Taylor & Francis journals in economics is a structured abstract (typically 150–300 words) with labeled sections, but specific format for this journal not confirmed in accessible sources.

### Citations
**ENFORCED:** Author-year (name-year) in-text citations with corresponding author-year bibliography. Format: (Author, Year) for single citations; (Author1, Year; Author2, Year) for multiple citations. Follows Harvard referencing style conventions. Differs from FIELD_SKILL, which specifies author-year but does not mandate Harvard specifically.

### Headings
Not explicitly documented in accessible sources. FIELD_SKILL norm (Roman or Arabic numerals; first-level headings in bold with initial capitals for proper nouns) likely applies by default for Taylor & Francis journals.

### Tables and Figures
Not explicitly documented. FIELD_SKILL norm likely applies.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Journal is "not interested in quantitative exercises that are devoid of a theoretical perspective" — this is a stated editorial preference that implies theoretical grounding is mandatory, stronger than FIELD_SKILL's general emphasis on identification and robustness.

### Formatting
Manuscripts should be typed with double spacing throughout and a 4 cm left-hand margin (per Taylor & Francis standard for this journal tier).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Word Limit
**ENFORCED (Delta):** The journal enforces a 7,000–9,000-word limit inclusive of all notes and references. This is a RELAXATION relative to typical top-tier economics journals (ABS 4*/4), which have fewer or softer word constraints. This is an intentional difference: a lower-tier journal may compress article length. Authors accustomed to longer ABS 4 papers must tighten scope and expression.

### Theoretical Grounding Requirement
**GUIDELINE ONLY (Delta):** Editorial statement ("not interested in quantitative exercises devoid of theoretical perspective") emphasizes that purely empirical, atheoretical work will not be considered. This is consistent with FIELD_SKILL's emphasis on clear identification and argumentation but is stated more explicitly here as a gate-keeping criterion. Submit only if your quantitative work is explicitly tied to a theoretical argument or framework.

### Citation Style Formality
**PRACTICE ONLY (possible delta):** The explicit mention of Harvard referencing style suggests the journal may prefer humanities-inflected author-year (with commas and semicolons in specific patterns) rather than the bare author-year parenthetical style of some ABS 4 economics journals. Verify exact formatting in the full style guide if accessible; treat as advisory pending confirmation.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding word limit:** The 7,000–9,000-word constraint is tighter than ABS 4 journals. Rambling introductions, overly detailed methods, or exhaustive robustness tables will trigger desk rejection. Prioritize clarity and concision; cut ruthlessly.

2. **Atheoretical empiricism:** The editorial statement on quantitative exercises without theory is a hard gate. Do not submit purely descriptive or pattern-finding work without an explicit theoretical claim or framework—even if it is well-identified and robust.

3. **Over-reliance on Harvard formatting:** While Harvard style is mentioned, the full stylistic requirements (punctuation, capitalization, abbreviation of place names) are not accessible in public guidelines. Request the full style guide from the editorial office before final submission to avoid formatting revisions in proof stage.

4. **Misalignment with social economics scope:** The journal's scope emphasizes well-being, inequality, institutional and evolutionary approaches, and pluralistic (non-neoclassical) perspectives. Standard neoclassical optimization exercises without explicit engagement with social/institutional factors may face higher rejection risk. Ensure framing and motivation align with the journal's stated commitments.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
