# Journal Writing Skill: Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 4
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** SAGE (official journal of the Society for Personality and Social Psychology, SPSP)
- **Scope:** International outlet for rapid dissemination of original empirical contributions in all areas of personality and social psychology; occasionally publishes special topic issues, selected symposia, invited addresses.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/psp
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (titles/abstracts/coverage, not full text — paywalled) — "Overconfidently Conspiratorial..." (Pennycook, Binnendyk, Rand, 2025, DOI 10.1177/01461672251338358); "Perceiving to Provide..." ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Article types limited to two: Empirical Research Papers and Replication Studies (narrower than the general PSYCH field, which also covers integrative reviews/theoretical syntheses at other venues). Replication submissions require a prior email to the Editor justifying rationale/interest value and go through a two-stage evaluation (proposal, then final product). Title Page (with author info, Acknowledgments, Statements and Declarations, ethics/consent/COI/funding/data-availability sub-headings) is submitted separately from the anonymized manuscript body and is not sent to reviewers.

### Length
Hard cap: manuscripts must not exceed 10,000 words, including abstract, references, and notes, but excluding title page, tables, and figures. Word count must appear on the title page. Exceptions are rare and must be justified by study complexity/number.

### Abstract
Unstructured, hard limit of 150 words (not a soft target) — "submissions that do not meet this requirement will not be considered." Clinical trial registry name/URL/number must appear at the end of the abstract if applicable. Minimum 4-5 specific keywords required after the abstract.

### Citations
APA reference style (per FIELD_SKILL default), but with an explicit added requirement: authors must cite and reference all data sets, software/programs, analytic tools/apps, and code packages (with version numbers) not original to the manuscript, including a DOI where possible. Self-citations must not be anonymized but must be phrased in third person during review (e.g., "Jones and Xi (2020) have shown," not "we have previously shown"). Preprint citations should be updated to the peer-reviewed version once available; citing preprints otherwise is discouraged.

### Headings
No mandated template, but heading levels and section boundaries must be unambiguous (Word format preferred, no fixed template required). Title Page must contain a literal "Statements and Declarations" heading with mandatory sub-headings (Ethical considerations, Consent to participate, Consent for publication, Declaration of conflicting interest, Funding statement, Data availability), each populated or marked "Not applicable."

### Tables and Figures
Figures numbered consecutively in order of first appearance; 300dpi minimum resolution; color figures free online (print color billed separately). No table/figure rule differs materially from FIELD_SKILL practice of placing near first reference.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No AI chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT) may be listed as authors. Third-party-submitted manuscripts (e.g., by a writing/editing service acting as submitter rather than an author) may be refused by the Editor. Manuscripts not conforming to formatting/open-science requirements are returned or desk-rejected before peer review — compliance is verified at submission, not merely encouraged. Do not report "observed"/post hoc power computed from the same test's effect size estimate.

**Open science (TOP Level II, adopted 2021) — mandatory, itemized, and unusually granular for the field:**
- Authors must post materials (verbatim wording of all IV/DV instructions, manipulations, measures), analysis code, any non-public data, and a codebook to a trusted repository, with the link placed at the **start of the first Method section** (not just "somewhere in the manuscript").
- Authors must explicitly state, per study, whether each of: study design, planned sample size/stopping rule, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and planned analyses was preregistered, with a time-stamped link if yes.
- Deviations from preregistration must be flagged and explained in the main text; exploratory (non-preregistered) analyses must be clearly marked as such.
- Exceptions to any of the above must be flagged at submission and justified to the Editor in the cover letter, noted in the Author Note.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Statistical Reporting
Tightens FIELD_SKILL's general "exact statistics + effect sizes + CIs" norm into itemized, mandatory checklist items verified at submission: exact p-values to 3 decimal places; at least one power analysis per study (with full reproducibility detail — program, specific test, all input values); mandatory reporting of exclusions (count, reasons, distribution across conditions) and disclosure of all measured variables/conditions, with a required disclosure sentence in the manuscript text (e.g., "We report all manipulations, measures, and exclusions"). This is a legitimate ABS-4 tightening of the FIELD_SKILL baseline, confirmed as enforced (not merely encouraged) by the spot-checked articles' consistent multi-study/large-N/preregistered designs (e.g., Pennycook et al.'s 8 studies, N = 4,181; the meritocracy study's N = 4,171 across 3 surveys + 1 experiment).

### Structural Deviations
Article types are narrower than the general PSYCH (GENERAL) field baseline: PSPB accepts only Empirical Research Papers and Replication Studies (no stand-alone theoretical-review or commentary format, unlike some other venues covered in FIELD_SKILL). Replication submissions carry a distinct two-stage evaluation process not described in FIELD_SKILL.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Journal explicitly "encourages" (GUIDELINE ONLY, not enforced) inclusive-methods and complete/inclusive demographic reporting practices via its ACEMAP recommendations — e.g., reporting demographic variables beyond age/gender when relevant to local context or research question, considering qualitative/person-centered/community-based methods, and discussing constraints on generality tied to sample composition. These appear as "we encourage" framings in the guidelines, distinct from the hard-required items above.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting an abstract over 150 words or a structured abstract — PSPB states this will cause the submission not to be considered, not merely flagged for revision.
- Placing the open-data/materials/code repository link anywhere other than the start of the first Method section, or omitting the itemized preregistration statement per study.
- Writing self-citations in first person ("we previously showed...") in the anonymized manuscript body, which breaks double-anonymization.
- Exceeding 10,000 words (including abstract and references) without pre-clearing an exception through the submission process.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
