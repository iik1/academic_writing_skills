# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Behavioral Finance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis (Routledge)
- **Scope:** Interdisciplinary research on the psychological and behavioral dimensions of financial markets — investor sentiment, biases (herding, overconfidence, disposition effect), decision-making under uncertainty, retail trading behavior, and prospect-theory applications to asset pricing and fund flows.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://files.taylorandfrancis.com/hbhfauth.pdf (403 to automated fetch; content recovered via a mirrored instructions page, https://dremanbehavioralfinance.org/journals/instructions.html, which appears to reproduce the journal's long-standing author instructions) and https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/hbhf20
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 (title/abstract/front-matter level; full text was paywalled for all). Sample: "Retail Trading Around Earnings Announcements: Evidence from Robinhood Traders" (Liang, Najand, Selover & Sun, 2024, Vol 27:1); "Business Cycle Variations in Manager and Investor Sentiment Indices" (Al-Zoubi, 2026, Vol 27:1); "The Influence of Social Media Emotions: Evidence from the Kodak Manic Episode" (Cioroianu et al., 2026, Vol 27:1); "Salience, Fundamentals, and Mispricing" (Li, Li, Li & Chen, 2025, Vol 26:4); "Social Media, Investment Knowledge, and Meme Stock Trading" (Pandey & Guillemette, 2025, Vol 26:4); "The Importance of Risk Preference Parameters in Prospect Theory: Evidence from Mutual Fund Flows" (2024, Vol 26:4); "Synchronicity and Sentiment: Decoding Earnings Quality and Market Returns in EU under Economic Policy Uncertainty" (2024, Vol 26:4); "Buy Now, Pay Later Loans, Social Norms, and Consumer Indebtedness" (2024, Vol 26:4); "Behavioral Biases of Financial Planners: The Case of Retirement Funding Recommendations" (2024, Vol 26:3); "Limited or Biased: Modeling Subrational Human Investors in Financial Markets" (2024); "Asymmetric Information, Liquidity Needs, and Migration to ETFs: An Experimental Investigation" (2024); "How is Trading Behavior in Commodity Futures Affected by the 52-Week High and Low?" ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Main text followed by separate pages, in order, for: references, endnotes, tables, and figures (i.e., tables/figures are NOT embedded in-text at submission — they go at the end, one exhibit per page). No other mandated section list is specified beyond the standard research-article shape.

### Length
Maximum ~30 manuscript pages, double-spaced, margins of at least one inch on all sides. Manuscript pages numbered consecutively.

### Abstract
Brief summary required, plus exactly five keywords for searchability. No structured/labeled-subsection format specified (unstructured, single block).

### Citations
**Numbered/bracket in-text citation style, not author-year parenthetical**: e.g., "Smith [1990] suggests that...". This is a direct conflict with FIELD_SKILL's author-year parenthetical norm — HARD_CONSTRAINT overrides. Reference list follows APA formatting (guidance cites APA 4th/5th ed. across sources — treat as "APA-style," alphabetical by author), listed alphabetically on a separate page. Periods (not commas) separate authors' names from titles in reference entries. Page numbers required for direct quotations.

### Headings
No distinctive heading convention specified beyond standard numbered/titled sections.

### Tables and Figures
All tables and figures must be numbered with titles; one exhibit per page. Equations centered on their own line, numbered consecutively in parentheses. Greek letters/symbols should be identified in the margin (non-black annotation) for typesetting clarity.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Equations and formulas are explicitly discouraged in the main text — push heavy notation to appendixes. Minimize endnotes/footnotes.

### Other
Explicit tone instruction (unusual for a finance journal, see Voice delta below): "Write to express, not to impress, and write the way you talk. Keep it simple; brevity and conciseness are valued, but be sure to create interest. Colorful language and examples are encouraged." Submission via ScholarOne Manuscripts, 30 MB combined file limit.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Voice and Register
GUIDELINE ONLY / RELAXATION. The guidelines explicitly invite plainer, more accessible prose ("write the way you talk," "colorful language and examples are encouraged") than the FIELD_SKILL norm of high formality and dense technical register. This is a stated relaxation appropriate to the journal's ABS 2 tier and interdisciplinary (finance + psychology) readership. Titles and abstracts in the spot-check sample (e.g., "Meme Stock Trading," "Kodak Manic Episode") lean toward accessible, narrative framing more than typical ABS 4/4* finance titles, consistent with this stated norm — but abstract text itself still reads as standard academic register, so treat this as a modest, not dramatic, shift.

### Citation Density
No delta observed at abstract level (not assessable without full text). Follows FIELD_SKILL norm as far as can be judged.

### Structural Deviations
PRACTICE-level observation: several sampled titles/abstracts foreground a specific behavioral mechanism or data source in the title itself (e.g., "Evidence from Robinhood Traders," "Evidence from the Kodak Manic Episode," "Evidence from Mutual Fund Flows") more consistently than is typical of top-tier finance journal titles. This is a low-stakes framing pattern, not a structural rule — noted as PRACTICE ONLY, not enforced.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED: the mandated separation of tables/figures/references onto trailing pages (rather than in-text integration) is a manuscript-formatting delta from typical top finance journal practice, though it is standard practice for many non-4*/4 journals and largely a submission-formatting artifact rather than a stylistic one.

## Common Pitfalls
- Using author-year parenthetical citations (the FIELD_SKILL default) instead of the journal's required numbered/bracket style, e.g., "Smith [1990]."
- Embedding tables/figures in-text at submission instead of placing them on separate trailing pages as required.
- Writing in the dense, jargon-heavy register typical of ABS 4*/4 finance journals rather than the more accessible tone the guidelines explicitly request.
- Overloading the main text with equations instead of relegating heavy notation to an appendix.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
