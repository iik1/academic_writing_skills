# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Management Accounting Research

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** American Accounting Association
- **Scope:** Advances the theory and practice of management accounting through publication of high-quality applied and theoretical research using any well-executed research method. Accepts quantitative archival, experimental, qualitative, and analytical papers.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://aaahq.org/Research/Journals/Journal-of-Management-Accounting-Research/Guide-for-Authors
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 3 article titles/metadata identified (full-text access limited by paywall); see Sources

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscripts must be submitted in this order:
1. AI use disclosure statement (required)
2. Title page (separate, with conflict of interest disclosures for each author; for anonymous review)
3. Abstract (labeled, separate from text)
4. Text beginning with I. INTRODUCTION
5. References
6. Numbered figures (captions only)
7. Numbered tables
8. Exhibits
9. Appendices

### Length
- **General limit:** 55 pages maximum including references, figures, tables, and appendices
- This applies to initial submissions and revisions

### Abstract
- Maximum 150 words
- Should summarize topic, methods, and findings in accessible language
- Appears as a separate labeled section before the text

### Citations
- **Format:** Author-date throughout (e.g., "Berry 2003", "Scholes et al. 2008")
- Multiple authors with six or more: use "et al." format (e.g., "Dikolli et al. 2013")
- Consistent citation density by section follows field norms

### Headings
**Four-level hierarchy (strictly enforced):**
- **Level 1:** Centered, bold, ALL CAPITALS, roman numerals (I, II, III, IV, etc.)
  - Example: "I. INTRODUCTION"
- **Level 2:** Flush left, bold, mixed case
- **Level 3:** Flush left, bold, italic, mixed case
- **Level 4:** Paragraph indent, bold, lowercase

Deviation from this hierarchy is not acceptable.

### Tables and Figures
- Tables must use Microsoft Word or Excel table editors (not manually formatted)
- Include: Arabic numerals, complete titles, defined variables in notes, source attribution where applicable
- Figures should be interpretable in greyscale
- Submit figure graphics as separate files in approved formats: EPS, TIFF, PDF, JPEG, or PNG

### Explicit Prohibitions and Requirements
- **Plagiarism:** Copying or paraphrasing without appropriate attribution (including copying from one's own previous work) is prohibited
- **AI Disclosure:** Generative AI and AI-assisted tool use must be disclosed; failure to disclose is misconduct
- **Data Integrity:** Falsification and fabrication of research data are prohibited
- **Authorship:** Misrepresenting authorship or failing to include qualifying contributors is prohibited
- **Confidentiality:** Reviewers must not upload manuscripts to AI tools without explicit permission
- **Human Subjects:** Manuscripts reporting surveys, interviews, qualitative studies, or experiments utilizing human subjects must verify approval or exemption
- **Reporting Standards:** Ensure reporting of descriptive statistics and of models and tests of hypotheses is complete, including standard deviations, cell sizes, degrees of freedom, and complete ANOVA tables
- **Mathematical Notation:** Employed only where its rigor and precision are necessary; equations numbered in parentheses

### Language
- Gender-neutral language is encouraged
- Authors should strive to be succinct and clear

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Word and Page Limits
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).** The field baseline does not specify journal-specific limits. JMAR enforces a strict 55-page maximum including all elements. This is a significant constraint for papers that approach typical ABS 4/4* journal lengths.

### Heading Format and Hierarchy
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines and standard across AAA journals).** FIELD_SKILL notes that "practice varies across journals" and mentions both Roman numeral (e.g., "I. INTRODUCTION") and Arabic numeral (e.g., "1. Introduction") conventions. JMAR mandates Roman numerals with strict formatting (centered, all capitals for Level 1; specific placement and styling for Levels 2–4). This is non-negotiable and is standard across AAA journals.

### AI Disclosure
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).** This is a newer requirement not covered in FIELD_SKILL. All manuscripts must include an AI use disclosure statement at the beginning of the submission. Non-disclosure is treated as research misconduct.

### Reporting Completeness for Empirical Work
**GUIDELINE ONLY (stated but emphasis may exceed field norms).** The Editorial Policy explicitly requires complete reporting of descriptive statistics, ANOVA tables, standard deviations, cell sizes, and degrees of freedom. While FIELD_SKILL notes that "robustness is expected" and "descriptive statistics are near-universal," JMAR emphasizes this more rigidly and explicitly as a condition of acceptance.

### Table Formatting Tool
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).** Tables must use Microsoft Word or Excel table editors. This is more prescriptive than FIELD_SKILL's general norm and rules out other formatting approaches.

### Human Subjects Documentation
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines).** The policy requires explicit verification of IRB approval or exemption status for any studies involving human subjects (surveys, interviews, experiments, qualitative work). This is in line with field norms but stated more explicitly in JMAR's editorial policy than in FIELD_SKILL.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- **Voice and Register:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (active voice, first-person plural "we," high formality, confidence calibration by identification strength)
- **Sentence and Paragraph Style:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium to long sentences, dense paragraphs, high technical density)
- **Argumentation Pattern:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (CARS-model introduction structure, explicit gap framing, explicit contribution positioning)
- **Methods Conventions:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (past tense in methods, high procedural detail, identification strategy emphasis, expected robustness checks)
- **Results and Reporting Norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (tables as primary vehicle, prose narration, statistical significance reporting, parallel structure with hypotheses, null results not suppressed)
- **Discussion and Conclusion Norms:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (merged discussion/conclusion, limitations paragraph expected, confident but measured tone)
- **Citation Density by Section:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (high in introduction and theory; medium in methods and conclusion; low to medium in results)
- **Paraphrase vs. Quotation:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (paraphrase is universal; direct quotation absent except in qualitative/interpretive work)
- **Hedging and Epistemic Caution:** Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (medium overall, calibrated to identification strength, well-identified results stated directly, mechanistic claims softened)

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding 55-page limit:** Manuscripts over 55 pages (including all elements) will be desk-rejected or subject to revision demands. Plan accordingly; this is significantly tighter than some ABS 4/4* journals.

2. **Incorrect heading hierarchy or formatting:** Using Arabic numerals, centering level 2 headings, or failing to italicize level 3 headings triggers automatic desk rejection for format non-compliance. Follow the four-level hierarchy exactly.

3. **Omitting AI disclosure:** Failure to include an AI use disclosure statement (even if the answer is "no AI used") results in rejection. Include it explicitly at the start of the manuscript.

4. **Incomplete descriptive statistics or ANOVA tables:** The Editorial Policy explicitly requires complete reporting (all standard deviations, cell sizes, degrees of freedom, full ANOVA tables). Editors check this closely. Incomplete statistics are a common rejection trigger at revision.

5. **Failing to document human subjects approval:** Any study involving human subjects (including interviews, survey experiments, or qualitative fieldwork) must include explicit IRB approval or exemption statement. Absence of this statement results in rejection.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
