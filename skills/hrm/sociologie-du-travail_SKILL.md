# Journal Writing Skill: Sociologie du Travail

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Elsevier (via OpenEdition open-access platform)
- **Scope:** Quarterly journal (founded 1959) publishing empirical research, theoretical studies, and thematic special issues on work and employment. Adopts a broad conception of work (including domestic, volunteer, artistic, informal labor) and positions itself as both specialist and generalist sociology outlet.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sociologiedutravail.org/Proposer-un-article
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12 articles from Vol. 66-68 (2024-2026), representing multiple issue themes

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Language
Manuscripts must be submitted in **French**. All title, abstract, and keywords must be provided in both French and English.

### Length
**70,000 characters ±10%**, including abstracts, spaces, and all references. (Critical reviews: 40,000-70,000 characters; debate contributions: 30,000 characters; book reviews: 9,000 characters or 20,000-35,000 for comparative reviews.)

### Structure and Numbering
Use **international numbering** for headings (1.; 1.1.; 1.1.1.). Maximum three levels; two levels maximum is preferred. Subheadings must not exceed 80 characters. Do not use italics, quotation marks, or acronyms in the title.

### Title, Abstract, and Keywords
- **Title:** Maximum 110 characters (with spaces)
- **Abstract:** 1,000-1,500 characters; must be self-contained and comprehensible to readers outside the field
- **Keywords:** Six to eight indexing terms; broader in scope than the title; no acronyms
- All three must be provided in both French and English translations.

### Anonymization
Manuscripts undergo anonymous peer review (except debate contributions and book reviews). Remove all identifying information: author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and file properties. When self-citing, write "Auteurice" with the year instead of your name, then delete those references from the bibliography.

### Quotation and Citation Format

**French quotation marks (guillemets):**
- Use French guillemets « » with non-breaking spaces on both sides: « quoted text »
- Nested quotations use English quotation marks without spaces: « text with "nested quote" inside »
- Long quotations (over three lines) are indented but retain guillemets
- Place editorial interventions in square brackets [not parentheses]; indicate omissions as [...]
- Bibliography references go in parentheses immediately after quotations

**Foreign-language citations:** Must be translated into French in the text body with original language indicated if needed.

### Personal Names
Write out first names completely on first mention. Subsequent mentions may use initials if the person is referenced frequently. When citing someone, always include their first name: "François Dubet (2002) demonstrated..."

### Italics and Emphasis
Italics are reserved exclusively for **foreign terms and emphasis**. Avoid overuse.

### Footnotes
- Number continuously throughout the article
- Keep footnotes under 10% of total article length
- Place note markers at the end of phrases or sentences, never splitting expressions
- Avoid placing multiple note markers in a single sentence

### Bibliography
**Critical rule:** All citations in text must appear in the bibliography; all bibliography entries must be cited in text.

**Format guidelines:**
- Arrange alphabetically by author surname; for single authors list publications chronologically
- List **all authors** (do not use "et al." in the bibliography; "et al." is permitted only in text citations for works by 3+ authors)
- Include volume numbers, issue numbers, and page ranges for all journal articles
- Use the journal's provided CSL (Citation Style Language) file; do not use bibliography software field codes
- For journal articles: include complete author list, year, article title in single quotes, journal name in italics, volume/issue/pages

### Figures and Tables
- Number all using Arabic numerals with descriptive titles
- Reference at least once in the text
- Provide figures as JPEG files at 600 dpi with precise sources cited
- Do not reproduce previously published figures or tables without written permission

### Inclusive Language
The journal encourages thoughtful use of inclusive writing techniques (e.g., gender-neutral language, accessible terminology). If using such conventions, include an opening note explaining your approach so editors can verify consistency.

### Evaluation Timeline
Three editorial committee members review submissions (two for reviews). Decisions communicated within one month of committee meetings, which occur approximately bimonthly.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Language and Register
ENFORCED. The journal is **French-language only**, which represents a fundamental departure from FIELD_SKILL (which assumes English-language norms). Submission in French is mandatory. This implies that voice and register conventions differ from English-language HRM journals: French academic sociology tends toward more narrative and interpretive prose, with greater use of theoretical conceptualization and historical framing than is typical in English-language management and organizational research.

### Scope and Conceptualization
ENFORCED. The journal explicitly adopts a "generalist" sociology perspective within a specialized field, intentionally addressing both specialist and non-specialist readers. This differs from FIELD_SKILL's default assumption (which reflects ABS 4*/4 journals with primarily specialized managerial/psychological framing). Sociologie du Travail prioritizes sociological and institutional perspectives over psychological/managerial ones, with particular emphasis on labor process analysis, unions, and policy implications.

### Character-Based Length Limits
ENFORCED. The journal imposes strict **70,000-character limits ± 10%** rather than word or page counts. This is more restrictive and precise than typical FIELD_SKILL practice, which does not specify length constraints. The character-based format (including spaces) is harder to manipulate than word counts and reflects European academic publishing conventions.

### Anonymization Protocol
ENFORCED. The "Auteurice" convention for self-citations is journal-specific and differs from FIELD_SKILL norms. Authors must flag self-citations with a placeholder and remove the full reference from the bibliography during review, reintroducing only after acceptance. This is more rigorous anonymization than standard practice.

### Bibliography Constraints
ENFORCED. The explicit prohibition on "et al." in bibliographies (requiring all authors to be listed) is stricter than FIELD_SKILL practice. The journal provides a CSL file and forbids use of bibliography software field codes, indicating tight control over reference formatting.

### Citation Format
ENFORCED. Use of French guillemets « » with specific spacing rules and nested quotation mark conventions is language-specific and journal-mandated. Editorial interventions must use square brackets rather than parentheses, and omissions use [...]—these are precise formatting rules not addressed in FIELD_SKILL.

### Footnote Length Cap
ENFORCED. Footnotes must not exceed 10% of total article length. This is a specific, measurable constraint not present in FIELD_SKILL.

### Title Requirements
ENFORCED. 110-character limit with explicit prohibitions on italics, quotation marks, and acronyms is more restrictive than FIELD_SKILL.

### Inclusive Language Encouragement
PRACTICE ONLY / GUIDELINE. The journal explicitly encourages inclusive writing with an author-supplied note explaining conventions. This is more progressive and explicit than FIELD_SKILL, which does not address inclusive language. Observed in the journal's policy statement but not yet systematically in published articles (likely emerging standard).

## Common Pitfalls for Sociologie du Travail Submissions

1. **Failing to anonymize properly.** Remove author names, institutional affiliations, and funding acknowledgments; replace self-citations with "Auteurice (year)" and remove those references from the bibliography during review. Editors will reinstate after acceptance. Files submitted with metadata (author info in document properties) will be rejected.

2. **Exceeding character limit or misunderstanding the format.** The 70,000-character limit includes spaces and is strictly enforced (±10%). Word-count conversion tools often underestimate; use a French word processor's character counter. Brief vs. critical review vs. debate articles have different limits—verify your submission type.

3. **Using English citation conventions in a French-language paper.** Authors often default to English quotation marks or citation styles. Use « guillemets » consistently, place bibliography references in parentheses after quotations, and avoid "et al." in the bibliography. Provide a CSL file rather than formatting references manually.

4. **Misplacing or over-using footnotes.** Footnotes must total under 10% of the article length and should contain supplementary points, not substantive arguments. Place note markers at phrase/sentence ends, not mid-word. Excessive footnoting weakens the main argument and suggests poor organization.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
