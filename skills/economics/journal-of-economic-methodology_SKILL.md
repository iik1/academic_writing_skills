# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Economic Methodology

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis (Routledge Journals)
- **Scope:** Economic methodology and philosophy of economics. The journal distinguishes between methodology (the relationship between economics and broad questions about scientific knowledge) and methods (particular techniques in specific fields). Covers historical, sociological, and epistemological perspectives on economic inquiry. Includes peer-reviewed research articles, commentaries, symposia, and book reviews.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/rjec20 (full guidelines paywalled; standard Taylor & Francis format applies)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (2024–2026, mixed access levels via PMC, LSE Research Online, Mercatus Center, IDEAS/RePEc, and abstracts)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

**Note:** The Journal of Economic Methodology's detailed submission guidelines are not publicly accessible in free form. The journal uses standard Taylor & Francis submission requirements. The following are inferred from article structure and editorial scope rather than explicit guidelines.

### Structure
Most articles follow: Introduction (problem and research question) → Critical engagement with existing literature/framework → Main analytical or philosophical argument → Conclusion with implications. Shorter than FIELD_SKILL baseline: typical range 7–10 pages (c. 6,000–8,000 words), not the 25–40 page empirical papers of ABS 4*/4 journals.

### Length
Articles are notably shorter than the field baseline. Commentaries and responses often 5–8 pages. Research articles typically 8–12 pages. No explicit word limit published, but practice suggests implicit 8,000–10,000 word target for main contributions.

### Abstract
Structured abstracts (common): problem statement, main argument, conclusions. Word limit appears to be 150–200 words (inferred from published abstracts). Format favors problem-solution-implication structure rather than FIELD_SKILL's results-preview model.

### Citations
Author-date parenthetical format (APA-adjacent). Numbered endnotes used for supplementary commentary and qualifications, not primary citations. Philosophy and methodology literature heavily represented. References appear both inline and in alphabetical bibliography. Density varies by article type: critical responses show higher citation density in exposition sections than in arguments.

### Headings
Arabic numerals standard (matching FIELD_SKILL). Section headings are numbered but descriptive, e.g., "1. Introduction," "2. The framework of Gilboa et al.," "3. Critical analysis." Subsections common (e.g., "2.1", "2.2").

### Tables and Figures
Fewer than in FIELD_SKILL baseline. When used, tables present conceptual distinctions or comparisons (not data summaries). Figures are minimal. No requirement for summary statistics tables.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None identified from available sources. Standard academic integrity, anonymization during review, and original work expectations apply.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
**PRACTICE ONLY — moderate deviation:** Articles retain "we" as standard (even for single-author pieces), consistent with FIELD_SKILL. However, hedging intensity is notably **higher than field baseline**. Phrases like "it may seem," "we do not think," "one cannot but wonder," and "appears to" are common throughout the body, not reserved for external validity caveats. This reflects the journal's focus on philosophical argument and critique rather than causal claims—where assumptions cannot be definitively resolved, verbal qualification marks the author's epistemic stance. The register remains formal, but tone is more conversational and less economically technical than FIELD_SKILL (fewer econometric terms, more philosophy-of-science vocabulary).

### Citation Density
**FIELD DEFAULT in general structure, PRACTICE ONLY in application:** Like FIELD_SKILL, introduction citation density is high. But the *function* of citations differs: rather than establishing a gap (FIELD_SKILL), citations here frame *philosophical and methodological controversy*. Authors quote and paraphrase prior methodological claims extensively. Textual close reading of other authors' work is central to the contribution. Conclusion citation density is higher than FIELD_SKILL baseline, because conclusions often include new methodological recommendations grounded in cited alternatives.

### Argument Structure
**PRACTICE ONLY — philosophical critique model:** Introduction closely follows FIELD_SKILL's structure (problem, research question, roadmap). However, the *problem* is typically posed as a philosophical or methodological puzzle or an instance of insufficient self-criticism in the discipline, not an empirical gap. Main sections often follow a "exposition-then-critique" pattern: the author summarizes a target paper's framework carefully, then identifies logical inconsistencies, undeclared assumptions, or conceptual confusions. This argumentative arc (fair exposition + targeted critique) is more characteristic of philosophy than empirical economics.

### Results and Interpretation
**MAJOR DELTA — qualitative argument replaces quantitative reporting:** FIELD_SKILL expects quantitative results with economic significance alongside statistical significance. This journal has no regression tables, coefficient estimates, or statistical testing. Instead, "results" are logical conclusions, conceptual clarifications, or philosophical positions. Interpretation is immediate: a logical point *is* a result. Magnitude and significance are conveyed through argumentative force, not numerical precision.

### Structural Deviations
**PRACTICE ONLY — shorter, commentary-heavy publication model:** This ABS 2 journal publishes many short commentaries (5–8 pages) responding directly to recent papers. The FIELD_SKILL baseline assumes all papers are primary research contributions; here, a significant fraction are *secondary analyses* (critical responses, methodological appraisals). When a commentary dominates an issue, article structure may be more tightly focused: introduction identifies the target and the critique → body develops the critique → brief conclusion. No separate literature review, no methods section, no robustness checks. Full research articles follow closer to FIELD_SKILL structure but remain shorter and less empirically detailed.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
**Book review section:** The journal hosts a regular book review section and occasional book review symposia. These are substantial (8–12 pages) and follow standard essay structure (contextual framing, substantive engagement with arguments, evaluation, implications for the field). Not counted as primary research articles for this analysis.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Tense:** FIELD DEFAULT. Mixed tense (past for procedures, present for theory) as in FIELD_SKILL.
- **Person:** FIELD DEFAULT. First-person plural throughout.
- **Active voice preference:** FIELD DEFAULT. Active voice strongly preferred; passive reserved for data/procedure description.
- **Sentence and paragraph length:** FIELD DEFAULT. Medium to long sentences, dense paragraphs of 4–8 sentences typical.
- **Technical vocabulary:** FIELD DEFAULT. Discipline-specific terminology used freely (epistemology, incommensurability, reductionism, etc.). Readers assumed trained in philosophy of economics and economic methodology.
- **Null results:** FIELD DEFAULT. Not a primary form in this journal (which does not report empirical null results), but logical dead-ends are handled transparently.
- **Conclusion style:** FIELD DEFAULT. Conclusions synthesise findings, note limitations, and suggest future directions. Shorter than introduction.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Over-specification of quantitative claims.** Do not attempt to present weak empirical evidence as rigorous methodology. This journal values philosophical rigor over statistical power. If your paper involves quantitative elements (e.g., citation analysis, survey data), ensure the argument does not depend on precise coefficient values; focus instead on qualitative patterns and their conceptual implications.

2. **Ignoring the target paper's own framing.** Commentaries and critiques are most effective when they fairly represent the view being examined before diverging. Strawmanning or misquoting weakens arguments in this philosophical audience. Invest in accurate exposition before launching criticism.

3. **Treating methodology as a side issue.** This journal publishes work *about* economics methodology, not work in fields that happen to use economic methods. The epistemological or methodological component must be central, not a preface to a standard empirical study. If your paper's primary contribution is empirical or theoretical (not methodological), target a different journal.

4. **Assuming quantitative hedging.** FIELD_SKILL notes that confidence intervals carry uncertainty and verbal hedging is redundant on regression coefficients. This journal has no regression coefficients. Instead, epistemic caution is expressed through verbal qualification. Phrases like "suggests," "appears to support," and "is consistent with" mark areas of genuine philosophical uncertainty. Do not over-hedge weak arguments, but do not omit qualifications where logical alternatives remain open.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.

## Analytic Notes

This journal sits at ABS tier 2, lower than the ABS 4*/4 baseline used to construct FIELD_SKILL. The primary delta is **mission** rather than **standards**: FIELD_SKILL assumes empirical or theoretical contributions to economics proper. This journal publishes methodological and philosophical contributions *about* economics. That shift in focus reframes what counts as evidence (logical consistency vs. statistical significance), what constitutes a result (conceptual clarity vs. coefficient estimates), and how arguments are structured (philosophical critique vs. empirical demonstration). Voice and citation norms remain recognisably economic, but applied in a more interpretive, less quantitative idiom.
