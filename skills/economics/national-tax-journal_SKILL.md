# Journal Writing Skill: National Tax Journal

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** University of Chicago Press
- **Scope:** High-quality original research on governmental tax and expenditure policies, emphasizing economic, theoretical, and empirical analyses with policy implications. Published quarterly by the National Tax Association since 1948.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/ntj/instruct (full access requires institutional subscription)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 articles from Vol. 78-79 (2025-2026): "Tariffs as Fiscal Policy" (Clausing & Obstfeld), "Fiscal Consolidation: Lessons for the United States" (Gale et al.), "The Foreign Market Effect and Estimating Tax-Motivated Income Shifting" (Deng & Rane), "Assessment Regressivity and the Homestead Exemption" (McMillen & Singh), "Fiscal Stimulus or Debt Relief" (Brang et al.), "Importers, Exporters, and Job Creation" (Kamal), and articles from Vol. 79 No. 2 (2026).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Standard structure for empirical papers: Introduction, Background/Institutional Setting (where relevant), Data, Empirical Strategy/Identification, Results, Robustness and Extensions, Conclusion. Most issues include a Forum section featuring invited papers on single current policy topics. Regular peer-reviewed research articles are the standard format.

### Length
- Regular articles: maximum 50 double-spaced pages (including tables, figures, footnotes, references, and appendices intended to appear in print)
- Short Papers: maximum 6,000 words (exclusive of references), may include up to 5 figures or tables with streamlined one-round review process

### Abstract
- 100 words or fewer
- Must include keywords and JEL classification codes
- Unstructured format (not explicitly stated as structured requirement)

### Citations
- Author-date system (parenthetical citations with author's last name and year)
- University of Chicago Press citation conventions apply
- Footnotes carry supplementary material and qualifications, not primary citations (standard field practice)

### Headings
- No mandatory specific heading names are stated in public guidelines; standard empirical structure (Introduction, Methods, Results, Conclusion) observed in spot-checked articles
- Roman or Arabic numerals acceptable for section numbering (field standard)

### Tables and Figures
- Contributions to article page count within the 50-page limit
- Table and figure notes required (standard field practice)

### Data and Replication Requirements
- Empirical papers require submission of code, programs, and datasets to NTJ Dataverse upon acceptance
- Proprietary data exemptions available upon request
- Online-only appendix strongly encouraged for methodological detail sufficient for replication

### Copyright and Open Access
- Copyright assigned to National Tax Association upon acceptance
- Gold open access option available at $2,500 (request at submission or by acceptance)

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Manuscripts submitted simultaneously to other journals not permitted
- All listed authors must have approved final manuscript and agreed to submission

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates from econ field norms.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm for ECON. High density in Introduction, medium in Methods, low to medium in Results.

### Hedging and Epistemic Caution
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. Clear claims are preferred; hedging reserved for external validity, causal interpretation beyond identified parameters, data limitations, and mechanism uncertainty.

### Structural Deviations
The journal is ABS 2 (one tier below the FIELD_SKILL baseline of ABS 4*/4). In practice, spot-checked articles adhere closely to the introduction-results-conclusion flow and section norms outlined in FIELD_SKILL. No relaxation of structural expectations observed.

### Policy Emphasis
RELAXATION: Unlike the ABS 4*/4 baseline, NTJ explicitly emphasizes policy implications as a core criterion. All abstracts and introductions in recent issues lead with policy relevance. "Policy implications" language appears more prominently and directly than in top-tier economics journals, where policy context is present but not always foregrounded.

### Voice and Register
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm. First-person plural ("we find," "we estimate") is standard throughout recent articles. Active voice is preferred. Formality is high with no contractions.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Replication data neglect.** Submitting empirical work without clear plan for posting code and data to Dataverse upon acceptance. NTJ enforces this requirement strictly.
2. **Exceeding page limits.** Double-spaced pages include tables, figures, footnotes, and references—easy to exceed 50 pages without careful management.
3. **Missing JEL codes.** Omitting JEL classification in abstract submission; editors will request before desk review.
4. **Weak policy framing.** While FIELD_SKILL tolerates implicit policy relevance, NTJ reviewers expect explicit articulation of policy implications in introduction and conclusion.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
