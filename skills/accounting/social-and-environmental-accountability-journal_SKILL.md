# Journal Writing Skill: Social and Environmental Accountability Journal

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Taylor & Francis / Routledge
- **Scope:** International peer-reviewed journal committed to creating new academic literature in social, environmental and sustainable development accounting, accountability, reporting and auditing. Welcomes diverse theoretical, methodological and empirical approaches.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=reaj20
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 9 articles (2025-2026)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Manuscripts may follow different structures depending on article type:
- **Empirical papers:** Follow standard ACCOUNT field structure (Introduction, Hypothesis Development, Methods, Results, Discussion/Conclusion)
- **Conceptual papers, essays, commentaries, polemics, and reviews:** Structure varies; not bound to standard hypothesis-testing format. May use thematic or discursive organization.
- **Manuscripts reporting or proposing engagement:** Flexible structure accommodating action research or participatory approaches.

All article types submit through ScholarOne Manuscripts with double-anonymized peer review.

### Length
**RELAXATION: Articles are deliberately shorter than typical ACCOUNT field norms.** Key feature of SEAJ is that papers are shorter than word length anticipated in academic journals in the social sciences. Observed article page counts (approximate word equivalents):
- Commentary/short pieces: 8-16 pages (c. 2,400-4,800 words)
- Standard articles: 13-33 pages (c. 3,900-9,900 words)
- Editorials/special issue introductions: 16 pages (c. 4,800 words)

No explicit manuscript page or word limit found in public guidelines; authors should refer to ScholarOne submission system or contact editorial office for specific limits.

### Abstract
Manuscript must include an abstract. Specific structured vs. unstructured format not found in accessible guidelines; assume author-date citation baseline (see below). No explicit word limit located for abstracts.

### Keywords
Manuscript should include keywords for discoverability. Taylor & Francis standard guidance recommends 5-6 keywords where journal instructions do not specify a set number.

### Citations
**ENFORCED: Author-date (Harvard-style) citations throughout.** In-text citations use parenthetical format:
- Single/two authors: (Author Year)
- Three authors: (Author, Author, and Author Year)
- Four or more authors: (Author et al. Year)
References list entries follow author-date bibliography format with full publication details.

### Headings
No explicit journal-specific heading convention found. Assume FIELD_SKILL baseline (Roman or Arabic numerals with varying case depending on journal convention at submission; authors should follow instructions in ScholarOne or contact editors if unclear).

### Tables and Figures
Follows FIELD_SKILL baseline. Tables and figures carry panel labels and notes; place at end of manuscript or after references. Statistical choices (e.g., standard-error clustering) should be explicit in table notes.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No explicit language or content prohibitions identified beyond standard peer-review ethics (double-anonymized review, conflict of interest disclosure, IRB approval for human subjects research where applicable).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Article Structure Flexibility
**RELAXATION / PRACTICE ONLY:** While quantitative empirical papers follow the standard ACCOUNT structure, approximately 40-50% of SEAJ articles deviate significantly from the hypothesis-testing format. Conceptual papers, essaysay, commentaries, and qualitative work often organize around themes, arguments, or case-study narratives rather than hypothesis development. The journal explicitly welcomes "all contributions" including conceptual, essays, commentaries, polemics, and reviews. FIELD_SKILL assumes near-universal hypothesis-testing structure; SEAJ is deliberately more flexible. Authors writing non-empirical work should not force it into the canonical structure.

### Manuscript Length
**ENFORCED RELAXATION:** SEAJ markedly shorter than ACCOUNT field baseline. FIELD_SKILL does not specify word limits, but field norms at the ABS 4*/4 level typically yield 40-60 page manuscripts. SEAJ articles average 10-30 pages, with some as short as 8 pages. This is an explicit, stated feature: "papers are shorter than the word length typically anticipated in academic journals in the social sciences." Do not write to the field baseline length; compress accordingly.

### Disciplinary Scope
**PRACTICE ONLY (observed in article sample):** While FIELD_SKILL covers primarily quantitative, archival, and experimental accounting research, SEAJ's article portfolio reflects broader engagement with qualitative, interpretive, and activist accounting scholarship (e.g., articles on counter-accounting, indigenous perspectives, anti-corruption accountability, ocean accounting). The journal's scope encompasses social and environmental accounting traditions that are less represented in top-tier ACCOUNT journals. Voice, argumentation, and evidence presentation may reflect these traditions more visibly than the FIELD_SKILL baseline.

### Article Types
**ENFORCED:** SEAJ explicitly accepts multiple article types beyond standard "empirical/archival/experimental" categories. Accepted types include: conceptual papers, empirical papers, essays, manuscripts reporting or proposing engagement, commentaries, polemics, book/article reviews, thematic reviews. Each type can have distinct structure and argument style. FIELD_SKILL assumes empirical article structure throughout; SEAJ requires authors to align structure with the article type.

## Common Pitfalls
> Submission mistakes specific to THIS journal

1. **Forcing non-empirical work into hypothesis-testing structure.** If writing a conceptual paper, essay, or commentary, adopt a thematic or discursive structure rather than introducing hypotheses that will not be tested. SEAJ explicitly welcomes these formats; reviewers expect them to be organized accordingly.

2. **Exceeding typical article length.** SEAJ articles are deliberately shorter than field norms. Submitting a 50-page manuscript (standard for ABS 4 journals) will likely exceed editorial expectations for this journal. Target 8-30 pages (2,400-9,900 words) depending on article type.

3. **Overlooking article-type specification in submission.** SEAJ requires authors to declare whether the submission is a conceptual paper, empirical paper, essay, engagement paper, commentary, polemic, or review. Misclassifying the article type can delay review or lead to reviewer expectations misaligned with the submitted work.

4. **Citation errors in Harvard author-date format.** The journal uses author-date citations strictly. In-text citations must use (Author Year) or Author (Year) format with correct pluralization for multiple authors; reference list entries must include full publication details. Inconsistent or non-compliant citation formatting can trigger desk rejection or revision requests.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
