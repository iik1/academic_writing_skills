# Journal Writing Skill: Business Ethics Quarterly

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Cambridge University Press (sponsored by the Society for Business Ethics)
- **Scope:** Multidisciplinary journal on the ethics of business — internal organizational ethics, the role of business in social/political/cultural frameworks, and the ethical quality of markets. Publishes philosophy, social science, and professional-field scholarship, often crossing disciplinary lines.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-ethics-quarterly/information/instructions-for-authors-submission-guidelines ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-ethics-quarterly/information/instructions-contributors
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 17 (front-matter/abstracts across Vol. 36 Iss. 1, 2026, and FirstView, 2024-2026): "Can Work Be Meaningful Under Algorithmic Management?"; "The Politicization of Business Ethics"; "The Normative Core of Relational Stakeholder Strategies"; "The Role of Empathy in Leadership Ethics"; "Balancing, Shielding, Filtering"; "How and Where Do Shared Goals Exist?"; "Beyond the Is and Ought"; "In Defense of an 'Ethics Judgment Rule'"; "Rethinking Organizational Ethical Culture"; "Social Upgrading in Global Supply Chains"; "Moral Distress in Bioethics and Business Ethics"; "The Sustainable Corporation?"; "The Danger of Contextual Integrity"; "Practice, Reason, and the Good"; "A New Direction for Epistemic Injustice in Business"; "A Black Radical Critique of ESG"; "Multi-stakeholder Initiatives and Legitimacy: A Deliberative Systems Perspective"

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed section template is imposed. Philosophical/normative articles (the plurality of content) use argument-named section headings rather than generic Theory/Methods/Results/Discussion labels (e.g., "Deliberative Democracy and the Turn Towards Deliberative Systems"). Empirical and qualitative articles retain more conventional Method/Findings/Discussion structures. First page must carry only the abstract, up to six keywords, and citation-style declaration — no author-identifying information anywhere in the manuscript body. End-of-document order: Acknowledgements, References, Tables/Figures, Appendix.

### Length
12,000 words maximum for the body text plus footnotes (excludes abstract, tables, figures, references), and no more than 50 pages total including all materials. This is markedly shorter than FIELD_SKILL's typical 10,000–18,000+ word range for empirical/qualitative MGMT articles. Commentaries: ≤6,000 words, expedited review, must appear within one year of the original article's publication. Book reviews: 1,500–1,800 words.

### Abstract
Unstructured, ≤150 words (tighter than FIELD_SKILL's 150–250 word range). Up to six keywords, multi-word terms count as one keyword each.

### Citations
Author chooses ONE of four styles at submission: AMJ style guide, Chicago author-date, Chicago notes (humanities), or Chicago author-date (preferred for book reviews). This is a deviation from FIELD_SKILL's default APA-style assumption — Chicago notes/footnote citation is an option here that does not appear as a MGMT-field norm. All notes are footnotes, not endnotes, and the reference list heading is "References" regardless of style chosen.

### Headings
First-level: centered, all capitals (introduction should not be given its own labeled heading). Second-level: left-margin, italicized, headline case. Third-level: left-margin, regular type, headline case. Arabic numeral heading numbers permitted.

### Tables and Figures
No pasted-in statistical software output. Single-page display preferred, no cell borders. Figures must use sans-serif fonts (Arial, Calibri). No color in final print figures. Explanatory titles/notes required; tables should resemble previously published BEQ examples.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No prior publication or concurrent submission. No author-identifying information in the manuscript body. No color figures in final publication. No cell borders on tables. No posting of the manuscript online during review. No posting of published PDF offprints online. Oxford comma is mandatory; either British or American spelling is acceptable but must be applied consistently; uncommon foreign phrases are italicized; statistics reported to a maximum of two decimal places. Guidelines explicitly instruct authors to "avoid biased language."

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
A large share of published articles are pure normative/conceptual philosophy pieces (MacIntyrean, Schmittian, Aristotelian, Wittgensteinian frameworks) with no empirical or methods section at all — proportionally much higher than typical MGMT outlets in FIELD_SKILL, which treat Theoretical/Conceptual as one of four roughly co-equal templates. ENFORCED/PRACTICE — confirmed across the large majority of the sampled articles, and consistent with the guidelines' emphasis on "original theoretical contribution" as the acceptance bar even for empirical submissions.

### Citation Density
Philosophical articles engage a small set of named theorists/frameworks in sustained close reading (e.g., MacIntyre, Schmitt, Habermas, Wittgenstein, Heidegger, Aristotle) rather than the broad citation-dense literature sweeps typical of FIELD_SKILL's Theory/Introduction sections. PRACTICE ONLY — observed in the philosophy-heavy subset; empirical/qualitative BEQ articles (e.g., the Amazon supplier-relations case study, the video-based empathy study) follow FIELD_SKILL citation density norms.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Named-theorist framing appears directly in titles and section headings more often than in mainstream MGMT outlets (e.g., "A MacIntyrean Perspective," "Seen from a Schmittian Perspective"). PRACTICE ONLY — RELAXATION of the FIELD_SKILL norm that section headings name the substantive argument rather than a scholar; here scholar-named framing is itself the norm for a meaningful share of articles, consistent with BEQ's ABS-3 tolerance for a more humanities-inflected register than higher-tier MGMT journals.

## Dimensions With No Delta
- Voice and person: FIELD DEFAULT ("we"/occasional "I" for single-authored theoretical work; hedging conventions unchanged)
- Empirical methods reporting (regression, qualitative coding, case study procedure) where present: FIELD DEFAULT
- Discussion/Conclusion structure: FIELD DEFAULT
- Propositions vs. hypotheses convention: FIELD DEFAULT

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating BEQ as an empirical-management journal and defaulting to a Theory–Hypotheses–Methods–Results template; a purely conceptual/normative submission with argument-named sections is equally, and often more, typical here.
- Exceeding the 12,000-word body limit by assuming FIELD_SKILL's longer empirical-article norms apply.
- Submitting a >150-word abstract (FIELD_SKILL's upper bound of 250 words is too long for BEQ).
- Failing to declare a single consistent citation style (AMJ or one of the two Chicago variants) up front, or mixing footnote and author-date conventions.
- Including author-identifying details in the manuscript body, or pasting raw statistical software output into tables.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
