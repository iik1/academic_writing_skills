# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Economics, Finance and Administrative Science

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing (in association with Universidad ESAN, Peru)
- **Scope:** Publishes empirical and applied research across economics, finance, and administrative/management science, with a strong representation of Latin American, African, and emerging-market authors and settings. Broader topical range than a pure finance journal (tax policy, corporate governance, marketing education, pensions, behavioral finance, macroeconomics all appear).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jefas (guidelines page itself returned CAPTCHA/403 on direct fetch; general Emerald author-guidelines conventions used as proxy, confirmed against actual JEFAS abstracts below). Submission portal: https://revistas.esan.edu.pe/index.php/jefas
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (abstracts/front-matter via IDEAS/RePEc, full text paywalled): JEFAS-10-2018-0111 (Isidore & Christie, 2019); JEFAS-02-2017-0042 (Nel & le Roux, 2017); JEFAS-04-2019-0054 (Mroua & Trabelsi, 2020); JEFAS-05-2019-0069 (2020, external debt/growth); Rivera & Alarcón 2020 (marketing-management education quality); Celikay 2020 (tax burden, OECD); Abdullahi, Chua & Mohd 2020 (inflation targets, oil exporters); Samlal 2020 (corporate governance, Morocco); Night & Bananuka 2020 (e-tax adoption, Uganda); Ruiz Estrada & Koutronas 2020 ("pensionomics").

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Standard Emerald journal structure: title page, structured abstract, keywords, JEL classification codes, main text, references. No further JEFAS-specific structural requirements found beyond standard Emerald conventions.

### Length
Emerald's general author guidance for this journal class cites roughly 1,000–4,000 words as a nominal target, but published JEFAS articles (e.g., 34-page article spans) suggest this is not strictly enforced for empirical papers. Treat as GUIDELINE ONLY, not a hard cap — could not confirm an enforced limit.

### Abstract
**HARD, confirmed in every article checked**: Emerald's mandatory **structured abstract**, max 250 words, under labeled subheadings — typically Purpose, Design/methodology/approach, Findings, and Originality/value as mandatory components, with Research limitations/implications and Practical implications included when applicable. This is a direct override of FIELD_SKILL's single-paragraph unstructured abstract norm.

### Citations
No JEFAS-specific citation format found beyond standard Emerald house style (author-year); consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Headings
No JEFAS-specific heading convention found; standard Emerald numbered-section style presumed.

### Tables and Figures
No JEFAS-specific rules found beyond general Emerald formatting conventions.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Standard Emerald-wide requirements: institutional email and ORCID required at submission; conflict-of-interest and ethics/IRB declarations required where human subjects are involved; submissions must be original and not previously published. No content-specific prohibitions found.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
**ENFORCED.** Structured abstract with labeled subheadings (Purpose / Design-methodology-approach / Findings / Research limitations-implications / Practical implications / Originality-value), capped around 250 words, replaces FIELD_SKILL's single-paragraph unstructured abstract. Confirmed in all 10 sampled articles across 2017–2025.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
**PRACTICE ONLY / RELAXATION.** Topical scope is broader than the ABS 4*/4 finance baseline implies — sampled articles include tax policy, HR/marketing education quality, pension system design, and corporate governance survey research alongside conventional asset-pricing/behavioral-finance topics. As an ABS 1 journal, methodological rigor expectations (e.g., depth of robustness treatment, identification strategy scrutiny) appear more relaxed than FIELD_SKILL's ABS 4*/4-derived norms — several sampled abstracts describe single-method survey/questionnaire designs (Likert-scale surveys, cross-sectional correlational designs) without the explicit identification-strategy framing FIELD_SKILL describes as near-universal. This is a RELAXATION of rigor expectations, not a new norm — full-text was not accessible to confirm the extent.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting an unstructured, single-paragraph abstract — JEFAS (via Emerald house style) requires the labeled-subheading structured abstract format; this is the most likely mechanical rejection point for authors used to top-tier finance journal conventions.
- Assuming ABS 4*/4-level identification-strategy rigor is mandatory — this ABS 1 journal has published survey/questionnaire-based studies with lighter methodological scrutiny than FIELD_SKILL describes as standard; authors targeting JEFAS should not over-invest in defending identification strategy at the expense of clarity, though doing so is never a liability.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
