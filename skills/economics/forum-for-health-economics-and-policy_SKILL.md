# Journal Writing Skill: Forum for Health Economics and Policy

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Walter de Gruyter (De Gruyter Brill)
- **Scope:** Health economics and health policy, with particular emphasis on the value of medical innovation — pharmacoeconomics, cost-effectiveness/GCEA, R&D investment, insurance/Medicare policy, and market failures in health care markets.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.degruyterbrill.com/publication/journal_key/FHEP/downloadAsset/FHEP_Instructions%20for%20Authors.pdf (generic De Gruyter "Information for Authors" template — contains unreplaced `%%Journal_Name%%` placeholders in the editorial-office footer, indicating this is boilerplate shared across many De Gruyter journals rather than FHEP-bespoke guidance)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 (abstract/front-matter level): "How Default Choice and Priority Rule Affects Organ Donation" (Das & Mitra, 2025); "Optimal Taxation of Cigarettes and E-Cigarettes" (2023); "California Hospitals' Rapidly Declining Traditional Medicare Operating Margins" (Gaudette & Bhattacharya, 2023); "Utilization Management in the Medicare Part D Program and Prescription Drug Utilization" (Andersen, 2021); "Association between Economic Growth, Mortality, and Healthcare Spending in 31 High-Income Countries" (Sanmarchi et al., 2021); "Using the Health and Retirement Study for Research on the Impact of Working Conditions on the Individual Life Course" (Mullen, 2022); "Reducing Nonresponse and Data Linkage Consent Bias in Large-Scale Panel Surveys" (Sakshaug, 2022); plus titles/scope reviewed for "Estimating the Value of Ensifentrine..." (2025), "Two Approaches for Measuring Treatment Value Under Uncertainty" (2025), "The Muslim Ban and Preventive Care for Children of Middle Eastern Ancestry" (2024).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Loosely specified: (1) Introduction (heading optional), (2) subsequent body sections with tables/figures, (3) Appendices, (4) explanation of symbols, (5) References, (6) figures supplied separately. No mandated section names or count beyond this generic shell — the guidelines note "the final structure of the article will depend on the focus." No standalone abstract/data/methods/results template is imposed.

### Length
No explicit word or page limit stated in the guidelines.

### Abstract
No structured-abstract requirement and no stated word limit. Observed articles use short, unstructured, single-paragraph abstracts (FIELD DEFAULT).

### Citations
**Chicago Manual of Style author-date system** (parenthetical in-text citation + author-date reference list), per the Chicago Manual of Style's Author-Date guidance. This is a specific style mandate — distinct from the generic "author-year" description in FIELD_SKILL, though functionally similar in-text (still parenthetical author-year).

### Headings
No mandated heading case convention beyond "be consistent in whether or not you use headline case." No numbering convention specified.

### Tables and Figures
Standard De Gruyter production rules: tables numbered with Arabic numerals, self-explanatory with captions; large/complex tables should go to supplementary material or an appendix; figures submitted as separate files (not embedded), minimum 300 dpi (1200 dpi for line drawings), grayscale-safe design encouraged (patterning over shading for bar charts). These are production/typesetting rules, not structural or rhetorical norms.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Tables must not contain lists, images, or dashed lines. Mathematical expressions must not be rendered as images. No underlining for emphasis, foreign terms, or titles — italics only.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Several spot-checked articles are **methods/survey-oriented pieces** rather than standard empirical identification papers — e.g., Mullen (2022) surveys a dataset's utility for a research agenda, and Sakshaug (2022) reviews methods for reducing survey nonresponse bias. These lack a dedicated Empirical Strategy/Identification section and read more like a research-agenda or methods-review essay than an ABS 4-style empirical paper. RELAXATION — this format is not seen at the ABS 4*/4 level per FIELD_SKILL and reflects FHEP's openness to narrower applied/expository contributions alongside standard empirical papers.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
JEL classification codes appear inconsistently — present in some articles (e.g., Andersen 2021: I11, I13) and absent in others (Sanmarchi et al. 2021; Mullen 2022; Sakshaug 2022). PRACTICE ONLY / low-enforcement; not a stated requirement in the guidelines and not reliably followed.

## Common Pitfalls
- Using a numbered-footnote or author-number citation style instead of Chicago author-date — guidelines explicitly require Chicago Manual of Style author-date.
- Embedding figures inline in the Word document instead of submitting them as separate files as instructed.
- Assuming a rigid IMRaD template is mandatory — FHEP's own guidelines describe structure as flexible and focus-dependent, so methods-review or research-agenda-style contributions are acceptable alongside standard empirical papers.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
