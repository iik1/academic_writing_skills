# Journal Writing Skill: Business Perspectives and Research

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN (journal is a general management/business journal; observed content skews finance, governance, and organizational behavior rather than ethics/CSR specifically)
- **Publisher:** SAGE (in association with K. J. Somaiya Institute of Management, Mumbai)
- **Scope:** Publishes conceptual, empirical, and applied research testing, extending, or building management theory; strong empirical/quantitative orientation with heavy representation of Indian and other emerging-market firm-level studies (corporate governance, earnings management, entrepreneurship, finance, HR/OB).
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/bpr
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 21 titles/authors surveyed across Vol. 14 Issues 1–3 (2026) for topical/title pattern; 6 read in abstract-level detail (Goswami, Maji & Hussain 2023; Goel & Khandelwal 2023; Arora & Soni 2023; Isha & Mangala 2023; Fayaz, Khandai & Zupic 2023; Iram, Bilal, Ahmad & Latif 2022). Full text was not accessible (no institutional proxy); access limited to abstracts, titles, and guideline pages via RePEc/IDEAS and search-engine snippets. This is a good-faith sample appropriate for an ABS 1 journal per the assignment instructions.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Title page (separate, fully anonymized — all identifying information must be removed from the main manuscript and placed only on the title page) → Abstract → Keywords → main manuscript body → Statements and Declarations section → References. No PRISMA/other mandated template beyond this; heading levels simply must be "clear" with sections "clearly defined" (no prescribed labels beyond FIELD_SKILL's article-type templates).

### Length
Initial submission: strictly 6,000–8,000 words. Final accepted/revised version may run up to ~14,000 words. This is narrower at the low end than FIELD_SKILL's general 10,000–15,000+ word range for quantitative deductive management papers — RELAXATION relative to field norm, driven by ABS 1 tier.

### Abstract
Unstructured, exactly 200 words (not the 150–250 range FIELD_SKILL allows generally — narrower, single fixed target). Must concisely state purpose, major findings, and conclusions. Clinical trials require registration details appended at the end of the abstract (not relevant to management submissions in practice).

### Citations
APA style (guidelines cite 6th edition specifically, though SAGE has since moved several journals to APA 7th — verify current edition at submission time). Every in-text citation must have a corresponding reference-list entry and vice versa (checked mechanically). No stated reference-count limit.

### Headings
No prescribed heading scheme beyond FIELD_SKILL's article-type templates; guidelines only require heading levels be clear and sections clearly defined.

### Tables and Figures
Figures numbered consecutively in order of appearance; minimum 300dpi resolution; color figures free online. No other journal-specific table/figure rules stated.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Preprints not accepted (stricter than many management outlets).
- AI chatbots cannot be listed as authors.
- Any identifying author information in the main manuscript body is prohibited (double-blind review — must appear only on the title page).
- Minimum 4–6 keywords required, listed after the abstract, as specific as possible to the research topic (not stated in FIELD_SKILL).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
Preprint prohibition and strict double-blind anonymization requirement (identifying information confined to a separate title page) are stated more explicitly and strictly than typical FIELD_SKILL guidance — ENFORCED via guidelines, consistent with SAGE's standard double-blind policy for this title.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Titles frequently use a colon-separated main title + descriptive/methodological subtitle (e.g., "Board Composition and Performance of Indian State-owned Enterprises: Moderating Role of Leverage"; "An Optimal Proportion for Independent Directors in the Boardroom: An Empirical Study") — PRACTICE ONLY, not stated in guidelines, but common enough across the sampled titles to note. FIELD_SKILL does not address title conventions.

Empirical content sampled skews strongly toward quantitative firm-level and archival studies (panel regressions, DEA, event studies) on Indian and other emerging-market contexts — PRACTICE ONLY / topical tendency, not a stated editorial restriction, and does not override FIELD_SKILL's methods guidance.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting outside the 6,000–8,000-word initial-submission band (this journal enforces a narrower band than typical management journals).
- Leaving identifying information (author names, affiliations, acknowledgments) in the main manuscript rather than confining it to the title page — flagged for strict double-blind review.
- Submitting a manuscript previously posted as a preprint.
- Fewer than 4 or more than 6 keywords, or keywords that are too generic rather than topic-specific.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
