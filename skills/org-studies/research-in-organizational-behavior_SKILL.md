# Journal Writing Skill: Research in Organizational Behavior

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** ORG STUD
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** An annual series publishing commissioned integrative review and
  theory-synthesis chapters spanning individual, group, organizational, and
  environmental levels of analysis in organizational behavior; not a venue
  for original empirical studies.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/research-in-organizational-behavior/publish/guide-for-authors

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
The journal publishes **commissioned papers only** ("Research in
Organizational Behavior publishes commissioned papers only" — guide for
authors). It is not an open submission venue for empirical articles;
authors with a comprehensive OB integrative-review idea submit a proposal
to the editorial team rather than a full manuscript. This is the single
most important constraint: the entire FIELD_SKILL empirical-article
structure (Methods, Results/Findings, Hypotheses) does not apply. Chapters
are integrative reviews or theory syntheses, not new-data empirical
studies.

### Length
No explicit word/page limit is stated in the accessible guidance. Chapters
in practice run long-form (published examples span roughly 30-100+ pages),
consistent with an integrative-review format rather than a standard
journal-article length.

### Abstract
The guide instructs that the abstract must "stand alone," stating the
purpose, principal results/argument, and major conclusions, since abstracts
are often indexed and displayed separately from the article. No fixed word
count or structured-fields format is specified.

### Citations
No distinct citation style is mandated beyond standard Elsevier author-date
practice; consult the general Elsevier guide for formatting details.

### Headings
No prescribed heading template. Guidance instead calls for "argument-driven
section headings" organized by conceptual logic rather than a fixed
variable/section inventory — i.e., headings should track the chapter's
thesis, not follow a Methods/Results template.

### Tables and Figures
Guidance emphasizes inclusion of a conceptual figure depicting mechanisms,
cross-level relationships, or unresolved theoretical tensions, rather than
data tables of statistical results.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Editorial guidance discourages: manuscripts that read as standard empirical
articles; flat literature surveys without a clear thesis; papers that mix
levels of analysis without explaining the cross-level logic; and topics
better suited to specialty empirical venues (e.g., AMJ, JOB, OBHDP).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
FIELD_SKILL describes empirical-article macro-structure (Abstract →
Introduction → Theory/Hypotheses → Methods → Results → Discussion) as
CONSENSUS for the ORG STUD field. This journal's chapters instead follow
an integrative-review structure: Introduction framing the fragmented or
underdeveloped state of a literature → thematic sections organized around
constructs, mechanisms, or process stages (not variables or hypotheses) →
proposed integrative framework/model → agenda for future research. There
is no Methods or Results section in the empirical sense; both spot-checked
chapters present a synthesized conceptual model in place of a tested one.
ENFORCED (stated in guidelines and confirmed in both full-text chapters).

### Citation Density
FIELD_SKILL describes a density gradient across sections (dense in
intro/theory, sparse in results, dense again in discussion). In this
journal's integrative-review format, citation density remains high
throughout the entire chapter, not just in intro/discussion, since the
chapter's core content (not just its framing) is synthesis of prior
literature. PRACTICE ONLY (observed in both spot-checked chapters; not
separately stated in guidelines beyond the general injunction against
"flat" surveys).

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Chapters close with an explicit "agenda for future research" section that
functions as a primary deliverable of the chapter (not a brief coda as in
FIELD_SKILL's Discussion/Conclusion norm), since the contribution of an
integrative review is partly to define open questions for the field.
PRACTICE ONLY (observed in both spot-checked chapters).

Hypotheses, in the FIELD_SKILL sense of numbered, testable predictions,
are absent; the deliverable is instead a proposed integrative framework or
typology. RELAXATION of the FIELD_SKILL quantitative-tradition norm
(consistent with the journal's non-empirical remit; not itself a novel
positive norm).

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting an unsolicited standard empirical manuscript: the journal
  accepts commissioned chapters/proposals only, not open empirical
  submissions.
- Writing a chapter with Methods/Results/Hypotheses sections modeled on a
  quantitative FIELD_SKILL article; this journal expects an integrative
  synthesis structure instead.
- Treating the literature review as a flat catalogue rather than building
  toward an explicit thesis or integrative framework, which editorial
  guidance specifically discourages.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
