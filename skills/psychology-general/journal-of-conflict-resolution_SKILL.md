# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Conflict Resolution

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** PSYCH (GENERAL)
- **Publisher:** SAGE Publications
- **Scope:** Interdisciplinary social-scientific research and theory on human
  conflict, with a strong emphasis on international and civil conflict
  (interstate war, civil war, terrorism, crisis bargaining, nuclear
  strategy), plus intergroup and interpersonal conflict. Empirical
  (quantitative/experimental) and formal (game-theoretic) work both
  common; this journal sits at the intersection of political science,
  economics, and psychology rather than being a mainstream psychology
  outlet.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/jcr
  (confirmed accessible via search-engine cache; direct fetch returned 403)
- **Field baseline:** psychology-general-field-writing-style

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No fixed section template is mandated; the journal accepts both formal
(game-theoretic/analytical) and empirical papers, so IMRaD is not
enforced as rigidly as FIELD_SKILL's empirical-report norm. Manuscripts
must include a separate anonymized title page (author name/affiliation
only there) for double-anonymized review — full text and all other files
must be free of identifying information.

### Length
Minimum ~8,000 words, maximum 11,000 words, double-spaced throughout,
footnotes/references/tables/graphs on separate pages. Word count must be
stated on the title page. FIELD_SKILL specifies no length norm, so this
is a journal-specific addition rather than a delta.

### Abstract
Unstructured abstract, maximum 150 words — shorter than typical empirical
psychology abstracts and with no required subheadings/fields.

### Citations
Chicago Manual of Style (author-date, in-text parenthetical), not
APA — the dominant format assumed implicitly by FIELD_SKILL for this
field. Reference lists follow Chicago author-date formatting, not APA.

### Headings
Not specified beyond general Chicago formatting; no field-mandated
heading taxonomy.

### Tables and Figures
Tables, graphs, and footnotes must be placed on separate pages at the
end of the manuscript for review, not integrated near first textual
reference — this is the opposite of FIELD_SKILL's norm of placing
tables/figures near their first mention.

### Explicit Prohibitions
No simultaneous submission to another journal; submission implies
commitment to publish if accepted. Mandatory data/replication-materials
deposit for quantitative empirical articles: all data, code, program
recodes, and an explanatory README must be provided before publication;
confidential/identifying respondent information must be stripped. The
journal will not publish an article until this material is received.
Double-anonymized review is strictly enforced — author-identifying
content must be absent from the manuscript body.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Structural Deviations
This journal routinely publishes purely formal/game-theoretic papers
(e.g., Larsen 2024 on nuclear first use, Güth et al. on ultimatum
bargaining, De Jaegher on repression) alongside empirical ones. For
formal papers, structure is organized around model setup, propositions/
results, and discussion rather than IMRaD — a mode FIELD_SKILL does not
address at all, since its baseline journals are near-exclusively
empirical or narrative-review. RELAXATION: empirical articles here are
not held to FIELD_SKILL's multi-study Study 1/Study 2/General Discussion
convention; single-study, single-dataset designs (e.g., the UCDP dataset
feature, weak-states terrorism study) are standard, consistent with this
journal's political-science/economics adjacent norms rather than
mainstream experimental psychology practice.

### Statistical Reporting
Content here draws heavily on political-science/international-relations
quantitative conventions (event-count models, survival/duration models,
dyadic panel data, formal proofs) rather than experimental-psychology
statistics (ANOVA, t-tests with Cohen's d). RELAXATION: FIELD_SKILL's
emphasis on effect sizes with confidence intervals for every focal test
is not visibly enforced at the abstract level; abstracts more often
foreground substantive/directional findings (e.g., "state failure favors
survival," "territorial control fosters termination") than effect-size
statistics, consistent with the ABS-3 tier and this journal's disciplinary
mix.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
Subject matter is concentrated on interstate and civil conflict, terrorism,
cyber conflict, and nuclear strategy rather than the interpersonal/social-
cognitive topics typical of FIELD_SKILL's baseline journals — authors
should expect reviewers versed in international-relations and political-
economy literatures, not mainstream social/personality psychology.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting with author-identifying material anywhere outside the
  separate title page — the journal enforces double-anonymized review
  strictly.
- Failing to deposit complete replication data/code/README before the
  publication deadline; the journal withholds publication until this is
  received.
- Using APA citation format instead of the required Chicago author-date
  style.
- Placing tables/figures inline rather than on separate pages as required
  for the review manuscript.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
