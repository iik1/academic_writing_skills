# Journal Writing Skill: Financial Review

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell (official journal of the Eastern Finance Association)
- **Scope:** Publishes original empirical, theoretical, and methodological research across all areas of financial economics (asset pricing, banking, corporate finance and governance, derivatives, investment management, market microstructure, M&A, risk management, securities pricing). Scope is broad and matches the FINANCE field baseline with no narrowing.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/15406288/homepage/forauthors.html (accessible via search-engine cache/summary; direct fetch returned HTTP 402)
- **Field baseline:** finance-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 15 (abstract/front-matter level via IDEAS/RePEc, spanning 2020, 2024, 2025, 2026 issues): "Fifty years of The Financial Review: A bibliometric overview" (Baker, Kumar & Pattnaik, 2020); "Determinants of commodity market liquidity" (Jain, Kayhan & Onur, 2024); "Share repurchases and managerial reference points" (Clarke, Norris & Schrowang, 2024); "Board cultural diversity and firm performance under competitive pressures" (Dodd, Frijns, Gong & Liao, 2024); "Dual-Class Shares and Firm Valuation" (Lel, Netter, Poulsen & Qin, 2025); "Risk Tolerance, Commitment Risk Across Policy Type, and the Demand for Life Insurance" (Carson, Cole & Fier, 2025); "Measuring Investor Outcomes" (Bessembinder, 2026); "Female CFOs and Relationship Lending" (Hao & Pham, 2026); "Hitting the Right Note: Lexical Complexity on ICO Success" (Thewissen et al., 2026); "Puzzles, Tensions, and the Research Agenda for Biodiversity Finance" (Lucey & Vigne, 2026); "The Effects of Foreign Directors on CEO and Director Compensation" (Borrelli, Cumming, Frye & Whyte, 2026); "Beyond Borders: How Institutional Ownership Drives Chinese Firms' International Expansion" (Xiong, Liu, Wu & Gao, 2026); "Does the Composition of the Payout Mix Affect Firms' Market Longevity?" ## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Required sections: abstract, introduction, methods, results, conclusions (standard IMRaD-style skeleton, consistent with FIELD_SKILL's canonical structure). No journal-specific section beyond this is mandated.

### Length
Text including references should be approximately 40 double-spaced pages (12-point font); total manuscript (with tables/figures/appendix) must not exceed 60 pages. FIELD_SKILL does not specify a length limit — this is a hard, journal-specific ceiling.

### Abstract
No stated word limit or structured-field requirement found in the guidelines; observed abstracts are unstructured single paragraphs, consistent with FIELD_SKILL.

### Citations
Guidelines state references "may be submitted in any style or format, as long as it is consistent throughout the manuscript" — a RELAXATION at the submission stage relative to FIELD_SKILL's implied author-year norm. In practice, published articles use standard author-year citation, so this relaxation applies to the initial submission/review process, not the final published format.

### Headings
No journal-specific heading convention stated; follows FIELD_SKILL norm (Arabic numbering).

### Tables and Figures
Each figure and table should start on a separate page, include legends, and be placed sequentially at the end of the manuscript (rather than embedded in-text) for the submission version. This is a submission-formatting requirement, not necessarily reflected in the final typeset article.

### Explicit Prohibitions
None stated beyond standard originality/exclusivity requirements typical of Wiley journals.

### Other
Offers "Free Format" submission (no mandatory formatting on initial submission). A submission fee ($175, payable via the Eastern Finance Association) applies to new submissions but not resubmissions after Revise & Resubmit — an administrative rather than writing-style matter, noted for completeness.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Structural Deviations
PRACTICE ONLY / low-prevalence: at least one sampled piece ("Puzzles, Tensions, and the Research Agenda for Biodiversity Finance") is a perspective/research-agenda article rather than a standard empirical IMRaD paper, proposing a prioritized research question list instead of reporting original data analysis. This indicates the journal occasionally accepts agenda-setting or perspective pieces alongside standard empirical papers, a category not addressed in FIELD_SKILL. Based on a single article in the sample — treat as an occasional format, not a norm to emulate by default.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
RELAXATION: citation format is unenforced at submission (any consistent style accepted), a lighter-touch requirement than the FIELD_SKILL-implied author-year expectation, though final published output still converges on author-year.

## Common Pitfalls
- Exceeding the 60-page total manuscript cap (or ~40 double-spaced pages of text) — this is a firmer ceiling than typical ABS 4/4* finance journals impose explicitly.
- Assuming a rigid citation style is required at submission; the journal accepts any consistent style for the initial manuscript under Free Format submission, but authors should not mistake this for license to skip consistency altogether.
- Submitting without the required $175 EFA processing fee proof, which delays desk review (administrative, not a writing issue, but a common submission mistake).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
