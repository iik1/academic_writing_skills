# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Contemporary Accounting and Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ACCOUNT
- **Publisher:** Elsevier
- **Scope:** Publishes high-quality manuscripts rigorously applying economics and legal theory to accounting/auditing, with emphasis on empirical research. Focus on Asia-Pacific region but accepts global submissions in financial contracts, corporate governance, capital markets, financial institutions, and economics of organizations.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-contemporary-accounting-and-economics/publish/guide-for-authors
- **Field baseline:** account-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 12+ (via RePEc titles from 2025–2026: Stethoscopes to Boardrooms CEO medical degree; Stock price crashes and systematic risk; Pay inequality and firm performance; Hunting for goodwill in de-SPAC; Digital mergers and acquisitions; Audit committee attributes; Climate change exposure; Key audit matter disclosures; Earnings announcement timing; Product competition and R&D transparency)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Manuscript sections must be **clearly defined and numbered** (1., 1.1, 1.1.1, 1.2, etc.)
- Acknowledgements must appear **only on the title page**, not as footnotes to the main text. This is a requirement of the journal's double anonymized review process.
- Separate submission required: title page with author details + anonymized manuscript (without author names or identifying information).

### Length
- **Abstract:** Concise, factual, **not exceeding 250 words**. Must briefly state purpose, principal results, and major conclusions.
- Abstract must stand alone and be comprehensible independent of the main text.
- No explicit word or page limit for full manuscript stated in public guidelines; follow Elsevier house norms (typically 8,000–15,000 words for empirical papers).

### Abstract
- **Format:** Unstructured narrative (single paragraph).
- **Word limit:** 250 words maximum.
- **Content:** Purpose, principal results, major conclusions.
- **Avoid:** References (unless essential; cite author and year if necessary); non-standard abbreviations (define at first mention if essential).

### Citations
- **Style:** Author-date (Elsevier standard). In-text: "(Author Year)" or "Author (Year)". Multiple citations separated by semicolons, in alphabetical or chronological order per house style.
- Follows FIELD_SKILL norms for citation density by section.

### Headings
- **Numbering:** Arabic numerals (1., 2., 3. for main sections; 1.1, 1.2 for subsections; 1.1.1, 1.1.2 for sub-subsections).
- **Formatting:** Heading style not explicitly mandated in public guidelines; follow Elsevier conventions (title case common in Elsevier titles).

### Manuscript Submission Format
- **File format:** Editable source files required (Word .doc/.docx or LaTeX .tex). PDF not acceptable for main submission.
- **Layout:** Single-column for Word submissions. Double-column permitted only for LaTeX.
- **Graphics and tables:** Editable source files for figures, tables, and text graphics required (not image files).

### Tables and Figures
- Placed after references or at end of manuscript.
- Tables carry **panel labels, variable definitions in notes, and explicit reporting of standard-error clustering** or other statistical choices.
- Follows FIELD_SKILL norms for table conventions.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Acknowledgements must not appear as footnotes in the anonymized manuscript; move to title page only.
- PDF is not an acceptable source file format for submission.
- Non-editable formats (e.g., image-only figures) are not acceptable.

### Ethics and Data Statements
- IRB approval required for experimental papers.
- Data availability statements increasingly required; see journal announcements for current policy.
- If AI or AI-assisted tools are used in the research design or methodology, describe their use reproducibly: name of model/tool, version numbers, manufacturer.

### Review Process
- Double anonymized peer review: author identities concealed from reviewers and vice versa.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Typical Article Structure:** Follows field baseline (Introduction, Hypothesis Development/Theory, Data and Research Design, Results, Discussion and Conclusion, References, Appendix).
- **Voice and Register:** First-person plural "we" dominant; active voice dominant; formal register; confidence calibration (well-identified findings stated declaratively; mechanistic interpretations hedged).
- **Sentence and Paragraph Style:** Medium-to-long sentences; dense paragraphs; high technical density; regression equations and notation written out fully.
- **Methods Conventions:** Past tense in methods; high procedural detail; identification strategy emphasized; robustness checks expected (3–5 minimum); descriptive statistics table near-universal.
- **Results and Reporting Norms:** Tables as primary vehicle; precise statistical narration; results organized by hypothesis; null results reported straightforwardly; economic significance expected alongside statistical significance.
- **Discussion and Conclusion Norms:** Merged section standard; brief restatement of research question; summary of findings; explicit limitations paragraph (strong norm); future research directions.
- **Hedging and Epistemic Caution:** Medium hedging calibrated to identification strength; causal claims reserved for exogenous identification; "suggest," "indicate," "consistent with" standard locutions.
- **Paraphrase vs. Quotation:** Paraphrase universal; direct quotation of prior scholarship essentially absent (quantitative tradition).

## Common Pitfalls
> Specific submission mistakes to avoid for THIS journal.

1. **Acknowledgements in footnotes or main text:** Must appear only on the title page (anonymization requirement). Placing acknowledgements in the body text violates double-blind review and may trigger desk reject.

2. **Submitting PDF or non-editable source files:** The journal requires .doc/.docx or .tex with editable tables and figures. Submitting a PDF or image-embedded figures will be rejected at the desk review stage.

3. **Single-anonymized submission:** Ensure the anonymized manuscript contains no author names, institutional affiliations, or identifying details (e.g., "our prior work by Author [2020]" should be "prior work by Author [2020]"). The title page submission is separate.

4. **Abstract exceeding 250 words:** The journal enforces the 250-word hard limit. Overlong abstracts will be returned for revision before review.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
