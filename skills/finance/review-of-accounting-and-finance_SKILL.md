# Journal Writing Skill: Review of Accounting and Finance

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** FINANCE
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing (ISSN 1475-7702)
- **Scope:** Publishes original empirical and review research spanning accounting and finance broadly — financial reporting quality, corporate governance, tax, capital structure, investment efficiency, market risk, and emerging technology adoption (e.g. blockchain, AI) in accounting/finance contexts. Global perspective; frequently publishes emerging-market and cross-country studies.

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Standard Emerald journal structure (no journal-specific deviation found). Structured abstract precedes the introduction (see Abstract below).

### Length
No journal-specific word/page limit was confirmed from accessible sources (guidelines page CAPTCHA-blocked). Follow standard Emerald practice of a concise, focused empirical paper; do not assume a specific cap without checking the current guidelines page directly at submission time.

### Abstract
**ENFORCED — deviates from FIELD_SKILL.** Emerald requires a **structured abstract** in four to seven labeled fields, not the single-paragraph abstract described in FIELD_SKILL: **Purpose**, **Design/methodology/approach**, **Findings**, and **Originality/value** are obligatory; **Research limitations/implications**, **Practical implications**, and **Social implications** are included where applicable. Each field capped around 100 words; total abstract ≤250 words. The abstract (and the paper generally, per Emerald's general house style) is written in **third person** ("This paper investigates...", not "We investigate..." or "I investigate..."). Confirmed present in all sampled RAF abstracts.

### Citations
Emerald Harvard style (a specific Emerald variant of Harvard, not APA). In-text: (Adams, 2006); (Adams and Brown, 2006); (Adams et al., 2006) — "et al." italicized. Reference list alphabetical, not numbered; DOI appended where available. This is a numbered/format deviation from FIELD_SKILL's generic "author-year parenthetical" description only in that Emerald mandates a specific Harvard variant with defined punctuation (surname, initials, (year), "title", Journal Name, Vol. X No. Y, pp. Z).

### Headings
Follows Emerald standard numbering (not confirmed to deviate from FIELD_SKILL's Arabic-numeral convention).

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific deviation identified from accessible sources.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Generative AI may not be used to draft or create original manuscript text ("copywriting"); AI-assisted copy-editing (grammar, structure, clarity of the author's own existing text) is permitted. This is a standard Emerald-wide policy, not unique to RAF, but is a hard constraint for submission.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Voice and Register
**RELAXATION / STRUCTURAL, ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL (derived from ABS 4*/4 journals) describes first-person "we"/"I" as the dominant voice. RAF, as an Emerald journal, requires third-person voice throughout the structured abstract, and Emerald house style generally favors third person over first person in the body text as well ("this paper," "the analysis," "the authors find"). Authors targeting RAF should shift away from the first-person-dominant register that FIELD_SKILL describes as standard for top-tier finance journals.

### Statistical Reporting
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm (no delta observed in sampled abstracts; full-text statistical reporting practices could not be verified due to paywall access).

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm in abstract/introduction framing observed; full-text citation density in results sections not verifiable from abstracts alone.

### Structural Deviations
**ENFORCED.** The mandatory structured abstract (see Hard Constraints) is the primary structural deviation from FIELD_SKILL, which assumes a single-paragraph unstructured abstract as the field norm. This is an Emerald house-wide requirement, not unique to RAF, but is not covered by FIELD_SKILL and must be followed for this journal.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
As an ABS 2 journal, RAF publishes a broader range of paper types than the ABS 4*/4 baseline, including literature reviews/bibliometric analyses (e.g. "Towards a sustainable future: a comprehensive review of Green Sukuk") alongside standard empirical papers — a RELAXATION of the FIELD_SKILL assumption that empirical/structural papers are the default article type. No further deviation confirmed.

## Common Pitfalls
- Submitting a conventional single-paragraph abstract instead of the mandatory Emerald structured abstract (Purpose / Design-methodology-approach / Findings / Originality-value, third person) — this is an automatic formatting rejection risk.
- Writing the abstract or paper in first person ("we find...") rather than Emerald's expected third-person register.
- Using APA or numbered citation style instead of the Emerald Harvard variant.
- Using generative AI to draft original text rather than only for copy-editing, which conflicts with Emerald's AI policy.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
