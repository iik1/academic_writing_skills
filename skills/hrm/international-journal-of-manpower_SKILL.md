# Journal Writing Skill: International Journal of Manpower

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Emerald Publishing
- **Scope:** Labour market and employment empirical research, particularly quantitative. Focus on firm-level evidence of labour demand, employment effects, policy impacts. Rejects fuzzy logic, bibliometric reviews, and purely conceptual papers.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/ijm (paywalled; access-limited)
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 1 full article from 2021 (COVID-19 labour market study); limited access to full text beyond this sample

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

### Structure
Follows standard IMRaD-with-extensions macro-structure: numbered title/abstract/keywords section, then numbered sections (introduction, literature review, data, methodology, analysis/results with subsections, conclusions), references, and appendices. Subsections use decimal numbering (6.1, 6.2, 6.3). Appendices permitted and commonly used for detailed tables/data summaries.

### Length
Not explicitly accessible; observed article is 36 pages including appendices. No explicit word limit stated in accessible sources.

### Abstract
Narrative/unstructured format (not structured with Design/Methodology/Findings fields despite search results suggesting otherwise). Observed abstract runs approximately 600 words (longer than typical 250-word limits in economics journals). No explicit statement of abstract word count found in publicly available guidelines.

### Citations
APA style (author-date, alphabetical reference list). Hyperlinked citations to external sources (ScienceDirect, enterprise survey URLs) observed in full-text PDF.

### Headings
Numbered section headings (1. Introduction; 2. Overview of...; 3. Review of Literature; 4. Data; 5. Methodology; 6. Analysis; 7. Conclusions). Subsections use decimal numbering (6.1, 6.2, 6.3). No decorative or unnumbered section breaks observed.

### Tables and Figures
Extensive use of tables in main text and especially in appendices. Tables have descriptive captions. Figures referenced in text. Appendices organized by topic (Appendix 1: Firm Closures; Appendix 2: Changes in the Way of Working, etc.).

### Explicit Prohibitions
Journal statement: "The Journal no longer entertains papers employing Fuzzy type methodologies, bibliometric and literature reviews, or purely conceptual papers." (ENFORCED - confirmed in search results)

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Abstract Format
**Delta: LONGER, UNSTRUCTURED, NARRATIVE.** The baseline expects variable-length abstracts that are typically shorter in structured format. This journal's abstract in the observed article runs ~600 words in a continuous narrative, not segmented by methodology/findings/conclusions. This is significantly more generous than typical HRM field abstracts. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (inferred from sample; not confirmed in guidelines). Authors writing for this journal should expect to write longer abstracts but should follow a narrative rather than structured format.

### Abstract Style & Register
**Delta: CONTEXT-HEAVY, EXPLICITLY HEDGED.** The observed abstract emphasizes contextual framing (the pandemic is ongoing, full consequences unknown) and extensive hedging ("suggests," "probably," "unlikely"). More explicit caveating of limitations within the abstract itself than field baseline typically requires. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (strong signal from observed article).

### Quantitative Methods Reporting
**Delta: GENERAL-TO-SPECIFIC APPROACH EXPECTED.** Methodology section explicitly justifies use of a general-to-specific econometric approach (starting with broad variable set, dropping redundant variables via F-tests). This is not a feature highlighted in the FIELD_SKILL baseline. The paper discusses working from "general to specific" as a deliberate methodological choice. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (explicitly stated in methodology, likely valued by editors for this empirical journal focused on "strong empirical/analytical quantitative research").

### Data Section Detail
**Delta: EXTENSIVE, TRANSPARENT DATA SOURCING.** Data section discusses sampling procedures, implementation reports, weighting decisions (and decision NOT to use weights), and non-response handling in unusual detail. Field baseline expects "detailed enough to support replication," but this journal goes further: explicit discussion of why weights were not used, why analyses combine multiple data sources, what the trade-offs are. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (strong signal from observed article; aligns with journal's stated focus on "strong empirical/analytical" work).

### Appendices
**Delta: EXTENSIVE APPENDICES ARE STANDARD.** The observed article devotes ~8 pages to appendices with detailed breakdown tables by country, sector, firm size, etc. This level of appendix detail is not unusual in HRM field baseline but appears systematic and valued here. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (confirmed in sample).

### Results Reporting Style
**Delta: DUAL-REPORTING (PROSE + TABLES + REGRESSION COEFFICIENTS).** Results sections report regression coefficients with detailed interpretation of marginal effects, significance levels, and magnitudes. Tables embed coefficients with standard errors in parentheses and significance stars. Prose narrates findings without re-deriving coefficients. No evidence of qualitative quotation (as would be seen in qualitative HRM work). **Classification: FIELD DEFAULT** (aligns with FIELD_SKILL norm for quantitative papers, but executed very methodically here).

### Hedging & Caution
**Delta: EXPLICITLY METHODOLOGICAL HEDGING.** Beyond typical hedging of causal claims, this journal appears to expect authors to hedge about research design choices, data limitations, and the knowledge state (e.g., "The pandemic is unprecedented, there is no clear theoretical model to test"). Hedging extends to the research framing itself, not just results. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (strong signal from observed article; reflects lower ABS tier tolerance for preliminary/emerging findings).

### Structured Equation Definitions
**Delta: CLEAR VARIABLE LABELING IN TABLES.** The regression tables include a "Variable" column with full descriptive labels (e.g., "Degree of restriction on internal movement") alongside abbreviated labels for interpretation. This supports transparency and replicability. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (consistent with field strength in "strong empirical work").

### Discussion of Limitations
**Delta: LIMITATIONS INTEGRATED THROUGHOUT, NOT DEFERRED TO CONCLUSION.** The paper discusses limitations in the methodology section (e.g., "A particular limitation of this study are important differences between countries..."), not reserved for a distinct subsection. **Classification: PRACTICE ONLY** (consistent with pragmatic approach to emerging research).

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** Formal academic register with first-person plural permitted (field default). 
- **Sentence and paragraph style:** Medium-to-long sentences with subordination for theoretical qualification and citation support (field default).
- **Signposting language:** Present and common at section transitions (field default).
- **Argumentation structure:** CARS-like introduction establishing practical/theoretical importance and gap (field default).
- **Explicit contribution statements:** Present and re-visited in discussion (field default).
- **Citation density:** Highest in introduction and literature review, lower in results, rising in discussion (field default).
- **Paraphrase vs. quotation:** Paraphrase strongly preferred; direct quotation of other scholars rare (field default).
- **Diplomatic disagreement:** Framed as extension, qualification, or boundary-condition specification (field default).
- **Null findings:** Reported straightforwardly, not omitted (field default).
- **Discussion and conclusion structure:** Discussion revisits stated contributions, distinguishes theoretical from practical implications, closes with limitations and future directions (field default).

## Common Pitfalls for This Journal

1. **Submitting papers that are purely conceptual, bibliometric, or literature-review-only.** This journal explicitly rejects these. Focus on empirical quantitative (or high-quality qualitative) research.

2. **Underspecifying data sources and methodological choices.** This journal values transparency about data sourcing, sampling, weighting decisions, and why alternative approaches were not used. Brevity on these points will not be appreciated.

3. **Failing to justify novel methodological approaches.** If using an approach not standard in the field (e.g., general-to-specific modeling), explain its rationale clearly in the methodology section.

4. **Overclaiming generalizability.** Given the journal's ABS 2 tier and focus on emerging crises or novel contexts, excessive caveating of limitations is better than overclaiming. However, see next pitfall.

5. **Hedging to the point of incoherence.** Balance extensive methodological hedging with clear, substantive findings. The observed article hedges the research design but presents findings directly.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
