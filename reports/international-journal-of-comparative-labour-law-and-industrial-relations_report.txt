DELTAS FOUND
============

Total distinct deltas: 4 (all documented and classified)

1. CITATION PLACEMENT AND DENSITY (ENFORCED DELTA)
   - Journal mandates footnote-only citation style
   - Deviates from HRM field norm of increasingly common in-text parenthetical citation
   - More typical of legal scholarship than HRM journals
   - Hard constraint; non-compliance triggers revision request

2. MANUSCRIPT LENGTH CONSTRAINT (ENFORCED DELTA)
   - 12,000-word maximum (including footnotes) is tighter than ABS 4/4* HRM field norms
   - Field journals typically permit 15,000-18,000 words
   - Impacts literature review and discussion compression
   - Hard constraint from explicit guidelines

3. LEGAL SCHOLARSHIP REGISTER AND CONCEPTUAL FRAMING (PRACTICE DELTA)
   - Articles employ legal-scholarly language conventions (case law, regulatory analysis, doctrinal development)
   - Leans toward institutional/regulatory framing rather than psychology/organizational behaviour framing typical of top-tier HRM journals
   - Not a rejection criterion but a modal voice characteristic
   - Inferred from article samples (Kramer, Aloisi)

4. COMPARATIVE DIMENSION AS STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENT (ENFORCED DELTA)
   - Journal uniquely requires comparative or international (or regional/EU) analysis
   - Single-jurisdiction articles in-scope only if they substantively engage labour law systems in other countries
   - Reshapes introduction, literature review, and contribution framing
   - Hard constraint from explicit scope statement; desk-reject risk if absent
