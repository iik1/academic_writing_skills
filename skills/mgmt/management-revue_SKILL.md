# Journal Writing Skill: Management Revue

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 2
- **Field:** ETHICS-CSR-MAN
- **Publisher:** Nomos Verlagsgesellschaft mbH und Co. KG (until end 2024); IMR Press (from Volume 36, Issue 1, 2025)
- **Scope:** Peer-reviewed, interdisciplinary European journal publishing qualitative, quantitative, and purely theoretical work on management, organization, industrial relations, and related socio-economic topics across business administration, organizational behavior, economics, sociology, and psychology.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://ojs.nomos-journals.de/index.php/manrev/about/submissions
- **Field baseline:** mgmt-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (Brunzel et al. 2023; Saghih & Ilani 2023; Brinck et al. 2023; Hofmann 2024; Fortagne et al. 2024; Boers & Brozovic 2024; Nguyen & Haar 2024; Okoli & Ogwu 2024)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
- Articles must follow standard academic structure: title page with abstract and keywords, main body text, and end matter.
- No author name or affiliation information appears on the first page; only title, abstract, keywords, and JEL codes.
- Endnotes, references, appendices, tables, and figures are grouped at the end of the manuscript.
- Sequential page numbering throughout.

### Length
- Manuscripts must be **between 7,000 and 10,000 words** (approximately 30 pages in double-spaced type).
- This is a strict constraint; manuscripts outside this range are not compliant.

### Abstract
- Approximately 200 words maximum.
- Must be accompanied by **three to five keywords** and appropriate **JEL codes** (formatted as: JEL: J31, J33, J41, M52).
- Unstructured format (not broken into subsections like Purpose/Methods/Results/Conclusions).

### Citations
- **APA style** for all citations (Author, Year).
- In-text citations: (Author, Year) or Author (Year) when author is part of sentence.
- For three or more authors, use "et al." from first citation.
- Direct quotations require page numbers.

### Headings
- Hierarchical heading system (implied but not explicitly specified in guidelines).
- Heading levels appear to follow standard academic structure: main sections, subsections, sub-subsections.

### Tables and Figures
- Grouped at end of manuscript.
- Must be clearly labeled and referenced in text.
- Table and figure captions should be descriptive.

### Explicit Prohibitions
- Plagiarism is treated seriously and triggers institutional notification.
- Manuscripts must not be previously published or under consideration elsewhere.
- Double-blind review process enforced; no author identifying information in document.

### Submission Method
- Electronic submission as Word file through the Nomos OJS platform (or IMR Press platform from 2025).

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Manuscript Length Constraint
**Delta: ENFORCED.** FIELD_SKILL baseline does not specify strict word limits; Management Revue enforces 7,000–10,000 words precisely. This is more restrictive than typical top-tier management journals (which often allow 12,000–18,000 words). Authors must write concisely; the constraint shapes section depth and example count.

### JEL Codes Requirement
**Delta: ENFORCED.** Not mentioned in FIELD_SKILL. Management Revue requires three to five keywords plus appropriate JEL codes (economic taxonomy codes). This reflects the journal's positioning at the intersection of management and economics. Authors must research JEL classifications relevant to their work (e.g., J31 for labor economics, M52 for organizational behavior).

### Abstract Format (Unstructured)
**Delta: FIELD DEFAULT.** FIELD_SKILL notes both structured and unstructured abstracts appear across outlets. Management Revue uses unstructured format, consistent with FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Citation Density Pattern in Empirical Articles
**Delta: PRACTICE ONLY (observed in 4/8 spot-checked articles).** Quantitative empirical articles in Management Revue show slightly lower citation density in Results sections compared to FIELD_SKILL baseline. Results are presented with minimal literature engagement; interpretation and contextualization occur in Discussion. This is not explicitly stated in guidelines but is consistent with APA norms and observed across Fortagne et al. (2024), Hofmann (2024), Saghih & Ilani (2023).

### Word Limit Impact on Contribution Statement
**Delta: PRACTICE ONLY.** The 7,000–10,000 word constraint (strictly enforced) requires authors to be explicit and concise in stating contributions. Most spot-checked empirical articles state 2–3 contributions in tightly integrated sentences rather than numbered lists. Theory sections are narrower in scope than in longer FIELD baseline journals. Not a hard rule, but a pragmatic adaptation to length.

### Qualitative and Theoretical Work Observed
**Delta: PRACTICE ONLY.** Spot-checked articles include qualitative empirical work (Brunzel et al. 2023 — qualitative discourse analysis of CEO rhetoric) and mixed-methods work, alongside quantitative studies. FIELD_SKILL acknowledges qualitative work but is primarily oriented toward quantitative deductive studies. Management Revue actively publishes all three types.

## Dimensions With No Delta

- **Voice and person:** FIELD DEFAULT. Use of "we" in multi-authored work; active constructions; academic but direct tone. Consistent across all spot-checked articles.
- **Theory section and hypothesis/proposition use:** FIELD DEFAULT. Empirical quantitative studies develop hypotheses (H1, H2…); qualitative and theoretical work develops propositions or conceptual frameworks. Observed in Fortagne et al. (2024), Hofmann (2024), Saghih & Ilani (2023).
- **Methods precision (quantitative):** FIELD DEFAULT. Fixed-effects models, robustness checks, significance reporting with standard errors and asterisks (p-notation). Fortagne et al. (2024) reports structural equation modeling with R² and path coefficients.
- **Discussion of limitations:** FIELD DEFAULT. All spot-checked articles address limitations of scope, measurement, generalizability, or causal claims openly.
- **APA citation style:** FIELD DEFAULT (confirmed in guidelines; consistent across all articles).
- **Headings and section labeling:** FIELD DEFAULT. Sections labeled by substantive content (e.g., "Intuitive-Analytic Dynamics" rather than "Analysis"; "Crisis Response Behavior"). Theory, Methods, Findings/Results, Discussion, Conclusion appear where empirically appropriate.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Exceeding or falling short of 7,000–10,000 word count.** This journal enforces the constraint strictly. Plan section depth accordingly. Remove exploratory tangents and lengthy literature reviews; keep focused.
2. **Forgetting JEL codes.** Manuscripts without JEL codes may be returned desk-rejected before review. Identify the 3–5 codes that best classify your work in the JEL taxonomy (e.g., M52 Organizational behavior; J24 Human capital).
3. **Ambiguous abstract or keywords.** With only 200 words and 3–5 keywords, clarity is essential. Abstract must state research question, method/approach, and main finding. Poor keywords reduce discoverability; choose terms from both economics and management fields.
4. **Author identification on first page.** Even in Word documents, ensure title page contains only title, abstract, keywords, and JEL codes. Remove author names, affiliations, and contact information to maintain double-blind review integrity.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
