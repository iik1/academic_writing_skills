# Journal Writing Skill: Dynamic Games and Applications

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** ECON
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature (Birkhäuser imprint historically; hybrid open-access journal)
- **Scope:** Devoted to all classes of dynamic games (differential games, discrete-time dynamic games, evolutionary games, repeated and stochastic games) and their applications across biology, computer science, ecology, economics, engineering, management science, operations research, political science, and psychology. Publishes regular papers, technical notes, survey articles, and case studies. Editor-in-Chief: Georges Zaccour. It is a cross-disciplinary game-theory journal, not an economics journal in the ABS-4-baseline sense — economics is one of several application domains alongside control engineering, biology, and computer science.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/13235/submission-guidelines (Springer Nature standard journal guidelines; requires accepting a cookie-consent redirect, content summarized via search snippets rather than direct fetch)
- **Field baseline:** econ-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 8 (front-matter/abstract or fuller text level access): "A Dynamic Adverse Selection Multiagent Model with Off-Menu Actions" (Zhang & Zhu, full text via arXiv 2304.06842, published DGA); "Mixed Market Structure and R&D: A Differential Game Approach" (DGA 2024, 14:97–132, abstract/JEL codes); "Information Compression in Dynamic Games" (DGA 2025, abstract); "Public Goods Games in Disease Evolution and Spread" (DGA 2025, abstract); "A Stackelberg Game for Mean-Field Backward Stochastic System Under Partial Information" (DGA 2025, abstract); "Linear-Quadratic Discrete-Time Dynamic Games with Unknown Dynamics" (arXiv preprint corresponding to DGA-style submission, partial); plus special-issue tables of contents for the "Dynamic Games in Economics in Memory of Ngo Van Long" and "In Memory of Tamer Başar" collections (titles/topics only).

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No single mandated IMRaD-style section list — structure varies substantially by paper type (theory/mechanism-design papers, differential-game/control papers, applied economics papers). No universal "Data," "Empirical Strategy," or "Results" sections are required or even common: most DGA papers are theoretical/mathematical and organized around model setup, theorem/proof development, and applications, not empirical estimation. Regular papers, technical notes, survey articles, and case studies are all accepted formats — the journal does not enforce one manuscript type.

### Length
No explicit word/page limit found in public guidelines (thin public documentation for this dimension).

### Abstract
Unstructured, single paragraph, no explicit field/keyword-count limit stated in guidelines. Confirmed by spot-check: abstracts summarize the technical contribution in dense, unhedged mathematical language (e.g., "We introduce the payoff-flow conservation principle to ensure dynamic incentive compatibility and present a unique process...").

### Citations
Author-year in-text citation, consistent with FIELD_SKILL — no delta observed.

### Headings
**ENFORCED (stated in guidelines):** Decimal numbering system for section headings (e.g., 3.1, 3.1.2), with a maximum of three heading levels. This is a Springer Nature house-style requirement, not stated in FIELD_SKILL (which only specifies Roman/Arabic numerals with no depth limit). Confirmed in spot-checked article (Zhang & Zhu): sections numbered 1, 2, 2.1, 2.1.1 etc., consistent with the decimal system.

### Tables and Figures
No journal-specific figure/table rule found beyond generic Springer formatting; not distinguishable from FIELD_SKILL norm in the accessible sample. Note: because most sampled papers are theory/mechanism-design papers, figures are frequently used to illustrate model structure, state-space partitions, or timing diagrams (schematic figures) rather than empirical plots — this is a PRACTICE ONLY pattern tied to the journal's technical/applied-math orientation, not a stated rule.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Standard Springer Nature research-integrity language: no dual submission/prior publication (unless the paper is an explicit expansion of prior work, which must be disclosed), no authorship changes after acceptance without justification, mandatory disclosure of reviewer conflicts when suggesting reviewers. These are generic publisher policies, not journal-specific writing-style prohibitions.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL
> Only dimensions where this journal deviates. Omit any with no delta.

### Voice and Register
**PRACTICE ONLY / structural, not merely stylistic.** FIELD_SKILL describes ECON ABS 4*/4 voice as "we"-driven, active, non-mathematical-notation-heavy prose organized around identification/results narration. DGA papers (at least the theory/mechanism-design subset, which dominates the accessible sample) are written in a much denser, definition-theorem-proof register: formal Definitions, Theorems, Propositions, Corollaries, and Lemmas are numbered and stated as free-standing formal objects, with proofs deferred to a long lettered appendix (Appendix A, B, C... in the sampled article, running to page 56 of 56). This is closer to applied-mathematics/control-theory journal convention than to ABS econ-journal convention. First person "we" is retained (matches FIELD_SKILL), but the surrounding apparatus (Assumption 1, Assumption 2, Definition 6, Theorem 2, Corollary 4, etc., each set off and cross-referenced by number) is much heavier than typical even for ABS 4* theory papers.

### Statistical Reporting
Not applicable to the sampled theoretical/mechanism-design papers — no regression coefficients, standard errors, or empirical estimates appear. For any DGA submission with an empirical or applied-economics component (e.g., the "Dynamic Games in Economics" special-issue papers), FOLLOWS FIELD_SKILL norm by default absent evidence otherwise; the applied-differential-game economics papers sampled (e.g., "Mixed Market Structure and R&D") report JEL classification codes, which is a FIELD DEFAULT for economics papers not otherwise noted in FIELD_SKILL as journal-specific — flagged here as PRACTICE ONLY since it appears in the economics-application subset but is standard economics practice, not a DGA-specific requirement.

### Structural Deviations
**ENFORCED via decimal headings (see Hard Constraints).** Beyond that, the "Related Literature" placement differs from FIELD_SKILL's near-universal pattern: several sampled papers integrate literature engagement directly into a long, discursive Introduction (paragraph-form citation clusters, e.g., "(Jiang, Mazalov, Gao and Wang (2021); Du, Qiao, Zhang, Xue, Zhang, Ma and Chen (2022))"), rather than a standalone Related Literature section — this is PRACTICE ONLY, consistent with variation FIELD_SKILL already allows ("often omitted... surveyed within the Introduction"), so no delta label needed beyond noting it is the norm here rather than the exception.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
**PRACTICE ONLY.** A distinctive DGA convention (control-theory/applied-math influence) is the "Summary of Main Results" subsection placed early in the Introduction (Section 1.1 in the sampled paper), previewing each Theorem/Proposition by number before the reader reaches the formal statements later in the paper. This differs from FIELD_SKILL's "preview of main results" convention, which previews substantive findings in plain economic language rather than by theorem number. Also common: a "Notations" table as a titled appendix section (Table of Notations) summarizing all mathematical symbols — not a FIELD_SKILL feature, reflects the journal's applied-mathematics readership.

## Common Pitfalls
1. Treating this as a standard empirical-economics ABS journal and forcing an Introduction–Data–Empirical Strategy–Results structure: most DGA papers are theoretical, and this structure will look out of place for a model-and-proofs submission.
2. Under-using formal Definition/Theorem/Proposition/Corollary numbering: the sampled papers rely heavily on this apparatus, more than is typical even for ABS 4* economic theory journals; DGA submissions should number and cross-reference formal results explicitly.
3. Embedding full proofs in the main text: the dominant practice in the sample is to state results in the body and relegate all proofs to a lettered technical appendix, keeping the main text readable at the level of results and interpretation.
4. Ignoring the decimal heading-numbering requirement (e.g., using "Section 3" prose headers or Roman numerals) — this is an enforced Springer Nature house-style rule for this journal, unlike the more permissive FIELD_SKILL default.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

<!-- academic-humanizer:start v0.3.3 -->
These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
