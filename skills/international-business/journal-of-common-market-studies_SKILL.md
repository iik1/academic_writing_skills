# Journal Writing Skill: Journal of Common Market Studies

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 3
- **Field:** IB&AREA
- **Publisher:** Wiley-Blackwell
- **Scope:** Leading forum for theoretical and empirical work on European integration, EU politics, economics, and policy; publishes full research articles, Research Notes, Agenda Articles, Symposia contributions, and short policy commentaries.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14685965/homepage/forauthors.html (author guidelines page returns 402/paywall in this environment; constraints below sourced via search-engine cache snippets of that page and the associated guide.htm page)
- **Field baseline:** international-business-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 with abstract content, plus several title-only records, across 2016-2026 (e.g., "Frames and Issue Linkage: EU Trade Policy in the Geoeconomic Turn" 62(4) 2024; "The Politics of the European Minimum Wage" 62(3) 2024; "The European Union's Regulatory Power..." 62(6) 2024; "European Integration and the New Global Disorder" 59(5) 2021; "Another Theory is Possible" 54(1) 2016; "The End of a Noble Narrative?" 54(1) 2016; "Europe in the Postgrowth Era" 2025; "Much More Than a Report" 2025; "The Day After NextGenerationEU" 2026)

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)
> These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
No single mandated section template is specified in the accessible guidelines beyond standard research-article conventions; the journal instead defines distinct manuscript types with different structural expectations: full research articles; Research Notes; Agenda Articles; Symposia contributions; and short policy commentaries that explicitly link original research findings to current EU policy debates.

### Length
Full articles: maximum 8,500 words inclusive of footnotes, appendices, references, and figures/tables/charts; should normally exceed 6,500 words, especially if no tables/figures are included. Word count excludes the abstract and text inside tables. Figures/tables/charts are counted toward the limit using a rule of thumb (half-page figure/table = 250 words; full page = 500 words). Manuscripts over the limit are desk-returned unsubmitted, not sent to review. Research Notes, Agenda Articles, and Symposia contributions: 3,500-5,000 words. Policy commentaries: 2,000-3,000 words, explicitly required to connect original research to live EU policy debates rather than stand as general commentary.

### Abstract
Anonymized, unstructured abstract, maximum 150 words (noticeably shorter than the ~150-250 word FIELD_SKILL range floor). Guidance explicitly instructs authors to write an "engaging" summary and to avoid making the abstract identical to any part of the introduction.

### Citations
Author-date style but not APA; author instructions specify a Harvard-derived house format (e.g., books: Last, F.N. (Year) Title, Edition, City: Publisher; articles: author, year in parentheses, article title in single quotes, journal name italicized, volume/issue, pages).

### Headings
No explicit journal-specific heading rules found beyond standard Wiley house style; follows FIELD_SKILL norm.

### Tables and Figures
Counted against the word limit using the explicit half-page/full-page word-equivalent rule described above (250/500 words) — a journal-specific mechanic not present in FIELD_SKILL.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Previously published material, including working papers, is not acceptable for publication. Authors may use AI tools only as a writing companion, not a replacement for their own writing/analysis; AI use for spelling/grammar/general editing does not require disclosure, but other uses may be probed during review. Duplication-checking software is applied to submissions.

No delta observed; the spot-checked sample skewed toward conceptual/qualitative and policy-analytic pieces (framing analysis, narrative analysis, case studies) rather than heavy quantitative work, consistent with the journal's political-economy/politics orientation, but this reflects topic mix rather than a stated statistical-reporting norm.

### Citation Density
Follows FIELD_SKILL norm based on abstract-level evidence; full-text citation density could not be verified due to paywall access.

### Structural Deviations
RELAXATION: the tiered manuscript-type system (full article / Research Note / Agenda Article / Symposium / policy commentary), each with its own word-count band, is more granular than FIELD_SKILL's binary empirical/conceptual distinction. This is a journal-specific publishing-format choice rather than a redefinition of the field's article-structure norm.

### Other Journal-Specific Patterns
ENFORCED: the abstract must not duplicate introduction text — an explicit anti-redundancy instruction not present in FIELD_SKILL. ENFORCED: abstracts are anonymized as part of the double-blind review process (a submission-mechanics detail, not a writing-style one, but relevant to drafting).

## Common Pitfalls
- Writing a 200-250 word abstract per the FIELD_SKILL default instead of the journal's 150-word maximum.
- Padding a full research article past 8,500 words (inclusive of tables/figures/references) without accounting for the journal's word-equivalent counting rule for figures and tables, risking desk-return without review.
- Submitting a paper that overlaps substantially with a previously circulated working paper without adapting it, given the journal's explicit prohibition on previously published material.
- Drafting the abstract as a near-copy of the introduction's opening paragraph, which the guidelines explicitly discourage.

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
