# Journal Writing Skill: Indian Journal of Labour Economics

## Journal Profile
- **ABS 2024 Rating:** 1
- **Field:** HRM&EMP
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature
- **Scope:** Labour economics, industrial relations, employment, demographics, and human resource management, with emphasis on India and other developing countries. Provides a forum for analytical and policy research.
- **Guidelines URL:** https://link.springer.com/journal/41027/submission-guidelines (paywalled; public summary available)
- **Field baseline:** hrm-field-writing-style
- **Articles spot-checked:** 10 recent articles from Vol. 69, Issue 1 (March 2026), via IDEAS/RePEc and Springer listings

---

## Hard Constraints (Author Guidelines)

These override FIELD_SKILL where they conflict.

### Structure
Follows decimal-system headings with maximum three levels. Article types include original articles, research notes, perspectives, and reviews. Standard IMRaD structure is observed in quantitative empirical work.

### Length
Maximum 6,000 words including tables, appendices, and all other content (excluding front matter and references).

### Abstract
Structured abstract: 150–250 words. Must not contain undefined abbreviations or unspecified references.

### Keywords
4–6 keywords required for indexing.

### Citations
APA format (Version 7 recommended, including all authors up to 20; for larger groups, give first 19 names followed by ellipsis and final author). Reference list includes only works cited in text and published or in-press. References cited by name and year in parentheses.

### Heading Conventions
Decimal system (1., 1.1, 1.1.1 etc.) with no more than three hierarchical levels.

### Tables and Figures
Rules not explicitly stated in public guidelines; follow Springer Nature standard practice (title above, captions descriptive, self-contained).

### Submission Format
LaTeX strongly preferred (submit original source including all style files and figures, plus compiled PDF). Word files also accepted. Double-blind review (authors must anonymize manuscript and provide separate title page with authorship details).

### JEL Codes
Required. An appropriate number of JEL classification codes must be provided.

### Explicit Prohibitions
Manuscript must not be under simultaneous consideration elsewhere. Work must be original and unpublished in any form or language (partial or full) unless it is an explicit expansion of previously published work. Author group, corresponding author, and author order must be finalized at submission; changes generally not permitted in revision stages, and impossible after acceptance.

## Observed Deltas from FIELD_SKILL

### Citation Style and Density
FIELD_SKILL notes that HRM field citation density is highest in introduction and literature review, lowest in results, rising again in discussion. Indian Journal of Labour Economics follows this norm. No deviation observed. **FIELD DEFAULT**

### Voice and Register
FIELD_SKILL describes formal, impersonal register with permitted first-person plural and more narrative prose in qualitative work. Spot-checked article titles and abstracts from IJLE (e.g., "Neoliberal Capitalism and the World of Work: Back to the Basics," "Structural Transformation, Labour Reallocation and Productivity Growth in India") suggest a register aligned with FIELD_SKILL: formal, policy-oriented, and theoretical. No distinctive deviation from field baseline observed. **FIELD DEFAULT**

### Hedging and Caution
FIELD_SKILL notes moderate-to-high hedging, highest in discussion and conclusions, and careful use of causal language in observational designs. Journal-specific guidance not found; article titles suggest serious engagement with causal inference (e.g., "Does Artificial Intelligence Moderate the Growth Effectiveness of Sectoral Employment") consistent with field norms. **FIELD DEFAULT**

### Methods Reporting
FIELD_SKILL specifies detailed methods sections including sample construction, measures, reliability statistics, robustness checks, and discussion of endogeneity and bias. No journal-specific deviation found. **FIELD DEFAULT**

### Results Presentation
FIELD_SKILL expects results reported with reference to tables, narrated in prose without full re-derivation of statistics. Qualitative papers organized thematically. No journal-specific deviation observed. **FIELD DEFAULT**

### Quantitative versus Qualitative Balance
FIELD_SKILL notes no consensus on quantitative vs qualitative preference at the field level. IJLE published titles indicate a heavy quantitative/empirical emphasis (e.g., "Labour Contractualisation and Productivity across Technology and Size Class Categories in India's Organised Manufacturing Sector," "Chronic Unemployment in India," "The Debate of Augmentation and Automation"). This reflects the journal's explicit scope as "a balanced platform for original research, empirical testing, and policy applications in labour economics." **PRACTICE ONLY — empirical quantitative work predominates, but qualitative research is accepted.**

### Theoretical Framing
FIELD_SKILL describes engagement with seminal frameworks (AMO theory, social exchange, job demands-resources, institutionalist perspectives). IJLE articles surveyed show engagement with labour economics theory and institutional/policy contexts (note on "Neoliberal Capitalism," "Hill–Valley Divide and Discourse," "Wage Collapse and Gender Differences") rather than psychological or HRM-specific frameworks. The journal is positioned at the labour economics end of HRM&EMP, not the organizational psychology end. **RELAXATION — IJLE emphasizes labour economics and institutional/development perspectives over psychological/managerial HRM frameworks, consistent with its South Asian, policy-focused scope.**

### Policy and Practical Implications
FIELD_SKILL notes practical/policy implications are treated as required component at this field level. IJLE explicitly positions itself as a forum for "analytical and policy research," and article titles frequently signal policy relevance (e.g., "Labour Reforms," "Wage Collapse," platform worker empowerment). **ENFORCED — policy/development implications are expected and integral to the journal's mission.**

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Exceeding word limit.** The 6,000-word ceiling is strict and includes all content except front matter and references. Lengthy literature reviews or result sections common in HRM field papers will require discipline.

2. **Submitting with author identifiers not redacted.** Double-blind review is mandatory; ensure all identifying information (author names, institutional affiliations, acknowledgements to specific projects or funders that reveal identity) is removed from the main manuscript and placed on a separate title page.

3. **Omitting JEL codes or providing too few.** JEL codes are required; failure to include an "appropriate number" (typically 3–4 for empirical labour economics papers) may trigger desk rejection.

4. **Over-theorizing from HRM/psychology rather than labour economics/institutional perspectives.** The journal's scope and audience are labour economists, development researchers, and policy analysts, not organizational psychologists. Framings grounded in institutional theory, economic sociology, or labour process theory are more aligned with the journal's intellectual orientation than purely psychological HRM framings (e.g., psychological contract theory without institutional grounding).

## Avoiding AI-Writing Patterns

These conventions apply regardless of field or journal. Apply them in addition to everything above; only deviate where this journal's own guidelines explicitly require otherwise.

1. **No participial result clauses.** Findings should not carry an "-ing" tail asserting an interpretation the sentence has not earned ("...rose by 12 points, highlighting the importance of enforcement"; "...underscoring the need for validation"; "...demonstrating that norms shifted"). State the finding. If the implication is worth stating, state it as its own sentence.
2. **No sentence-initial transition glue.** Do not stitch paragraphs together with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Ultimately," "Overall," "In essence," "Indeed," or "That said" at the start of a sentence. Transitions belong inside the argument, not bolted onto it.
3. **Limit rule-of-three constructions.** One or two triads per paper is natural ("clear, concise, and compelling"); a triad per paragraph is a tell. Vary the structure.
4. **Replace importance-inflation words with the actual magnitude.** Avoid "crucial," "critical," "pivotal," "vital," "paramount," "invaluable," "essential" as importance markers, and "significantly" or "dramatically" as intensifiers (also statistically ambiguous). Avoid "vast," "myriad," "plethora." State the size of the effect instead of asserting its importance.
5. **Avoid the 2023-era word list.** "Delve," "explore" (as metaphor), "unpack," "navigate," "landscape," "realm," "tapestry," "journey," "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "foster," "bolster," "garner," "showcase," "boast," "seamless," "holistic," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "intricate," "interplay." Also avoid "robust" outside its statistical sense and "comprehensive" as self-description.
6. **No empty balance.** Avoid "while challenges remain," "a double-edged sword," "strikes a balance," "it is not without limitations," "both approaches have merits" — these simulate judgment without making one. Do not open more than one or two sentences per section with a concessive "While X, Y" construction.
7. **Quantify instead of gesturing.** Avoid "a number of," "various," "several key," "a range of," "numerous studies" without citations attached. Give the count or the citation.
8. **No anthropomorphised prose.** Avoid "the data tell a story," "the analysis paints a picture," "this paper seeks to," "the results speak to."
9. **No throat-clearing openers.** Avoid "In recent years, there has been growing interest in...," "With the advent of...," "In today's rapidly evolving...." Delete and start with the claim.
10. **No padding verb phrases.** Replace "serves to," "acts as," "stands as," "functions as," "is able to," "in order to," "due to the fact that," "utilize," "prior to," "commence" with their one-word equivalents.
11. **No wrap-up restatement.** A conclusion should add the implication or end — not repeat what was just said via "Taken together, these results suggest...," "In sum," or "In conclusion" followed by a restated summary.
12. **Vary rhythm.** Avoid clustering sentence lengths in a narrow band, making every paragraph roughly equal, or opening every section the same way.
13. **No interpretive adverb openers.** Avoid "Interestingly," "Surprisingly," "Notably," "Strikingly" — if the fact is surprising, the reader will notice.
14. **No chatbot leakage.** Avoid "Certainly," "Here's," "Let's examine," "we can see that," "as mentioned above" — residue from copy-pasted chat output.
15. **No em dashes.** Do not use em dashes (—) at all. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead, whichever the sentence actually calls for.
16. **No antithesis padding.** Avoid the "X, not Y" contrast construction as a rhetorical flourish ("a correlation, not a coincidence"; "a policy shift, not a technical adjustment") unless the contrast is doing real argumentative work and stated once.
17. **No hedges without content.** Avoid "somewhat," "arguably," "to some extent," "in some sense," "it could be argued," "seems to," "may potentially" when they are not carrying a specific, defensible qualification. Hedge only where the uncertainty is real and specify its source.
18. **No fillers.** Cut "basically," "essentially," "simply," "just," "really," "very," "quite," "actually" unless the sentence fails without them.
19. **No flourish labels.** Do not bold or set off rhetorical labels like "The Bottom Line:," "Key Insight:," "Takeaway:," "Bottom line," "The upshot" — state the point in the sentence itself.
20. **No self-praise.** Do not characterize the paper's own contribution as "novel," "innovative," "unique," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "groundbreaking." Describe what it does; let the reader judge novelty.
21. **No topic-announcing openers.** Avoid "This section discusses...," "This paper will discuss...," "Let's turn to...," "We now turn our attention to...." Open with the content itself.
